## Short notes

- Regionalism: strong loyalty to a region based on language, ethnicity, culture or economic grievance; can be positive (demands for autonomy, development) or negative (secessionism, 'sons of the soil' hostility to migrants).
- Causes: linguistic and cultural identity, uneven regional development, historical distinctiveness, political mobilisation by regional elites, and migration pressures.
- JVP Committee (Nehru, Patel, Pattabhi Sitaramayya, 1948-49) and Dar Commission (1948) initially advised against reorganising states on language.
- Potti Sriramulu's death after a fast (Dec 1952) led to Andhra State (1 Oct 1953), the first linguistic state.
- States Reorganisation Commission (1953: Fazl Ali, K.M. Panikkar, H.N. Kunzru) led to the States Reorganisation Act 1956 - 14 states and 6 Union Territories.
- Later changes: Maharashtra and Gujarat (1960), Nagaland (1963), Punjab-Haryana (1966), Himachal statehood (1971), Meghalaya, Manipur, Tripura (1972), Sikkim (1975), Mizoram, Arunachal, Goa (1987), Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand, Jharkhand (2000), Telangana (2014), J&K split into two UTs (2019).
- Dravidian movement: DMK founded 1949; anti-Hindi agitation of 1965; DMK formed government in Madras in 1967 - a landmark for regional parties.
- 'Sons of the soil' politics: Shiv Sena (1966) in Bombay against migrants; similar anti-outsider movements in Assam (Assam Movement 1979-85).
- Regional parties: Shiromani Akali Dal (1920), DMK, AIADMK (1972), Telugu Desam (1982), Asom Gana Parishad (1985), Biju Janata Dal, Trinamool Congress, among others.
- Rajamannar Committee (Tamil Nadu, 1969) and the Anandpur Sahib Resolution (Akali Dal, 1973) demanded greater state autonomy.
- 1967 general election ended Congress dominance in several states; short-lived Samyukta Vidhayak Dal coalitions and frequent defections ('Aaya Ram Gaya Ram' - Haryana MLA Gaya Lal, 1967).
- Central coalitions: Janata Party (1977, Morarji Desai - first non-Congress government); National Front (1989, V.P. Singh); United Front (1996-98, Deve Gowda, I.K. Gujral).
- NDA under Vajpayee (1999-2004) was the first coalition to complete a full term; UPA I and II (2004-14) under Manmohan Singh.
- From 1989 to 2014 no single party won a Lok Sabha majority; the BJP won majorities in 2014 and 2019, while in 2024 it fell short and governs with NDA allies.
- Coalition features: Common Minimum Programme, coordination committees, stronger bargaining by regional parties, sometimes instability; anti-defection law (1985) and 91st Amendment (2003) tried to curb horse-trading.
- Sarkaria Commission (1983, report 1988) and Punchhi Commission (2007, report 2010) examined Centre-State relations, including the Governor's role when no party has a majority.
- S.R. Bommai (1994): majority must be tested on the floor of the House, and Art 356 is subject to judicial review - limiting misuse against opposition-ruled states.

## Punjab angle

- The Punjabi Suba movement led to the Punjab Reorganisation Act 1966: Haryana created, hill areas went to Himachal Pradesh, Chandigarh became a shared capital and UT.
- The Anandpur Sahib Resolution (1973) of the Shiromani Akali Dal sought greater autonomy for states; the Rajiv-Longowal Accord (1985) tried to address Punjab's demands.
- Akali Dal-BJP coalitions governed Punjab in 1997-2002 and 2007-2017; the alliance ended in 2020 over the farm laws.
- Unresolved Punjab-Haryana issues - Chandigarh, SYL canal and river waters - are classic examples of regional politics.

## Deep dive

### Punjab — regional politics timeline (year — event)

- 1920 — Shiromani Akali Dal and SGPC formed during the Gurdwara Reform Movement; 1925 Sikh Gurdwaras Act.
- 15 July 1948 — PEPSU (Patiala and East Punjab States Union) formed from eight princely states.
- 1950s — Punjabi Suba agitation under Master Tara Singh; 1955 morcha; Sant Fateh Singh led from 1960.
- 1956 — "Regional Formula" (Punjabi and Hindi regions with regional committees of the legislature); PEPSU merged into Punjab on 1 Nov 1956.
- 1965–66 — Parliamentary committee under Hukam Singh backed a Punjabi-speaking state; the Shah Commission (Justice J.C. Shah, 1966) drew boundaries.
- 1 Nov 1966 — Punjab Reorganisation: Haryana formed, hill areas to Himachal, Chandigarh a UT and shared capital.
- 1967 — first non-Congress government in Punjab under Gurnam Singh (Akali-led front); toppled by Lachhman Singh Gill's defection (Nov 1967).
- 1969–70 — Darshan Singh Pheruman died fasting for Chandigarh (1969); the Indira Gandhi award (Jan 1970) proposed Chandigarh to Punjab and Abohar–Fazilka to Haryana — never implemented.
- 1973 — Anandpur Sahib Resolution; 1978 — reaffirmed at Ludhiana; 1977 — Parkash Singh Badal's SAD–Janata government.
- 1982 — Dharam Yudh Morcha; 1985 — Rajiv–Longowal Accord and Surjit Singh Barnala's SAD government.
- Feb 1992 — SAD boycott; assembly election held with very low turnout.
- 1996 — SAD's Moga Declaration shifted to a broader "Punjabi" identity and partnership with the BJP; SAD–BJP governments 1997–2002 and 2007–2017.
- 2020 — SAD left the NDA over the farm laws; 2022 — AAP won 92 of 117 seats.
- 2024–25 — Akal Takht declared SAD chief Sukhbir Badal "tankhaiya" (Aug 2024) and pronounced religious punishment (Dec 2024); he was re-elected party president in April 2025.

### Regionalism — theory and current flashpoints

- Types: supra-state (North-East identity), inter-state (Punjab–Haryana water, Karnataka–Maharashtra Belagavi), intra-state (Vidarbha, Gorkhaland, Bundelkhand, Kodagu).
- Myron Weiner's "Sons of the Soil" (1978) analysed nativist movements; Paul Brass studied language politics; Rajni Kothari described the "Congress system" (1964).
- Accommodation tools: linguistic states, Art 371 provisions, Sixth Schedule councils, territorial councils (Gorkhaland Territorial Administration 2012, Bodoland Territorial Region 2020).
- Ladakh: agitation for statehood and Sixth Schedule status; violence in Leh on 24 Sept 2025 and Sonam Wangchuk's detention under the NSA (Sept 2025).
- Language disputes: Tamil Nadu's opposition to the three-language formula under NEP 2020; Maharashtra withdrew its order on Hindi as a third language in primary schools (June 2025).
- Delimitation: southern states and Punjab met in Chennai (Joint Action Committee, 22 March 2025) to oppose a purely population-based seat shift; the April 2026 defeat of the 131st Amendment Bill reflected this coalition.
- Economic regionalism: special category status demands (Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha) after the 14th Finance Commission ended the distinction for devolution.

### Coalition politics — data and institutions

- Single-party majorities in the Lok Sabha: Congress 1952–1984 except 1977 (Janata Party alone had 295); none from 1989 to 2009; BJP 282 (2014), 303 (2019); in 2024 BJP 240 and NDA 293.
- In 2024 the NDA depends on the TDP (16 seats) and JD(U) (12 seats).
- Short-lived governments: Charan Singh (1979, never faced Parliament), Chandra Shekhar (1990–91, minority), Vajpayee's 13-day government (1996), Deve Gowda (1996–97), I.K. Gujral (1997–98).
- Vajpayee's government fell by one vote on 17 April 1999 after the AIADMK withdrew support.
- William Riker's "minimum winning coalition" (1962); "coalition dharma" (Vajpayee); Common Minimum Programmes (United Front 1996, UPA 2004).
- UPA-era National Advisory Council (2004–14) shaped rights-based laws (RTI, MGNREGA, Forest Rights, Food Security).
- Governor and hung assemblies: Sarkaria's order of preference (pre-poll alliance, largest party with support, post-poll coalition); Rameshwar Prasad (2006) — Bihar dissolution unconstitutional; Shivraj Singh Chouhan (2020) — Governor can order a floor test.
- State-level coalitions: Kerala's LDF/UDF alternation, J&K PDP–BJP (2015–18), Maharashtra MVA (2019–22), Bihar's repeated alliance switches.

### Regional parties — facts to remember

- Founding years: National Conference (1932, renamed 1939), DMK (1949), Shiv Sena (1966), AIADMK and JMM (1972), TDP (1982), AGP (1985), BJD (1997), TMC (1998), NCP (1999), TRS (2001, renamed BRS 2022), YSRCP (2011), AAP (2012).
- SAD (1920) is among the oldest regional parties; splinters include SAD (Amritsar) led by Simranjit Singh Mann.
- AAP became a national party in April 2023 — a regional-origin party with governments in Delhi (till 2025) and Punjab.
- The ECI de-listed over 300 registered unrecognised parties in Aug 2025 for not contesting elections since 2019.
- Regional parties gained ministries and bargaining power in coalitions, and pressed for Finance Commission and Governor reforms.
- TDP, founded by N.T. Rama Rao in March 1982, won Andhra Pradesh in Jan 1983 — the fastest rise to power by a regional party; AGP repeated it in Assam (1985) right after the Assam Accord.

### Traps

- First linguistic state: Andhra State (1 Oct 1953) — Andhra Pradesh came in 1956 after merging Telangana areas.
- Two different Shah Commissions: J.C. Shah on Punjab–Haryana boundaries (1966) and J.C. Shah on Emergency excesses (1977–78).
- Rajamannar Committee (Tamil Nadu, 1969) and the West Bengal memorandum (1977) were autonomy demands like the Anandpur Sahib Resolution.
- Anandpur Sahib Resolution adopted in 1973 by the SAD working committee; endorsed at the 1978 Ludhiana conference.
- Punjab's Legislative Council was abolished in 1970 — unrelated to coalition instability but often confused in date questions.
- The Rajiv–Longowal Accord promised Chandigarh to Punjab by 26 Jan 1986 — the transfer never happened.
