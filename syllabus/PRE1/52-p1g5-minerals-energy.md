## Short notes

- Minerals: metallic - ferrous (iron ore, manganese, chromite) and non-ferrous (copper, bauxite, lead, zinc); non-metallic (limestone, mica, gypsum); energy minerals (coal, petroleum, natural gas, uranium, thorium).
- Mineral belts: north-eastern plateau (Chhota Nagpur and Odisha plateau - Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal); south-western plateau (Karnataka, Goa, Kerala); north-western (Aravallis of Rajasthan, Gujarat); Himalayan belt (small deposits).
- Iron ore: haematite and magnetite; Odisha (Mayurbhanj, Kendujhar) is the largest producer; also Jharkhand (Noamundi), Chhattisgarh (Bailadila), Karnataka (Kudremukh) and Goa (export via Marmagao).
- Manganese: Odisha, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka; used in steel-making.
- Bauxite (aluminium ore): Odisha (Kalahandi, Koraput - Panchpatmali), Gujarat, Jharkhand; Odisha is the largest producer.
- Copper: Khetri (Rajasthan), Malanjkhand (Madhya Pradesh), Singhbhum (Jharkhand); mica: Koderma (Jharkhand), Nellore (Andhra Pradesh), Rajasthan.
- Coal: about 98% from Gondwana formations (Damodar valley - Jharia, Raniganj, Bokaro; Son, Mahanadi, Godavari valleys); Tertiary coal in Assam and Meghalaya; lignite at Neyveli (Tamil Nadu).
- Jharkhand, Odisha and Chhattisgarh hold most coal reserves; India's coal output crossed 1 billion tonnes in 2024-25; coal still generates the largest share of electricity.
- Petroleum: Digboi (Assam, oldest oilfield; refinery from 1901), Mumbai High (offshore, 1970s), Ankleshwar and Kalol (Gujarat), Krishna-Godavari basin (gas); India imports over 85% of its crude oil.
- Atomic minerals: uranium from Jaduguda (Jharkhand) and Tummalapalle (Andhra Pradesh); thorium from monazite sands of Kerala coast; Homi Bhabha's three-stage nuclear programme aims to use thorium.
- Nuclear power stations: Tarapur (first, 1969), Rawatbhata, Kalpakkam, Narora, Kakrapar, Kaiga, Kudankulam; target of 100 GW nuclear capacity by 2047.
- Non-fossil sources crossed 50% of India's installed power capacity in 2025, five years ahead of the 2030 NDC target; Panchamrit goal - 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030 and net zero by 2070.
- Solar: National Solar Mission (2010); Rajasthan and Gujarat lead; Bhadla Solar Park (Rajasthan) among the world's largest; PM-KUSUM (2019) for farm solar pumps; PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana (2024) for rooftop solar.
- Wind: Tamil Nadu and Gujarat lead; Muppandal (Tamil Nadu) is a major wind farm; offshore wind potential off Gujarat and Tamil Nadu.
- Other sources: geothermal (Puga valley, Ladakh; Manikaran, Himachal), tidal (Gulf of Khambhat, Gulf of Kachchh, Sundarbans), biomass and waste-to-energy; National Green Hydrogen Mission (2023) targets 5 million tonnes by 2030.
- Conservation and governance: MMDR Act 1957 amended in 2015 (auctions, District Mineral Foundations) and 2023 (critical minerals); National Critical Mineral Mission (2025); KABIL to secure overseas critical minerals.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab is poor in metallic minerals; sand and gravel are its main minor minerals, and illegal sand mining is a governance issue.
- Punjab's power comes from thermal plants (Ropar, Lehra Mohabbat, Talwandi Sabo, Rajpura, Goindwal Sahib) and hydro shares in Bhakra-Beas projects and Ranjit Sagar Dam.
- Paddy straw offers biomass and compressed biogas potential that can cut stubble burning.

## How PPSC asks it

Expect location-based questions (mine/field to state), mineral to type matches, nuclear plant locations and renewable-energy targets. Memorise one or two leading locations for each mineral.

## Deep dive

### Mapping list — Indian minerals (mineral — key places)

- Gold: Hutti (Karnataka; main producer), Kolar Gold Fields (closed 2001), Ramagiri (Andhra Pradesh).
- Zinc–lead: Rampura Agucha (Rajasthan; one of the world's largest zinc mines), Zawar (Udaipur); copper: Khetri (Rajasthan), Malanjkhand (MP), Singhbhum (Jharkhand).
- Chromite: Sukinda valley (Odisha); diamonds: Panna (MP); tungsten: Degana (Rajasthan); gypsum: Bikaner–Nagaur–Jaisalmer (Rajasthan); salt: Gujarat is the largest producer.
- Lithium: GSI reported about 5.9 million tonnes of inferred resources at Salal-Haimana, Reasi (J&K) in February 2023; a lithium block at Katghora (Korba, Chhattisgarh) was auctioned in 2024.
- Rare earths come from beach-sand monazite in Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Odisha, handled by IREL; graphite from Odisha and Jharkhand.
- Coal-bed methane in Raniganj and Jharia; natural gas from Krishna–Godavari (KG-D6) and Bassein; shale prospects in the Cambay basin.
- Refineries: Jamnagar (world's largest refining complex), Guru Gobind Singh Refinery at Bathinda (HPCL–Mittal Energy), Panipat, Mathura, Numaligarh, Barmer (under construction).
- Strategic Petroleum Reserves at Visakhapatnam, Mangaluru and Padur (about 5.33 million tonnes); new caverns planned at Chandikhol and Padur.

### Critical minerals and mining policy (year — step)

- 1957 — MMDR Act; 2015 amendment introduced auctions, District Mineral Foundations (mining-affected areas) and the National Mineral Exploration Trust.
- 2019 — National Mineral Policy; KABIL (NALCO, HCL and MECL joint venture) set up to secure overseas minerals — it acquired lithium blocks in Catamarca, Argentina in 2024.
- 2021 — amendment removed the captive/merchant mine distinction; 2023 — amendment let the Centre auction 24 critical and strategic minerals and created exploration licences.
- June 2023 — first list of 30 critical minerals; India joined the US-led Mineral Security Partnership the same year.
- 2023 — Offshore Areas Mineral (Development and Regulation) Amendment Act enabled auctions of offshore mineral blocks.
- January 2025 — National Critical Mineral Mission (about ₹16,300 crore plus about ₹18,000 crore from PSUs) for exploration, processing, recycling and overseas assets.

### Energy data, institutions and nuclear programme

- Installed power capacity was about 552 GW in August 2026, with non-fossil sources about 54% (crossing 300 GW); coal still produces roughly three-quarters of electricity.
- Large hydropower (above 25 MW) has been counted as renewable energy since March 2019; pumped storage is the main grid-scale storage option.
- Coal ranks: anthracite (small, J&K), bituminous (Gondwana; most used), lignite (Neyveli, Barmer, Kutch), peat; commercial coal-mine auctions began in 2020.
- India imports over 85% of its crude oil; Russia became the largest supplier after 2022; exploration moved from NELP to the Open Acreage Licensing Policy (2016).
- Three-stage nuclear programme (Homi Bhabha): PHWRs on natural uranium → fast breeders (the 500 MWe PFBR at Kalpakkam began core loading in 2024) → thorium reactors.
- Kakrapar Unit 3 (2023) is the first indigenous 700 MWe PHWR; Kudankulam uses Russian VVER-1000 reactors; the Nuclear Energy Mission (Budget 2025-26) funds small modular reactors toward 100 GW by 2047.
- Efficiency: UJALA LED programme (2015), BEE star labelling and the Energy Conservation Building Code; India also backs 'One Sun One World One Grid' (PPSC 2021 asked).

### World minerals and energy — mapping list

- Iron ore: Pilbara (Australia), Carajás (Brazil), Kiruna (Sweden), Mesabi Range (USA), Krivoy Rog (Ukraine).
- Copper: Chuquicamata and Escondida (Chile), Peru, and the Copperbelt of DR Congo–Zambia (Katanga).
- Gold: Witwatersrand (South Africa), Kalgoorlie (Australia), Nevada (USA); China is the top producer.
- Bauxite: Weipa (Australia), Guinea, Jamaica; tin: Kinta valley (Malaysia), Bangka (Indonesia), Bolivia.
- Uranium: Kazakhstan is the largest producer, followed by Canada (Athabasca basin), Namibia and Australia (Olympic Dam).
- Lithium: the 'lithium triangle' of Chile, Argentina and Bolivia holds large brine reserves; Australia leads hard-rock production.
- Oil: Ghawar (Saudi Arabia) is the largest oilfield, Burgan (Kuwait) second; Venezuela has the largest proved reserves; China is the largest coal producer and consumer, India second.

### Punjab minerals and power

- Punjab has almost no metallic minerals; sand and gravel from the Sutlej, Beas and Ravi beds are its main minor minerals, and illegal riverbed mining worsens floods and harms the Indus dolphin habitat.
- State thermal plants: Guru Gobind Singh STP (Ropar) and Guru Hargobind TP (Lehra Mohabbat); private plants at Talwandi Sabo and Rajpura; PSPCL bought the 540 MW GVK Goindwal Sahib plant in 2024; Bathinda's old plant closed in 2018.
- PSPCL mines coal from its captive Pachhwara Central block in Jharkhand, hauled across the country by rail.
- Hydro: Punjab shares Bhakra–Beas output through BBMB and owns Ranjit Sagar (600 MW); the Shanan project at Joginder Nagar (Himachal), leased to Punjab in 1925, became a dispute when the 99-year lease ended in 2024.
- HMEL's Bathinda refinery (commissioned 2012) is Punjab's biggest industrial investment and anchors a petrochemical complex.
- Since July 2022 households get 300 free units of electricity a month, adding to the subsidy bill already driven by free farm power.

### Traps and PPSC patterns

- Gondwana coal (about 98% of reserves) is older and better than Tertiary coal of the North-East.
- Jharia and Bokaro are in Jharkhand; Raniganj is in West Bengal.
- Zawar is zinc–lead, not uranium; Jaduguda is uranium (PPSC 2018 tested these pairs).
- Lignite = brown coal (Neyveli), not 'young bituminous'.
- Monazite gives thorium and rare earths; it is not a uranium ore.
- Large hydro counts as renewable in India (since 2019) but not in some international statistics.

## Practice MCQs (12)

**1.** India's oldest oil refinery and oilfield is at:

(a) Ankleshwar, Gujarat  
(b) Mumbai High  
(c) Barauni, Bihar  
(d) Digboi, Assam

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Digboi, Assam** — Oil was struck at Digboi in the 1880s and the refinery started in 1901.

</details>

**2.** Kudremukh in Karnataka is known for:

(a) Bauxite  
(b) Mica  
(c) Iron ore  
(d) Copper

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Iron ore** — Kudremukh has magnetite iron ore deposits.

</details>

**3.** The main source of thorium in India is:

(a) Monazite sands of the Kerala coast  
(b) Khetri belt of Rajasthan  
(c) Jaduguda mines of Jharkhand  
(d) Neyveli lignite field

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Monazite sands of the Kerala coast** — Monazite on Kerala beaches is rich in thorium.

</details>

**4.** Consider the statements: 1. Gondwana coal accounts for the bulk of India's coal reserves. 2. Neyveli in Tamil Nadu is known for lignite. Which is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Both 1 and 2** — About 98% of coal is Gondwana; Neyveli is India's main lignite field.

</details>

**5.** Jaduguda in Jharkhand is associated with mining of:

(a) Coal  
(b) Mica  
(c) Uranium  
(d) Manganese

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Uranium** — Jaduguda has India's first uranium mine.

</details>

(a) 500 GW  
(b) 175 GW  
(c) 300 GW  
(d) 1,000 GW

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

</details>

**7.** Consider the following statements: 1. Large hydropower projects above 25 MW have been counted as renewable energy in India since 2019. 2. Kakrapar Unit 3 is India's first indigenous 700 MWe pressurised heavy water reactor. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Both 1 and 2** — Both are correct: large hydro above 25 MW was declared renewable by the Government of India in March 2019, and Kakrapar Unit 3 (KAPP-3) is India's first indigenous 700 MWe PHWR (commercial operation 2021). Facts as of Nov 2026.

</details>

**8.** Consider the following statements: 1. Rampura Agucha is known for zinc–lead. 2. Sukinda valley is known for chromite. 3. Hutti in Karnataka is known for copper. Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only  
(b) 2 and 3 only  
(c) 1 and 3 only  
(d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 1 and 2 only** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Hutti in Karnataka is India's main gold mine, not copper, so statement 3 is wrong and 'all three' is the tempting trap. Copper is mined at Khetri (Rajasthan) and Malanjkhand (MP).

</details>

**9.** About 5.9 million tonnes of inferred lithium resources were reported by GSI in 2023 at:

(a) Salal-Haimana, Reasi (Jammu and Kashmir)

(c) Kolar (Karnataka)  
(d) Degana (Rajasthan)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Salal-Haimana, Reasi (Jammu and Kashmir)** — Katghora was the first lithium block auctioned in 2024; Degana is known for tungsten.

</details>

**10.** District Mineral Foundations were created by the amendment to the MMDR Act in:

(a) 2015  
(b) 2021  
(c) 2010  
(d) 2023

**11.** Which country is the world's largest producer of uranium?

(a) Canada  
(b) Namibia  
(c) Australia  
(d) Kazakhstan

</details>

**12.** The Shanan hydropower project, at the centre of a Punjab–Himachal dispute after its 99-year lease ended in 2024, is located at:

(a) Talwara, below the Pong Dam (Punjab)  
(b) Joginder Nagar (Mandi, Himachal Pradesh)
