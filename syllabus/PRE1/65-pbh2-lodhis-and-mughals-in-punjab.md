# Lodhis and Mughals in Punjab

## Short notes

- Bahlol Lodi, governor of Sirhind and Lahore, seized Delhi in 1451 and founded the Lodi dynasty (1451–1526), the last Delhi Sultanate dynasty.
- Daulat Khan Lodi, governor of Lahore under Ibrahim Lodi, invited Babur along with Alam Khan; Guru Nanak served in his modikhana (granary) at Sultanpur Lodhi.
- Babur raided Punjab several times — Bhera (1519), Saidpur/Eminabad (sacked about 1520, lamented in Guru Nanak's Babarvani) — and took Lahore (1524).
- First Battle of Panipat (21 April 1526): Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi, ending Lodi rule; Daulat Khan's hopes of ruling Punjab ended.
- Sher Shah Suri (1540–45) built Rohtas fort (Jhelum) to control the Gakhars and improved the Grand Trunk Road (Sadak-e-Azam) with sarais.
- Humayun regained Punjab and Delhi after the battles of Machhiwara and Sirhind (1555) against Sikandar Shah Suri.
- Akbar was enthroned at Kalanaur (Gurdaspur) on 14 Feb 1556 under Bairam Khan's guardianship; the platform 'Takht-i-Akbari' survives.
- Akbar's subas: Lahore and Multan covered most of Punjab; the Sarkar of Sirhind was in the Suba of Delhi. Akbar held court at Lahore (about 1585–98) and is said to have visited Guru Amar Das at Goindwal.
- Jahangir: Prince Khusrau rebelled in 1606; Guru Arjan was executed at Lahore (1606); Guru Hargobind was held in Gwalior fort; Nur Mahal Sarai (Jalandhar) was built under Nur Jahan.
- Shah Jahan: Shalimar Gardens of Lahore; battles with Guru Hargobind (Amritsar, Lahira/Mehraj, Kartarpur, around 1634–35). Aurangzeb: Badshahi Mosque, Lahore; Guru Tegh Bahadur executed (1675).
- Mughal administration: mansabdari, jagirdari, zabt land revenue; Lahore was a key provincial capital on the GT Road, with kos minars and sarais (e.g., Doraha, Nur Mahal).
- Wazir Khan, faujdar of Sirhind, ordered the execution of the younger Sahibzadas (1704–05); Banda Singh Bahadur killed him at Chappar Chiri (1710) and sacked Sirhind.
- Banda Singh made Mukhlisgarh (renamed Lohgarh) his capital, issued coins in the Gurus' names and gave land to tillers; captured at Gurdas Nangal (1715) and executed in Delhi (1716) under Farrukhsiyar.
- Later Mughal governors of Lahore (Abdus Samad Khan, Zakariya Khan, Yahiya Khan, Mir Mannu) persecuted the Sikhs, who formed jathas and the Dal Khalsa.
- Chhota Ghallughara (1746, Kahnuwan marshes): massacre of Sikhs by Yahiya Khan and Lakhpat Rai.
- Nadir Shah invaded in 1739; Ahmad Shah Abdali invaded Punjab repeatedly between 1747/48 and 1767; Third Battle of Panipat (1761).
- Vadda Ghallughara (5 Feb 1762, Kup–Rahira near Malerkotla): Abdali killed thousands of Sikhs and later blew up Harmandir Sahib.
- Sikhs took Sirhind (1764) and occupied Lahore (1765), striking their own coins — the effective end of Mughal–Afghan control in central Punjab.

## Punjab angle

- Mughal-era monuments in Indian Punjab: Nur Mahal Sarai (Jalandhar), Aam Khas Bagh (Sirhind), Doraha Sarai (Ludhiana), Takht-i-Akbari (Kalanaur).

## How PPSC asks it

Questions test events located in Punjab (Kalanaur coronation, Machhiwara/Sirhind 1555, Sultanpur Lodhi, Rohtas) and Mughal–Sikh conflict milestones. Memorise who ruled when and the governor–event pairs.

## Deep dive

### Lodi Punjab and the road to Panipat (c.1450–1526)

- Sayyid prelude: Khizr Khan (1414–21), founder of the Sayyid dynasty, had been governor of Multan. Punjab was the springboard for both the Sayyids and the Lodis.
- Bahlol Lodi, nephew of Islam Khan Lodi (governor of Sirhind), held Sirhind and Lahore and took the Delhi throne in 1451, founding the Lodi dynasty.
- Tatar Khan Lodi governed Punjab under the early Lodis. His son Daulat Khan Lodi governed Lahore from about 1500 and kept his headquarters at Sultanpur (Lodhi), where Guru Nanak worked in his modikhana.
- Ibrahim Lodi (1517–26) alienated Afghan nobles (Lohanis, Farmulis, Niazis). Daulat Khan's son Dilawar Khan was humiliated at Delhi, and Ibrahim's uncle Alam Khan claimed the throne. Both invited Babur.
- Babur's Punjab campaigns: 1519 (Bajaur, Bhera); 1520 (Sialkot and the sack of Saidpur/Eminabad); 1524 (defeated the Lodi army near Lahore, took Lahore and Dipalpur, gave Jalandhar and Sultanpur to Daulat Khan, who soon turned hostile); 1525–26 (Daulat Khan surrendered at Malot, then Panipat). Texts count four or five invasions; PSEB uses five.
- First Battle of Panipat (21 April 1526): Babur used the tulughma flanking move and the Ottoman (Rumi) wagon-and-gun line under the gunners Ustad Ali and Mustafa.
- Guru Nanak witnessed Babur's sack of Saidpur; his four Babarvani hymns are a contemporary Punjabi source on the invasion.

### Sur interlude and Mughal reconquest (1540–1557)

- After Kannauj/Bilgram (1540), Sher Shah drove Humayun out through Lahore and the Salt Range. Haibat Khan Niazi became governor of Punjab.
- Rohtas fort (begun 1541, near Jhelum) was built to hold the Gakhars and block Humayun's return. It is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site in Pakistan.
- Sher Shah rebuilt the Peshawar–Sonargaon road (later the Grand Trunk Road) with sarais every 2 kos, and set up a dak (post) system and standard rupiya coinage.
- Islam Shah Sur (1545–53) fought the Gakhars and Niazis and fortified Mankot in the Shivalik foothills.
- Humayun's return in 1555: victory at Machhiwara (May 1555) over the Sur commanders, then at Sirhind (June 1555) over Sikandar Shah Sur, with Bairam Khan in command. Delhi was retaken in July 1555.
- Akbar was enthroned at Kalanaur (Gurdaspur) on 14 Feb 1556 while campaigning in Punjab. Sikandar Sur surrendered at Mankot in 1557.

### Mughal administration and economy in Punjab

- Akbar's Ain-i-Akbari divided the region into the Suba of Lahore (five doabs: Bist Jalandhar, Bari, Rachna, Chaj and Sindh Sagar) and the Suba of Multan. The cis-Sutlej Sarkar of Sirhind belonged to the Suba of Delhi.
- Officials: subedar/nazim (governor), diwan (revenue), bakhshi (military pay), sadr and qazi (religious grants and justice), faujdar (sarkar-level order), kotwal (city), amil/karori (pargana revenue), with qanungo, chaudhri, muqaddam and patwari at the local level.
- Land revenue: Todar Mal's zabt/dahsala (from 1580) in measured areas, with batai (crop share) and kankut (crop estimate) elsewhere. Mansabdars held jagirs; madad-i-maash grants went to religious figures.
- Akbar held court at Lahore from about 1585 to 1598 to watch the north-west (Uzbeks, Kabul); Lahore fort was rebuilt in brick.
- Jahangir was buried at Shahdara, Lahore (1627). Nur Jahan's tomb and the Nurmahal sarai (Jalandhar district, c.1618–20) date from his reign.
- Shah Jahan: Shalimar Garden, Lahore (1641–42); the Shah Nahar (Hasli) canal from the Ravi built under Ali Mardan Khan; Wazir Khan's mosque (1634–41) built by the governor Hakim Ilmuddin Ansari; Sarai Amanat Khan (Tarn Taran district) built by the Taj Mahal calligrapher.
- Aurangzeb built the Badshahi Mosque, Lahore (1673–74). Bahadur Shah died at Lahore in 1712 during the campaign against Banda Singh.
- Economy: Lahore and Multan were trade hubs on the Kabul–Qandahar routes; textiles, sugar, Salt Range rock salt and horses were traded, and Khatri and Multani bankers were active. The kos minars and sarais along the Grand Trunk Road (Doraha, Shambhu, Nurmahal) survive in Indian Punjab.

### Mughal–Sikh relations, 1605–1716: turning points

- Akbar: cordial relations; tradition records a visit to Guru Amar Das at Goindwal and remission of revenue at Guru Arjan's request after a famine.
- Jahangir: after Prince Khusrau's revolt (1606), Guru Arjan was tortured and died at Lahore; the Tuzuk records the order. Guru Hargobind was later held at Gwalior (dates vary, c.1609–12).
- Shah Jahan: clashes in 1628–35 (Amritsar, Lahira, Kartarpur), after which Guru Hargobind moved to the Shivalik foothills at Kiratpur.
- Aurangzeb: Ram Rai at court (1661); jizya reimposed (1679); Guru Tegh Bahadur executed at Delhi (1675); hill rajas joined Mughal faujdars against Anandpur (1700–04).
- Wazir Khan (faujdar of Sirhind) and Zabardast Khan (Lahore governor) besieged Anandpur in 1704. The younger Sahibzadas were executed at Sirhind, and the Nawab of Malerkotla protested.
- Bahadur Shah (1707–12): Guru Gobind Singh accompanied him south. After Banda's rise, his edict of 10 Dec 1710 ordered Sikhs ('Nanak-worshippers') killed wherever found, and he besieged Lohgarh.
- Farrukhsiyar (1713–19): Abdus Samad Khan and his son Zakariya Khan captured Banda at Gurdas Nangal (Dec 1715). Banda and hundreds of followers were executed at Delhi (1716).

### Lahore governors and the Sikhs, 1716–1753 (PSEB Class XII Ch 12)

- Abdus Samad Khan (1713–26) ran a campaign of extermination with rewards for Sikh heads. Sikhs dispersed to the jungles and the Lakhi Jungle of Malwa.
- Zakariya Khan (1726–45) first repressed, then offered a nawabship and a jagir (1733). Kapur Singh accepted on the Panth's behalf and the jathas were reorganised into the Budha Dal and the Taruna Dal (1734).
- Martyrdoms under Zakariya: Bhai Mani Singh (1734; some texts give 1737) after a dispute over the Diwali gathering fee; Bhai Taru Singh (1745). Massa Ranghar, who desecrated the Harmandir, was killed by Mehtab Singh and Sukha Singh (1740).
- Nadir Shah's invasion (1739): Sikh bands raided his returning baggage. Tradition records his warning to Zakariya about the Sikhs' resilience.
- Yahiya Khan (1745–47) and his diwan Lakhpat Rai carried out the Chhota Ghallughara (1746, Kahnuwan marshes, Gurdaspur): thousands of Sikhs were killed or captured.
- Ahmad Shah Abdali's first invasion was defeated at Manupur near Sirhind (11 March 1748) by the Mughal army. Muin-ul-Mulk (Mir Mannu) distinguished himself and became governor of Lahore.
- Mir Mannu (1748–53) first worked through his diwan Kaura Mal, a Sikh sympathiser who secured the end of the Ram Rauni siege (1748). Later he persecuted the Sikhs harshly; he died in Nov 1753.
- After Mannu came chaos under his widow Mughlani Begum. Adina Beg Khan, the faujdar of the Jalandhar Doab and an opportunist, invited the Marathas, who held Lahore in 1758. Adina died the same year.

### Abdali's invasions and the end of Mughal–Afghan rule (PSEB Class XII Ch 15)

- 1st (1747–48): repulsed at Manupur. 2nd (1748–49): Mir Mannu ceded the revenue of four mahals (Sialkot, Pasrur, Gujrat and Aurangabad).
- 3rd (1751–52): Lahore and Multan were formally ceded to Abdali (1752); Mannu stayed on as his governor.
- 4th (1756–57): Delhi and Mathura were sacked; his son Timur Shah became viceroy at Lahore with Jahan Khan as commander. Baba Deep Singh fell fighting to reach the Harmandir (Nov 1757).
- 5th (1759–61): Third Battle of Panipat (14 Jan 1761) against the Marathas. On his return Sikh bands freed captives, which raised their prestige.
- 6th (1761–62): Vadda Ghallughara (5 Feb 1762, Kup–Rahira near Malerkotla); the Harmandir was blown up (April 1762). The Sikhs fought back at Amritsar the same year.
- 7th (1764–65): Sikhs avoided pitched battle. Qazi Nur Muhammad's Persian Jangnama, an eyewitness account, grudgingly praises Sikh conduct. Sirhind fell to the Sikhs (Jan 1764, Zain Khan killed) and Lahore was occupied by Lehna Singh, Gujjar Singh and Sobha Singh (1765), who struck the Gobindshahi/Nanakshahi rupee.
- 8th (1766–67): inconclusive. Abdali offered Lahore to Lehna Singh, who declined, and gave Amar Singh of Patiala the title Raja-i-Rajgan (1767). Abdali died in 1772; Zaman Shah's raids of 1797–98 formed the backdrop to Ranjit Singh's rise.
- Why Afghans and Mughals failed: Sikh guerrilla tactics (dhai phat, hit and retreat), the Sarbat Khalsa and Dal Khalsa organisation, Mughal–Afghan–Maratha rivalry, Abdali's troubles at home, and peasant support through the rakhi system.

### PPSC traps in this period

- Chhota Ghallughara: 1746, Yahiya Khan and Lakhpat Rai, Kahnuwan. Vadda Ghallughara: 1762, Abdali, Kup–Rahira.
- Manupur (1748) was Abdali's defeat by the Mughals; Panipat (1761) was the Marathas' defeat by Abdali.
- Kalanaur (1556) was Akbar's enthronement; Sirhind (1555) was Humayun's decisive win. Chappar Chiri (1710) was Banda's victory over Wazir Khan, outside Sirhind town.
- Daulat Khan Lodi governed Lahore, not Delhi; his father was Tatar Khan. Alam Khan was Ibrahim's uncle, not Daulat Khan's son.
- Adina Beg was faujdar of the Jalandhar Doab who invited the Marathas (1758). Kaura Mal was Mir Mannu's diwan and friendly to the Sikhs.
- Mughal monuments in Indian Punjab: Nurmahal Sarai, Sarai Amanat Khan, Doraha and Shambhu sarais, Aam Khas Bagh (Sirhind) and Takht-i-Akbari (Kalanaur). The Lahore monuments are in Pakistan.
