## Short notes

- Sector shares (Punjab Economic Survey 2025-26): agriculture 23%, industry 29%, services 48% of GSVA.
- Industry is dominated by MSMEs; there are few large units because of distance from ports, the border location and limited raw materials.
- Ludhiana: hosiery and woollens ('Manchester of India'), bicycles and parts (Hero Cycles, 1956), auto parts, sewing machines, machine tools.
- Jalandhar: sports goods (industry shifted from Sialkot after Partition), hand tools, leather and rubber goods, valves and sanitary fittings.
- Mandi Gobindgarh: steel re-rolling mills ('Steel Town of Punjab'); Batala: foundries, agricultural implements, machine tools.
- Amritsar: textiles, food processing, tourism and handicrafts; Phagwara: JCT (textiles); Barnala: Trident (textiles/paper); Rajpura: industrial cluster near Delhi–Amritsar NH.
- Mohali: IT/ITES, electronics, pharma, tractors (Swaraj, originally Punjab Tractors Ltd, 1970), Semi-Conductor Laboratory (under the Department of Space).
- Central PSUs and units: Rail Coach Factory, Kapurthala (1986); Patiala Locomotive Works (formerly Diesel Loco Modernisation Works); National Fertilizers at Nangal and Bathinda.
- Guru Gobind Singh Refinery, Bathinda (HPCL–Mittal Energy, commissioned 2012) is the state's largest industrial investment.
- Thermal power: Ropar (GGSSTP) and Lehra Mohabbat (GHTP) under PSPCL; private plants at Rajpura (Nabha Power) and Talwandi Sabo; PSPCL bought the 540 MW Goindwal Sahib plant from GVK (Jan 2024, ₹1,080 crore). Bathinda's Guru Nanak Dev plant closed in 2018.
- Hydro and renewables: Ranjit Sagar and Shahpurkandi (Ravi), Anandpur Sahib hydel, Mukerian hydel; biomass and solar promoted through PEDA; CBG plants based on paddy straw.
- Policy: Industrial and Business Development Policy 2026, unveiled by the CM on 7 Mar 2026 with sectoral policies and scheme guidelines (replaces the 2022 policy: incentives, industrial parks, MSME focus); Invest Punjab single-window system; Punjab Right to Business Act 2020 (easier approvals for MSMEs).
- Services: trade, transport, real estate, banking, IT in Mohali, education and health hubs; religious and heritage tourism (Golden Temple, Anandpur Sahib, Wagah/Attari).
- Remittances from the large diaspora (Doaba especially) support consumption, housing and services.
- Major occupations: farming and farm labour (largest employer), dairying, small manufacturing, construction, transport (truck operators), armed forces and police, government service.
- Labour: in-migration of workers from UP and Bihar for farm, industrial and construction work; out-migration of Punjabi youth abroad (study visas).
- Issues: industrial flight to Himachal (tax holiday areas such as Baddi), power and logistics costs, high youth unemployment and skill gaps, the drug problem affecting the workforce.

## Deep dive

### Economic Survey 2025-26 — industry and services data

- Services employ about 41% of Punjab's workforce (PLFS 2023-24) compared with about 30% nationally.
- Punjab has about 8.08% of India's registered food-processing factories — fourth in the country.
- About 740 registered IT units; exports from the Mohali–Chandigarh IT hub grew nearly 14% over five years.
- Tourism: 2.77 crore domestic and 5.4 lakh foreign visitors in 2024; tenth in India for foreign arrivals; tourist inflow grew about 19% a year on average in 2010–24.
- Working-age population share 72.6% (2023) — a large labour supply that the economy has struggled to absorb.

### Industrial history and clusters

- Partition cut Punjab off from Lahore, Sialkot and Gujranwala; refugee entrepreneurs rebuilt industries in Ludhiana, Jalandhar and Batala.
- Ludhiana: hosiery and knitwear, bicycles (Hero Cycles, 1956), auto and cycle parts, sewing machines, textiles (Vardhman, Oswal, Nahar groups).
- Jalandhar: sports goods (after the Sialkot shift), hand tools, leather, rubber and surgical goods; Batala: foundries and agricultural machinery.
- Mandi Gobindgarh–Khanna: steel re-rolling and iron and steel scrap trade; Phagwara: sugar and textiles; Barnala: Trident (towels, yarn, paper).
- Tractors: Punjab Tractors (Swaraj, 1970, Mohali) and Sonalika (Hoshiarpur, 1996) grew out of the Green Revolution market.
- Mohali: IT/ITES, pharma, electronics, the Semi-Conductor Laboratory; Rajpura: Nabha Power and an Integrated Manufacturing Cluster approved under the national industrial corridor programme (Aug 2024).
- Bathinda: Guru Gobind Singh Refinery of HPCL-Mittal Energy (commissioned 2012) with a petrochemical expansion; Kapurthala: Rail Coach Factory (1986); Patiala: Patiala Locomotive Works.
- Food processing: rice shellers across Malwa, Markfed and Verka plants, potato and fruit processing; mega food parks are part of the diversification strategy.

### Power sector — capacity map

- State thermal (PSPCL): Guru Gobind Singh Super Thermal Plant, Ropar (originally 1,260 MW, older units retired) and Guru Hargobind Thermal Plant, Lehra Mohabbat (920 MW).
- Goindwal Sahib (540 MW) bought from GVK in Jan 2024 and renamed Shri Guru Amar Dass Thermal Plant.
- Private thermal: Talwandi Sabo Power (1,980 MW, Mansa) and Nabha Power, Rajpura (1,400 MW).
- Hydro: Ranjit Sagar (600 MW), Shahpurkandi, Mukerian and UBDC hydel; share in BBMB power; Shanan (110 MW) lease ended March 2024 and is now disputed with Himachal.
- Coal: PSPCL's captive Pachhwara Central block (Jharkhand) resumed supply in 2022, cutting fuel costs.
- Demand: paddy-season peak load now exceeds 16,000 MW; free farm power (since 1997) and 300 free units for homes (since July 2022) make subsidy dues the utility's biggest issue.
- Renewables: solar parks and rooftops promoted by PEDA, solar pumps under PM-KUSUM, and paddy-straw compressed biogas plants.

### Services and occupations

- Trade, transport and logistics: Punjab sits on the Delhi–Amritsar–Katra and Amritsar–Jamnagar corridors and the Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor's Ludhiana end.
- Banking and finance: high deposit base driven by NRI remittances, though the credit–deposit ratio is lower than in industrial states.
- Tourism: Golden Temple, Attari–Wagah retreat, Jallianwala Bagh, Partition Museum, Anandpur Sahib (Virasat-e-Khalsa), Qila Mubarak Patiala and rural/farm tourism.
- Education and health hubs: Ludhiana (PAU, CMC, DMC), Amritsar (GNDU), Patiala (Punjabi University), Mohali (IISER, NIPER, ISB campus).
- Defence services: Punjab contributes a large share of soldiers (Sikh, Punjab and Sikh Light Infantry regiments, Dogras); ex-servicemen are a major social group.
- Informal and gig work, transport (truck operators' unions), construction and remittance-driven housing make up much of non-farm employment.
- Emigration is itself an 'occupation path' — IELTS centres, travel agents and immigration consultants form a sizeable service industry.

### Policy framework

- Industrial and Business Development Policy 2022 (effective 17 Oct 2022 per the notified text; cleared by the state cabinet with a plan for 15 new industrial parks): incentives for MSMEs, start-ups and green industry.
- Punjab Right to Business Act 2020: fast-track in-principle approvals for new MSMEs, with self-declarations replacing most prior clearances.
- Invest Punjab (Punjab Bureau of Investment Promotion) — the single-window agency for investment approvals and incentives.
- Punjab's first Logistics Policy (late 2022) to cut freight costs from a landlocked border state.
- Central link: Integrated Manufacturing Cluster at Rajpura–Patiala under the industrial corridor programme; PLI schemes for food processing and textiles.
- Skill: Punjab Skill Development Mission; Industrial Training Institutes; new focus on IT and food technology.

### Issues and debates

- Industrial flight after the 2003 central tax package for Himachal and Uttarakhand (Baddi–Barotiwala–Nalagarh belt) drew units out of Punjab.
- Locational disadvantages: far from ports, border-state risk, high freight costs; the closure of the Attari land route to Pakistan trade limits regional trade.
- MSME stress: power tariff and cross-subsidy debates, delayed payments, competition from China in cycles, hosiery and hand tools.
- Youth unemployment and skill mismatch feed emigration and the drug problem.
- Fiscal limits: high committed expenditure leaves little for industrial infrastructure (see the budget data in the public-finance node).
- Opportunities: food processing, agro-machinery, IT in the Mohali tri-city region, logistics hubs, renewable energy from crop residue.

### PPSC traps

- Mandi Gobindgarh = steel; Batala = foundries; Jalandhar = sports goods; Ludhiana = hosiery and cycles — classic matching.
- Rail Coach Factory is at Kapurthala (1986), not Kapurthala's neighbour Jalandhar; Patiala Locomotive Works was earlier DLMW.
- The Bathinda refinery belongs to HPCL-Mittal Energy, not to Indian Oil.
- Goindwal Sahib plant was bought from GVK (2024); Bathinda's Guru Nanak Dev thermal plant was closed in 2018.
- Services (48% of GSVA) is the largest sector, though agriculture still employs a large share of workers.
- Free farm power (1997) and free domestic power up to 300 units (2022) are different schemes.
