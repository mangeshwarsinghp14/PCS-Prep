## Short notes

- India's geographical area is about 328.7 million ha; land-use statistics use 'reporting area', which is slightly less.
- Nine-fold land-use classification: forests; land put to non-agricultural uses; barren and unculturable land; permanent pastures and grazing land; land under miscellaneous tree crops; culturable waste (unused 5+ years); current fallow (up to 1 year); other fallow (1-5 years); net sown area.
- Net sown area is about 140 million ha (around 45% of reporting area); cropping intensity = gross cropped area / net sown area x 100 - Punjab has among the highest.
- Cropping seasons: Kharif (June-Sept, with south-west monsoon) - rice, maize, jowar, bajra, cotton, groundnut, soybean; Rabi (Oct/Nov-March) - wheat, gram, mustard, barley; Zaid (short summer season) - watermelon, cucumber, vegetables, fodder.
- Farming types by moisture: irrigated (protective vs productive irrigation) and rainfed - dryland (under 75 cm rain; drought-resistant crops) and wetland (excess rain).
- Rice: kharif crop needing high temperature and rainfall; West Bengal, UP and Punjab lead production; India is the world's largest rice exporter.
- Wheat: rabi crop needing cool growing season and bright sunshine at ripening; UP, Madhya Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana lead; India is the second-largest producer.
- Pulses: India is the largest producer and consumer; gram and tur (arhar) are major; grown mostly in dryland areas.
- Oilseeds: groundnut (Gujarat), rapeseed-mustard (Rajasthan), soybean (Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra); India still imports large quantities of edible oil.
- Cash crops: cotton (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Telangana - black soil; Bt cotton approved 2002); jute (West Bengal); sugarcane (UP largest producer, then Maharashtra); tea (Assam); coffee (Karnataka).
- Green Revolution (mid-1960s): HYV seeds, assured irrigation, fertilisers and pesticides; concentrated in Punjab, Haryana, western UP, coastal Andhra and Tamil Nadu - mainly a wheat revolution, widening regional disparities.
- Agricultural planning: Intensive Agricultural District Programme (1960-61), High Yielding Varieties Programme (1966), agro-climatic regional planning (1988).
- Average operational holding is 1.08 ha (Agriculture Census 2015-16); small and marginal holdings are about 86% of the total.
- Problems: dependence on erratic monsoon, low yields, small and fragmented holdings, incomplete land reforms, disguised unemployment, indebtedness, and land degradation.
- Land degradation: about 29.7% of India's area is affected by degradation/desertification (ISRO Desertification Atlas, 2018-19); irrigation-induced waterlogging, salinity and alkalinity are common in canal areas.
- Responses: Soil Health Card (2015), PM Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (2015, 'Per Drop More Crop'), National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture, crop diversification and natural farming.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab's wheat-paddy rotation gives very high cropping intensity and productivity but has caused groundwater decline, soil nutrient loss and stubble burning.
- Punjab Preservation of Subsoil Water Act 2009 delays paddy transplanting to save groundwater.
- Punjab is a leading contributor of wheat and rice to the central pool; crop diversification to maize, pulses, cotton and horticulture is the state's key policy goal.
- Salinity and waterlogging affect parts of south-west Punjab (Muktsar, Fazilka).

## How PPSC asks it

Expect questions on land-use categories (definitions of fallow and culturable waste), crop seasons, leading producer states and Green Revolution features. Memorise crop-climate requirements and top states.

## Deep dive

### Agricultural statistics to quote (with year)

- Agriculture Census 2015-16: about 14.6 crore operational holdings; marginal holdings (under 1 ha) about 68% and small (1–2 ha) about 18%; average 1.08 ha, while Punjab's average (about 3.6 ha) is among the highest.
- Agriculture and allied sectors contribute roughly 18% of India's GVA but employ about 46% of the workforce (PLFS 2023-24) — the core of disguised unemployment arguments.
- India is the largest producer of milk, pulses and jute and among the top two for rice (also the largest rice exporter), wheat, sugarcane, cotton, fruits and vegetables.
- A little over half of the gross cropped area is irrigated; groundwater supplies most of it.
- Fertiliser use is dominated by subsidised urea; nutrient-based subsidy (2010) covers P and K fertilisers.
- Foodgrain output has set successive records, crossing 350 million tonnes in 2024-25.

### Agricultural geography — theories and regions

- Von Thünen's model (1826, 'The Isolated State'): concentric rings of land use around a market determined by transport cost and perishability.
- Derwent Whittlesey (1936) classified world agriculture into 13 types — nomadic herding, livestock ranching, shifting cultivation, rudimentary sedentary tillage, intensive subsistence (rice-dominated and non-rice), commercial plantation, Mediterranean, commercial grain, livestock and grain (mixed), subsistence crop and stock, commercial dairy and specialised horticulture.
- Shifting cultivation names: jhum (North-East India), podu (Andhra–Odisha), bewar/dahiya (Madhya Pradesh); milpa (Mexico and Central America), ladang (Malaysia–Indonesia), roca (Brazil), conuco (Venezuela), masole (Congo), ray (Vietnam).
- Crop combination analysis (J.C. Weaver, 1954) and crop diversification indices are used to delimit agricultural regions.
- Planning Commission (1988) set 15 agro-climatic regions; ICAR's NARP set 127 agro-climatic zones.
- Commercial grain farming (Prairies, Pampas, Steppes, Downs) versus intensive subsistence farming (monsoon Asia) is a frequent comparison.

### Revolutions, missions and schemes (year — focus)

- Colour revolutions: Green (foodgrains, mid-1960s), White (milk — Operation Flood 1970, Verghese Kurien), Yellow (oilseeds — Technology Mission on Oilseeds 1986), Blue (fisheries), Golden (horticulture), Silver (eggs/poultry), Round (potato), Grey (fertilisers); M.S. Swaminathan called for an 'Evergreen Revolution'.
- NFSM (2007), RKVY (2007), MIDH (2014-15), Soil Health Card (2015), e-NAM (2016), PMFBY (2016; farmer premium 2% kharif, 1.5% rabi, 5% commercial/horticultural crops), PM-KISAN (2019; ₹6,000 a year).
- Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (2020, ₹1 lakh crore financing facility); Digital Agriculture Mission (2024) with AgriStack farmer IDs.
- National Mission on Edible Oils (oil palm 2021, oilseeds 2024) and the Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses (announced in Budget 2025-26) target import dependence.
- PM Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana (approved 2025) targets 100 low-productivity districts.
- National Mission on Natural Farming (November 2024) and PKVY (2015) promote chemical-free farming.

### Land resources, records and degradation

- ISRO's Desertification and Land Degradation Atlas (2021): about 97.85 million ha (29.7% of India) degraded in 2018-19; water erosion is the leading process, followed by vegetation degradation and wind erosion.
- Wastelands Atlas of India 2019 (Department of Land Resources and NRSC): wastelands about 55.8 million ha (about 17%) in 2015-16.
- Land records: Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme (2008, revamped 2016); SVAMITVA (April 2020) uses drones to give property cards for rural abadi land; ULPIN ('Bhu-Aadhaar') is a 14-digit plot ID.
- Land reforms: zamindari abolition, tenancy reform, ceilings and consolidation; Punjab was a pioneer in consolidation of holdings under the East Punjab Holdings (Consolidation and Prevention of Fragmentation) Act 1948.
- NITI Aayog's Model Agricultural Land Leasing Act 2016 (T. Haque committee) sought to legalise tenancy to help tenants get credit and insurance.
- Land-use change pressure: urbanisation and highways on fertile land, especially along the Delhi–Amritsar corridor.

### Punjab agriculture file

- Punjab has about 1.5% of India's area but around four-fifths of it is net sown, cropping intensity is close to 190% and nearly all of it is irrigated, mostly by over 14 lakh tube-wells.
- Yields of wheat and rice are among the highest in India; Punjab is a leading contributor to the central pool of both.
- Johl Committee reports (1986 and 2002) recommended diverting area from the wheat–paddy rotation to crops such as maize, pulses, oilseeds and horticulture.
- Physical regions for agriculture: Kandi (rainfed foothills), Bet (riverine floodplain), central plain (wheat–paddy core) and the south-west cotton belt with waterlogging and salinity.
- Punjab Contract Farming Act 2013 and the Punjab Mandi Board's dense network of purchase centres shape marketing.
- PAU Ludhiana's Happy Seeder and Surface Seeder, short-duration paddy varieties (PR-126) and direct seeded rice are the main technology answers to stubble and water problems.

### Traps and PPSC patterns

- Culturable waste = unused for over 5 years; current fallow = up to 1 year; other fallow = 1–5 years.
- Net sown area counts land once; gross cropped area counts it as many times as it is cropped.
- PMFBY premiums: 2% kharif, 1.5% rabi, 5% commercial/horticulture — options often swap the first two.
- Jhum is shifting cultivation; terrace farming is different.
- PPSC 2025 asked rice's climatic needs; 2018 asked MSP and equatorial crops — revise crop–climate tables.
- The Green Revolution was mainly a wheat revolution at first and widened regional inequality.

**1.** In land-use classification, 'fallow land other than current fallow' has been left uncultivated for:

(a) 1 to 5 years  
(b) Less than 1 year

**(a) 1 to 5 years** — Current fallow is under 1 year; culturable waste is more than 5 years.

**2.** Which of the following is a kharif crop?

(a) Wheat  
(b) Mustard  
(c) Gram  
(d) Cotton

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Cotton** — Cotton is a kharif crop (sown June-July, harvested Oct-Dec). Wheat, mustard and gram are rabi crops sown in Oct-Nov, which is why they are the wrong options.

</details>

**3.** Consider the statements:

Which is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** — Zaid crops are grown in the short summer season between rabi and kharif.

</details>

(a) Bright sunshine and dry weather  
(b) Heavy rainfall

**(a) Bright sunshine and dry weather** — A cool growing season and warm, sunny ripening period suit wheat.

**7.** Consider the following statements: 1. In von Thünen's model, land use around a market is arranged in rings according to transport cost. 2. Derwent Whittlesey classified world agriculture into 13 agricultural regions. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Both 1 and 2** — Both are correct: von Thunen's model arranges land use in concentric rings by transport cost from the market, and Whittlesey (1936) identified 13 agricultural regions. 'Neither' or a single statement is therefore wrong.

</details>
