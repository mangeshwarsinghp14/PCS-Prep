## Short notes

- Constitution: water is a State subject (Entry 17, List II), subject to Union control of inter-state rivers (Entry 56, List I); Article 262 and the Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956 govern disputes.
- Indus Waters Treaty, 19 Sept 1960, Karachi (Nehru–Ayub Khan, brokered by the World Bank): eastern rivers Ravi, Beas, Sutlej to India; western rivers Indus, Jhelum, Chenab to Pakistan; Permanent Indus Commission set up.
- In April 2025 India placed the Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance after the Pahalgam terror attack.
- 1955 inter-state agreement shared the surplus Ravi–Beas water (15.85 MAF): Rajasthan 8.0, Punjab 5.9, PEPSU 1.3, J&K 0.65 MAF.
- Punjab Reorganisation Act 1966, Sections 78–80: gave the Centre powers over Bhakra–Beas assets; Bhakra Management Board formed on 1 Oct 1967 and renamed Bhakra Beas Management Board (BBMB) on 15 May 1976.
- 1976 Central notification (during the Emergency): of the 7.2 MAF of undivided Punjab, 3.5 MAF to Haryana, 3.5 MAF to Punjab, 0.2 MAF to Delhi.
- 31 Dec 1981 agreement: water reassessed at 17.17 MAF — Rajasthan 8.6, Punjab 4.22, Haryana 3.5, J&K 0.65, Delhi 0.2 MAF.
- Satluj–Yamuna Link (SYL) canal: 214 km long (122 km in Punjab, 92 km in Haryana); foundation stone laid by Indira Gandhi at Kapoori village (Patiala) on 8 April 1982; the Akalis launched the Kapoori morcha against it.
- Rajiv–Longowal Accord (24 July 1985) referred the dispute to a tribunal: the Eradi Tribunal (Justice V. Balakrishna Eradi, 1986), whose 1987 report raised Punjab to 5.0 MAF and Haryana to 3.83 MAF; its final decision is still pending.
- The Supreme Court (2002, reaffirmed 2004) ordered Punjab to complete the SYL canal; Punjab passed the Termination of Agreements Act 2004 to cancel water accords.
- In Nov 2016 the Supreme Court, answering a Presidential reference, held the 2004 Act invalid; Punjab then returned the land acquired for SYL to its owners.
- Punjab's stand: the riparian principle (Haryana is not riparian to the Ravi–Beas), reassessment of current water availability, and 'no surplus water to share'.
- Inter-state friction in 2025: Punjab opposed BBMB's decision to release extra water to Haryana; the Vidhan Sabha (special session, May 2025) passed a resolution against it and sought repeal of the Dam Safety Act 2021.
- Bhakra dam: on the Sutlej at Bhakra, Bilaspur (HP), reservoir Gobind Sagar; dedicated to the nation in 1963; Nangal dam is downstream in Punjab.
- Pong dam: on the Beas in Kangra (HP), reservoir Maharana Pratap Sagar; Beas–Sutlej Link diverts Beas water from Pandoh to the Sutlej system.
- Ravi projects: Ranjit Sagar (Thein) dam near Pathankot (commissioned around 2000–01); Shahpurkandi dam, completed in 2024, stops surplus Ravi water from flowing to Pakistan and irrigates Punjab and J&K (Kathua–Samba).
- Headworks and canals: Madhopur (Ravi) – Upper Bari Doab Canal (1859, the oldest); Ropar (Sutlej) – Sirhind Canal (1882) and Bist Doab Canal; Harike – Indira Gandhi (Rajasthan) Canal plus Ferozepur and Sirhind feeders; Ferozepur/Hussainiwala – Gang (Bikaner) Canal (1927).
- Groundwater (state assessment 2024-25): extraction stage about 156% in 2025 (164% in 2023); 111 of 153 blocks over-exploited.
- Laws: Punjab Preservation of Subsoil Water Act 2009 (no paddy transplanting before a notified June date); Punjab Water Resources (Management and Regulation) Act 2020 set up the Punjab Water Regulation and Development Authority (PWRDA).
- Quality concerns: Budha Nallah (Ludhiana) and Kali Bein pollution; arsenic and uranium in parts of Malwa groundwater; floods across all 23 districts in Aug–Sep 2025 raised questions on dam-release management.

## Punjab angle

- Free farm power (since 1997) and the paddy–wheat cycle drive heavy tubewell pumping; there are over 14 lakh tubewells.
- Direct-seeded rice, a later transplanting date, and canal lining/renovation are the state's main water-saving measures.

Expect chronology questions (1955 → 1966 → 1976 → 1981 → 1985 → 2004 → 2016), MAF shares, SYL length split, dam–river–reservoir pairs, and headworks–canal matching.

## Deep dive

### Headworks and canals (headworks — river — canals — note)

- Madhopur (Ravi): Upper Bari Doab Canal (1859) — the oldest canal system of the Punjab, irrigating Majha.
- Ropar (Sutlej): Sirhind Canal (1882) for Malwa and the Bist Doab Canal for Doaba.
- Nangal (Sutlej, below Bhakra): Bhakra Main Line canal and the Nangal Hydel Channel; Bhakra canals were opened by Nehru on 8 July 1954.
- Harike barrage (Sutlej–Beas, 1952): Rajasthan Feeder (renamed Indira Gandhi Canal in 1984, work started 1958), Sirhind Feeder and Ferozepur Feeder.
- Hussainiwala (Ferozepur): Eastern Canal and the Bikaner (Gang) Canal of 1927 for the Ganganagar region.
- Kandi Canal (Hoshiarpur–Rupnagar foothills) and the Shahpurkandi project (Ravi) extend canal water to the dry north-east.
- Irrigation mix: tubewells irrigate roughly 70% and canals under 30% of the net irrigated area (early-2020s Statistical Abstract data); the state says canal water now reaches more tail ends.

### Dams and multipurpose projects

- Bhakra dam (Sutlej, Bilaspur, HP): concrete gravity dam about 226 m high; reservoir Gobind Sagar; dedicated to the nation on 22 Oct 1963.
- Beas dam at Pong (Kangra, HP): earth-fill dam completed in 1974; reservoir Maharana Pratap Sagar (a Ramsar site).
- Beas–Sutlej Link (Pandoh to Dehar power house, 1977): diverts Beas water into the Sutlej system.
- Ranjit Sagar (Thein) dam on the Ravi near Pathankot: commissioned around 2000–01; 600 MW; owned by Punjab (not BBMB).
- Shahpurkandi dam (Ravi, downstream of Ranjit Sagar): completed in 2024; irrigates Punjab and J&K and stops Ravi water from flowing to Pakistan.
- BBMB manages Bhakra, Pong and the Beas–Sutlej Link for Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Himachal, Delhi and Chandigarh; a 2022 change to BBMB rules ended the convention of Punjab and Haryana holding the two whole-time member posts, which Punjab opposed.
- Shanan hydel project (Joginder Nagar, HP; built 1932 under a 1925 lease to Punjab): the lease ended in March 2024 and Himachal now claims the plant — an ongoing inter-state dispute.

### Groundwater — status, causes and laws

- Ground Water Resources of Punjab 2024-25 (reported June 2026): stage of extraction 156% in 2025, down from 164% in 2023; over-exploited blocks 111 of 153 (117 in 2023); water levels improved in 81 blocks.
- Causes: paddy area grew from a few lakh hectares in the 1960s to over 30 lakh ha; free farm power since 1997; assured MSP procurement; more than 14 lakh tubewells.
- Deeper aquifers are being mined in central Punjab (Sangrur, Moga, Barnala, Patiala belt), raising pumping costs and pushing farmers to submersible pumps.
- Quality: uranium and fluoride in parts of Malwa, arsenic along the Beas–Ravi belt of Majha, nitrate from fertiliser use; the Bathinda–Bikaner 'cancer train' became a symbol of the health concern.
- Laws and bodies: Punjab Preservation of Subsoil Water Act 2009; Punjab Water Resources (Management and Regulation) Act 2020 and the PWRDA, which levies groundwater extraction charges on industrial and commercial users (from 2023) while exempting farm and domestic use.
- Measures: direct-seeded rice incentive, short-duration varieties, zone-wise transplanting dates, the 'Paani Bachao Paisa Kamao' metering pilot (2018), canal lining and recharge structures.

### Inter-state dispute — legal details beyond the dates

- Constitutional basis: Article 262 lets Parliament bar courts from water disputes; the Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956 (amended 2002) provides tribunals.
- Punjab Reorganisation Act 1966: Section 78 (rights in Beas projects), Section 79 (Bhakra Management Board), Section 80 (Beas Construction Board).
- Ravi–Beas Water Tribunal (Justice V. Balakrishna Eradi) set up in 1986 under the Rajiv–Longowal Accord; its 1987 report is still awaiting a final decision after references.
- Punjab Termination of Agreements Act (July 2004) annulled the 1981 accord; the Supreme Court's opinion on the Presidential reference (10 Nov 2016) held the Act invalid.
- Punjab then de-notified the land acquired for the SYL canal (2016) and returned it to owners; the Supreme Court has since pressed the Centre to mediate and to survey the canal land.
- Punjab's case: riparian rights (Haryana and Rajasthan are not riparian to the Ravi–Beas), fall in available water since the 1981 estimate, groundwater crisis; Haryana's case: binding agreements and decrees.
- In 2025 a BBMB decision to release extra water to Haryana led to a standoff at Nangal dam, a special Vidhan Sabha resolution and litigation in the Punjab and Haryana High Court.

### Indus Waters Treaty and Punjab

- A ten-year transition allowed Pakistan to build replacement works (Mangla, Tarbela and link canals) with an international Indus Basin Development Fund.
- Permanent Indus Commission meets annually; disputes go to a Neutral Expert or a Court of Arbitration (as in the Kishenganga and Ratle cases).
- India sent notices seeking modification of the treaty in Jan 2023 and Aug–Sept 2024, and placed it in abeyance on 23 April 2025 after the Pahalgam attack.
- Punjab's stake: full use of eastern-river water through Shahpurkandi, Ujh and the second Ravi–Beas link below Ujh, so that surplus water no longer crosses into Pakistan.
- Historical link: the Partition of the canal system in 1947 led to the April 1948 Standstill dispute when East Punjab stopped water to West Punjab canals — the trigger for World Bank mediation.

### Floods, drainage and pollution

- Major floods: 1988 (worst of the 20th century), 1993, 2019 (Sutlej breaches in Jalandhar), July 2023 (Ghaggar and Sutlej), Aug–Sept 2025 (all 23 districts declared flood-affected; described as the worst since 1988).
- Flood management relies on dhussi bunds (earthen embankments) along rivers, drains, and reservoir operations at Bhakra, Pong and Ranjit Sagar — whose release timing is often debated.
- Budha Nallah (Ludhiana): dyeing and dairy effluents; a rejuvenation project with new treatment plants was launched in 2020.
- Kali Bein: cleaned by volunteers under Sant Balbir Singh Seechewal from 2000; the 'Seechewal model' treats village pond water for irrigation.
- Harike wetland receives polluted Sutlej flows, affecting Rajasthan canal water quality; an NGT-appointed monitoring committee (2018) oversees Punjab river pollution.
- The 16th Finance Commission (2026–31) made Ludhiana and Amritsar eligible for special grants of up to ₹5,000 crore each for wastewater management.

### Traps and one-liners

- Ranjit Sagar (Ravi) is a Punjab project; Bhakra and Pong are BBMB projects located in Himachal Pradesh.
- Rajasthan gets Ravi–Beas water via Harike and the Indira Gandhi Canal, though it is not a riparian state.
- Eradi Tribunal (1986) ≠ the Supreme Court's 2002 decree on SYL; the 2016 judgment was an advisory opinion on a Presidential reference.
- Sections 78–80 of the 1966 Act are the 'water and power' sections Punjab wants amended.
- Stage of groundwater extraction above 100% means more water is pumped than is recharged each year.
- Bhakra (in Himachal) is the storage dam; Nangal dam (in Punjab), about 13 km downstream, diverts water into the Bhakra Main Line and Nangal Hydel Channel.
