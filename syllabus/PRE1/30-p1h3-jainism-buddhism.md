## Short notes

- 6th century BCE saw many new sects; causes: costly Vedic rituals, rigid varna order, Brahmanical dominance, new agrarian economy needing cattle, and rising traders who sought social status.
- Jainism has 24 tirthankaras: Rishabhadeva the first, Parshvanatha the 23rd (four vows), Mahavira the 24th.
- Vardhamana Mahavira: born at Kundagrama near Vaishali; father Siddhartha (Jnatrika clan), mother Trishala (Lichchhavi princess).
- Mahavira attained kaivalya under a sal tree at Jrimbhikagrama on the Rijupalika river after 12 years of austerity; died at Pavapuri (near Rajgir) aged 72.
- Jain triratna: right faith, right knowledge, right conduct; five vows: ahimsa, satya, asteya, aparigraha and brahmacharya (added by Mahavira).
- Jain philosophy: anekantavada and syadvada (many-sidedness of truth); strict non-violence; no creator god; liberation through austerity.
- Split into Digambaras (sky-clad; Bhadrabahu, who went south with Chandragupta Maurya during a famine) and Svetambaras (white-clad; Sthulabhadra).
- Jain councils: Pataliputra (c. 300 BCE, Sthulabhadra) and Vallabhi (5th–6th century CE, Devardhi Kshamashramana) where the 12 Angas were compiled in Ardhamagadhi Prakrit.
- Jain monuments: Udayagiri-Khandagiri caves (Kharavela, Odisha), Gomateshwara at Shravanabelagola (erected c. 983 CE by Chamundaraya), Dilwara temples at Mount Abu.
- Gautama Buddha (Siddhartha): born at Lumbini in the Shakya clan of Kapilavastu; father Suddhodana, mother Mahamaya; raised by Prajapati Gautami.
- Life events: renunciation (Mahabhinishkramana) at 29; enlightenment at Bodh Gaya under a pipal on the Niranjana at 35; first sermon at Sarnath (Dharmachakra-pravartana); Mahaparinirvana at Kushinagar at 80.
- Teachings: Four Noble Truths, Eightfold Path, Middle Path, impermanence and no permanent self (anatta); Buddhist triratna: Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha.
- Symbols: lotus/bull (birth), horse (renunciation), Bodhi tree (enlightenment), wheel (first sermon), stupa (parinirvana).
- 1st Council: Rajagriha (c. 483 BCE), under Ajatashatru, presided by Mahakassapa — Sutta and Vinaya Pitakas compiled.
- 2nd Council: Vaishali (c. 383 BCE), under Kalashoka — split into Sthaviravadins and Mahasanghikas. 3rd: Pataliputra (c. 250 BCE), Ashoka, Moggaliputta Tissa — Abhidhamma Pitaka.
- 4th Council: Kashmir under Kanishka (1st–2nd century CE), presided by Vasumitra with Ashvaghosha — division into Hinayana and Mahayana.
- Tripitakas are in Pali; Jatakas narrate the Buddha's previous births; Mahayana texts used Sanskrit; Nalanda and Vikramashila became great Buddhist universities.
- Decline in India: revival of Brahmanism and Bhakti, loss of royal patronage, internal divisions, and attacks on monasteries in the medieval period.

## Punjab angle

- Taxila (Takshashila), in the ancient Gandhara-Punjab region, was a major centre of learning and Buddhism; the Gandhara school of Buddhist art flourished nearby under the Kushanas.
- Sanghol (Fatehgarh Sahib) has a Buddhist stupa and monastery; a large hoard of Kushana-period carved railing pillars (Mathura style) was found there in the 1980s.
- Chaneti (near Jagadhri, now Haryana) and Sanghol show that Buddhism spread through the eastern Punjab plains in Mauryan-Kushana times.

## How PPSC asks it

PPSC/UPSC test councils (place, king, president, outcome), places of the Buddha's and Mahavira's life events, tirthankaras, vows and symbols. A four-row council table and a life-event map cover most questions.

## Deep dive

### Timeline (year — event)

- c. 6th century BCE — 'Second urbanisation' in the Ganga valley, 16 mahajanapadas (Anguttara Nikaya list), and over 60 sramana sects (Buddhist texts speak of 62 views).
- c. 563/480 BCE — Birth of Siddhartha at Lumbini (Ashoka's Rummindei pillar marks it); scholars now prefer the 'short chronology' with death c. 400 BCE.
- c. 540/599 BCE — Birth of Vardhamana at Kundagrama (Vaishali); death at Pavapuri c. 468/527 BCE — two chronologies exist.
- c. 483 BCE — First Buddhist Council at Saptaparni cave, Rajagriha, under Ajatashatru; president Mahakassapa; Ananda recited the Sutta, Upali the Vinaya.
- c. 383 BCE — Second Council at Vaishali under Kalashoka; president Sabakami; split into Sthaviravadins and Mahasanghikas.
- c. 250 BCE — Third Council at Pataliputra under Ashoka; president Moggaliputta Tissa; Abhidhamma Pitaka (Kathavatthu) finalised; missions sent abroad.
- c. 1st–2nd century CE — Fourth Council at Kundalvana, Kashmir, under Kanishka; president Vasumitra, vice-president Ashvaghosha; Mahayana–Hinayana split formalised; texts in Sanskrit.
- c. 300 BCE — First Jain council at Pataliputra (Sthulabhadra); 5th–6th century CE — Second Jain council at Vallabhi (Devardhi Kshamashramana) — canon written down.
- 7th–12th century — Buddhism declines in India; Nalanda sacked by Bakhtiyar Khalji's troops (c. 1193–1200); revived in the modern era by Anagarika Dharmapala (Maha Bodhi Society, 1891) and B.R. Ambedkar's conversion at Nagpur (14 October 1956).

### Buddhist doctrine and texts — deeper layer

- Four Noble Truths: dukkha, samudaya (cause, tanha), nirodha (cessation), magga (path); the path is the Eightfold Path grouped as sila (conduct), samadhi (concentration), prajna (wisdom).
- Three marks of existence: anicca (impermanence), dukkha (suffering), anatta (no permanent self); pratityasamutpada = dependent origination (12 links).
- Tripitaka (Pali canon): Vinaya (monastic rules), Sutta (discourses, 5 nikayas — Digha, Majjhima, Samyutta, Anguttara, Khuddaka), Abhidhamma (philosophy).
- Khuddaka Nikaya contains the Dhammapada, Theragatha, Therigatha (poems of nuns) and the Jatakas (about 550 stories of the Buddha's previous lives).
- Non-canonical: Milindapanha (dialogue of Menander and Nagasena, set at Sakala/Sialkot in Punjab), Dipavamsa and Mahavamsa (Sri Lankan chronicles), Buddhacharita of Ashvaghosha.
- Mahayana: Bodhisattva ideal, Buddha image worship, Sanskrit texts; Nagarjuna (Madhyamika, 'shunyata'); Asanga and Vasubandhu (Yogachara/Vijnanavada).
- Vajrayana (c. 8th century) — tantric Buddhism of Bengal–Bihar, Vikramashila (founded by Dharmapala of the Palas); carried to Tibet by Padmasambhava and Atisha Dipankara.
- Eight great places (ashtamahasthanas): Lumbini, Bodh Gaya, Sarnath, Kushinagar (the four main) plus Shravasti, Sankassa, Rajagriha, Vaishali.

### Jainism — deeper layer

- Mahavira's titles: Jina (conqueror), Nigantha Nataputta (in Buddhist texts), Kevalin; his first disciple was Jamali (also his son-in-law) who later split away.
- Jain metaphysics: jiva (soul) and ajiva (non-soul); karma as fine matter sticking to the soul; liberation (kaivalya/moksha) by stopping inflow (samvara) and shedding karma (nirjara).
- Syadvada — seven-fold predication ('maybe it is, maybe it is not…'); anekantavada — reality has many aspects.
- Canon: 12 Angas (the 12th, Drishtivada, is lost), 12 Upangas, Prakirnas, Chhedasutras, Mulasutras; Kalpasutra (attributed to Bhadrabahu) has biographies of tirthankaras.
- Digambaras hold that the original canon is lost and that women cannot attain liberation in this birth; Svetambaras accept the Vallabhi canon and female liberation (Mallinatha, the 19th tirthankara, was a woman in their tradition).
- Later sects: Sthanakavasi and Terapanthi (reject image worship) among Svetambaras; Acharya Hemachandra (12th century) at Kumarapala's Chalukya court in Gujarat.
- Royal patrons: Chandragupta Maurya, Kharavela of Kalinga (Hathigumpha inscription), Amoghavarsha (Rashtrakuta), Ganga and Hoysala kings, Kumarapala (Chalukya).

### Comparison table — Buddhism vs Jainism

- Founder status — Buddhism: Buddha a founder; Jainism: Mahavira the 24th tirthankara of an older line.
- Soul — Buddhism denies a permanent self (anatta); Jainism holds that all beings, even stones and water, have souls (jiva).
- Path — Buddhism: Middle Path between indulgence and extreme asceticism; Jainism: rigorous austerity, fasting to death (sallekhana) allowed.
- Ahimsa — both preach it; Jain practice is far stricter (avoids agriculture for monks, filtering water, mouth-cloth).
- Language — Buddhism: Pali (Theravada), later Sanskrit (Mahayana); Jainism: Ardhamagadhi Prakrit, later Sanskrit and Apabhramsha.
- Spread — Buddhism spread to Sri Lanka, Central, East and South-East Asia; Jainism stayed largely in India (Gujarat, Rajasthan, Karnataka).
- Social base — both attracted merchants (setthis) and rejected Vedic sacrifice; neither attacked the varna order as directly as is sometimes claimed.
- Survival — Jainism survived through lay community organisation; Buddhism declined in India after losing royal and monastic support.

### Art, sites and Punjab–Gandhara connections

- Stupas: Sanchi (Ashoka, enlarged by Shungas; Satavahana gateways), Bharhut (Shunga), Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda (Satavahana–Ikshvaku), Dhamek stupa at Sarnath (Gupta).
- Chaitya halls and viharas: Bhaja, Karle (largest chaitya), Kanheri, Ajanta (Caves 9, 10 early; later Mahayana caves), Ellora Caves 1–12 (Buddhist).
- Taxila (Takshashila) — Dharmarajika stupa, Jaulian and Mohra Moradu monasteries; a great centre of learning where tradition places Panini, Chanakya and Jivaka.
- Gandhara school (1st–5th century CE): grey schist and stucco, Greco-Roman drapery and wavy hair; Kushana patrons (Kanishka's casket from Shah-ji-ki-Dheri, Peshawar).
- Sanghol (Fatehgarh Sahib, Punjab): a Buddhist stupa was exposed in 1968; in February 1985 Punjab's archaeology department found 117 carved Kushana sculptural pieces (69 pillars, 35 crossbars, 13 coping stones) in the Mathura style.
- Chaneti (Yamunanagar, Haryana) has an Ashokan-era brick stupa; Hiuen Tsang (7th century) describes Buddhist establishments at Jalandhar (Jalandhara) and Sthaneshvara (Thanesar).
- The Milindapanha's setting at Sakala (Sialkot) and Menander's conversion make Punjab a stage for Indo-Greek Buddhism.

### Why did these sects arise and why did Buddhism decline? (Mains-ready points)

- Rise: iron-plough agriculture needed cattle that Vedic sacrifice consumed; new towns, coinage (punch-marked coins) and traders wanted status denied by the Brahmanical order.
- Kshatriya resentment of Brahmana claims; both founders were Kshatriyas from republican (gana-sangha) clans — Shakyas and Jnatrikas.
- Use of people's languages (Pali, Prakrit) and an organised sangha made the message portable.
- Royal patronage: Bimbisara, Ajatashatru, Ashoka, Kanishka, Harsha, Palas for Buddhism; Mauryas, Kharavela, Rashtrakutas for Jainism.
- Decline of Buddhism: corruption and wealth in monasteries, use of Sanskrit (distance from people), Bhakti and Shankaracharya's revival, absorption of the Buddha as an avatar of Vishnu.
- Loss of patrons after the Palas and destruction of Nalanda, Vikramashila and Odantapuri during Turkish raids (c. 1200).
- Historiography: older accounts call it a 'protest movement'; Romila Thapar and Upinder Singh stress its links with urbanisation and the sramana tradition rather than pure 'revolt'.

### PPSC-style asks and traps

- Council–place–patron–president matching is the single most asked format.
- Lumbini is in Nepal; Kapilavastu is claimed by both Tilaurakot (Nepal) and Piprahwa (UP); Piprahwa yielded a relic casket with an inscription (1898).
- 'Tathagata' and 'Shakyamuni' refer to the Buddha; 'Nirgrantha' (free from bonds) refers to Mahavira's followers.
- Mahavira did not found Jainism; Parshvanatha's four vows plus Mahavira's brahmacharya = five vows (mahavratas for monks, anuvratas for laity).
- Bodh Gaya (Mahabodhi temple complex) became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2002; the present temple is largely Gupta-period in origin, much restored.
- Ajivikas (Makkhali Gosala; fatalism, niyati) were patronised by Bindusara and Ashoka (Barabar caves) — do not confuse them with Jains.
- Buddha's last words to Ananda and death at Kushinagar = mahaparinirvana; the Mahaparinibbana Sutta (Digha Nikaya) records this.
