## Short notes

- Porus (Puru): ruler between the Jhelum and the Chenab who fought Alexander at the Battle of the Hydaspes (Jhelum), 326 BC.
- Dulla Bhatti: Punjabi folk hero who rebelled against Akbar; remembered in Lohri songs as protector of poor girls.
- Banda Singh Bahadur (born Lachhman Dev, 1670–1716): sent by Guru Gobind Singh; won at Chappar Chiri (1710) and took Sirhind; capital Lohgarh; gave land to tillers; executed in Delhi in 1716.
- Nawab Kapur Singh organised the Budha Dal and Taruna Dal (1734); Jassa Singh Ahluwalia led the Dal Khalsa (1748), was titled 'Sultan-ul-Qaum' and founded the Kapurthala line.
- Jassa Singh Ramgarhia (Ramgarhia misl, Ram Rauni fort); Baghel Singh entered Delhi's Red Fort in 1783 and marked Sikh historical sites in Delhi with gurdwaras.
- Baba Deep Singh: martyred defending Harmandir Sahib (1757); Bhai Mani Singh: scribe of the Damdami Bir, martyred at Lahore in the 1730s; Bhai Taru Singh: martyr of the same period.
- Maharaja Ranjit Singh (1780–1839): 'Sher-e-Punjab', founder of the Sikh kingdom; Hari Singh Nalwa, his greatest general, died at Jamrud (1837).
- Maharaja Duleep Singh, the last Sikh ruler, lost the kingdom in 1849 and the Koh-i-noor passed to the British; Maharani Jind Kaur resisted the British.
- Baba Ram Singh (1816–1885): Namdhari (Kuka) leader from Bhaini Sahib; pioneered boycott and swadeshi ideas; exiled to Rangoon.
- Dyal Singh Majithia founded The Tribune (1881, Lahore) and was associated with Punjab National Bank (1894/95).
- Lala Lajpat Rai (1865–1928, born Dhudike, Moga): 'Punjab Kesari'; Arya Samaj and DAV leader; deported to Mandalay in 1907; died after the lathi charge during the Simon Commission protest at Lahore (1928).
- Sardar Ajit Singh (Bhagat Singh's uncle) led the 1907 'Pagri Sambhal Jatta' agitation; Sohan Singh Bhakna was the first president of the Ghadar Party; Kartar Singh Sarabha was hanged at 19 (1915).
- Bhagat Singh (1907–1931): born at Banga (Lyallpur, now Pakistan), ancestral village Khatkar Kalan (SBS Nagar); hanged 23 March 1931 with Rajguru and Sukhdev; memorial at Hussainiwala.
- Udham Singh (1899–1940, Sunam): shot Michael O'Dwyer at Caxton Hall, London, on 13 March 1940 to avenge Jallianwala Bagh; used the name 'Ram Mohammad Singh Azad'.
- Madan Lal Dhingra (Amritsar) shot Curzon Wyllie in London (1909); Baba Kharak Singh led the Akali 'Keys affair'; Master Tara Singh and Sant Fateh Singh led the Punjabi Suba movement.
- Statesmen: Sir Chhotu Ram and Sir Fazl-i-Husain (Unionist Party, 1923); Partap Singh Kairon (CM 1956–64, architect of PAU and the Green Revolution); Giani Zail Singh (CM, then President 1982–87).
- Prime ministers from (undivided) Punjab: Inder Kumar Gujral (born Jhelum) and Dr Manmohan Singh (born Gah; PM 2004–14; died Dec 2024).
- Social service: Bhai Kanhaiya (served water to wounded of both sides — a forerunner of the Red Cross idea); Bhagat Puran Singh founded Pingalwara, Amritsar (1947).
- Sport and science: Milkha Singh ('Flying Sikh'), Balbir Singh Senior (three Olympic hockey golds, 1948–56); Dr Gurdev Singh Khush, rice breeder (World Food Prize 1996).

## Punjab angle

- Many personalities are tied to places: Khatkar Kalan (Bhagat Singh), Sunam (Udham Singh), Sarabha (Ludhiana), Dhudike (Lajpat Rai), Bhaini Sahib (Ram Singh).

## Deep dive

### Ancient to late-Mughal figures (beyond the brief)

- Panini — grammarian of Gandhara (Shalatula, near Attock); his Ashtadhyayi (c. 5th–4th c. BC) is the foundational Sanskrit grammar; Taxila was the region's great learning centre.
- Chanakya (Kautilya) — traditionally linked with Taxila; mentor of Chandragupta Maurya, who drove out Alexander's garrisons from the Punjab.
- Ambhi — ruler of Taxila who submitted to Alexander (326 BC), in contrast to Porus who fought at the Hydaspes (Jhelum).
- Daulat Khan Lodi — Lodi governor of Punjab who invited Babur (1524); Babur's victory at Panipat (1526) followed.
- Rai Bular Bhatti — chief of Talwandi (Nankana Sahib) and early patron of Guru Nanak; Bhai Mardana (rabab) and Bhai Bala were Nanak's companions.
- Zakariya Khan (governor of Lahore 1726–45) and Mir Mannu (Mu'in-ul-Mulk, 1748–53) led the harshest Mughal-era campaigns against the Sikhs; Adina Beg was the last Mughal-appointed governor (d. 1758).
- Ahmad Shah Abdali — invaded repeatedly between 1747 and 1767; Vadda Ghallughara (great massacre) at Kup–Rahira near Malerkotla on 5 Feb 1762; he blew up the Harmandir Sahib in 1762. Chhota Ghallughara (lesser massacre) was in 1746 at Kahnuwan (Gurdaspur).
- Nawab Sher Mohammad Khan of Malerkotla protested (the 'haa da naara', 1705) against the bricking-in of the younger Sahibzadas at Sirhind — the reason Sikhs revere Malerkotla.
- Wazir Khan — Mughal faujdar of Sirhind who executed the younger Sahibzadas (1705); killed by Banda Singh Bahadur's forces at Chappar Chiri (1710).

### The ten Gurus — identity cards for matching questions

- Guru Nanak (1469–1539): born Talwandi (Nankana Sahib); four udasis (journeys); settled Kartarpur on the Ravi; Japji, Babarvani; institution of sangat and langar.
- Guru Angad (1539–52): Khadur Sahib; standardised Gurmukhi; promoted physical fitness (mall akhara); Mata Khivi ran the langar.
- Guru Amar Das (1552–74): Goindwal Sahib baoli (84 steps); manji system for preaching; opposed sati and purdah; composed Anand Sahib.
- Guru Ram Das (1574–81): founded Ramdaspur (later Amritsar) and dug the sarovar; composed the Lavan of the Anand Karaj.
- Guru Arjan Dev (1581–1606): completed Harmandir Sahib; compiled the Adi Granth (1604) with Bhai Gurdas as scribe; founded Tarn Taran and Kartarpur (Jalandhar); first Sikh martyr, Lahore 1606.
- Guru Hargobind (1606–44): doctrine of miri–piri (two swords); built the Akal Takht (1606); detained at Gwalior fort — Bandi Chhor Divas; later moved to Kiratpur.
- Guru Har Rai (1644–61): Kiratpur; maintained a herbal dispensary and a cavalry; Guru Har Krishan (1661–64): the child Guru, died of smallpox at Delhi (Gurdwara Bangla Sahib).
- Guru Tegh Bahadur (1664–75): identified at Baba Bakala; founded Chakk Nanaki (Anandpur Sahib) in 1665; martyred at Delhi in 1675 (Sis Ganj); 350th martyrdom anniversary observed in 2025.
- Guru Gobind Singh (1675–1708): created the Khalsa at Anandpur (Baisakhi 1699); battles of Bhangani (1688), Chamkaur (1704), Khidrana/Muktsar (1705); Zafarnama to Aurangzeb; final recension of the Granth at Damdama Sahib (1706); declared the Granth the eternal Guru at Nanded (1708).

### Misl and Sikh-kingdom era — name, role, why it matters

- Baba Ala Singh — founder of Patiala state; built Qila Mubarak (1763); Phulkian misl (Patiala, Nabha, Jind).
- Jassa Singh Ahluwalia — leader of the Dal Khalsa (1748); minted coins at Lahore (1761); Kapurthala dynasty descends from the Ahluwalias.
- Sardarni Sada Kaur — head of the Kanhaiya misl and Ranjit Singh's mother-in-law; crucial ally in his capture of Lahore (1799).
- Maharaja Ranjit Singh (1780–1839): born Gujranwala; took Lahore 1799; proclaimed Maharaja 1801; Treaty of Amritsar with the British (Metcalfe, 1809) fixed the Sutlej as boundary; took Multan (1818), Kashmir (1819), Peshawar (1834); got the Koh-i-noor from Shah Shuja (1813).
- Ranjit Singh's army was modernised by European officers — Ventura, Allard, Avitabile and Court; the Fauj-i-Khas was the elite brigade; the coins were Nanakshahi.
- Court figures: Fakir Azizuddin (foreign minister), Diwan Mokham Chand (general; Attock 1813), Hari Singh Nalwa (Nowshera 1823, governor of Kashmir and Peshawar; killed at Jamrud 1837), Dogra brothers Dhian Singh (wazir) and Gulab Singh.
- First Anglo-Sikh War (1845–46): Mudki, Ferozeshah, Buddowal, Aliwal, Sobraon (10 Feb 1846, where Sham Singh Attariwala fell); Treaty of Lahore (9 Mar 1846); separate Treaty of Amritsar (16 Mar 1846) sold Kashmir to Gulab Singh; Treaty of Bhairowal (Dec 1846) set up British-controlled regency.
- Second Anglo-Sikh War (1848–49): began with Diwan Mulraj's revolt at Multan; Chattar Singh and Sher Singh Attariwala fought at Ramnagar, Chillianwala (13 Jan 1849) and Gujrat (21 Feb 1849); Punjab annexed by Dalhousie on 29 Mar 1849.
- Maharani Jind Kaur — regent mother of Duleep Singh; exiled to Chunar and Nepal; reunited with her son in England; Bhai Maharaj Singh led anti-British resistance (1848–49) and died in exile at Singapore (1856).

### Colonial era and freedom struggle — additions to the brief

- Sir Ganga Ram (1851–1927) — civil engineer and philanthropist, called the father of modern Lahore; built Renala Khurd hydel scheme and the Ganga Ram Hospital.
- Lala Har Dayal — Stanford lecturer, ideologue and founder-secretary of the Ghadar Party (1913).
- Bhai Parmanand — Arya Samaj scholar and Ghadar sympathiser; sentenced in the first Lahore Conspiracy Case (1915) and sent to the Andamans.
- Baba Gurdit Singh (Sarhali) — chartered the Komagata Maru (1914) to test Canada's 'continuous journey' rule.
- Sukhdev Thapar (born Ludhiana) — HSRA organiser in Punjab, hanged with Bhagat Singh and Rajguru on 23 Mar 1931; Bhagwati Charan Vohra wrote 'The Philosophy of the Bomb' and died in 1930.
- Kishan Singh Gargaj and Master Mota Singh — leaders of the Babbar Akali movement (1921–24) in Doaba.
- Seva Singh Thikriwala — leader of the Punjab Riyasti Praja Mandal (1928) against princely rule; died in Patiala jail in 1935.
- Sohan Singh Josh and Teja Singh Sutantar — Kirti–Kisan and communist peasant leaders; Teja Singh Sutantar later led the PEPSU tenants (muzara) struggle.
- Sir Sikandar Hayat Khan — Unionist Premier of Punjab (1937–42), signed the Sikandar–Jinnah Pact (1937); Khizr Hayat Tiwana — last Unionist Premier, resigned 2 Mar 1947.
- Captain Mohan Singh (raised the first INA, 1942) and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon (Red Fort trial, 1945) are the Punjab INA names to remember.

### After 1947 — statesmen, scientists, sport and the arts

- Chief Ministers: Gopi Chand Bhargava (first CM of East Punjab, 1947); Partap Singh Kairon (1956–64); Gurnam Singh (first non-Congress government, 1967); Parkash Singh Badal (five terms: 1970, 1977, 1997, 2007, 2012); Beant Singh (assassinated 1995); Charanjit Singh Channi (first Dalit CM, Sept 2021); Bhagwant Mann (from March 2022).
- Giani Zail Singh — CM of Punjab (1972–77), Union Home Minister, President of India (1982–87).
- Science: Har Gobind Khorana (born Raipur, now in Pakistan; Nobel Medicine 1968); S. Chandrasekhar (born Lahore; Nobel Physics 1983); Narinder Singh Kapany (born Moga; pioneer of fibre optics).
- Agriculture: M.S. Randhawa (administrator, PAU Vice-Chancellor, art historian of Kangra painting); Gurdev Singh Khush (rice breeder, World Food Prize 1996).
- Sport: Milkha Singh; Balbir Singh Senior; wrestler Dara Singh (Dharmuchak, Amritsar); Bishan Singh Bedi (Amritsar); Harbhajan Singh (Jalandhar); hockey captain Harmanpreet Singh (Amritsar; Olympic bronzes 2020 and 2024); Harmanpreet Kaur (Moga; captain of India's women's ODI World Cup winning side, 2025).
- Arts and cinema: Prithviraj Kapoor (Lyallpur), Balraj Sahni (Rawalpindi), Sahir Ludhianvi; painters Sobha Singh and Amrita Sher-Gil; Nek Chand (Rock Garden).
- Public service: Kiran Bedi (born Amritsar; first woman IPS officer, 1972); Rajkumari Amrit Kaur (Kapurthala family; India's first Health Minister, AIIMS founder).

### Women in Punjab's history

- Mata Khivi (Guru Angad's wife) — organiser of the langar, the only woman named in the Guru Granth Sahib (in a var by Satta and Balwand).
- Mata Gujri — mother of Guru Gobind Singh; martyred with the younger Sahibzadas at Sirhind (Thanda Burj), 1705.
- Mai Bhago — led the forty 'deserters' (Chali Mukte) back to fight at Khidrana (1705).
- Sada Kaur (Kanhaiya misl) and Maharani Jind Kaur — two powerful women of the Sikh state.
- Bibi Gulab Kaur (Ghadar), Durgawati Devi 'Durga Bhabhi' (HSRA), and Bibi Amar Kaur (Bhagat Singh's sister, activist).
- Amrita Pritam — first woman to win the Sahitya Akademi award (1956) and the Jnanpith (1981) in Punjabi.
- Bibi Jagir Kaur — first woman president of the SGPC (1999).

### Titles, birthplaces and traps PPSC likes

- Titles: Sher-e-Punjab (Ranjit Singh), Punjab Kesari (Lajpat Rai), Shaheed-e-Azam (Bhagat Singh), Sultan-ul-Qaum (Jassa Singh Ahluwalia), Flying Sikh (Milkha Singh), Birha da Sultan (Shiv Kumar Batalvi), Nightingale of Punjab (Surinder Kaur).
- Udham Singh shot Michael O'Dwyer (Lt-Governor in 1919), not General Dyer, who died in England in 1927.
- Madan Lal Dhingra shot Curzon Wyllie in London (1909) — a different event from Udham Singh's (1940).
- Bhagat Singh's group aimed at J.A. Scott (who ordered the lathi charge on Lajpat Rai) but killed J.P. Saunders (17 Dec 1928).
- Two 'Treaties of Amritsar': 1809 (Ranjit Singh–British, Sutlej boundary) and 1846 (British–Gulab Singh, sale of Kashmir).
- Birthplace vs ancestral village: Bhagat Singh was born at Banga (Lyallpur, Pakistan); his ancestral village is Khatkar Kalan (SBS Nagar).
- Hari Singh Nalwa died at Jamrud (1837), at the mouth of the Khyber — not in a battle on the Sutlej.

**10.** The painter Sobha Singh, known for widely reproduced portraits of the Sikh Gurus, settled and worked in his later life at:

(a) Dalhousie, in Chamba  
(b) Kasauli, in Solan  
(c) Andretta, in Kangra

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Andretta, in Kangra** — Sobha Singh (1901–86), born at Sri Hargobindpur in Gurdaspur district, made Andretta near Palampur his home from 1949; his studio there is now a museum. Norah Richards, the pioneer of Punjabi theatre, also lived at Andretta.

**16.** Consider the following statements:  
1. Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev were hanged at Lahore in 1929.  
2. Bhagat Singh's group shot dead J.A. Scott, the police officer who ordered the lathi charge on Lajpat Rai.

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Neither 1 nor 2** — Neither is correct. The three were hanged on 23 March 1931; 1929 was the year of the Assembly bomb. The group meant to kill Superintendent J.A. Scott but shot Assistant Superintendent J.P. Saunders on 17 December 1928.
