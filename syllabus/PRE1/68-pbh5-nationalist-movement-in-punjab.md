# Nationalist movement in Punjab

**Paper I — General Studies** › Punjab · Subject: Punjab History · Priority: High

> Official syllabus line: Nationalist movement in Punjab.

## Short notes

- Kuka (Namdhari) movement under Baba Ram Singh (from 1857) used boycott and swadeshi ideas against British institutions; suppressed after the Malerkotla episode (1872).
- 1907 agrarian agitation against the Colonisation Bill and canal water rates: 'Pagri Sambhal Jatta'; Lala Lajpat Rai and Sardar Ajit Singh deported to Mandalay.
- Ghadar Party (1913, Pacific coast of the USA): president Sohan Singh Bhakna, secretary Lala Har Dayal; paper 'Ghadar'; Yugantar Ashram in San Francisco; mostly Punjabi emigrants.
- Komagata Maru (1914): ship chartered by Baba Gurdit Singh carrying 376 passengers was refused entry at Vancouver; firing on returning passengers at Budge Budge (29 Sept 1914).
- The Ghadar uprising planned for Feb 1915 was betrayed by the informer Kirpal Singh; Lahore Conspiracy Case — Kartar Singh Sarabha and others hanged (16 Nov 1915).
- Rowlatt Act protest: Dr Satyapal and Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew arrested on 10 April 1919 at Amritsar, leading to riots.
- Jallianwala Bagh massacre (13 April 1919, Baisakhi): troops under Brig-Gen Reginald Dyer fired on a peaceful crowd; Lt Gov Michael O'Dwyer backed him; martial law and the 'crawling order' followed.
- Responses: Tagore renounced his knighthood; Gandhi returned the Kaiser-i-Hind medal; Hunter Committee (official) and a Congress inquiry committee; Gujranwala was bombed from the air.
- Gurdwara Reform (Akali) movement (1920–25) was a mass non-violent struggle; Gandhi called the Keys affair victory the first decisive battle of India's freedom.
- Babbar Akali movement (1921–24, Doaba): militant offshoot that targeted British informers; led by Kishan Singh Gargaj and others.
- Naujawan Bharat Sabha (1926, Lahore) was founded by Bhagat Singh and comrades; Kirti Kisan Party (1927–28) organised peasants and workers.
- HSRA formed in 1928 (Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi); Lajpat Rai was fatally injured in the lathi charge against the Simon Commission at Lahore (30 Oct 1928); Saunders shot (17 Dec 1928).
- Bhagat Singh and B.K. Dutt threw bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly (8 April 1929); Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev were hanged at Lahore on 23 March 1931 and cremated at Hussainiwala.
- Lahore Congress session (Dec 1929, president Jawaharlal Nehru) on the Ravi bank adopted Purna Swaraj; the tricolour was hoisted on 31 Dec 1929, leading to 26 Jan 1930 as Independence Day.
- Praja Mandal movement in the princely states (Punjab Riyasti Praja Mandal, 1928): Seva Singh Thikriwala died in Patiala jail (1935).
- Udham Singh shot Michael O'Dwyer in London (13 March 1940) and was hanged at Pentonville (31 July 1940).
- Captain Mohan Singh raised the first Indian National Army (1942); the INA Red Fort trial (1945) of Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Sahgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon united national opinion.
- Muslim League's Lahore Resolution (23 March 1940) demanded separate states; Punjab became the main site of Partition violence in 1947.

## Punjab angle

- Martyrdom dates are state observances: 23 March (Shaheedi Diwas, Hussainiwala), 31 July (Udham Singh), 16 Nov (Kartar Singh Sarabha); Jallianwala Bagh memorial was renovated in 2021.

## How PPSC asks it

Chronology and pairing: Ghadar leaders, Komagata Maru, Jallianwala responses, NBS/HSRA milestones and the Lahore Congress. Expect 'arrange in order' and 'who was associated with' questions.

## Deep dive

### Kuka (Namdhari) movement, 1857–72

- Forerunner: Baba Balak Singh of Hazro (d. 1862) preached simple 'naam' worship. His disciple Baba Ram Singh (1816–85), a former soldier of the Lahore army, made Bhaini Sahib (Ludhiana) the centre.
- On Baisakhi 1857 Ram Singh founded the Sant Khalsa: white hand-spun dress, a woollen rosary, and 'kuk' (ecstatic cries) at worship, which gave the name Kuka.
- Social code: simple anand marriage at minimal cost (including mass and inter-caste weddings), and opposition to dowry, female infanticide, child marriage, meat and liquor.
- Political method: a parallel organisation of about 22 subas and naib-subas, a private postal service, boycott of British courts, schools, government service and the post, and swadeshi cloth. Historians call it an early form of non-cooperation.
- Cow protection turned it violent: attacks on butchers at Amritsar (June 1871) and Raikot (July 1871) led to hangings.
- Malerkotla episode (Jan 1872): after a raid on Malerkotla, about 65 captured Kukas were blown away from cannon (17–18 Jan 1872) on the orders of Deputy Commissioner J.L. Cowan, endorsed by Commissioner T.D. Forsyth. Both officers were later removed from service.
- Ram Singh was deported to Rangoon and then Mergui (Burma), where he died in 1885. Namdharis observe the Malerkotla martyrdom day in January; the official records were published as 'Papers relating to the Kuka sect' (1872).
- Assessment: a socio-religious reform movement with anti-British politics. Most historians do not treat it as a planned uprising; officials exaggerated the threat.

### 1907 agrarian unrest ('Pagri Sambhal Jatta')

- Grievances: the Punjab Colonisation (Amendment) Bill (1906), which brought harsher inheritance rules, fines and resumption of grants if conditions were broken; a rise in Bari Doab canal water rates; and a land-revenue increase in Rawalpindi district.
- Leaders: Sardar Ajit Singh (Bhagat Singh's uncle), Kishan Singh (Bhagat Singh's father), Sufi Amba Prasad and Lala Lajpat Rai. Ajit Singh's Bharat Mata Society (Anjuman-i-Muhibban-i-Watan) organised meetings.
- At a Lyallpur meeting in March 1907, Banke Dayal, editor of the Jhang Sial, sang 'Pagri Sambhal Jatta', which became the anthem of the agitation.
- Rawalpindi disturbances followed (April–May 1907). On 9 May 1907 Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh were deported to Mandalay under Regulation III of 1818.
- Lord Minto vetoed the Colonisation Bill (1907) and the deportees were released in Nov 1907. The protest achieved its main aim.
- Ajit Singh left India in 1909, worked abroad (Persia, Europe, South America), returned in 1947 and died at Dalhousie on 15 Aug 1947.
- Significance: the first mass peasant protest in colonial Punjab, linking urban nationalists and colonists, and part of the Swadeshi-era radicalism.

### Ghadar movement and Komagata Maru (1913–1917)

- Background: Punjabi migrants on the North American Pacific coast (lumber mills, railways, farms) faced racial exclusion. Canada's 'continuous journey' regulation (1908) blocked Indian immigration. Taraknath Das ('Free Hindustan') and Lala Har Dayal (Stanford) politicised the migrants.
- Founding (1913, Astoria, Oregon; 21 April is the date usually given): the Hindi Association of the Pacific Coast, with Sohan Singh Bhakna as president, Har Dayal as general secretary and Pandit Kanshi Ram as treasurer. Its headquarters was the Yugantar Ashram, San Francisco.
- Paper 'Ghadar' (first issue 1 Nov 1913, in Urdu, followed by Gurmukhi and other editions; the poems were collected as 'Ghadar di Goonj'). Kartar Singh Sarabha, then a Berkeley student, helped print it.
- Komagata Maru (1914): the Japanese ship chartered by Baba Gurdit Singh left Hong Kong (4 April), reached Vancouver (23 May) with 376 passengers, was kept offshore for two months (only about 20–24 were admitted) and was forced out on 23 July. At Budge Budge near Calcutta (29 Sept 1914) police firing killed about 20.
- On the outbreak of World War I several thousand Ghadarites sailed home (on ships such as the Korea and Tosa Maru). Many were detained at ports under the Ingress into India Ordinance (1914).
- The uprising planned for 21 Feb 1915 (brought forward to 19 Feb), with Rash Behari Bose at Lahore, was betrayed by the police informer Kirpal Singh and failed. The 5th Light Infantry mutinied at Singapore (15 Feb 1915).
- First Lahore Conspiracy Case (tried under the Defence of India Act 1915): seven were hanged on 16 Nov 1915, including Kartar Singh Sarabha (aged 19) and Vishnu Ganesh Pingle. Others, such as Sohan Singh Bhakna and Bhai Parmanand, had death sentences commuted and were sent to the Andamans.
- Hindu–German Conspiracy Trial, San Francisco (1917–18): Ram Chandra, Har Dayal's successor, was shot dead in the courtroom.
- Legacy: secular, republican and egalitarian ideology (Harish Puri). Former Ghadarites later led the Kirti, Babbar Akali and Ad-Dharm movements and the communist groups. Desh Bhagat Yadgar Hall in Jalandhar holds the annual Ghadri Babian da Mela.

### 1919: Rowlatt Satyagraha, Jallianwala Bagh and martial law

- The Rowlatt Act (Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act, March 1919) allowed detention without trial. All-India hartals were held on 30 March and 6 April, and the Ram Navami procession at Amritsar (9 April) showed Hindu–Muslim unity.
- 10 April 1919: Saifuddin Kitchlew and Satyapal were deported to Dharamsala. Firing near the Hall Bridge led to mob violence: bank officials were killed and the missionary Marcella Sherwood was assaulted. Dyer arrived on 11 April.
- 13 April (Baisakhi): a crowd of about 15,000–20,000, many unaware of the ban, gathered in the walled Bagh. Dyer brought about 90 soldiers (50 with rifles) and two armoured cars that could not enter. He fired about 1,650 rounds for some ten minutes without warning.
- Toll: the official (Hunter) figure was 379 dead and about 1,200 wounded; the Congress inquiry estimated about 1,000 dead. Curfew prevented help for the wounded.
- Martial law (from 15 April) in Lahore, Amritsar, Gujranwala (bombed from the air on 14 April), Kasur and Sheikhupura: the 'crawling order' in the lane where Sherwood was attacked, public floggings, forced salaaming, and summary tribunals.
- Disorders Inquiry (Hunter) Committee (appointed 14 Oct 1919) had three Indian members (Chimanlal Setalvad, Jagat Narayan, Sultan Ahmad Khan), whose minority report was more critical. Dyer was relieved of command. The Commons censured him (July 1920) but the Lords backed him, and a British newspaper fund raised a large purse for him.
- Congress Punjab inquiry: Motilal Nehru, then Gandhi, with C.R. Das, Abbas Tyabji and M.R. Jayakar (report 1920). The Amritsar Congress session (Dec 1919) was presided over by Motilal Nehru.
- Protests: Tagore renounced his knighthood (letter of 30 May 1919); Sankaran Nair resigned from the Viceroy's Council; Gandhi returned the Kaiser-i-Hind medal (Aug 1920) as he launched Non-Cooperation.
- Udham Singh shot O'Dwyer, not Dyer (who died in 1927), at Caxton Hall, London, on 13 March 1940. He was hanged on 31 July 1940 and his remains were brought home in 1974.
- Memorial: the Jallianwala Bagh National Memorial Act (1951); the flame-shaped memorial by Benjamin Polk was inaugurated on 13 April 1961 by President Rajendra Prasad; a 2019 amendment removed the Congress President as an ex-officio trustee; the renovated complex opened on 28 Aug 2021.

### Gurdwara Reform (Akali) movement, 1920–25: morcha by morcha

- Causes: Udasi mahants treated shrines as private estates; a government-appointed manager (sarbrah) ran the Golden Temple (Arur Singh honoured Dyer); the Rakab Ganj wall was demolished (1913–14); the Central Sikh League was formed (Dec 1919). Returning soldiers and Ghadarites radicalised Sikh politics.
- 12 Oct 1920: reformers took control of the Golden Temple and Akal Takht after the priests refused offerings from newly initiated 'low-caste' Sikhs. The SGPC was formed on 15 Nov 1920 (175 members; Sunder Singh Majithia first president) and the Shiromani Akali Dal on 14 Dec 1920.
- Tarn Taran (Jan 1921). Nankana Sahib massacre (20 Feb 1921): Mahant Narain Das's men killed Bhai Lachhman Singh Dharowal's jatha, well over 100 Sikhs. Gandhi visited; the mahant was later sentenced to life.
- Keys Affair (Nov 1921–Jan 1922): the Deputy Commissioner of Amritsar seized the toshakhana keys and Baba Kharak Singh and others were arrested. The government returned the keys on 19 Jan 1922, and Gandhi hailed it as the first decisive battle for India's freedom won.
- Guru ka Bagh (Aug–Nov 1922, near Ajnala): non-violent jathas were beaten daily for cutting firewood on gurdwara land. C.F. Andrews's eyewitness account drew national sympathy. It ended when Sir Ganga Ram leased the land.
- Jaito (1923–25): Maharaja Ripudaman Singh of Nabha was forced to abdicate (July 1923); an akhand path at Gangsar gurdwara was interrupted; the Shaheedi jatha was fired on (21 Feb 1924). Jawaharlal Nehru, K. Santhanam and A.T. Gidwani were arrested at Jaito (Sept 1923).
- The Sikh Gurdwaras Act 1925, under Governor Malcolm Hailey, put historic gurdwaras under an elected SGPC and set up a Gurdwara Judicial Commission. The Akalis split over accepting conditions for the release of prisoners.
- Significance: a mass, disciplined non-violent movement that ran parallel to Non-Cooperation; the birth of the SGPC and Akali Dal as lasting political institutions; strong participation by peasants and ex-soldiers.

### Babbar Akalis, Kirti, NBS–HSRA and Praja Mandals

- Babbar Akalis: militants who rejected Akali non-violence after Nankana. The Chakravarti Jatha (1921; Kishan Singh Gargaj, Master Mota Singh) became the Babbar Akali Jatha (1922). They published the cyclostyled 'Babbar Akali Doaba' and carried out 'sudhar' (killings of informers, the jholichuks) in the Doaba (1922–23).
- Babbar Akali case: six were hanged at Lahore on 27 Feb 1926: Kishan Singh Gargaj, Santa Singh, Dalip Singh Dhamian, Karam Singh Manko, Nand Singh Ghurial and Dharam Singh Hayatpur (Encyclopaedia of Sikhism).
- Kirti: a Punjabi monthly from Amritsar (Feb 1926) started by Santokh Singh, a Ghadarite back from Moscow. The Kirti Kisan Party (1928) followed under Sohan Singh Josh, who was tried in the Meerut Conspiracy Case (1929). The Kirti group later merged into the Communist Party of India.
- Naujawan Bharat Sabha (March 1926, Lahore): Bhagat Singh, Bhagwati Charan Vohra and Ram Chandra. HSRA (Sept 1928, Feroz Shah Kotla). Jatin Das died after a 63-day hunger strike in Lahore jail (13 Sept 1929). Bhagat Singh wrote 'Why I am an Atheist' (1930).
- Praja Mandal movement in the princely states: the All India States People's Conference (1927); the Punjab Riyasti Praja Mandal (1928) with Seva Singh Thikriwala, who died in Patiala jail (20 Jan 1935). Its demands were responsible government and an end to begar.
- Muzara (tenant) struggles in Patiala state, led by the Lal (Red) Communist Party under Teja Singh Sutantar, continued into the PEPSU period (Kishangarh clash, 1949).

### PPSC traps

- The Ghadar Party was founded at Astoria, Oregon (its headquarters was in San Francisco). Its first president was Sohan Singh Bhakna, not Har Dayal.
- Komagata Maru was a Japanese ship chartered by Gurdit Singh. The Budge Budge firing (1914) was in Bengal, not Canada.
- Udham Singh killed Michael O'Dwyer (Lt-Governor), not Reginald Dyer (Brigadier-General).
- The Hunter Committee was a government committee. The Congress committee was a separate inquiry led by Motilal Nehru and Gandhi.
- Gandhi's 'first decisive battle' remark referred to the Keys Affair (1922), not Nankana or Jaito.
- Sequence for 'arrange chronologically' questions: Kuka (1872) → Pagri Sambhal (1907) → Ghadar (1913) → Komagata Maru (1914) → Jallianwala (1919) → Nankana (1921) → Keys (1922) → Guru ka Bagh (1922) → Jaito (1923–24) → Gurdwara Act (1925) → NBS (1926) → Babbar hangings (1926) → Simon Commission lathi charge (1928) → Purna Swaraj (1929) → Bhagat Singh's execution (1931).
