## Short notes

- Article 40 (DPSP) directs the state to organise village panchayats as units of self-government.
- Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) recommended 'democratic decentralisation' with three tiers - Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti (block), Zila Parishad (district).
- Rajasthan was first to adopt Panchayati Raj (Nagaur, 2 Oct 1959), followed by Andhra Pradesh.
- Ashok Mehta Committee (1977) recommended a two-tier system (Zila Parishad and Mandal Panchayat) and participation of political parties.
- G.V.K. Rao Committee (1985) called the system 'grass without roots' and made the district the key unit; L.M. Singhvi Committee (1986) recommended constitutional recognition; P.K. Thungon Committee (1988) also backed it.
- The 64th Amendment Bill (1989) failed in the Rajya Sabha; the 73rd Amendment Act 1992 came into force on 24 April 1993 - observed as National Panchayati Raj Day.
- Part IX (Arts 243 to 243-O) and the Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects such as agriculture, minor irrigation, drinking water, rural roads, primary education, health, poverty alleviation).
- Compulsory features: Gram Sabha; three-tier structure (intermediate level optional for states with population under 20 lakh); direct elections to all seats; minimum age 21; five-year term with fresh election within six months of dissolution.
- Reservation: seats for SC/ST in proportion to population; at least one-third seats and chairperson posts for women; states may reserve for backward classes.
- State Election Commission (Art 243K) conducts panchayat elections; State Finance Commission (Art 243-I) set up every five years by the Governor to review panchayat finances.
- Voluntary features: devolution of powers on Eleventh Schedule subjects, giving panchayats taxation powers and grants, representation of MPs/MLAs, reservation for backward classes.
- Many states now reserve 50% seats for women in PRIs; Bihar was the first to do so (2006).
- Part IX does not apply to Nagaland, Meghalaya, Mizoram, hill areas of Manipur and Darjeeling (Gorkha Hill Council), among other exempted areas.
- PESA Act 1996 (on Dileep Singh Bhuria Committee's advice) extends Part IX to Fifth Schedule areas in 10 states, empowering Gram Sabhas over minor forest produce, land alienation and local resources.
- 74th Amendment (1992): Part IXA (Arts 243P-243ZG), Twelfth Schedule (18 subjects), Nagar Panchayats, Municipal Councils and Corporations, ward committees, District Planning Committee (243ZD) and Metropolitan Planning Committee (243ZE).
- Ministry of Panchayati Raj (2004) schemes: Rashtriya Gram Swaraj Abhiyan, Gram Panchayat Development Plan, eGramSwaraj portal, SVAMITVA (2020, drone mapping of rural inhabited land for property cards).
- Union Finance Commission grants to local bodies flow under Art 280(3)(bb) and (c); the 15th Finance Commission tied part of these grants to water and sanitation.
- Persisting problems: weak devolution of the '3Fs' (funds, functions, functionaries), low own-source revenue, proxy representation of elected women ('sarpanch pati'), irregular state finance commissions.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab Panchayati Raj Act 1994 created the three-tier system in the state; Punjab has over 13,000 gram panchayats.
- Punjab reserves 50% seats for women in panchayats and urban local bodies (approved in 2017).
- Punjab has no Fifth or Sixth Schedule areas, so PESA does not apply to it.

## Deep dive

### Part IX — Article-wise map (Article — provision)

- 243 — definitions (Gram Sabha, intermediate level, panchayat area, village).
- 243A — Gram Sabha: powers as the state legislature provides.
- 243B — three-tier panchayats; intermediate tier optional for states with population under 20 lakh.
- 243C — composition; village chairperson elected as state law provides; chairpersons of intermediate and district panchayats elected indirectly from among elected members.
- 243D — reservation for SC/ST (proportionate), one-third for women (including chairpersons); backward-class reservation optional.
- 243E — five-year term; elections before expiry or within six months of dissolution; a body dissolved early serves only the remainder.
- 243F — disqualifications (minimum age 21).
- 243G — powers and responsibilities, plans for economic development and social justice, Eleventh Schedule subjects.
- 243H — taxes, duties, tolls, fees; grants-in-aid from the state.
- 243-I — State Finance Commission every five years; 243J — audit of accounts.
- 243K — State Election Commission, removable only like a High Court judge; 243L — application to UTs.
- 243M — exempted areas; 243N — existing laws continue for at most one year; 243-O — courts barred in electoral matters except by election petition.

### Committees and milestones (deeper detail)

- 2 Oct 1952 — Community Development Programme; 1953 — National Extension Service; their limited success led to the Balwant Rai Mehta study team (appointed Jan 1957, report Nov 1957).
- Jan 1958 — National Development Council accepted the Mehta report.
- K. Santhanam Committee (1963) — panchayat finances; Dantwala (1978) — block-level planning; Hanumantha Rao (1984) — district planning.
- G.V.K. Rao (1985) — district as the basic unit, DDC as chief executive of the Zila Parishad.
- L.M. Singhvi (1986) — constitutional recognition, Nyaya Panchayats, more finances.
- P.K. Thungon sub-committee (1988) — constitutional status; Sarkaria (1988) — regular elections.
- 64th Bill (1989) passed by the Lok Sabha but defeated in the Rajya Sabha; a 1990 Bill lapsed; the 73rd passed in Dec 1992, received assent on 20 April 1993 and came into force on 24 April 1993.
- Ministry of Panchayati Raj formed 2004; Gram Panchayat Development Plans from 2015; SVAMITVA launched 24 April 2020; eGramSwaraj 2020; Panchayat Advancement Index baseline report released 2025.
- Gram Nyayalayas Act 2008 partly met the Nyaya Panchayat idea.

### Devolution — evidence and debates

- Ministry of Panchayati Raj's devolution index ranks states on the framework, functions, finances, functionaries, capacity and accountability; Karnataka topped the 2024 report.
- Women make up about 46% of elected representatives in panchayats (Union government, 2025) — more than the one-third floor because 21 states and two UTs reserve about 50%.
- 15th Finance Commission: about Rs 4.36 lakh crore for local bodies (2021–26), of which about Rs 2.37 lakh crore for rural bodies; 60% tied to water and sanitation.
- Conditions for FC grants: online availability of audited accounts and, for urban bodies, notified floor rates of property tax.
- RBI's first study of panchayat finances (January 2024) found about 95% of panchayat revenue comes as grants from higher governments; own revenue (property tax, fees, fines) is a tiny share.
- Proxy representation: a Ministry committee (set up September 2023 after a Supreme Court order, chaired by Sushil Kumar) recommended exemplary penalties and training to end "pradhan pati" practices (report, February 2025).
- Kerala's People's Plan Campaign (1996) devolved 35–40% of plan funds; Karnataka and Maharashtra are other models.
- DRDAs were merged into Zila Parishads in many states after central support for DRDA administration ended in 2022.

### PESA and exempted areas

- PESA 1996 (Dileep Singh Bhuria Committee) applies to Fifth Schedule areas in 10 states.
- Gram Sabha powers: approve plans and beneficiaries, ownership of minor forest produce, prevent land alienation, control money-lending, manage village markets, regulate intoxicants.
- Prior recommendation of the Gram Sabha or panchayat is mandatory for prospecting licences and mining leases for minor minerals; consultation before land acquisition.
- Forest Rights Act 2006 complements PESA by vesting community forest rights in Gram Sabhas.
- Sixth Schedule autonomous councils are a parallel tribal self-government system with legislative powers.

### Punjab — PRI structure and recent events

- Punjab Panchayati Raj Act 1994 (Act 9 of 1994): Gram Sabha (all voters), Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti (block) and Zila Parishad.
- Punjab State Election Commission set up under the Punjab State Election Commission Act 1994.
- Aug 2023: the state dissolved all gram panchayats early; after a High Court challenge it told the court (end-Aug 2023) it would withdraw the notification.
- Sept 2024 amendment: panch and sarpanch elections held without party symbols; the CM announced Rs 5 lakh incentive for villages electing sarpanches unanimously.
- 15 Oct 2024: gram panchayat polls for over 13,000 panchayats with about 77% turnout (State Election Commissioner Raj Kamal Chaudhuri); many sarpanches were returned unopposed.
- 14 Dec 2025: Zila Parishad and Panchayat Samiti elections (on party symbols, turnout near 48%); results on 17 Dec 2025 gave the ruling AAP a large majority.
- Punjab Panchayati Raj (Amendment) Bill 2026 (published 10 Aug 2026): a new Panchayat Development Secretary cadre as executive head of gram panchayats, merging panchayat secretaries and gram sewaks; old cadre declared "dying".
- Shamlat land under the Punjab Village Common Lands (Regulation) Act 1961 is the main own income of panchayats; one-third of cultivable land on lease is reserved for SCs.
- 50% reservation for women in PRIs; no Fifth/Sixth Schedule areas, so PESA does not apply.

### Traps

- The Gram Sabha is not elected; it is every registered voter in the village.
- Reservation of chairperson posts for SC/ST is in proportion to the state's population; for women at least one-third.
- The State Election Commissioner is appointed by the Governor but removable only like a High Court judge.
- Panchayat election disputes go by election petition to the authority set by state law (243-O), not under the RPA 1951.
- Members of Parliament and MLAs may be given representation in panchayats only if state law provides — it is voluntary.
- National Panchayati Raj Day is 24 April; Rajasthan (Nagaur, 1959) first adopted Panchayati Raj after the Mehta report.
