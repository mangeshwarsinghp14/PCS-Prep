## Short notes

- India has about 2.4% of world area, about 4% of world water resources and nearly 18% of world population.
- Average annual precipitation is about 4,000 cubic km; mean annual surface flow is about 1,869 cubic km; utilisable water is about 1,122 cubic km (690 surface + 432 groundwater).
- Per capita water availability fell from about 1,816 cubic m (2001) to 1,545 cubic m (2011); below 1,700 is 'water-stressed' and below 1,000 is 'water-scarce'.
- Agriculture uses the largest share of water (around 80% or more); groundwater supplies most irrigation.
- Sources of irrigation: wells and tube-wells (largest share of net irrigated area), canals (northern plains, river valleys), tanks (peninsular India - Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh).
- Groundwater: national stage of extraction around 60%, but Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan and Delhi extract more than annual recharge (over-exploited blocks).
- Water quality problems: urban sewage, industrial effluents and farm chemicals; arsenic (West Bengal, Bihar), fluoride (Rajasthan, Andhra) and uranium (parts of Punjab) in groundwater; Ganga and Yamuna have heavily polluted stretches.
- Conservation: watershed development (Haryali, Neeru-Meeru in Andhra Pradesh, Arvary Pani Sansad in Alwar, Rajasthan), rainwater harvesting (Tamil Nadu made it compulsory for buildings), recycling of wastewater.
- Traditional systems: johads and tankas (Rajasthan), kuls (Himachal), zabo (Nagaland), bamboo drip irrigation (Meghalaya), baolis (stepwells).
- Programmes: Jal Shakti Ministry (2019), Jal Jeevan Mission (2019), Jal Shakti Abhiyan 'Catch the Rain', Atal Bhujal Yojana (2019, community groundwater management in 7 states), Mission Amrit Sarovar (2022, 75 ponds per district), Namami Gange (2014).
- National Water Policies 1987, 2002 and 2012 give drinking water first priority; water is a State subject (Entry 17), while inter-state rivers fall under Union Entry 56.
- Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956 (under Art 262); tribunals for Krishna, Godavari, Narmada, Cauvery, Ravi-Beas (Eradi Tribunal 1986), Mahadayi and others; Art 262 lets Parliament bar courts from such disputes.
- River interlinking: National Perspective Plan (1980) with Himalayan and Peninsular components; Ken-Betwa is the first link project taken up (foundation stone Dec 2024).
- Indus Waters Treaty 1960 (World Bank brokered): eastern rivers Ravi, Beas, Sutlej to India; western rivers Indus, Jhelum, Chenab mainly to Pakistan; India placed the treaty in abeyance in April 2025.
- Multipurpose projects (Nehru's 'temples of modern India') provide irrigation, power and flood control but cause displacement and ecological damage (Narmada Bachao Andolan, Tehri).

## Punjab angle

- Punjab's groundwater stage of extraction is above 150% - the highest among major states - due to free farm power and paddy cultivation.
- Sutlej-Yamuna Link (SYL) canal dispute: Punjab's Termination of Agreements Act 2004 was held invalid by the Supreme Court in 2016; the canal remains incomplete.
- Key projects: Bhakra (Sutlej), Pong (Beas) and Ranjit Sagar/Thein (Ravi) dams; Harike barrage at the Sutlej-Beas confluence.
- Uranium and other contaminants in Malwa groundwater, and the polluted Buddha Nullah in Ludhiana entering the Sutlej, are major water-quality concerns.

## How PPSC asks it

Questions test water availability figures, irrigation sources by region, traditional harvesting systems with states, IWT river allocation and inter-state disputes. Memorise the water-stress thresholds and IWT rivers.

## Deep dive

### Mapping list — dams and river projects (dam — river — state)

- Indus basin: Bhakra (Sutlej, Himachal; Gobind Sagar), Pong (Beas, Himachal; Maharana Pratap Sagar), Pandoh (Beas, diverts to Sutlej), Ranjit Sagar/Thein (Ravi, Punjab–J&K border), Shahpur Kandi (Ravi, Punjab; completed 2024), Salal and Baglihar (Chenab), Uri (Jhelum), Kishanganga (Kishanganga).
- Ganga basin: Tehri (Bhagirathi, Uttarakhand; India's tallest dam), Rihand (Rihand, UP; Govind Ballabh Pant Sagar), Gandhi Sagar and Rana Pratap Sagar (Chambal), Maithon and Panchet (Damodar Valley Corporation), Farakka barrage (Ganga, West Bengal; 1975).
- Peninsular: Hirakud (Mahanadi, Odisha; long earthen dam), Sardar Sarovar and Indira Sagar (Narmada), Nagarjuna Sagar and Srisailam (Krishna), Almatti (Krishna, Karnataka), Tungabhadra (Karnataka), Krishna Raja Sagar (Kaveri, Karnataka), Mettur (Kaveri, Tamil Nadu), Polavaram (Godavari, Andhra; national project).
- Kerala and Maharashtra: Idukki (Periyar; arch dam), Mullaperiyar (Periyar; 1895; Tamil Nadu–Kerala dispute), Koyna (Koyna, Maharashtra; 1967 Koyna earthquake is the classic case of reservoir-induced seismicity).
- Damodar Valley Corporation (1948) was modelled on the Tennessee Valley Authority (USA); the Damodar was once called the 'Sorrow of Bengal'.
- Ken–Betwa link (Daudhan dam) is the first river-interlinking project taken up (foundation stone December 2024).

### Water governance — institutions, laws and tribunals

- River Boards Act 1956 (never used) and Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956 (amended 2002 to add time limits); a 2019 bill for a single permanent tribunal lapsed.
- Tribunals: Krishna (1969; second one 2004), Godavari (1969), Narmada (1969; award 1979), Ravi–Beas (Eradi, 1986), Cauvery (1990; award 2007, modified by the Supreme Court in 2018 which led to the Cauvery Water Management Authority), Vansadhara (2010), Mahadayi (2010; award 2018), Mahanadi (2018).
- National Water Mission (2009, under NAPCC) aims at a 20% gain in water-use efficiency.
- Dam Safety Act 2021 created a National Committee on Dam Safety and a National Dam Safety Authority; India has about 6,000 large dams, third after China and the USA.
- Ganga was declared a 'national river' in 2008; Namami Gange (2014) is run by the National Mission for Clean Ganga.
- Jal Jeevan Mission (launched 15 August 2019) was extended to 2028 in Budget 2025-26.

### Transboundary waters

- Indus Waters Treaty (1960, Karachi; Nehru–Ayub Khan; World Bank): Permanent Indus Commission meets yearly; disputes over Kishanganga and Ratle went to a Neutral Expert and a Court of Arbitration in parallel.
- India sought modification of the treaty in January 2023 and September 2024, and placed it in abeyance in April 2025 after the Pahalgam attack.
- Ganga Water Treaty with Bangladesh (December 1996) shares lean-season flow at Farakka for 30 years, so it is due for renewal in 2026.
- Nepal: Kosi (1954) and Gandak (1959) agreements and the Mahakali Treaty (1996, Pancheshwar project); Bhutan: Chukha, Tala, Mangdechhu and Punatsangchhu hydropower.
- China began building a mega dam on the lower Yarlung Tsangpo (Brahmaputra) in July 2025; India plans the Siang Upper Multipurpose Project (about 11,000 MW) partly as a buffer.
- Teesta water-sharing with Bangladesh remains unsigned since 2011.

### Irrigation and water-use efficiency

- PMKSY (2015) components: Accelerated Irrigation Benefit Programme, Har Khet Ko Pani, Per Drop More Crop (micro-irrigation) and watershed development.
- Canal (flood) irrigation is only about 35–40% efficient, drip irrigation about 90%, sprinklers about 70–75%.
- Virtual water (asked by PPSC 2021): exporting rice exports the thousands of litres of water used to grow each kilogram.
- NITI Aayog's Composite Water Management Index (2018) warned that about 600 million Indians faced high to extreme water stress.
- Water budgeting at village level (Hiware Bazar) and aquifer mapping (NAQUIM) are demand-side tools that answers should mention.
- Treated wastewater reuse: Nagpur sells treated sewage to a thermal power plant — the standard example of urban reuse.

### Punjab water file

- Canals: Upper Bari Doab Canal (1859, from Madhopur headworks on the Ravi), Sirhind Canal (1882, from Ropar on the Sutlej), Bhakra Main Line (Nangal), Bist Doab Canal, and the Sirhind and Rajasthan feeders from Harike; the Kandi canal serves Hoshiarpur's foothills.
- Tube-wells irrigate roughly 70% of Punjab's net irrigated area; canal water does not reach many tail-ends.
- 1955 inter-state conference allocated surplus Ravi–Beas water (15.85 MAF): Rajasthan 8.0, Punjab 7.2, J&K 0.65; the 1981 agreement reallocated 17.17 MAF: Rajasthan 8.6, Punjab 4.22, Haryana 3.5, J&K 0.65, Delhi 0.2.
- Sutlej–Yamuna Link: about 214 km (122 km in Punjab); foundation at Kapoori (1982); Rajiv–Longowal Accord (1985) led to the Eradi Tribunal; Punjab's 2004 termination law was held invalid in 2016 and the canal remains unfinished.
- Bhakra Beas Management Board (under the Punjab Reorganisation Act 1966) runs Bhakra and Pong; in May 2025 Punjab resisted extra releases to Haryana, deploying police at Nangal.
- Shahpur Kandi dam (2024) and the Indus treaty's abeyance raise the question of how much more Ravi water Punjab can store and use.

### Traps and PPSC patterns

- Water is a State subject (Entry 17, List II) subject to Entry 56, List I on inter-state rivers; Article 262 lets Parliament bar courts from such disputes.
- The Mahakali Treaty is with Nepal, not Bhutan.
- Koyna, not Tehri, is the textbook example of reservoir-induced seismicity.
- Tehri is the tallest dam; Hirakud is famous for length.
- PPSC 2018 asked Baglihar and Dulhasti (both on the Chenab) — know which project is on which river.
- Atal Bhujal Yojana does not cover Punjab.

## Practice MCQs (12)

**1.** Under the Indus Waters Treaty 1960, India has full use of which rivers?

(a) Indus, Jhelum and Chenab  
(b) Chenab, Sutlej and Indus  
(c) Ravi, Beas and Sutlej  
(d) Jhelum, Ravi and Beas

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Ravi, Beas and Sutlej** — The eastern rivers went to India; the western rivers mainly to Pakistan.

</details>

**2.** Which is the largest source of irrigation in India by net irrigated area?

(a) Lift irrigation from rivers  
(b) Tanks  
(c) Wells and tube-wells  
(d) Canals

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Wells and tube-wells** — Groundwater through wells and tube-wells irrigates the largest area.

</details>

**3.** A country is considered 'water-stressed' when annual per capita water availability falls below:

(a) 1,700 cubic m  
(b) 1,000 cubic m  
(c) 2,500 cubic m  
(d) 500 cubic m

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 1,700 cubic m** — By the Falkenmark indicator a country is water-stressed below 1,700 cubic m per person per year. The 1,000 cubic m option is tempting but marks 'water scarcity', and 500 cubic m marks absolute scarcity.

</details>

**4.** Consider the statements: 1. The Inter-State River Water Disputes Act was enacted under Article 262. 2. Punjab is one of the states covered by the Atal Bhujal Yojana. Which is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 1 only** — Atal Bhujal Yojana covers Gujarat, Haryana, Karnataka, MP, Maharashtra, Rajasthan and UP - not Punjab.

</details>

**5.** Arvary Pani Sansad, a community water-management body, is associated with:

(a) Anantapur, Andhra Pradesh  
(b) Ralegan Siddhi, Maharashtra  
(c) Alwar, Rajasthan  
(d) Jhabua, Madhya Pradesh

</details>

**7.** Consider the following statements: 1. The Ganga Water Treaty between India and Bangladesh (1996) was signed for 30 years. 2. The Mahakali Treaty was signed with Bhutan. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only

</details>

**8.** Consider the following statements: 1. Under the 1981 agreement, Rajasthan received the largest share of surplus Ravi–Beas water. 2. The SYL canal is meant to carry Haryana's share of Ravi–Beas water. 3. The Eradi Tribunal was set up following the Rajiv–Longowal Accord. Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only  
(b) 2 and 3 only  
(c) 1 and 3 only

**9.** Reservoir-induced seismicity is classically associated with the 1967 earthquake at:

(a) Hirakud  
(b) Bhakra  
(c) Koyna  
(d) Tehri

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Koyna** — The Koyna (Maharashtra) quake followed the filling of the Shivajisagar reservoir.

</details>

**10.** Shahpur Kandi dam, completed in 2024, is located on the:

(a) Chenab  
(b) Sutlej  
(c) Beas  
(d) Ravi

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Ravi** — It lies downstream of Ranjit Sagar dam in Pathankot district.

</details>

**11.** The Damodar Valley Corporation (1948) was modelled on the:

(a) Aswan High Dam (Egypt)  
(b) Three Gorges project (China)  
(c) Hoover Dam project (USA)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Tennessee Valley Authority (USA)** — DVC was India's first multipurpose river valley authority.

</details>

**12.** The Upper Bari Doab Canal takes off from headworks on the Ravi at:

(a) Madhopur  
(b) Harike  
(c) Ropar  
(d) Hussainiwala

**(a) Madhopur** — Ropar feeds the Sirhind Canal; Harike feeds the Rajasthan and Sirhind feeders.

</details>
