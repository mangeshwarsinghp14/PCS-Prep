## Short notes

- Part XV (Arts 324-329): Art 324 vests superintendence of elections to Parliament, state legislatures, President and Vice-President in the Election Commission of India.
- Art 325: one general electoral roll per constituency, no exclusion on religion, race, caste or sex; Art 326: adult suffrage (18 years since the 61st Amendment); Art 329 bars courts from interfering except via election petitions.
- ECI set up on 25 Jan 1950 (National Voters' Day since 2011); multi-member body permanently since 1993; CEC can be removed only like a Supreme Court judge.
- CEC and Other ECs Act 2023: selection panel of PM, Leader of Opposition and a Cabinet minister named by the PM - replacing the interim CJI-inclusive panel set by Anoop Baranwal (2023).
- Representation of the People Act 1950: allocation of seats, delimitation, qualifications of voters and preparation of electoral rolls.
- Representation of the People Act 1951: conduct of elections, qualifications and disqualifications of members, corrupt practices (Sec 123), election disputes, registration of parties (Sec 29A).
- Lily Thomas (2013) struck down Sec 8(4) of RPA 1951 - MPs/MLAs convicted with a sentence of two years or more are disqualified immediately.
- A candidate may contest from at most two constituencies (Sec 33(7), added 1996).
- NOTA introduced after PUCL v Union of India (2013); EVMs first used in Paravur, Kerala (1982); VVPAT first used in Noksen, Nagaland by-election (2013); in 2024 the SC rejected pleas for return to paper ballots or full VVPAT counting.
- Model Code of Conduct is not statutory - it evolved from Kerala (1960) and is enforced by ECI from announcement of the schedule till results.
- Electoral bonds scheme (2018) struck down on 15 Feb 2024 (Association for Democratic Reforms v Union of India) for violating voters' right to information.
- Delimitation Commissions: 1952, 1963, 1973, 2002; seat allocation frozen on the 1971 census until the first census after 2026 (84th Amendment 2001); boundaries redrawn on the 2001 census (effective 2008).
- Expenditure ceilings (2022): Rs 95 lakh per Lok Sabha candidate and Rs 40 lakh per assembly candidate in larger states.
- Election Laws (Amendment) Act 2021: voluntary linking of electoral rolls with Aadhaar and four qualifying dates a year (1 Jan, 1 Apr, 1 Jul, 1 Oct) for new voters.
- Reform committees: Tarkunde (1975), Dinesh Goswami (1990), Indrajit Gupta (1998, state funding), Law Commission 170th (1999) and 255th (2015) reports.
- Ram Nath Kovind High-Level Committee on simultaneous elections reported in March 2024; the 129th Constitution Amendment Bill (Dec 2024) on 'One Nation One Election' was referred to a Joint Parliamentary Committee.
- Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls was carried out in Bihar in 2025 and extended to other states in phases, including Punjab in 2026.
- Continuing reform issues: criminalisation of politics, money power, paid news, inner-party democracy, state funding, misuse of social media and deepfakes.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab sends 13 members to the Lok Sabha (4 reserved for SCs - Jalandhar, Hoshiarpur, Faridkot, Fatehgarh Sahib) and 7 to the Rajya Sabha.
- The Punjab Vidhan Sabha has 117 seats, of which 34 are reserved for Scheduled Castes.
- Punjab's electoral rolls went through the Special Intensive Revision in 2026 ahead of the 2027 assembly election.

## Deep dive

### Election timeline (year — event)

- 1951–52 — first general election (25 Oct 1951 to 21 Feb 1952) under CEC Sukumar Sen; a separate ballot box for each candidate.
- 1957 — Kerala elected the first non-Congress (Communist) state government.
- 1962 — indelible ink first used in a general election (made by Mysore Paints and Varnish Ltd).
- 1967–71 — simultaneous elections broke down after early dissolutions of assemblies (1968–69) and the Lok Sabha (1970); 1971 was the first mid-term Lok Sabha poll.
- 1989–90 — ECI briefly made multi-member; permanently multi-member from Oct 1993.
- 1993 — Electors' Photo Identity Cards introduced under CEC T.N. Seshan, who also enforced the Model Code strictly.
- 1998 — EVMs used in selected assembly seats; 2004 — EVMs in all Lok Sabha constituencies.
- 2013 — NOTA and first VVPAT use; 2019 — VVPAT in every constituency, with slips of five machines per assembly segment verified (after the 2019 Chandrababu Naidu petition).
- 2024 — 18th Lok Sabha election, turnout about 65.8%, the largest electorate in history.
- Feb 2025 — Gyanesh Kumar became CEC, the first appointed under the 2023 Act.
- 27 May 2026 — Supreme Court upheld the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of rolls, holding the ECI may examine citizenship for enrolment and that the exercise met the proportionality test.

### ECI and election law in court (case — year — principle)

- Mohinder Singh Gill (1978) — Art 324 is a reservoir of power where law is silent, but it must be used fairly.
- T.N. Seshan v Union of India (1995) — multi-member ECI upheld; CEC and ECs are equal; decisions by majority.
- Union of India v ADR (2002) and PUCL (2003) — voters' right to know candidates' criminal record, assets and education.
- Kuldip Nayar (2006) — open ballot for Rajya Sabha elections and removal of the domicile condition upheld.
- Indira Nehru Gandhi v Raj Narain (1975) — free and fair elections part of the basic structure.
- Sadiq Ali (1971) — ECI can decide which faction of a split party gets the symbol; Subhash Desai (2023) — legislative majority is not the only test for party identity.
- Lok Prahari (2018) — candidates must disclose sources of income of self, spouse and dependants.
- Chief Election Commissioner v M.R. Vijayabhaskar (2021) — media may report judges' oral remarks about the ECI.
- SIR judgment (27 May 2026, petitions led by civil-society groups) — SIR is within Art 324 and Sec 21 RPA 1950; the ECI does not decide citizenship finally, but can verify eligibility.

### Reform committees and recommendations (committee — year — key proposals)

- Tarkunde Committee (1975, set up by Jayaprakash Narayan) — multi-member ECI, lower voting age.
- Dinesh Goswami Committee (1990) — limited state funding in kind, EVMs, curbs on misuse of official machinery, multi-member ECI.
- Vohra Committee (1993) — nexus between criminals, politicians and bureaucrats.
- Indrajit Gupta Committee (1998) — state funding of elections in kind for recognised parties.
- Law Commission 170th Report (1999) — inner-party democracy, simultaneous elections, partial list system.
- NCRWC (2002, Justice M.N. Venkatachaliah) — disqualify persons charged with heinous offences; Second ARC 4th Report (2007) — fast-track trials of politicians.
- Law Commission 244th Report (2014) — disqualification on framing of charges for offences punishable with five years or more, with safeguards; 255th Report (2015) — expenditure, paid news, broad-based ECI selection.
- Kovind High Level Committee (report March 2024) — simultaneous elections in two steps, then local bodies within 100 days.
- Remote Voting Machine prototype for domestic migrants was demonstrated by the ECI in Jan 2023; no consensus yet.

### Delimitation and representation

- Delimitation Commissions under Acts of 1952, 1962, 1972 and 2002; the 2002 Commission was chaired by Justice Kuldip Singh; its orders have the force of law and cannot be challenged in court (Art 329(a)).
- Lok Sabha seats reserved: 84 for SCs and 47 for STs (2008 order).
- J&K Delimitation Commission (Justice Ranjana Prakash Desai, 2020–22): assembly raised to 90 (43 Jammu, 47 Kashmir).
- Assam delimitation (2023) used the 2001 Census under Sec 8A RPA 1950.
- April 2026: the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill and Delimitation Bill (cap 850 Lok Sabha seats) failed/withdrawn, so the next delimitation awaits Census 2027 (reference date 1 March 2027).
- North–South debate: a population-based reallocation would raise seats of UP and Bihar and reduce those of Tamil Nadu and Kerala; a uniform percentage increase for all states was floated as a compromise.

### Punjab elections — data

- Punjab Vidhan Sabha: 117 seats (34 SC, none ST); Lok Sabha: 13 (4 SC).
- 2022 assembly result: AAP 92, Congress 18, SAD 3, BJP 2, BSP 1, Independent 1 — Bhagwant Mann became CM.
- 2024 Lok Sabha in Punjab: Congress 7, AAP 3, SAD 1 (Bathinda), Independents 2 — Amritpal Singh (Khadoor Sahib, elected while detained under NSA) and Sarabjeet Singh Khalsa (Faridkot); BJP none.
- By-elections: Jalandhar West (July 2024) and Ludhiana West (June 2025) were won by AAP.
- SIR 2026 in Punjab: began 25 June 2026; draft roll in Aug; final roll published 12 Oct 2026 with about 2.01 crore electors — a net fall of about 13.6 lakh (6.3%) after deletions, with about 7.97 lakh new voters (CEO Anindita Mitra).
- Urban local body elections on 21 Dec 2024 covered five municipal corporations (Amritsar, Jalandhar, Ludhiana, Patiala, Phagwara) and dozens of councils and nagar panchayats — conducted by the State Election Commission, not the ECI.
- Next Punjab assembly election is due by early 2027.

### Traps

- Disputes on election of President and Vice-President go to the Supreme Court (Art 71); other disputes by election petition in the High Court.
- NOTA has no electoral effect: even if NOTA tops, the candidate with the most votes wins.
- Security deposit (Lok Sabha) is Rs 25,000 (Rs 12,500 for SC/ST); forfeited if the candidate gets less than one-sixth of valid votes.
- Rajya Sabha elections use open ballot only for party MLAs to show their vote to the party agent.
- Six national parties since April 2023: BJP, INC, BSP, CPI(M), AAP and NPP.
- Panchayat and municipal elections are by State Election Commissions (Arts 243K, 243ZA), not the ECI.
