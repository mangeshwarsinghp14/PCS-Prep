## Short notes

- Plans were prepared by the Planning Commission (1950) and approved by the National Development Council (1952); 12 Five-Year Plans ran from 1951 to 2017.
- First Plan (1951-56): agriculture, irrigation and power; based on the Harrod-Domar model; growth target 2.1%, achieved about 3.6%; projects such as Bhakra-Nangal, Hirakud, DVC; Community Development Programme (1952).
- Second Plan (1956-61): Mahalanobis model, rapid industrialisation and heavy industry; steel plants at Bhilai, Rourkela, Durgapur; IPR 1956.
- Third Plan (1961-66): aimed at a self-reliant, self-generating economy; failed (growth about 2.4% vs 5.6% target) due to the 1962 and 1965 wars and droughts.
- Plan holiday (1966-69): three Annual Plans; Green Revolution started, rupee devalued (1966).
- Fourth Plan (1969-74): 'growth with stability' and 'progressive achievement of self-reliance'; Gadgil formula for central assistance to states; bank nationalisation; 'Garibi Hatao' slogan (1971).
- Fifth Plan (1974-79): poverty removal and self-reliance; Minimum Needs Programme; ended a year early (1978) by the Janata government.
- Rolling Plan (1978-80) introduced by the Janata government, based on Gunnar Myrdal's idea of annual revision.
- Sixth Plan (1980-85): poverty eradication; IRDP and NREP; marked the start of liberalisation in some sectors.
- Seventh Plan (1985-90): 'food, work and productivity'; Jawahar Rozgar Yojana (1989); growth exceeded target.
- Annual Plans (1990-92) due to political instability and the balance-of-payments crisis.
- Eighth Plan (1992-97): first post-reform plan, focus on human resource development; Rao-Manmohan model; growth about 6.8% against 5.6% target.
- Ninth Plan (1997-2002): 'growth with social justice and equality'; growth fell short of target.
- Tenth Plan (2002-07): 8% growth target with monitorable targets for poverty, literacy and employment; growth close to target (about 7.6%).
- Eleventh Plan (2007-12): 'towards faster and more inclusive growth'; 9% target, about 8% achieved despite the 2008 crisis.
- Twelfth Plan (2012-17): 'faster, more inclusive and sustainable growth'; target cut from 9% to 8%; the last Five-Year Plan.
- NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission on 1 Jan 2015; it is a policy think tank without fund-allocation powers; its Governing Council includes all Chief Ministers and LGs; planning moved to vision and strategy documents.
- Overall record: plans built infrastructure, heavy industry, food security and institutions, but missed targets on employment, poverty and regional balance.

## Punjab angle

- Bhakra-Nangal (First Plan project) and the plan-era focus on irrigation and HYVs made Punjab India's granary.
- Punjab has its own State Planning Board for preparing the state's development plans and budget priorities.

## How PPSC asks it

Expect matching of plan number with its slogan, model or flagship event, plus the plan holiday and rolling plan. Memorise themes of the 4th, 8th, 9th, 11th and 12th Plans and which plans exceeded targets (1st, 8th).

## Deep dive

### Plan-by-plan ledger (period — model/leader — target vs achieved growth — signature)

- 1st (1951–56) — Harrod–Domar; drafted with K.N. Raj; target 2.1%, achieved about 3.6%; irrigation, power, Community Development (2 Oct 1952).
- 2nd (1956–61) — Mahalanobis four-sector model; target 4.5%, achieved about 4.2%; heavy industry, three steel plants, foreign-exchange crisis of 1957-58.
- 3rd (1961–66) — aimed at a 'self-reliant and self-generating' economy; target 5.6%, achieved far less because of the 1962 and 1965 wars and the 1965-66 drought.
- Annual Plans (1966–69) — 'plan holiday'; devaluation (1966); Green Revolution and HYV Programme launched.
- 4th (1969–74) — D.R. Gadgil as Deputy Chairman; 'growth with stability' and self-reliance; target 5.7%, achieved roughly half; bank nationalisation; Gadgil formula.
- 5th (1974–79) — drafted under D.P. Dhar; poverty removal and self-reliance; Minimum Needs Programme (1974); growth about 4.8–5% against 4.4% target; ended in 1978.
- 6th (1980–85) — poverty eradication; IRDP, NREP (1980), RLEGP (1983); growth about 5.5% against 5.2% target.
- 7th (1985–90) — 'food, work and productivity'; Manmohan Singh was Deputy Chairman 1985–87; growth about 6% against 5% target; Jawahar Rozgar Yojana (1989).
- 8th (1992–97) — 'indicative planning'; Pranab Mukherjee as Deputy Chairman; human development focus; growth about 6.8% against 5.6% target.
- 9th (1997–2002) — 'growth with social justice and equality'; target cut to 6.5%, achieved about 5.5%; first plan to emphasise the role of the private sector strongly.
- 10th (2002–07) — 8% target, about 7.6% achieved; monitorable targets (poverty, literacy, IMR, forest cover) and state-wise growth targets.
- 11th (2007–12) and 12th (2012–17) — Montek Singh Ahluwalia as Deputy Chairman; 11th: 9% target, about 8% achieved despite the 2008 crisis; 12th: target cut from 9% to 8%, actual growth below target.

### Planning machinery and Centre–State plan transfers

- Planning Commission (15 March 1950) — executive resolution; PM as Chairman; first Deputy Chairman Gulzarilal Nanda.
- National Development Council (1952) — PM, Union Cabinet, Chief Ministers; approved Five-Year Plans; it has not met since 2014 and its role has passed in practice to NITI Aayog's Governing Council.
- Plan transfers to states went through the Planning Commission (Normal Central Assistance), while statutory transfers went through the Finance Commission (Art 280) — the 'dual channel' debate.
- Gadgil formula (1969): fixed shares for special category states first, the rest shared by population, per capita income, tax effort and special problems.
- Gadgil–Mukherjee formula (1991): population 60%, per capita income 25%, fiscal management 7.5%, special problems 7.5%.
- Plan/non-plan expenditure classification was ended from the 2017-18 Budget, after the plans themselves ended (March 2017).
- States had State Planning Boards (Punjab's still exists) that prepared state Five-Year and Annual Plans.
- Centrally Sponsored Schemes grew under the plans; the Chief Ministers' Sub-Group (2015) rationalised them into 'core of the core', 'core' and 'optional' schemes.

### Signature programmes and the plan in which they began

- Community Development Programme (1952) — 1st Plan; Panchayati Raj after the Balwant Rai Mehta report (1957) — 2nd Plan.
- Khadi and Village Industries Commission (1957) — 2nd Plan; IADP (1960-61) — end of 2nd/start of 3rd Plan.
- Small Farmers Development Agency (1971) and Drought Prone Areas Programme (1973) — 4th Plan.
- Minimum Needs Programme (1974), Twenty Point Programme (1975), Food for Work (1977) — 5th Plan.
- IRDP (1980 nationwide), NREP (1980), TRYSEM (1979), DWCRA (1982), RLEGP (1983) — 6th Plan.
- Jawahar Rozgar Yojana (1989) — 7th Plan; PMRY and MPLADS (1993), NSAP (1995) — 8th Plan.
- SGSY (1999) — 9th Plan; SGRY (2001), NREGA (2005), NRHM (2005), Bharat Nirman (2005) — 10th Plan.
- RTE Act (2009), Aajeevika/NRLM (2011) — 11th Plan; NFSA (2013) — 12th Plan.

### Models and planning concepts

- Harrod–Domar: growth = savings rate ÷ capital–output ratio; justified raising savings and investment in the 1st Plan.
- Mahalanobis model: investment in capital goods first to raise long-run growth; assumed a closed economy and ignored agriculture and wage goods.
- Imperative vs indicative planning: India moved from directive public-sector targets (2nd–7th) to indicative targets for the private sector (8th onwards).
- Rolling plan: annual plan for the current year, a five-year plan revised every year and a long-term perspective plan.
- Perspective planning: the Perspective Planning Division (1958) prepared 15–20 year horizons for the plans.
- Decentralised planning: 73rd and 74th Amendments (1992) created District Planning Committees (Art 243ZD).
- Inclusive growth (11th Plan) and sustainability (12th Plan) shifted planning language from targets to outcomes.

### After plans — NITI Aayog era

- NITI Aayog set up by Cabinet resolution on 1 January 2015; Governing Council (PM, CMs, LGs) first met on 8 February 2015.
- Plan replacement: a 15-year vision, a 7-year strategy and a 3-year action agenda (2017-18 to 2019-20) were announced.
- 'Strategy for New India @75' (December 2018) set goals for 2022-23 across 41 areas.
- Development Monitoring and Evaluation Office (DMEO) under NITI evaluates schemes; Atal Innovation Mission (2016) promotes innovation.
- Competitive federalism tools: SDG India Index (from 2018), Composite Water Management Index, Export Preparedness Index, Fiscal Health Index (first edition 2025).
- Aspirational Districts Programme (January 2018, 112 districts) and Aspirational Blocks Programme (January 2023, 500 blocks).
- Fund transfers shifted to the Finance Commission and ministries; the 16th Finance Commission covers 2026–31.
- Long-term goal now framed as 'Viksit Bharat @2047'.

### Assessment of planning (what to write in answers)

- Achievements: rise in savings and investment rates, irrigation and power base, steel and capital goods, food self-sufficiency, scientific institutions.
- Growth accelerated from about 3.5% (1950–80) to 5.5% (1980s) and 6–8% in the 8th–11th Plans.
- Shortfalls: employment generation, poverty reduction until the 1980s, land reform, regional balance and quality of public enterprises.
- Plans were often hit by exogenous shocks: wars (1962, 1965, 1971), droughts (1965-66, 1979, 1987), oil shocks (1973, 1979, 1990).
- Over-centralisation and 'one-size-fits-all' Centrally Sponsored Schemes were a key reason for NITI Aayog's creation.
- Plans that exceeded targets: 1st, 5th, 6th, 7th, 8th; badly missed: 3rd, 4th, 9th.

### Punjab and the plans

- 1st Plan: Bhakra–Nangal and Harike headworks (1952) built the canal system feeding Punjab and Rajasthan.
- 3rd Plan/Annual Plans: Punjab Agricultural University (1962), Ludhiana IADP and HYV wheat made Punjab the plan-era success story.
- Thermal power: Guru Nanak Dev Thermal Plant at Bathinda (1970s) and Ropar (1980s) added plan-era capacity.
- Ranjit Sagar Dam on the Ravi was completed in 2000 (9th Plan era).
- Punjab State Planning Board continues to prepare development priorities and district plans after 2017.
- Critique: plan-era priority for wheat and paddy procurement locked Punjab into a water-intensive cropping pattern.

### Common traps

- The 1st Plan exceeded its target; the 2nd was close; the 3rd failed badly.
- The Rolling Plan was a Janata idea (1978), not Rajiv Gandhi's.
- 'Indicative planning' is linked with the 8th Plan, not the 7th.
- Monitorable targets began in the 10th Plan, not the 11th.
- NITI Aayog does not allocate plan funds; the Governing Council, not the NDC, is its main federal forum.
- Gadgil formula (1969) is for plan assistance; Finance Commission formulae are for tax devolution — different things.
