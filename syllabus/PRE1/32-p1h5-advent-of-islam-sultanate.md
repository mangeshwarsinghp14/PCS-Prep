# Advent of Islam & Sultanate

## Short notes

- Arabs under Muhammad bin Qasim conquered Sindh in 712 CE, defeating Raja Dahir — the first Islamic political foothold in India.
- Mahmud of Ghazni made about 17 raids (c. 1000–1027), including Somnath (1025); Alberuni came with him and wrote Kitab-ul-Hind; Firdausi wrote the Shahnama at his court.
- Muhammad Ghori lost the First Battle of Tarain (1191) to Prithviraj Chauhan, won the Second (1192), and defeated Jaichand of Kannauj at Chandawar (1194).
- Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526): Mamluk/Slave (1206–90), Khalji (1290–1320), Tughlaq (1320–1414), Sayyid (1414–51), Lodi (1451–1526).
- Qutbuddin Aibak: began the Qutb Minar and Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque, built Adhai-din-ka-Jhonpra (Ajmer); known as 'Lakh Bakhsh'; died in a polo accident at Lahore (1210).
- Iltutmish: real consolidator; formed the Turkan-i-Chahalgani (the Forty); organised iqtas; introduced the silver tanka and copper jital; received investiture from the Abbasid Caliph (1229); completed the Qutb Minar.
- Razia Sultan (1236–40) was the only woman to rule the Delhi Sultanate; opposed by the Turkish nobles.
- Balban: theory of kingship as 'shadow of God' (Zil-i-Ilahi); introduced sijda and paibos and the Persian festival Nauroz; broke the power of the Forty; created a military department (diwan-i-arz).
- Alauddin Khalji: market control with fixed prices (Shahana-i-Mandi), dagh (branding horses) and chehra (descriptive roll of soldiers), land revenue at half the produce; Malik Kafur's southern campaigns; repelled Mongols; built Alai Darwaza and Siri.
- Muhammad bin Tughlaq: shifted capital to Daulatabad (1327), issued bronze/copper token currency (1329–30), created a separate agriculture department (diwan-i-amir-kohi); Ibn Battuta served as his qazi of Delhi.
- Firoz Shah Tughlaq: dug canals from the Yamuna and Sutlej, levied jizya on Brahmanas, created diwan-i-khairat (charity) and diwan-i-bandagan (slaves); brought Ashokan pillars from Topra and Meerut to Delhi.
- Timur sacked Delhi in 1398; the Lodis were Afghans — Sikandar Lodi founded Agra (1504) and introduced the gaz-i-Sikandari; Ibrahim Lodi lost the First Battle of Panipat (1526) to Babur.
- Administration: Sultan with wazir (finance), diwan-i-arz (army), diwan-i-insha (correspondence), diwan-i-risalat; iqta system of revenue assignments; taxes such as kharaj, jizya, zakat, khams.
- Architecture: Indo-Islamic style with the true arch and dome, calligraphy and arabesque, use of red sandstone and marble (Alai Darwaza is an early example of a true dome).
- Literature: Amir Khusrau (called Tuti-i-Hind) wrote in Persian and Hindavi; Minhaj-us-Siraj's Tabaqat-i-Nasiri; Ziauddin Barani's Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi; Persian was the court language and Urdu/Hindavi grew.
- Sufism: Chishti silsila (Moinuddin Chishti at Ajmer, Nizamuddin Auliya at Delhi) stayed away from the court; Suhrawardi silsila (Bahauddin Zakariya at Multan) accepted state patronage.
- Society: new Turkish-Afghan ruling class, growth of towns and crafts, the Persian wheel and paper spread; the Vijayanagara (1336) and Bahmani (1347) kingdoms rose as the Sultanate weakened in the south.

## Punjab angle

- Lahore was the capital of the Ghaznavid province in India, and Punjab was the frontier through which Ghaznavid, Ghorid and Mongol invasions came.
- Razia Sultan was imprisoned in the fort of Tabarhind (Bathinda) by its governor Altunia, whom she later married.
- Ghazi Malik (later Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq) was governor of Dipalpur in Punjab and led defence against the Mongols.
- Baba Farid (Farid-ud-din Ganj-i-Shakar), Chishti saint of Pakpattan, wrote in early Punjabi; his verses are included in the Guru Granth Sahib.

## Deep dive

### Detailed timeline, 712–1526 (year — event)

- 712 — Muhammad bin Qasim (sent by Hajjaj, governor of Iraq) takes Debal and defeats Dahir; Multan taken; the 'Chachnama' (Persian, 13th century) narrates it.
- 977 — Sabuktigin founds Ghazni's power; 1001 — Mahmud defeats the Hindushahi Jayapala near Peshawar; 1008–09 — defeats Anandapala at Waihind.
- 1018 — Mahmud sacks Mathura and Kannauj; 1025 — Somnath; Punjab (Lahore) becomes a Ghaznavid province till 1186.
- 1175 — Muhammad Ghori takes Multan; 1178 — Ghori defeated near Mount Abu (Kayadara) by the Chalukya ruler of Gujarat; 1186 — ends Ghaznavid rule at Lahore.
- 1191 — First Tarain (Ghori wounded, Prithviraj III wins); 1192 — Second Tarain; 1194 — Chandawar (Jaichand of Kannauj); 1206 — Ghori killed on the Indus (traditionally by Khokhars).
- 1206 — Qutbuddin Aibak rules from Lahore; 1210 — Aibak dies at Lahore; 1211 — Iltutmish; 1221 — Chengiz Khan reaches the Indus chasing Jalaluddin Mangbarni; Iltutmish refuses asylum.
- 1229 — Iltutmish receives investiture from the Abbasid Caliph; 1236–40 — Razia; 1241 — Mongols sack Lahore; 1266–87 — Balban.
- 1290 — Jalaluddin Khalji; 1296 — Alauddin Khalji; 1303 — Chittor; 1307–11 — Malik Kafur's campaigns to Devagiri, Warangal, Dwarasamudra and Madurai.
- 1320 — Ghazi Malik (Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq); 1325 — Muhammad bin Tughlaq; 1327 — capital shifted to Daulatabad; 1329–30 — token currency; 1333–34 — Ibn Battuta reaches India and Delhi.
- 1336 — Vijayanagara founded; 1347 — Bahmani kingdom; 1351–88 — Firoz Shah Tughlaq; 1398 — Timur sacks Delhi (route through Talamba, Bhatnair, Samana, Kaithal, Panipat).
- 1414 — Khizr Khan (governor of Multan) founds the Sayyid dynasty; 1451 — Bahlul Lodi (governor of Sirhind and Lahore) founds the Lodi dynasty; 1504 — Sikandar Lodi founds Agra.
- 1526 — Babur defeats Ibrahim Lodi at Panipat, invited earlier by Daulat Khan Lodi (governor of Punjab) and Alam Khan.

### Administration — offices, revenue and iqta (term — meaning — sultan)

- Iqta — assignment of land revenue in lieu of salary; holder = muqti/wali; organised by Iltutmish, checked by Balban (khwaja/auditors), reduced by Alauddin (more khalisa), made hereditary by Firoz Shah.
- Khalisa — crown land whose revenue went directly to the treasury; Alauddin expanded it in the Delhi–doab region.
- Wazir — head of finance (diwan-i-wizarat); Diwan-i-arz — military department (Balban); Diwan-i-insha — royal correspondence; Diwan-i-risalat — religious affairs/appeals.
- Alauddin: diwan-i-mustakhraj (to recover arrears), diwan-i-riyasat and shahna-i-mandi (market control), barids and munhiyans (spies); revenue fixed at half the produce, measured by biswa.
- Muhammad bin Tughlaq: diwan-i-amir-kohi (agriculture department, loans called sondhar); taxation increase in the doab triggered revolt.
- Firoz Shah: diwan-i-khairat (charity), diwan-i-bandagan (slaves — about 180,000), diwan-i-istihqaq (pensions); abolished 24 taxes and kept 4 sharia taxes (kharaj, jizya, khams, zakat); levied jizya on Brahmanas.
- Village intermediaries: khut, muqaddam, chaudhari — Alauddin cut their privileges; Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq restored some and limited revenue increase to 1/10–1/11 a year.
- Coins: silver tanka (about 11 g) and copper jital (Iltutmish); Muhammad bin Tughlaq's bronze/copper token coins; Bahlul Lodi's copper 'bahloli'.

### Punjab — the frontier province of the Sultanate

- Lahore was the Ghaznavid capital in India (1030s–1186) and Aibak's seat (1206–10); poet Masud Sad Salman and the scholar al-Hujwiri (Data Ganj Bakhsh, 'Kashf-ul-Mahjub') lived there.
- Frontier iqtas of Multan, Uch, Dipalpur, Lahore, Samana and Sirhind (Sihrind) formed a defensive belt against Mongol raids.
- Balban's son Prince Muhammad, governor of Multan, was killed fighting the Mongols (1285); Amir Khusrau, in his service, was captured and escaped.
- Alauddin defeated the Mongols at Jaran-Manjur (1298), Kili (1299, Qutlugh Khwaja), Amroha (1305) and on the Ravi (1306); Ghazi Malik of Dipalpur claimed some 29 victories over Mongols.
- Tabarhind (Bathinda) fort: Razia was imprisoned there by Altunia (1240) and later killed near Kaithal (Haryana).
- Firoz Shah's canals (Rajiwah and Ulughkhani) drew water from the Yamuna and Sutlej towards Hisar; he founded Hisar-Firoza and Fatehabad — early irrigation history of Haryana–Punjab.
- Khokhars of the Salt Range and upper doabs resisted repeatedly; Jasrath Khokhar troubled the Sayyids (1420s–30s).
- Yahya bin Ahmad Sirhindi wrote 'Tarikh-i-Mubarakshahi' (c. 1434) — the main source for the Sayyids; Sirhind, Samana and Lahore were key Punjab towns.
- Sufi centres: Baba Farid at Pakpattan (Ajodhan) — Chishti; Bahauddin Zakariya at Multan — Suhrawardi; both shaped Punjabi religious culture.

### Sources and literature (author — work — significance)

- Minhaj-us-Siraj — Tabaqat-i-Nasiri (1260), dedicated to Sultan Nasiruddin Mahmud; key source for Ghorids and early Delhi.
- Ziauddin Barani — Tarikh-i-Firozshahi (1357) and Fatawa-i-Jahandari (political theory); champion of noble birth, critical of Muhammad bin Tughlaq.
- Amir Khusrau (1253–1325) — Khazain-ul-Futuh (Alauddin's conquests), Qiran-us-Sadain, Nuh Sipihr (praise of India), Tughlaq Nama, Ashiqa; 'Tuti-i-Hind'; wrote in Hindavi too.
- Isami — Futuh-us-Salatin (verse history, written at the Bahmani court); Shams Siraj Afif — Tarikh-i-Firozshahi (Firoz's reign); Firoz Shah — Futuhat-i-Firozshahi (autobiographical).
- Ibn Battuta (Morocco) — Rihla: postal system (horse and foot runners), Delhi under Muhammad bin Tughlaq; he served as qazi and was sent as envoy to China (1342).
- Alberuni — Kitab-ul-Hind (c. 1030): Indian science, religion and society; notes Indian insularity; he learnt Sanskrit.
- Translations: Firoz Shah had Sanskrit works translated into Persian (Dalail-i-Firozshahi); Zia Nakhshabi translated the Sanskrit 'Shukasaptati' as Tuti Nama.

### Architecture and technology (monument — builder — first/feature)

- Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque and Qutb Minar — begun by Aibak, Minar completed by Iltutmish, repaired by Firoz Shah (added storeys) and Sikandar Lodi; UNESCO site (1993).
- Adhai-din-ka-Jhonpra, Ajmer — Aibak; Sultan Ghari (1231) — Iltutmish's tomb for his son, the first monumental Islamic tomb in India.
- Balban's tomb — first true arch; Alai Darwaza (1311) — first true dome and the first building in Islamic style with full arch-dome principles.
- Siri fort and Hauz-i-Alai (Hauz Khas) — Alauddin; Tughlaqabad and Ghiyasuddin's tomb — sloping (battered) walls; Jahanpanah — Muhammad bin Tughlaq.
- Firozabad (Kotla Firoz Shah) with the Topra Ashokan pillar placed on top; Hauz Khas madrasa; hunting lodges.
- Lodi period — octagonal tombs (Sikandar Lodi's tomb with the first double dome in India), Moth ki Masjid; Lodi Gardens.
- New technologies: paper in wider use, spinning wheel (charkha), Persian wheel spreads in Punjab, true arch and lime mortar, stirrups and horseshoes in cavalry.

### Historiography and analytical themes

- Was the Sultanate a theocracy? Sultans sought Caliph's recognition but ruled by state laws (zawabit) when needed — Barani admits this; Alauddin said he acted for the good of the state.
- Mohammad Habib's 'urban revolution' thesis: Turkish rule ended caste restrictions on urban crafts and spread trade; critics call it overstated.
- Irfan Habib — technology and agrarian expansion; K.S. Lal and others — 'harsh' policies; balance both in Mains answers.
- Turkish nobility: 'Forty' (chahalgani) under Iltutmish, destroyed by Balban; Khalji 'revolution' widened the ruling class to non-Turks; Tughlaqs recruited foreigners and Indian Muslims.
- Composite culture: Hindavi/Urdu, qawwali, Sufi–Bhakti interaction, Indo-Islamic architecture using Indian craftsmen.
- Causes of the Sultanate's decline: Muhammad bin Tughlaq's experiments, Firoz's hereditary iqtas and slave corps, Timur's invasion, provincial kingdoms (Bengal, Jaunpur, Malwa, Gujarat, Bahmani).

### PPSC-style asks and traps

- Prithviraj Chauhan won the First Battle of Tarain (1191), not the Second; Tarain (Taraori) is in Karnal district, Haryana.
- Aibak ruled from Lahore; Iltutmish made Delhi the capital and is called the real consolidator of the Sultanate.
- Razia was not the only Muslim woman ruler in medieval India (e.g., Chand Bibi as regent) but the only one on the Delhi throne.
- Token currency was of bronze/copper, not paper (paper money was Kublai Khan's Chinese model that inspired it).
- Diwan-i-amir-kohi — Muhammad bin Tughlaq (agriculture), not Firoz; diwan-i-khairat — Firoz, not Alauddin.
- Sayyids traced descent from the Prophet; Khizr Khan ruled as Timur's deputy and took no royal title.
- Bahlul Lodi was the first Afghan ruler of Delhi; Ibrahim Lodi the last Sultan.

(b) Iltutmish
