## Short notes

- July 1991 reforms under PM P.V. Narasimha Rao and FM Manmohan Singh: stabilisation (control deficits and inflation, with IMF support) plus structural reforms (with World Bank support).
- The rupee was devalued in two steps in July 1991; gold reserves were pledged to raise foreign exchange.
- New Industrial Policy (24 July 1991): industrial licensing abolished except for a short list of industries; today only atomic energy and railway operations remain reserved for the public sector (17 in 1956).
- MRTP asset limits removed; the MRTP Act was later replaced by the Competition Act 2002 and the Competition Commission of India.
- FDI: automatic approval up to 51% in priority industries (1991); caps raised over time; the Foreign Investment Promotion Board was abolished in 2017.
- FERA 1973 (criminal law) replaced by FEMA 1999 (civil law); current account convertibility in August 1994; capital account only partially convertible (Tarapore Committees 1997, 2006).
- Trade reforms: steep cuts in import tariffs, removal of quantitative restrictions (completed by 2001), export promotion, EXIM/Foreign Trade Policies; India a founding WTO member in 1995.
- Financial sector: Narasimham Committees I (1991) and II (1998) - lower CRR/SLR, interest rate deregulation, entry of new private banks (1993), prudential norms; SEBI given statutory status (1992); NSE screen-based trading.
- Tax reforms: Raja Chelliah Committee (1991) - lower rates and wider base; VAT (2005) and GST (1 July 2017) unified indirect taxes.
- Privatisation: disinvestment from 1991-92; Rangarajan Committee (1993); strategic sales such as Modern Foods (2000), BALCO (2001) and Air India to the Tata group (2022); DIPAM (2016) manages disinvestment.
- New Public Sector Enterprise Policy (2021): minimal presence in strategic sectors, privatisation or closure elsewhere; Navratna (1997), Miniratna and Maharatna (2009-10) categories give autonomy to strong PSUs.
- Second-generation reforms: FRBM Act 2003, Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code 2016, GST 2017, inflation targeting (2016) and four Labour Codes brought into force on 21 Nov 2025.
- Globalisation: IT and business-services boom, outsourcing, rising FDI and FPI inflows.
- Trade agreements: SAFTA (2006), India-ASEAN (2010), CEPA with UAE (2022), ECTA with Australia (2022), TEPA with EFTA (in force 2025), CETA with UK (signed 2025); India-EU FTA concluded January 2026.
- Results: faster growth (average around 6-7% after 1991), large forex reserves, sharp fall in poverty, growth of services and a large middle class.
- Criticisms: 'jobless growth', neglect of agriculture and agrarian distress, rising inequality, weak manufacturing share, regional disparities.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab's MSME clusters (Ludhiana hosiery, bicycles, auto parts) faced import competition after liberalisation and later from tax incentives given to neighbouring hill states (2003 package for HP, J&K and Uttarakhand).
- Punjab's agriculture-dominated economy benefited less from services-led post-1991 growth than states with IT hubs.

## Deep dive

### First-generation reforms, 1991–2001 (year — measure)

- 1991 — Rupee devalued in two steps (1 and 3 July); Trade Policy abolishes most import licensing for capital goods and raw materials; New Industrial Policy (24 July) ends licensing for all but 18 industries.
- 1991 — Budget of 24 July 1991 (FM Manmohan Singh) begins fiscal correction; Narasimham Committee I (August) on banking.
- 1992 — SEBI gets statutory powers; Controller of Capital Issues abolished; LERMS dual exchange rate (March); foreign institutional investors allowed into stock markets.
- 1993 — Unified market-determined exchange rate (March); new private bank licences (e.g. HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank); Rangarajan Committee on disinvestment; Malhotra Committee on insurance set up.
- 1994 — Rupee convertible on current account (August, IMF Article VIII); NSE begins trading; India signs the Marrakesh agreement creating the WTO (in force 1 January 1995).
- 1996–97 — Disinvestment Commission (G.V. Ramakrishna); Navratna scheme (1997); Tarapore Committee on capital account convertibility (1997).
- 1998 — Narasimham Committee II on banking structure and NPAs.
- 1999 — FEMA replaces FERA (in force 2000); IRDA Act opens insurance to private firms with 26% FDI; separate Department of Disinvestment created.
- 2000–01 — Modern Foods (2000) and BALCO (2001) sold in strategic sales; remaining quantitative restrictions on imports removed by April 2001 after a WTO dispute with the US.

### Second-generation and recent reforms (2002–2026)

- 2002–03 — Competition Act (2002); FRBM Act (2003); Electricity Act (2003) delicenses generation.
- 2005 — State-level VAT replaces sales tax in most states; SEZ Act (2005).
- 2014–16 — Make in India (2014); Jan Dhan + Aadhaar + Mobile (JAM); Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (2016); flexible inflation targeting with a Monetary Policy Committee (2016); DIPAM (2016).
- 2017 — GST from 1 July (101st Amendment, 2016); FIPB abolished; Railway Budget merged into the General Budget.
- 2019 — Corporate tax for existing firms cut to 22% (15% for new manufacturing firms) without exemptions; ten public sector banks merged into four (announced 2019, effective April 2020).
- 2020–21 — Atmanirbhar Bharat package; PLI schemes for 14 sectors; New Public Sector Enterprise Policy (2021); insurance FDI raised to 74% (2021); National Monetisation Pipeline (2021).
- 2022 — Air India transferred to the Tata group (January); LIC IPO (May, about 3.5% stake sold).
- 2025 — GST rationalisation effective 22 September 2025 (two main slabs, 5% and 18%, plus 40% on demerit goods); four Labour Codes brought into force on 21 November 2025, consolidating 29 central labour laws.
- 2025–26 — Insurance FDI cap raised to 100% by an amendment of insurance laws (December 2025); civil nuclear power opened to private participation by the SHANTI Act (December 2025); new Income Tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026.

### Reform committees (name — year — subject)

- C. Rangarajan High Level Committee (1991–93) — balance of payments; recommended a market-determined exchange rate.
- M. Narasimham I (1991) and II (1998) — banking: lower CRR/SLR, prudential norms, asset reconstruction, fewer but stronger banks.
- Raja Chelliah Tax Reforms Committee (1991–93) — fewer slabs, lower rates, wider base, introduction of service tax (1994).
- R.N. Malhotra Committee (1993–94) — opening insurance to private and foreign players; led to IRDA (1999).
- C. Rangarajan Committee (1993) — disinvestment of up to 49% in most PSUs (74% in some).
- S.S. Tarapore Committees (1997, 2006) — roadmap for capital account convertibility.
- Vijay Kelkar Task Forces (2002) on direct and indirect taxes; Kelkar Committee (2015) on revitalising PPPs.
- T.K. Viswanathan Bankruptcy Law Reforms Committee (2015) — design of the IBC.
- Arvind Subramanian Committee (2015) — revenue-neutral rate for GST; Urjit Patel Committee (2014) — inflation targeting; N.K. Singh Committee (2017) — FRBM review.

### Privatisation, disinvestment and the PSU map

- Disinvestment = selling part of government equity; strategic sale = transfer of management control with a large stake.
- Strategic sales 1999–2004: Modern Foods (Hindustan Lever), BALCO and Hindustan Zinc (Sterlite), VSNL (Tata), IPCL (Reliance).
- Government-to-PSU transfers: HPCL stake to ONGC (2018); REC to PFC (2019) — counted as disinvestment but not privatisation.
- New PSE Policy 2021: four strategic sectors (atomic energy, space and defence; transport and telecom; power, petroleum, coal and minerals; banking, insurance and financial services) keep a minimum public presence; others to be privatised or closed.
- Air India (January 2022) was the first full privatisation since 2003; Neelachal Ispat Nigam went to Tata Steel (2022).
- PSU categories: Maharatna, Navratna and Miniratna give financial autonomy based on size and profitability.
- Asset monetisation (NMP 2021) leases brownfield assets for a fixed period instead of selling ownership.

### Globalisation: trade and investment opening

- Peak customs duty fell from 150% (1991-92) to 50% (1995-96) and about 10% by 2007-08; quantitative restrictions ended by 2001.
- Insurance FDI: 26% (2000) → 49% (2015) → 74% (2021) → 100% (2025).
- Defence FDI: 74% under automatic route from 2020; most sectors are now on the automatic route.
- Still prohibited for FDI: lottery and gambling, chit funds, Nidhi companies, real-estate business (not construction development), manufacture of tobacco cigarettes, atomic energy.
- FTAs of the 2020s: UAE CEPA (2022), Australia ECTA (2022), EFTA TEPA (in force 2025), UK CETA (signed 2025), EU FTA concluded January 2026.
- Services exports (IT-BPM, global capability centres) became the main gain from globalisation; India's total exports reached about US$ 860 billion in 2025-26.

### Outcomes and debates

- Growth: average real growth rose from about 5.5% (1980s) to over 6% after 1991 and about 8–9% in 2003–08.
- External: reserves rose from about US$ 1 billion (1991) to over US$ 600 billion in the 2020s; external debt became mostly long-term.
- Poverty fell faster after reforms (Tendulkar poverty 45.3% in 1993-94 to 21.9% in 2011-12).
- Bhagwati and Panagariya ('Why Growth Matters', 2013) credit reforms for poverty reduction; Drèze and Sen ('An Uncertain Glory', 2013) stress neglect of health and education.
- Rob Jenkins called the process 'reform by stealth' — reforms pushed without open political debate.
- Critiques: jobless or job-poor growth, agrarian distress, rising inequality, stagnant manufacturing share (about 15–17% of GDP), regional divergence.
- Reform of factor markets (land, labour) lagged product-market reform until the Labour Codes of 2025.

### Punjab after 1991

- Agriculture-heavy Punjab gained less from services-led growth; its per capita income, once at or near the top among major states, slipped in rank after the 1990s.
- Ludhiana and Jalandhar MSMEs (hosiery, bicycles, auto parts, sports goods) faced Chinese competition after tariff cuts.
- The 2003 special industrial package for Himachal Pradesh, J&K and Uttarakhand pulled units across Punjab's borders (e.g. to Baddi).
- Punjab's Industrial and Business Development Policy 2026 (approved 7 March 2026) offers 20 incentive options and an extra 25% incentive for nine priority sectors and for border and Kandi areas.
- Free farm power (from 1997) and MSP-based procurement kept Punjab in the wheat–paddy cycle despite trade liberalisation.
- The Attari land route became Punjab's link to Afghan and Pakistani trade (ICP 2012) until closure in 2025.

### Traps

- FEMA was passed in 1999 but came into force on 1 June 2000.
- Current account convertibility came in 1994; full capital account convertibility has not been adopted.
- LERMS (1992) was a dual rate; the unified market rate came in March 1993.
- Only atomic energy and railway operations remain reserved for the public sector (not defence).
- The 1991 industrial policy kept licensing for 18 industries; the list has since shrunk to a handful (defence items, hazardous chemicals, tobacco products, etc.).
- The FIPB was abolished in 2017; FDI approvals now go to the administrative ministries via the Foreign Investment Facilitation Portal.
