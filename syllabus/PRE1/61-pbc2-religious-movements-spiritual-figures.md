# Religious movements & spiritual figures

## Short notes

- Religions (Census 2011): Sikhism 57.7%, Hinduism 38.5%, Islam 1.9%, Christianity 1.3%; Punjab is the only Sikh-majority state.
- Ancient roots: the Rigveda was composed in the Sapta Sindhu region; Buddhist stupa and finds at Sanghol (Fatehgarh Sahib); Taxila was a famous centre in undivided Punjab.
- Nath jogis (Tilla Jogian) and Bhakti saints influenced the region; Guru Nanak's dialogue with the Siddhs is recorded in Siddh Gosht.
- Early Sikh sects: Udasis (founded by Baba Sri Chand, Guru Nanak's son), Nirmalas (scholars), Sewapanthis (Bhai Kanhaiya), Nihangs (warrior order).
- Five Takhts: Akal Takht (Amritsar), Kesgarh Sahib (Anandpur Sahib), Damdama Sahib (Talwandi Sabo, Bathinda), Patna Sahib (Bihar), Hazur Sahib (Nanded, Maharashtra).
- Nirankari movement: Baba Dayal Das (1783–1855) of Rawalpindi; opposed idol worship and ritualism and stressed nam simran. It is different from the Sant Nirankari Mission (1929).
- Namdhari (Kuka) movement: Baba Ram Singh founded it at Bhaini Sahib (Ludhiana) on Baisakhi 1857; stressed simple living, cow protection, swadeshi and boycott of British courts, schools and goods.
- Kuka attack on Malerkotla (Jan 1872): over 60 Kukas were blown away from cannons on the orders of Deputy Commissioner L. Cowan; Ram Singh was exiled to Rangoon.
- Singh Sabha movement: first Singh Sabha at Amritsar (1873), Lahore Singh Sabha (1879); led to Khalsa College Amritsar (1892) and the Chief Khalsa Diwan (1902); promoted Punjabi education and Sikh identity.
- Arya Samaj: Swami Dayanand founded the Lahore branch in 1877; DAV school/college at Lahore (1886, Lala Hansraj); split in 1893 into the College party and the Gurukul party (Swami Shraddhanand).
- Ahmadiyya movement: founded by Mirza Ghulam Ahmad at Qadian (Gurdaspur) in 1889.
- Dev Samaj: founded by Shiv Narayan Agnihotri at Lahore in 1887; Brahmo Samaj reached Lahore in 1863.
- Ad-Dharm movement: Babu Mangu Ram Mugowalia (Mugowal, Hoshiarpur), 1925–26; a Dalit assertion movement recognised as a separate religious category in the 1931 Census.
- Radha Soami Satsang Beas: Dera Baba Jaimal Singh on the Beas, founded 1891; a major present-day spiritual organisation.
- Gurdwara Reform (Akali) movement: SGPC formed 15 Nov 1920, Shiromani Akali Dal 14 Dec 1920; aimed to free gurdwaras from corrupt mahants.
- Key Akali morchas: Nankana Sahib massacre (20 Feb 1921, Mahant Narain Das); Keys affair (1921–22, Gandhi hailed the victory); Guru ka Bagh (1922); Jaito morcha (1923–25, over the removal of the Nabha ruler).
- Sikh Gurdwaras Act 1925 placed historic gurdwaras under the elected SGPC; the Sikh Rehat Maryada (code of conduct) was later approved by the SGPC.
- Major Hindu shrines: Durgiana Mandir (Amritsar), Devi Talab Mandir (Jalandhar), Ram Tirath (Amritsar, linked with Valmiki), Kali Mata Mandir (Patiala); Muslim heritage in Malerkotla, and Rauza Sharif at Sirhind.

## Punjab angle

- Reform movements fed directly into politics: Akali Dal from the Gurdwara Reform movement; Arya Samaj and Singh Sabha shaped education and the Punjabi–Hindi language debates.

## How PPSC asks it

Founder–place–year matching (Namdhari 1857 Bhaini Sahib, Singh Sabha 1873, Ahmadiyya 1889 Qadian, Ad-Dharm 1925–26) and the sequence of Akali morchas.

## Deep dive

### Sufi orders and saints of the Punjab

- Ali Hujwiri (Data Ganj Bakhsh, d. c. 1072, Lahore) wrote Kashf-ul-Mahjub, the oldest Persian treatise on Sufism; his shrine is in Lahore.
- Chishti order: Baba Farid (Fariduddin Ganjshakar, 1173–1266) of Pakpattan; Faridkot honours him with the Baba Sheikh Farid Aagman Purb each September.
- Suhrawardi order: Bahauddin Zakariya of Multan (13th c.) made Multan a major Sufi centre.
- Naqshbandi order: Sheikh Ahmad Sirhindi (d. 1624), called Mujaddid Alf Sani (reviver of the second millennium), opposed Akbar's eclecticism; his tomb is the Rauza Sharif at Sirhind (Fatehgarh Sahib), site of an annual Urs.
- Qadiri order: Mian Mir of Lahore (d. 1635), friend of Guru Arjan — Sikh tradition credits him with laying the foundation stone of the Harmandir Sahib; Sultan Bahu and Bulleh Shah also belong to the Qadiri line.
- Haider Shaikh (15th c.) — Sufi founder of Malerkotla; his dargah draws Hindus, Sikhs and Muslims.
- Sakhi Sarwar (Lakhdata, Nigaha in Dera Ghazi Khan) — his 'Sultani' cult spread through Punjabi villages; Pir Budhu Shah of Sadhaura fought for Guru Gobind Singh at Bhangani (1688).
- Popular folk-religion figures shared across communities: Gugga Pir (Chhapar mela), Khwaja Khizr (river spirit), Sheetla Mata (Jarag mela).

### Sikh sects, deras and later spiritual movements

- Udasis — ascetic order traced to Baba Sri Chand; their mahants controlled many gurdwaras until the Gurdwara Reform movement (1920–25).
- Nirmalas — scholarly order trained in Sanskrit learning; spread Sikh teachings in north India; akharas at Haridwar and Kankhal.
- Nihangs — warrior order (Budha Dal, Taruna Dal); blue dress, display of gatka and horsemanship at Hola Mohalla.
- Namdhari succession: Baba Balak Singh → Baba Ram Singh (founded the Sant Khalsa, 1857) → Hari Singh → Partap Singh → Jagjit Singh (d. 2012) → Uday Singh; headquarters Bhaini Sahib (Ludhiana); they accept a living Guru.
- Nirankaris: Baba Dayal (d. 1855) → Baba Darbara Singh; headquarters moved from Rawalpindi to Chandigarh after 1947 — distinct from the Delhi-based Sant Nirankari Mission (Baba Buta Singh, 1929), whose clash with Sikhs at Amritsar on Baisakhi 1978 was a prelude to the 1980s crisis.
- Radha Soami: founded by Shiv Dayal Singh at Agra (1861); the Beas branch (Dera Baba Jaimal Singh, 1891) is the largest dera in Punjab.
- Dera Sachkhand Ballan (Jalandhar) — centre of the Ravidassia community; announced a separate Ravidassia religion and scripture (Amritbani) in 2010.
- Other influential deras: Dera Sacha Sauda (Sirsa, Haryana, 1948) with a large Malwa following; Divya Jyoti Jagrati Sansthan (Nurmahal, Jalandhar, 1983).
- Akhand Kirtani Jatha (Bhai Randhir Singh) and Damdami Taksal (Mehta Chowk, Amritsar) are influential orthodox Sikh bodies.

### Shrines and sacred geography to locate on a map

- Akal Takht (Amritsar) built by Guru Hargobind in 1606; Takht Sri Damdama Sahib (Talwandi Sabo) recognised by the SGPC as the fifth Takht in 1966.
- Gurdwaras of the Gurus' lives: Ber Sahib (Sultanpur Lodhi, Guru Nanak's enlightenment by the Kali Bein); Khadur Sahib; Goindwal Sahib; Tarn Taran (largest sarovar); Baba Bakala; Kiratpur Sahib (Patalpuri, where ashes are immersed); Chamkaur Sahib; Fatehgarh Sahib.
- Dera Baba Nanak (Gurdaspur) — Indian end of the Kartarpur Corridor to Gurdwara Darbar Sahib, Kartarpur (Pakistan), opened 9 Nov 2019.
- Hindu sites: Ram Tirath (Amritsar; Valmiki ashram, Luv–Kush tradition), Durgiana Mandir (Amritsar), Devi Talab Mandir (Jalandhar), Kali Mata Mandir (Patiala); Chintpurni and Naina Devi lie just across the border in Himachal Pradesh.
- Jain: Mata Chakreshwari Devi temple near Sirhind (Fatehgarh Sahib). Buddhist: Sanghol (Fatehgarh Sahib) stupa and monastery of the Kushana age.
- Muslim: Malerkotla — the state's only Muslim-majority district — with the Haider Shaikh dargah; Rauza Sharif at Sirhind.
- Christian: American Presbyterian Ludhiana Mission (1834) set up the region's first printing press (1836); Christian Medical College, Ludhiana grew from a women's medical school founded by Dr Edith Brown in 1894.
- Qadian (Gurdaspur) — world headquarters of the Ahmadiyya community until 1947 and still its Indian centre.

### Scripture, institutions and concepts

- Adi Granth compiled by Guru Arjan (1604); final recension by Guru Gobind Singh at Damdama Sahib (1706); installed as Guru Granth Sahib in 1708.
- Standard print: 1,430 angs; arranged by raga (31 main ragas); contributions of six Gurus, fifteen Bhagats (Kabir, Namdev, Ravidas, Farid, Trilochan, Beni, Dhanna, Jaidev, Surdas, Parmanand, Pipa, Sain, Sadhna, Ramanand, Bhikhan), Bhatts and Gursikhs.
- Dasam Granth (compositions attributed to Guru Gobind Singh, including Jaap Sahib, Bachittar Natak, Chandi di Var, Zafarnama); Varan of Bhai Gurdas are called the key to the Granth.
- Panj Pyare (1699): Daya Singh, Dharam Singh, Himmat Singh, Mohkam Singh, Sahib Singh; five Ks: kesh, kangha, kara, kachhera, kirpan.
- Sikh Rehat Maryada — code of conduct approved by the SGPC in 1945 and published in 1950; Nanakshahi calendar adopted in 2003 and later modified (2010).
- Core concepts: miri–piri, sangat–pangat, langar, seva, kirat karni, vand chhakna, nam japna, dasvandh (one-tenth of income).

### Census data and trends

- Census 2011: Sikhs 57.69%, Hindus 38.49%, Muslims 1.93%, Christians 1.26%; in 2001 the Sikh share was about 59.9% and Hindu about 36.9% — the Sikh share is slowly falling.
- Gurdaspur has the highest Christian share among districts (about 7.7% in 2011); recent church growth in the border belt is a debated social issue.
- Malerkotla (district since 2021) is the only Muslim-majority district; Muslims elsewhere are mostly migrants or Gujjar communities in the Kandi belt.
- Punjab's SC population (31.94%) is spread across Sikh (Mazhabi, Ravidassia/Ramdassia) and Hindu (Balmiki, Ad-Dharmi) groups — the base for dera followings.
- Census 1881 (Ibbetson) and 1931 (Ad-Dharmis counted separately) show how enumeration shaped religious identities in colonial Punjab.
- NFHS-5 (2019-21): 59% of households in Punjab have Sikh heads and 38% Hindu heads — close to Census proportions.

### Confusions and traps

- Nirankari (Baba Dayal, 19th-century reform within Sikhism) ≠ Sant Nirankari Mission (1929, a separate guru-led movement).
- Udasi order (Baba Sri Chand) ≠ Guru Nanak's udasis (his four missionary journeys).
- Damdama Sahib (Talwandi Sabo, Bathinda) is a Takht; Gurdwara Damdama Sahib in Delhi is not.
- Only three of the five Takhts are in Punjab (Akal Takht, Kesgarh, Damdama); Patna Sahib and Hazur Sahib are run by their own boards, not the SGPC.
- Ad-Dharm (1925–26, Mangu Ram) is a movement; the separate 'Ravidassia religion' was declared by Dera Ballan only in 2010.
- Sheikh Ahmad Sirhindi (Naqshbandi) is often confused with Baba Farid (Chishti) — note the order and century.

## Practice MCQs (16)

**1.** The Namdhari (Kuka) movement was founded at Bhaini Sahib by:

(a) Baba Ram Singh  
(b) Baba Dayal Das  
(c) Bhai Maharaj Singh  
(d) Baba Balak Singh

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Baba Ram Singh** — Baba Ram Singh founded it on Baisakhi 1857; Baba Balak Singh was his spiritual predecessor.

</details>

**2.** Who founded the Nirankari movement in the 19th century?

(a) Bhai Gurmukh Singh  
(b) Baba Dayal Das  
(c) Baba Buta Singh  
(d) Baba Ram Singh

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Baba Dayal Das** — Baba Dayal (1783–1855) of Rawalpindi; Baba Buta Singh founded the separate Sant Nirankari Mission in 1929.

</details>

**3.** The first Singh Sabha was established at Amritsar in:

(a) 1902  
(b) 1879  
(c) 1892  
(d) 1873

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) 1873** — Amritsar 1873; Lahore Singh Sabha 1879; Khalsa College 1892; Chief Khalsa Diwan 1902.

</details>

**4.** The Ad-Dharm movement in Punjab was led by:

(a) Babu Mangu Ram Mugowalia  
(b) B.R. Ambedkar  
(c) Kanshi Ram  
(d) Babu Jagjivan Ram

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Babu Mangu Ram Mugowalia** — Mangu Ram Mugowalia started it in 1925–26 in the Doaba region.

</details>

**5.** The Nankana Sahib massacre, a turning point of the Gurdwara Reform movement, took place in:

(a) 1921  
(b) 1923  
(c) 1919  
(d) 1925

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 1921** — On 20 February 1921, reformist Sikhs were killed by Mahant Narain Das's men.

</details>

**6.** Consider the statements:  
1. The Shiromani Akali Dal was formed before the SGPC.  
2. The Sikh Gurdwaras Act, 1925 was passed by the Central Legislative Assembly.  
Which is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Neither 1 nor 2** — Statement 1 is wrong: the SGPC came first (15 November 1920) and the Akali Dal followed (14 December 1920). Statement 2 is wrong: the Sikh Gurdwaras Act, 1925 was passed by the Punjab Legislative Council, not the Central Legislative Assembly.

</details>

**7.** The Ahmadiyya movement was founded at:

(a) Malerkotla  
(b) Pakpattan  
(c) Qadian  
(d) Sirhind

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Qadian** — Mirza Ghulam Ahmad founded it at Qadian (Gurdaspur) in 1889.

</details>

**8.** Which of the five Takhts is located in Bathinda district of Punjab?

(a) Takht Sri Damdama Sahib  
(b) Takht Sri Kesgarh Sahib  
(c) Takht Sri Hazur Sahib  
(d) Akal Takht

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Takht Sri Damdama Sahib** — Damdama Sahib is at Talwandi Sabo (Bathinda); Kesgarh Sahib is at Anandpur Sahib (Rupnagar).

</details>

**9.** Dera Baba Jaimal Singh at Beas, established in 1891, is the headquarters of:

(a) The Sant Nirankari Mission  
(b) The Divya Jyoti Jagrati Sansthan  
(c) The Namdhari Sikhs  
(d) Radha Soami Satsang Beas

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Radha Soami Satsang Beas** — Baba Jaimal Singh, a disciple of the Agra Radha Soami founder Shiv Dayal Singh, set up the Beas dera, now the largest in Punjab. The Namdharis are centred at Bhaini Sahib and the Divya Jyoti Jagrati Sansthan at Nurmahal.

</details>

**10.** The Dev Samaj, a reform society, was founded at Lahore in 1887 by:

(a) Lala Hans Raj  
(b) Pandit Shardha Ram Phillauri  
(c) Shiv Narayan Agnihotri  
(d) Swami Dayanand Saraswati

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Shiv Narayan Agnihotri** — Agnihotri, earlier a Brahmo, founded the Dev Samaj in 1887; it stressed moral conduct and women's education. Dayanand founded the Lahore Arya Samaj (1877) and Hans Raj led the DAV College (1886).

</details>

**11.** The Anand Marriage Act, which gave legal recognition to the Sikh marriage ceremony, was passed in:

(a) 1950  
(b) 1873  
(c) 1909  
(d) 1925

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) 1909** — It was passed in 1909 after a Singh Sabha campaign; the bill was introduced in the Imperial Legislative Council by Tikka Ripudaman Singh of Nabha. 1873 is the first Singh Sabha and 1925 the Sikh Gurdwaras Act.

</details>

**12.** Islamia College, Lahore (1892), was set up by which Muslim educational body founded at Lahore in 1884?

(a) Anjuman-i-Punjab  
(b) Anjuman-i-Himayat-i-Islam  
(c) Ahmadiyya Anjuman Ishaat-i-Islam  
(d) Anjuman-i-Islamia of Amritsar

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Anjuman-i-Himayat-i-Islam** — The Anjuman-i-Himayat-i-Islam ran schools, orphanages and Islamia College. The Anjuman-i-Punjab (1865) was G.W. Leitner's society that led to Punjab University, and the Lahore Ahmadiyya group dates from 1914.

</details>

**13.** The Udasi order of ascetics traces its origin to:

(a) Baba Sri Chand, elder son of Guru Nanak  
(b) Prithi Chand, elder son of Guru Ram Das  
(c) Baba Lakhmi Das, younger son of Guru Nanak  
(d) Dhir Mal, grandson of Guru Hargobind

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Baba Sri Chand, elder son of Guru Nanak** — Baba Sri Chand founded the celibate Udasi order; Udasi mahants later controlled many gurdwaras until the 1920s reform. Prithi Chand founded the Mina sect and Dhir Mal the Dhirmalias.

</details>

**15.** Consider the following statements:  
1. Munshi Ram, later Swami Shraddhanand, led the 'College' party.  
2. The Arya Samaj in Punjab split in 1893 into the 'College' party and the 'Gurukul' party.

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** — The 1893 split was over meat-eating and Western education, so the first statement is correct. Munshi Ram led the Gurukul (Mahatma) party and founded Gurukul Kangri (1902), while Lala Hans Raj and Lajpat Rai led the College party.

</details>

**16.** Consider the following statements:  
1. The Sant Nirankari Mission was founded by Baba Dayal.  
2. Dera Sachkhand Ballan near Jalandhar is a major centre of the Ravidassia community.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** — Baba Dayal (d. 1855) founded the 19th-century Nirankari reform movement, while the Sant Nirankari Mission was begun by Baba Buta Singh in 1929, so the first statement is wrong. Dera Ballan leads the Ravidassia community and declared a separate Ravidassia religion in 2010.

</details>
