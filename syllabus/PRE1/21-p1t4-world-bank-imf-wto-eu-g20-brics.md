## Short notes

- Bretton Woods Conference (July 1944, USA) created the IMF and the IBRD (World Bank); India was a founding member of both.
- IMF (Washington DC): short-term balance-of-payments support, surveillance through Article IV consultations, technical help; quotas decide voting power; head (Managing Director) traditionally European.
- Special Drawing Rights (SDR, 1969): IMF reserve asset valued on five currencies - US dollar, euro, Chinese renminbi (added 2016), Japanese yen, pound sterling.
- IMF reports: World Economic Outlook, Global Financial Stability Report, Fiscal Monitor.
- World Bank Group (5): IBRD (loans to middle-income), IDA (1960, concessional to poorest), IFC (private sector), MIGA (investment guarantees), ICSID (investment disputes - India is not a member); President traditionally American (Ajay Banga since 2023).
- World Bank publishes the World Development Report; its Doing Business report was discontinued in 2021.
- GATT (1947) was replaced by the WTO on 1 Jan 1995 (Marrakesh Agreement, Uruguay Round); HQ Geneva; 166 members; Ministerial Conference (every 2 years) is the top body.
- WTO core principles: Most Favoured Nation and National Treatment; key agreements: Agreement on Agriculture (amber, blue, green boxes), TRIPS, TRIMS, GATS; dispute settlement's Appellate Body has been non-functional since Dec 2019.
- India's WTO concerns: permanent solution for public stockholding for food security (interim 'peace clause' from Bali 2013), fisheries subsidies, special and differential treatment; MC13 Abu Dhabi (2024), MC14 Yaounde, Cameroon (March 2026) gave only partial outcomes.
- European Union: Treaty of Rome 1957 (EEC), Maastricht Treaty 1992 (EU from 1993), Lisbon Treaty 2007; 27 members after Brexit (31 Jan 2020); euro used by 21 members after Bulgaria joined on 1 Jan 2026.
- India-EU: strategic partners since 2004; Trade and Technology Council (2022); India-EU Free Trade Agreement negotiations were concluded at the summit in New Delhi in January 2026 (signature and ratification to follow).
- G20: formed 1999 after the Asian financial crisis as a finance ministers' forum; leaders' summits since 2008; 19 countries + EU + African Union (made permanent member at New Delhi, Sept 2023); no permanent secretariat, works through a troika.
- India's G20 presidency 2023: theme 'Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam - One Earth, One Family, One Future'; New Delhi Leaders' Declaration, Global Biofuels Alliance, India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC) announced.
- G20 rotation: South Africa hosted 2025 (first summit in Africa); the USA holds the 2026 presidency with the leaders' summit planned at Miami.
- BRIC acronym coined by Jim O'Neill (2001); first summit Yekaterinburg 2009; South Africa joined 2010; Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran and UAE joined from 1 Jan 2024, Indonesia in Jan 2025; a 'partner country' category was created at Kazan (2024).
- BRICS New Development Bank (agreed at Fortaleza 2014, HQ Shanghai, first president K.V. Kamath) and the US$100 billion Contingent Reserve Arrangement offer alternatives to Bretton Woods finance.
- India chairs BRICS in 2026 and hosted the 18th BRICS summit in New Delhi (September 2026).
- Other bodies under 'etc.': Asian Development Bank (Manila, 1966) and Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (Beijing, 2016; India the second-largest shareholder).

## Punjab angle

- WTO debates on MSP-based procurement and public stockholding directly affect Punjab, a leading contributor of wheat and rice to the central pool.
- Export clusters such as Ludhiana (hosiery, bicycles, auto parts) and Jalandhar (sports goods, hand tools) are affected by tariff cuts under FTAs like the India-EU and India-UK agreements.

## Deep dive

### IMF and World Bank — mechanics and India

- IMF Articles of Agreement came into force 27 Dec 1945; operations began 1947; Liechtenstein became the 191st member in 2024.
- Governance: Board of Governors → 25-member Executive Board; India's Executive Director also represents Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and Bhutan.
- Major decisions need 85% of votes, so the US (about 16.5%) has an effective veto; the 16th General Review of Quotas (Dec 2023) approved a 50% increase without changing shares.
- Lending tools: Stand-By Arrangement, Extended Fund Facility, Rapid Financing Instrument, Poverty Reduction and Growth Trust, Resilience and Sustainability Trust (2022).
- India and the IMF: borrowings in 1966, 1981 (large EFF) and 1991 (crisis, when gold was pledged); all repaid; India now lends to the Fund through its arrangements.
- Largest-ever SDR allocation: about US$650 billion in August 2021 (pandemic support).
- World Bank: IBRD's first loan went to France (1947); IDA (1960) gives soft loans — India is the largest cumulative IDA borrower and graduated from IDA lending in 2014; IDA21 replenishment of US$100 billion agreed Dec 2024.
- Indus Waters Treaty (1960) was signed by Nehru and Ayub Khan with the World Bank as a signatory: eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India, western (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) to Pakistan; India held it in abeyance from April 2025.
- World Bank flagship outputs: World Development Report, Global Economic Prospects, and B-READY (Business Ready, first edition 2024) which replaced Doing Business.

### WTO — structure and negotiating history

- GATT 1947 had 23 founding contracting parties including India; rounds: Kennedy (1964–67), Tokyo (1973–79), Uruguay (1986–94); Doha Development Agenda (2001) is stalled.
- Structure: Ministerial Conference → General Council (also sits as Dispute Settlement Body and Trade Policy Review Body) → Councils for Goods, Services and TRIPS.
- Decisions by consensus and the "single undertaking"; Director-General Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala (first woman and first African DG, from 2021; second term from Sept 2025).
- Ministerials: Singapore 1996 ("Singapore issues"), Seattle 1999 (collapsed), Doha 2001, Cancun 2003, Hong Kong 2005, Bali 2013 (Trade Facilitation Agreement, in force Feb 2017; peace clause), Nairobi 2015 (end of farm export subsidies).
- MC12 Geneva (2022): Agreement on Fisheries Subsidies (curbs subsidies for illegal fishing and overfished stocks) — entered into force in Sept 2025.
- MC14 Yaounde, Cameroon (26–30 March 2026): no consensus to extend the moratorium on customs duties on electronic transmissions, which was due to expire on 31 March 2026; further fisheries disciplines pushed to MC15.
- India's stands: permanent solution on public stockholding (India first invoked the peace clause for rice in 2020), opposition to bringing the Investment Facilitation for Development plurilateral into the WTO, and special and differential treatment.
- Disputes involving India: sugar subsidies panel loss (2021, complaints by Brazil, Australia, Guatemala) appealed "into the void"; ICT tariffs panel (2023, EU/Japan/Chinese Taipei).
- Boxes in the Agreement on Agriculture: amber (trade-distorting, capped; de minimis 10% for developing countries), blue (production-limiting), green (minimal distortion, unlimited).

### EU, G20 and BRICS — deeper facts

- EU institutions: European Council (leaders), Council of the EU (ministers), European Commission (President Ursula von der Leyen, second term from Dec 2024), European Parliament (720 MEPs after 2024), Court of Justice (Luxembourg), ECB (Frankfurt).
- Schengen: agreement 1985; Croatia joined 2023; Bulgaria and Romania became full members in Jan 2025. Copenhagen criteria (1993) govern accession; Ukraine and Moldova became candidates in 2022.
- EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism: reporting phase from Oct 2023, definitive (charging) phase from 1 Jan 2026 — hits Indian steel and aluminium exports.
- India–EFTA Trade and Economic Partnership Agreement (signed March 2024, in force 1 Oct 2025) is with Switzerland, Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein — not the EU.
- G20 members: Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, South Korea, Turkey, UK, USA, plus the EU and the African Union.
- G20 works through a Sherpa track and a Finance track plus engagement groups (B20, C20, L20, T20, Y20, W20).
- Johannesburg summit (22–23 Nov 2025) was the first in Africa and was boycotted by the US; the US 2026 presidency excluded South Africa and plans the leaders' summit at Doral, Miami, in Dec 2026.
- India hosted BRICS summits in 2012 (4th), 2016 (8th, Goa), 2021 (13th, virtual) and 2026 (18th, New Delhi) — the New Delhi Declaration was adopted by consensus on 12 Sept 2026; theme "Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability".
- BRICS partner countries (from 2025): Belarus, Bolivia, Kazakhstan, Cuba, Malaysia, Thailand, Uganda, Uzbekistan, Nigeria and Vietnam; Saudi Arabia was invited but has not formally confirmed membership.
- New Development Bank: five founders hold equal shares; later members include Bangladesh, UAE, Egypt and Algeria; President Dilma Rousseff; India regional office at GIFT City, Gujarat.

### Other bodies under "etc."

- Asian Development Bank (1966, Manila): Japan and the US are the largest shareholders; the President has always been Japanese (Masato Kanda since Feb 2025).
- AIIB (2016, Beijing): China largest, India second-largest shareholder and a major borrower; Jin Liqun (2016–2026) was succeeded by Zou Jiayi in Jan 2026.
- OECD (1961, Paris): India is not a member but a "key partner".
- SCO (2001, secretariat Beijing): India and Pakistan joined 2017, Iran 2023, Belarus 2024; summit at Tianjin, China (31 Aug–1 Sept 2025).
- FATF (1989, Paris): India member since 2010; Pakistan left the grey list in Oct 2022.
- BIS (1930, Basel) — "central bank of central banks"; World Economic Forum (Geneva) is a private foundation, not an intergovernmental body (Global Competitiveness, Global Gender Gap reports).
- Commonwealth (London Declaration 1949 let republics like India stay), NAM (Belgrade 1961), SAARC (1985, Kathmandu secretariat), BIMSTEC (1997, Dhaka secretariat; Charter in force 2024).
- Minilaterals: Quad, I2U2, IPEF (2022), IMEC (2023) — often asked as "which grouping includes India".

### Punjab and global economic bodies

- WTO: MSP procurement of wheat and paddy counts as amber-box market price support; Punjab is a top contributor to the central pool, so any cap on public stockholding directly hits its farmers.
- Indus Waters Treaty abeyance (2025) refocused attention on using Ravi waters in Punjab; the Shahpurkandi dam on the Ravi (completed 2024) stops surplus water flowing to Pakistan.
- World Bank and AIIB co-finance the Punjab Municipal Services Improvement Project — canal-based 24x7 water supply for Amritsar and Ludhiana.
- EU CBAM affects steel re-rolling clusters at Mandi Gobindgarh and Ludhiana; the India–EU FTA (concluded Jan 2026) may open markets for Ludhiana hosiery, bicycles and Jalandhar sports goods.
- IMF/World Bank data on remittances matter for Doaba households that depend on NRI inflows.
- India withdrew Most-Favoured-Nation treatment from Pakistan in Feb 2019 after Pulwama (a WTO-relevant step); trade through the Attari Integrated Check Post (opened 2012) collapsed, and the ICP was closed in April 2025 after the Pahalgam attack.

### Traps

- By convention the IMF Managing Director is European and the World Bank President American.
- India is not a member of ICSID, the OECD or APEC; it is a founding member of the IMF, IBRD, GATT/WTO and NDB.
- G20 has no permanent secretariat; Spain is a permanent guest, not a member.
- NDB HQ is Shanghai; Contingent Reserve Arrangement is a BRICS reserve pool, not a bank.
- SDR is a reserve asset and unit of account, not a currency traded by the public.
- Green-box support is not capped; blue-box support is exempt only if linked to production limits.

**7.** Consider the following statements:  
1. The Indus Waters Treaty, 1960 was signed at New Delhi.  
2. Under the treaty, the Jhelum, Chenab and Ravi were allocated to India.

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Neither 1 nor 2** — Statement 1 is wrong: the treaty was signed at Karachi on 19 September 1960 by Nehru, Ayub Khan and the World Bank. Statement 2 is wrong: India got the eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej), while the western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) went to Pakistan.

</details>
