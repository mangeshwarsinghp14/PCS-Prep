## Short notes

- Article 368: most amendments need a special majority (majority of total membership plus two-thirds of members present and voting) in each House; federal provisions also need ratification by half the state legislatures.
- Some changes (e.g. Arts 2, 3, 4, creation/abolition of Legislative Councils under Art 169, Fifth/Sixth Schedule matters) are made by simple majority and are not 'amendments' under Art 368.
- An amendment bill can be introduced in either House, by a minister or private member, without the President's prior sanction; there is no joint sitting on amendment bills; the 24th Amendment (1971) made Presidential assent compulsory.
- 1st (1951): added the Ninth Schedule, Art 15(4) for backward classes, and new grounds of restriction on speech (public order, friendly relations, incitement).
- 7th (1956): reorganised states on linguistic lines, abolished Part A/B/C/D classification - 14 states and 6 UTs.
- 24th (1971): affirmed Parliament's power to amend any part including Fundamental Rights, overriding Golaknath (1967); 26th (1971) abolished privy purses.
- 42nd (1976), the 'mini-constitution': added 'socialist, secular, integrity' to the Preamble, Fundamental Duties, Arts 39A, 43A, 48A, tribunals (Arts 323A/323B), shifted five subjects to the Concurrent List.
- 44th (1978): removed right to property from Part III (now Art 300A); replaced 'internal disturbance' by 'armed rebellion' in Art 352; Cabinet advice for emergency must be in writing; Arts 20 and 21 cannot be suspended in emergency.
- 52nd (1985) added the anti-defection Tenth Schedule; 91st (2003) deleted the one-third 'split' exception and capped the Council of Ministers at 15% of the Lower House (minimum 12 in states).
- 61st (1989): voting age lowered from 21 to 18 (Art 326).
- 73rd and 74th (1992, in force 1993): constitutional status to Panchayats (Part IX, 11th Schedule) and Municipalities (Part IXA, 12th Schedule).
- 86th (2002): Art 21A - free and compulsory education for children aged 6-14; Art 45 revised to early childhood care; 11th Fundamental Duty added.
- 97th (2011): co-operative societies - right to form co-operatives in Art 19(1)(c), Art 43B and Part IXB (the Supreme Court in 2021 struck down Part IXB's application to state co-operatives).
- 99th (2014): National Judicial Appointments Commission - struck down in Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association v Union of India (2015), restoring the collegium.
- 101st (2016): Goods and Services Tax - Art 246A (concurrent taxing power), Art 269A (IGST), Art 279A (GST Council); GST rolled out 1 July 2017.
- 102nd (2018): constitutional status to National Commission for Backward Classes (Art 338B); 105th (2021) restored states' power to prepare their own SEBC lists.
- 103rd (2019): up to 10% reservation for economically weaker sections (Arts 15(6), 16(6)) - upheld 3:2 in Janhit Abhiyan (2022); 104th (2020) extended SC/ST seat reservation in legislatures to 2030 and ended Anglo-Indian nomination.
- 106th (2023), Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam: one-third seats for women in Lok Sabha and state assemblies (incl. Delhi) for 15 years, effective after post-census delimitation; 129th Amendment Bill (2024, simultaneous polls) sent to a JPC.

## Punjab angle

- The 7th Amendment era reorganisation merged PEPSU into Punjab (1 Nov 1956) under the States Reorganisation Act.
- Punjab enacted the Punjab Panchayati Raj Act 1994 and Punjab Municipal laws to implement the 73rd and 74th Amendments.
- Amendments affecting federal provisions, such as the 101st (GST), required ratification by state legislatures including Punjab's.

## How PPSC asks it

Questions match amendment numbers to changes (61st voting age, 86th RTE, 101st GST, 103rd EWS, 106th women's reservation) and test Art 368 procedure. Memorise the number-to-content pairs and the cases that struck down or upheld them.

## Deep dive

### Amendments 1952–1978 not in the summary (No. — year — change)

- 4th (1955) — adequacy of compensation for acquired property made non-justiciable.
- 9th (1960) — transfer of Berubari Union to Pakistan, after the Re Berubari reference (1960) said a treaty ceding territory needs an Art 368 amendment.
- 10th (1961) Dadra and Nagar Haveli; 12th (1962) Goa, Daman and Diu; 14th (1962) Puducherry — added as UTs.
- 13th (1962) — Art 371A, special status for Nagaland.
- 15th (1963) — High Court judges' retirement age raised from 60 to 62.
- 17th (1964) — market-value compensation for land under personal cultivation within ceiling; more laws into the Ninth Schedule.
- 18th (1966) — "State" in Art 3 includes a UT; forming a new state includes uniting a part of a state/UT to another — the enabling change for the Punjab Reorganisation Act 1966.
- 25th (1971) — Art 31C (laws for Art 39(b)–(c) immune from Arts 14, 19); "amount" replaced "compensation".
- 31st (1973) — Lok Sabha elected seats raised from 525 to 545.
- 35th (1974) and 36th (1975) — Sikkim first an "associate state", then a full state.
- 38th and 39th (1975) — emergency and electoral disputes of the PM, President, VP placed beyond courts; the 39th's clause on the PM's election was struck down in Indira Gandhi v Raj Narain (1975).
- 43rd (1977) — restored judicial powers curtailed in 1976; 44th (1978) — restored safeguards (summary has details).

### Amendments 1980–2003

- 45th (1980) and later ones (62nd, 79th, 95th, 104th) extended SC/ST seat reservation every ten years.
- 53rd and 55th (1986) — Mizoram and Arunachal Pradesh; 56th (1987) — Goa statehood.
- 58th (1987) — authoritative Hindi text, Art 394A.
- 59th (1988) — allowed Emergency in Punjab on "internal disturbance" and suspension of Art 21 there, and extension of President's Rule beyond one year.
- 63rd (1989) — omitted the Punjab-specific Emergency provisions of the 59th; 64th (1990), 67th (1990) and 68th (1991) extended President's Rule in Punjab.
- 65th (1990) — multi-member National Commission for SCs and STs in place of a Special Officer.
- 69th (1991) — Delhi as National Capital Territory with a legislature (Arts 239AA, 239AB); 70th (1992) — Delhi and Puducherry MLAs added to the presidential electoral college.
- 77th (1995) — Art 16(4A) reservation in promotion for SC/ST; 81st (2000) — carry-forward backlog not counted in the 50% ceiling; 82nd (2000) — relaxation in qualifying marks; 85th (2001) — consequential seniority.
- 84th (2001) — seat freeze till the first census after 2026; 87th (2003) — delimitation boundaries on the 2001 census.
- 89th (2003) — separate National Commission for STs (Art 338A); 93rd (2005) — Art 15(5) reservation in private unaided institutions (upheld in Pramati, 2014).

### Amendments 2009–2023

- 97th (2011) — co-operatives; 98th (2012) — Art 371J for Hyderabad-Karnataka region.
- 100th (2015) — Land Boundary Agreement with Bangladesh: 111 Indian enclaves exchanged for 51 Bangladeshi enclaves.
- 101st (2016) — GST (originated as the 122nd Bill); 102nd (2018) — NCBC constitutional status (originated as the 123rd Bill).
- 103rd (2019) — EWS quota; 104th (2019, effective Jan 2020) — Anglo-Indian nomination ended, SC/ST reservation to 2030.
- 105th (2021) — restored states' power to identify SEBCs (originated as the 127th Bill, after the Maratha quota judgment of May 2021).
- 106th (2023) — women's reservation (originated as the 128th Bill): new Arts 330A, 332A, 334A and a clause in 239AA; one-third seats including within SC/ST seats; 15 years; rotation after each delimitation; not for Rajya Sabha or Legislative Councils.
- Trap: Bill numbers differ from Act numbers because many Bills lapse — always match the Act number.

### Amendment Bills 2024–2026 — status (verify-heavy)

- 129th Bill (Dec 2024, One Nation One Election): new Art 82A and changes to Arts 83, 172, 327 — terms of all assemblies to end with the Lok Sabha after an "appointed date". JPC chaired by P.P. Chaudhary; deadline extended in July 2026 to the last week of the Winter Session 2026.
- 130th Bill (introduced 20 Aug 2025): a PM, CM or minister arrested and detained for 30 consecutive days for an offence punishable with five years or more must be removed (or ceases to hold office) — changes Arts 75, 164 and 239AA; companion Government of UT (Amendment) and J&K Reorganisation (Amendment) Bills.
- 130th Bill JPC is chaired by Aparajita Sarangi; most of the Opposition boycotted it; its draft report was deferred in July 2026 for more consultation.
- Nov 2025: a "131st Amendment" proposal to put Chandigarh under Art 240 (President's regulations) appeared in the Winter Session list, provoked protests across Punjab, and was not introduced.
- Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill 2026 — introduced 16 April 2026 by Law Minister Arjun Ram Meghwal: amend Arts 81, 82 and 334A so delimitation could use the 2011 Census and women's reservation could start sooner.
- Companion Delimitation Bill 2026 proposed raising the Lok Sabha cap to 850 (up to 815 from states, 35 from UTs); a UT Laws (Amendment) Bill 2026 also accompanied it.
- Vote on 17 April 2026: 298 for, 230 against out of 528 present — short of the two-thirds (352) needed; the companion Bills were withdrawn. Southern states objected that a 2011-based shift would reward high population growth.
- Result: women's reservation stays tied to delimitation after the first census after 2023 — Census 2027 (reference date 1 March 2027) — so it is unlikely to apply to the 2029 Lok Sabha election.
- Earlier failures for comparison: the 64th (Panchayats) and 65th (Nagarpalika) Bills were defeated in the Rajya Sabha in 1989.

### Procedure and judicial review of amendments

- Art 368(2) proviso — ratification by half the states needed for changes to: election of the President (Arts 54, 55), extent of Union and state executive power (73, 162, 241, 279A), Supreme Court and High Courts, distribution of legislative powers, any List of the Seventh Schedule, representation of states in Parliament, and Art 368 itself.
- No time limit for state ratification; states cannot start an amendment (but Art 169 resolutions start creation/abolition of Councils by ordinary law).
- Art 368(4)–(5) (42nd Amendment) barring review of amendments was struck down in Minerva Mills (1980).
- Kihoto Hollohan (1992) struck down para 7 of the Tenth Schedule for lack of state ratification.
- Union of India v Rajendra N. Shah (2021) struck down Part IXB as it applied to state co-operatives for lack of ratification.
- L. Chandra Kumar (1997) — tribunal clauses could not exclude High Court review; Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record (2015) — 99th Amendment struck down.
- Janhit Abhiyan (2022) — 103rd upheld (3:2); In re Article 370 (Dec 2023) — abrogation upheld; Dr Balram Singh (Nov 2024) — 42nd Amendment's "socialist" and "secular" upheld.

### Punjab and amendments

- 7th (1956) — linked to the States Reorganisation Act under which PEPSU merged with Punjab.
- 18th (1966) — made the Punjabi Suba reorganisation constitutionally smooth.
- 59th, 63rd, 64th, 67th, 68th (1988–91) — the "Punjab amendments" on Emergency and President's Rule; Punjab was under President's Rule from May 1987 to Feb 1992.
- Anandpur Sahib Resolution sought constitutional amendment to limit the Centre to defence, foreign affairs, communications, currency and railways.
- 106th Amendment will mean 39 of Punjab's 117 assembly seats reserved for women once it applies; the 2022 election returned 13 women MLAs.
- Punjab opposed the 131st proposals on Chandigarh (Nov 2025) and joined southern states (Chennai joint action meeting, March 2025) in demanding fair delimitation.

### Traps

- The 61st Amendment (voting age 18) is the 1988 Act, brought into force in March 1989.
- 73rd and 74th Amendments were passed in Dec 1992; in force 24 April 1993 and 1 June 1993 respectively.
- The 42nd added Fundamental Duties (10); the 86th added the 11th duty.
- Only the 24th Amendment made Presidential assent to amendment Bills compulsory — the President cannot withhold or return them.
- Joint sitting is not available for amendment Bills (summary) — but also note a Bill can lapse on dissolution of the Lok Sabha if pending there.
- Amendments under Arts 2, 3, 4, 169, 239A and Fifth/Sixth Schedules are "deemed not amendments" under Art 368.
