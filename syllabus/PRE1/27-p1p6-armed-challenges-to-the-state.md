## Short notes

- Main types of armed challenge since 1947: secessionist insurgencies (North-East, Punjab, J&K), Left Wing Extremism, cross-border terrorism and ethnic conflicts.
- Telangana armed struggle (1946-51): CPI-led peasant uprising against the Nizam and landlords in Hyderabad State; Hyderabad integrated by 'Operation Polo' (Sept 1948).
- Naga insurgency: Naga National Council under A.Z. Phizo declared independence on 14 Aug 1947; armed struggle from the 1950s; Nagaland state 1963; Shillong Accord 1975.
- NSCN formed 1980, split in 1988 into NSCN (IM) and NSCN (K); ceasefire with NSCN (IM) since 1997; Framework Agreement signed in 2015.
- Mizo insurgency: Mizo National Front (Laldenga) revolted in 1966 after the Mautam famine; Mizo Accord 1986, widely seen as India's most successful peace accord; Mizoram state 1987.
- Assam: Assam Movement (1979-85) against illegal migration ended with the Assam Accord 1985 (cut-off 24 March 1971); ULFA formed 1979; Bodo accords 1993, 2003, 2020; peace accord with pro-talks ULFA faction in Dec 2023.
- Manipur: several insurgent groups; ethnic violence between Meitei and Kuki-Zo communities from May 2023; President's Rule imposed in Feb 2025.
- AFSPA 1958 gives special powers to armed forces in 'disturbed areas'; the Jeevan Reddy Committee (2005) recommended repeal; disturbed areas in Assam, Nagaland and Manipur have been reduced since 2022; Tripura withdrew AFSPA in 2015.
- Naxalism began with the Naxalbari uprising (Darjeeling, West Bengal, 1967) led by Charu Majumdar and Kanu Sanyal; CPI (ML) formed 1969.
- CPI (Maoist) formed in 2004 by merger of People's War Group and Maoist Communist Centre of India; PM Manmohan Singh called Naxalism the biggest internal security threat (2006).
- Government responses to LWE: SAMADHAN strategy (2017), development plus security approach, roads and mobile towers in affected areas; Supreme Court declared Salwa Judum unconstitutional (Nandini Sundar case, 2011).
- The Union government set 31 March 2026 as the target to end LWE; the number of most-affected districts fell sharply (from 12 to 6 by April 2025).
- J&K: militancy from 1989 (JKLF, then Pakistan-backed groups); Kargil war 1999; Pulwama attack (Feb 2019) followed by the Balakot air strike; Article 370 made inoperative in Aug 2019.
- Pahalgam terror attack (22 April 2025) was followed by Operation Sindoor (May 2025) against terror infrastructure in Pakistan and PoK, and India holding the Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance.
- Punjab militancy (1980s-early 1990s): Operation Blue Star (June 1984), Operation Black Thunder (1986, 1988); normalcy restored by about 1993.
- Mumbai attacks (26/11, 2008) led to the NIA Act 2008, strengthening of coastal security and the NATGRID project.
- Legal framework: UAPA 1967 (2019 amendment allows designating individuals as terrorists), NIA Act 2008, National Security Act 1980; earlier TADA (1985-95) and POTA (2002-04) lapsed or were repealed.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab militancy (1980s-early 1990s): Operation Blue Star (June 1984), the Rajiv-Longowal Accord (July 1985) and police operations restored peace by about 1993.
- Current threats along Punjab's 553-km border with Pakistan include drones dropping drugs and weapons; BSF jurisdiction in Punjab was extended from 15 km to 50 km from the border in 2021.
- Drug trafficking and gangster networks with cross-border links are treated as an internal security issue in Punjab.

## Deep dive

### Punjab militancy — detailed timeline

- 13 April 1978 — Akali–Nirankari clash in Amritsar (13 killed) — the starting point of the confrontation.
- 24 April 1980 — Nirankari chief Baba Gurbachan Singh killed; 9 Sept 1981 — Lala Jagat Narain (Punjab Kesari) killed.
- Aug 1982 — Dharam Yudh Morcha under Harchand Singh Longowal to press Anandpur Sahib demands.
- 25 April 1983 — DIG A.S. Atwal shot at the Golden Temple; after the Dhilwan bus killings President's Rule was imposed (6 Oct 1983).
- 1–10 June 1984 — Operation Blue Star (led by Maj. Gen. K.S. Brar), followed by Operation Woodrose in the countryside.
- 31 Oct 1984 — Indira Gandhi assassinated; anti-Sikh violence in Delhi and elsewhere (Nanavati Commission report 2005).
- 23 June 1985 — Air India Flight 182 ("Kanishka") bombed off Ireland, 329 killed — planned by Canada-based extremists.
- 24 July 1985 — Rajiv–Longowal Accord; 20 Aug 1985 — Longowal assassinated; Sept 1985 — Barnala government.
- 30 April 1986 — Operation Black Thunder I; 10 Aug 1986 — former Army chief Gen. A.S. Vaidya killed in Pune.
- May 1987 – Feb 1992 — President's Rule (Punjab amendments 59th–68th); May 1988 — Operation Black Thunder II by NSG and Punjab Police under DGP K.P.S. Gill.
- Feb 1992 — elections; Beant Singh CM; militancy largely ended by 1993; 31 Aug 1995 — Beant Singh assassinated.
- Human-rights legacy: the Punjab mass cremations case (after activist Jaswant Singh Khalra's disappearance in 1995) was sent by the Supreme Court to the NHRC in 1996.

### Punjab — contemporary security challenges (2015–2026)

- 27 July 2015 — Dinanagar (Gurdaspur) police station attack; 2 Jan 2016 — Pathankot Air Force Station attack.
- May 2022 — RPG attack on the Punjab Police Intelligence headquarters at Mohali; 29 May 2022 — singer Sidhu Moosewala killed by a gangster network.
- Feb 2023 — supporters of Amritpal Singh ("Waris Punjab De") stormed Ajnala police station; he was detained under the NSA (April 2023) in Dibrugarh and elected MP in 2024.
- Drones from Pakistan drop drugs and weapons along the 553-km border; BSF jurisdiction widened to 50 km (2021) — opposed by the Punjab Assembly.
- 2024–25 — a series of grenade attacks on police posts in Amritsar and Gurdaspur linked to Babbar Khalsa modules.
- March 2025 — "Yudh Nashian Virudh" (war against drugs) campaign by the Punjab government.
- Gangster–terror nexus: MHA designated Goldy Brar a terrorist under UAPA (2024); Sikhs for Justice banned (2019); Gurpatwant Singh Pannun designated (2020).
- BNS 2023 (in force 1 July 2024): Sec 111 organised crime, Sec 113 terrorist act, Sec 152 acts endangering sovereignty and integrity (replacing sedition).
- Abroad: the June 2023 killing of Hardeep Singh Nijjar in Canada strained India–Canada ties; both sides restored High Commissioners in 2025.

### North-East — accords and status (accord — year — outcome)

- Shillong Accord (1975, NNC); Mizo Accord (30 June 1986); Assam Accord (15 Aug 1985).
- Bodo accords: 1993 (Bodoland Autonomous Council), 2003 (Bodoland Territorial Council), 27 Jan 2020 (Bodoland Territorial Region).
- NLFT (Tripura) 2019; Bru-Reang resettlement agreement (Jan 2020); Karbi Anglong Agreement (Sept 2021); Dimasa (DNLA) April 2023; TIPRA Motha tripartite accord (March 2024).
- Manipur: UNLF — the first valley-based insurgent group to sign a peace agreement (29 Nov 2023); ethnic violence between Meitei and Kuki-Zo from 3 May 2023.
- Manipur President's Rule from 13 Feb 2025 was revoked in Feb 2026 when Y. Khemchand Singh became CM (4 Feb 2026).
- ULFA (pro-talks faction) tripartite settlement — 29 Dec 2023; ULFA-I under Paresh Baruah remains outside talks.
- Inter-state boundary deals: Assam–Meghalaya MoU (March 2022), Assam–Arunachal Namsai Declaration (April 2023).
- AFSPA case law: Naga People's Movement for Human Rights (1997) upheld the Act with safeguards; Extra-Judicial Execution Victim Families (2016) — no absolute immunity for excessive force.

### Left Wing Extremism — evolution and response

- Phases: Naxalbari (1967), Srikakulam (1968–70), People's War Group (1980, Kondapalli Seetharamaiah), MCC in Bihar–Jharkhand, merger into CPI (Maoist) in 2004.
- Peak violence: Chintalnar/Tadmetla, Dantewada (April 2010, 76 CRPF personnel killed); Jhiram Ghati (May 2013, Congress leaders killed).
- Operations: Green Hunt (2009), Prahar, Kagar (2024–26), Black Forest in the Karregutta hills on the Chhattisgarh–Telangana border (April–May 2025).
- 21 May 2025 — CPI (Maoist) general secretary Nambala Keshava Rao ("Basavaraju") killed in Abujhmarh, Narayanpur.
- By the 31 March 2026 target date the Home Minister told Parliament that of 21 central committee/politburo members, 12 had been killed, 7 had surrendered and one arrested; surrenders in Chhattisgarh rose from 736 (2024) to 1,573 (2025).
- Development tools: Security Related Expenditure scheme, Special Central Assistance for most-affected districts, Road Requirement Plan, mobile towers, Eklavya schools, Chhattisgarh's "Niyad Nellanar" scheme for villages near security camps.
- Debates: PESA and Forest Rights Act implementation, displacement by mining, and human-rights concerns over encounters.

### J&K and cross-border terrorism

- Triggers: disputed 1987 assembly election; Dec 1989 kidnapping of Rubaiya Sayeed; 1990 exodus of Kashmiri Pandits.
- 13 Dec 2001 Parliament attack → Operation Parakram; 2016 Uri attack → surgical strikes (29 Sept 2016).
- Oct 2024 — first assembly election since 2014; Omar Abdullah's National Conference government in a UT set-up.
- Operation Mahadev (July 2025) killed the terrorists behind the Pahalgam attack.
- The Supreme Court (Dec 2023) asked for restoration of statehood "at the earliest"; the demand remains pending.
- Laws used: J&K Public Safety Act 1978, UAPA, NIA Act; Pakistan was removed from the FATF grey list in Oct 2022.

### Legal and institutional framework — debates

- Preventive detention under Art 22: NSA 1980 allows up to 12 months; Advisory Board review within three months.
- TADA (1985–95) lapsed; POTA (2002) repealed in 2004; MCOCA (1999) is a state law on organised crime.
- UAPA amendments 2004, 2008, 2012 and 2019 (individuals can be designated terrorists; NIA can seize property).
- NIA (2008); 2019 amendment covers offences against Indians abroad, human trafficking and cyber-terrorism; NSG (1984) is the "Black Cat" counter-terror force.
- Sedition (Sec 124A IPC) was kept in abeyance by the SC in S.G. Vombatkere (May 2022); the BNS replaced it with Sec 152.
- Kargil Review Committee (1999, K. Subrahmanyam) led to intelligence reforms and the Multi-Agency Centre; Prakash Singh (2006) gave seven directives on police reform.
- Art 355 obliges the Union to protect states against external aggression and internal disturbance — basis for deploying central forces.
