## Short notes

- Punjab Economic Survey 2025-26: agriculture and allied make up about 23% of GSVA; agriculture shrank by about 2.5% in 2025-26 because of the 2025 floods.
- About 83% of geographical area is net sown; cropping intensity is about 190%; nearly the whole net sown area is irrigated, mostly by tubewells (over 14 lakh).
- Rabi–kharif cycle: wheat (about 35 lakh ha) and paddy (about 31–32 lakh ha) dominate; other crops are cotton (south-west Malwa), maize (Kandi), sugarcane (Doaba/Gurdaspur), basmati, potato and vegetables.
- Food security role: Punjab historically supplies roughly one-third to nearly half of central-pool wheat and a large share of rice; the Centre set a 180 LMT paddy procurement target for Punjab for KMS 2026-27.
- Green Revolution (from 1966–67): HYV Mexican dwarf wheat (Kalyan Sona, PV-18), IR-8 rice, tubewells, fertilisers, MSP and assured procurement (FCI 1965; Punjab Mandi Board 1961). PAU Ludhiana (1962) led the research.
- Average operational holding is about 3.6 ha (Agriculture Census 2015-16), about three times the national 1.08 ha; tenancy and leasing are widespread.
- Problems: paddy–wheat monoculture, groundwater depletion, declining soil health, pesticide overuse (cotton whitefly 2015, pink bollworm), stagnant yields, farm debt and farmer suicides.
- Crop diversification: the Johl Committee reports (1986, 2002) urged shifting area from paddy to maize, pulses, oilseeds, fruits and vegetables; direct-seeded rice (DSR) gets an incentive of ₹1,500/acre (from 2022).
- Subsoil water law (2009) delays paddy transplanting to mid-June to save groundwater; short-duration varieties (e.g., PR-126) are promoted and sale of the long-duration Pusa-44 seed was banned in 2024.
- Stubble burning: paddy straw (around 20 million tonnes) is burnt each autumn; responses include Crop Residue Management machines (Happy Seeder, Super SMS, Super Seeder), ex-situ uses (biomass, CBG plants such as Lehragaga, Sangrur) and penalties.
- Horticulture: kinnow (Abohar–Fazilka belt; a King × Willow Leaf mandarin hybrid developed in California), guava, pear, litchi (Pathankot), seed potato (Jalandhar–Doaba), peas, honey.
- Marketing: a dense network of mandis and purchase centres; arhtiyas (commission agents); Rural Development Fund and market fee on procurement; strong MSP dependence for wheat and paddy.
- Animal husbandry: Punjab had the highest per capita milk availability in India in 2023-24 — 1,245 g/day against the national 471 g/day (NDDB).
- Breeds: Murrah and Nili-Ravi buffaloes (Nili-Ravi is native to the Punjab region), Sahiwal cattle, Holstein-Friesian crossbreds; Beetal goat (Gurdaspur–Amritsar).
- Dairy cooperatives: MILKFED (1973) markets the Verka brand; the Punjab Dairy Development Board runs training and breed improvement.
- Guru Angad Dev Veterinary and Animal Sciences University (GADVASU), Ludhiana (2005-06), leads livestock research; PAU covers crops.
- Fisheries: inland fish culture; saline-water shrimp (vannamei) farming in waterlogged south-western districts (Muktsar, Fazilka, Mansa, Bathinda).
- Policy debates: MSP legal guarantee, crop insurance, free farm power since 1997, and the need for a diversified, water-saving cropping pattern.

## Deep dive

### Latest numbers with year (beyond the brief)

- Economic Survey 2025-26 (released with the Budget on 8 Mar 2026): agriculture and allied = 22.77% of GSVA; grew 3.49% in 2024-25; projected −2.49% in 2025-26 (crop sub-sector hit by the 2025 floods).
- Share of national output 2024-25: 15.18% of India's wheat and 9.57% of its rice from about 1.5% of India's area.
- Central pool (up to Dec 2025): Punjab supplied 39.68% of wheat and 31.42% of rice procured.
- MSP: wheat RMS 2026-27 ₹2,585/quintal; paddy KMS 2026-27 ₹2,441 (common) and ₹2,461 (Grade A), approved by the CCEA on 13 May 2026 (+₹72).
- Wheat procurement RMS 2026-27: about 123 lakh tonnes from Punjab against a target of 122 lakh tonnes (late May 2026); all-India about 330 lakh tonnes against a revised target of 345 lakh tonnes.
- Budget 2026-27 (PRS): agriculture and allied allocation ₹15,281 crore, of which ₹7,715 crore is free power to farmers; agriculture takes 9.2% of state spending against a 5.7% average for states.
- New horticulture push (Budget 2026-27): a ten-year JICA-assisted programme to raise the area under fruits and vegetables by 300% by 2035.

### From canal colonies to the Green Revolution — the long view

- Canal colonies (1885–1940) in western Punjab (Lyallpur, Montgomery, Shahpur) made the province India's granary; most went to Pakistan in 1947.
- Malcolm Darling's 'The Punjab Peasant in Prosperity and Debt' (1925) and H. Calvert's 'Wealth and Welfare of the Punjab' (1922) documented colonial agrarian prosperity alongside heavy debt.
- After Partition: consolidation of holdings under the East Punjab Holdings (Consolidation and Prevention of Fragmentation) Act 1948 — Punjab was the first state to complete it; land reforms and rural electrification followed.
- Foundations laid by Partap Singh Kairon: PAU Ludhiana (1962), link roads, regulated mandis, power for tubewells.
- Green Revolution package (from 1966-67): dwarf wheat (Kalyan Sona, PV-18), IR-8 rice, fertiliser, assured MSP and FCI procurement; M.S. Randhawa and N.E. Borlaug are the key names.
- Second-generation problems from the 1980s: paddy–wheat monoculture, falling water tables, soil fatigue, rising costs — diagnosed by the Johl Committee (1986, 2002).
- Farm protests: the 2020 farm laws were opposed from Punjab first; the year-long Delhi border protest ended with repeal (Nov 2021); the 2024–25 Shambhu–Khanauri border protest was cleared by Punjab police on 19 Mar 2025.

### Cropping pattern by belt

- Central plain: paddy (kharif) – wheat (rabi) rotation; basmati in Majha and parts of Doaba and Malwa for export.
- South-west Malwa (Bathinda, Mansa, Muktsar, Fazilka): cotton belt, badly hit by whitefly (2015) and pink bollworm; area under cotton has shrunk sharply; kinnow orchards around Abohar–Fazilka.
- Kandi and Doaba: maize, sugarcane (Gurdaspur, Hoshiarpur, Jalandhar), seed potato (Jalandhar–Hoshiarpur–Kapurthala), vegetables.
- Pathankot: litchi; Amritsar–Gurdaspur: pear and mango; Hoshiarpur: citrus and mango; Patiala–Sangrur: guava and peas.
- Spring (zaid) maize has expanded in Doaba but draws on groundwater in the hottest months — a new sustainability concern.
- Pulses and oilseeds are marginal despite diversification schemes, because MSP procurement is assured mainly for wheat and paddy.

### Stubble burning — data and responses

- Farm-fire counts (CAQM, season totals): 71,304 (2021) → 49,922 (2022) → 36,663 (2023) → 10,909 (2024) → 5,114 (2025).
- Monitoring: satellite detection by the Punjab Remote Sensing Centre and ISRO-based protocols; daily district reports during Sept–Nov.
- In-situ management: Happy Seeder, Super Seeder, Super SMS and surface seeder; machines subsidised under the central Crop Residue Management scheme (since 2018).
- Ex-situ uses: biomass power, paddy-straw pellets for co-firing in thermal plants (CAQM mandate), compressed biogas plants such as Lehragaga (Sangrur).
- Penalties: environmental compensation was doubled by central rules in Nov 2024 (₹5,000 below 2 acres, ₹10,000 for 2–5 acres, ₹30,000 above 5 acres); red entries in land records and FIRs were also used.
- Underlying cause: short window between paddy harvest and wheat sowing after the 2009 law pushed transplanting into June.

### Farm economy, debt and policy debates

- Average operational holding is among the largest of the major states, yet tenancy and leasing-in by small farmers are common.
- Debt: average outstanding loan per agricultural household ₹2,03,249 (NSO 77th round, 2019) — third highest after Andhra Pradesh and Kerala; national ₹74,121.
- Punjab's 2017 crop-loan waiver (up to ₹2 lakh for small and marginal farmers) and later relief to landless labourers eased but did not end debt stress.
- Payments: since the 2021 rabi season, MSP is paid directly into farmers' accounts, reducing the arhtiyas' financial role.
- Diversification tools: DSR incentive (₹1,500/acre), maize and cotton promotion, assured-procurement pilots for alternative crops, and the demand for a legal MSP guarantee.
- A draft State Agriculture Policy prepared in 2023 by an expert committee led by Dr Sukhpal Singh recommended diversification and water-saving incentives.

### Animal husbandry, dairy and fisheries — extra detail

- Punjab's per capita milk availability (1,245 g/day, 2023-24) is the highest in India — about 2.6 times the national level.
- Lumpy skin disease (2022) killed thousands of cattle in Punjab and exposed gaps in veterinary cover.
- Institutions: GADVASU (Ludhiana, 2005-06), Punjab Livestock Development Board, Punjab Dairy Development Board; MILKFED (Verka) for collection and marketing.
- Fisheries: inland ponds and saline-water shrimp (vannamei) farming in waterlogged south-western districts turn degraded land productive.
- Allied: beekeeping (honey from mustard and eucalyptus), poultry around Ludhiana and Mohali, and agro-forestry (poplar, eucalyptus) in Doaba.

### PPSC patterns and traps

- Production share (15% of wheat) ≠ procurement share (about 40% of central-pool wheat).
- Free farm power began in 1997; the Subsoil Water Act is of 2009; PAU was founded in 1962 and GADVASU in 2005-06.
- Kinnow is a hybrid (King × Willow Leaf) developed in California and brought to Abohar; it is not a native fruit.
- Stubble counts fell by over 90% between 2021 and 2025, but air quality in Delhi depends on many other sources too.
- MSP is announced by the Centre on CACP advice; Punjab's Mandi Board and agencies only procure — a common statement-type trap.
- Johl Committee years: 1986 (first) and 2002 (second).

**12.** Consider the following statements about paddy-straw management in Punjab:

**14.** Which long-duration paddy variety (about 140–145 days) has Punjab banned from sale and sowing to save groundwater?

(a) Pusa-44  
(b) PR-131  
(c) PR-126  
(d) PR-121

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Pusa-44** — Pusa-44 matures in about 143 days and needs more irrigation, so Punjab banned its seed sale (2024, repeated for 2025). PR-126, PR-131 and PR-121 are PAU short- or medium-duration varieties the state promotes instead. (The Tribune, Apr 2025)

</details>

**15.** The incentive Punjab introduced in 2022 for farmers who adopt direct seeding of rice (DSR) is:

(a) ₹1,500 per hectare  
(b) ₹2,500 per acre  
(c) ₹1,500 per acre  
(d) ₹1,000 per acre

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) ₹1,500 per acre** — Punjab pays ₹1,500 per acre for DSR, which skips the puddling and flooded nursery of transplanted paddy and saves water and labour. Note the unit: the incentive is per acre, not per hectare. (Punjab Agriculture Department, 2022)

</details>

**16.** Free electricity for agricultural tubewells in Punjab was introduced in:

(a) 2009  
(b) 1987  
(c) 1997  
(d) 2002

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) 1997** — Free farm power began in 1997 and, with assured procurement of paddy, drove heavy groundwater pumping; in Budget 2026-27 the farm power subsidy is ₹7,715 crore. The year 2009 is that of the Subsoil Water Act, a common confusion. (PRS, Punjab Budget Analysis 2026-27)

</details>
