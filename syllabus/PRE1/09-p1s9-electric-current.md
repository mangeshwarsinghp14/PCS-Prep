## Short notes

- Electric current is the rate of flow of charge, I = Q/t; unit ampere; conventional current flows opposite to electron flow; 1 coulomb is the charge of about 6.25 x 10^18 electrons.
- Potential difference (volt) drives current; voltmeter is connected in parallel, ammeter in series.
- Ohm's law: V = IR at constant temperature; resistance (ohm) R = rho L/A - rises with length, falls with cross-section area, depends on material and temperature.
- Silver is the best conductor; copper and aluminium are used for wires; alloys like nichrome have high resistivity and do not oxidise easily, so they are used in heaters, irons and toasters.
- Bulb filaments are made of tungsten (very high melting point, about 3,400 C) and bulbs are filled with inert gases like argon or nitrogen to prolong filament life.
- Series: R = R1 + R2 + ..., same current; parallel: 1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + ..., same voltage. Household wiring is parallel so each appliance gets full voltage and works independently.
- Joule's law of heating: H = I^2 R t; used in heaters, electric irons, kettles, incandescent bulbs and fuses.
- A fuse is a low-melting wire connected in series in the live wire; it melts on overload or short circuit; MCBs do the same job automatically.
- Power P = VI = I^2 R = V^2/R (watt); commercial unit 1 kWh ('one unit') = 3.6 x 10^6 J.
- Indian domestic supply is AC at 220-230 V and 50 Hz (USA about 120 V, 60 Hz); wires: live (red/brown), neutral (black/blue), earth (green); earthing protects against shocks from leaking current.
- A bird on a single live wire is safe because there is no potential difference across its body.
- Oersted (1820) showed a current produces a magnetic field; right-hand thumb rule gives field direction; a current-carrying solenoid with soft iron core is an electromagnet.
- Fleming's left-hand rule gives force on a current-carrying conductor - basis of the electric motor (electrical to mechanical energy).
- Faraday's electromagnetic induction: a changing magnetic field induces current - basis of the generator (mechanical to electrical; Fleming's right-hand rule).
- Transformers step voltage up or down and work only on AC; power is transmitted at high voltage to cut I^2R losses.
- DC sources: cells and batteries (Volta made the first battery); lithium-ion batteries power phones and EVs (Chemistry Nobel 2019).
- Superconductors have zero resistance below a critical temperature (discovered by Kamerlingh Onnes, 1911, in mercury); semiconductors (silicon, germanium) underlie diodes, transistors and LEDs.
- LEDs use far less power than incandescent bulbs; the UJALA scheme (2015) distributed LED bulbs at low cost to cut electricity demand.

## Deep dive

### Circuit concepts and quick numericals

- Resistivity: silver about 1.6 x 10^-8 ohm-m, copper about 1.7 x 10^-8, nichrome about 1 x 10^-6 (roughly 60 times copper).
- Metals' resistance rises with temperature; semiconductors' falls — used in thermistors for temperature sensing.
- n equal resistors R give nR in series and R/n in parallel; Kirchhoff's junction law (charge conservation) and loop law (energy conservation) solve networks.
- Bulb rating: R = V^2/P, so a 220 V, 100 W bulb has about 484 ohm. In series the lower-wattage (higher-resistance) bulb glows brighter; in parallel the higher-wattage bulb glows brighter.
- Bill example: five 9 W LED bulbs used 6 hours a day for 30 days use 5 x 9 x 6 x 30 = 8,100 Wh = 8.1 units.
- A galvanometer becomes an ammeter with a low resistance (shunt) in parallel, and a voltmeter with a high resistance in series.
- Three effects of current: heating (Joule), magnetic (Oersted) and chemical (electrolysis — electroplating, copper refining, anodising; Faraday's laws of electrolysis, 1833–34).

### Electromagnetism and devices

- Electromagnets lift scrap, ring electric bells, switch relays and power MRI scanners and maglev trains.
- A DC motor uses a split-ring commutator; an AC generator uses slip rings; turbines in thermal, hydro and wind plants all spin generators.
- Transformer ratio Vs/Vp = Ns/Np; power stations step voltage up to 400–765 kV for transmission and substations step it down to 230 V; laminated cores cut eddy-current loss.
- Eddy currents are used in induction cooktops, electromagnetic brakes and metal detectors.
- An inverter converts DC to AC, a rectifier (diodes) AC to DC; phone chargers are switched-mode power supplies.
- HVDC lines cut losses over long distances and link grids — e.g. the ±800 kV Champa (Chhattisgarh)–Kurukshetra (Haryana) line.
- 'One Nation, One Grid' was achieved in December 2013 when the southern grid was synchronised with the rest of India.
- Safety: MCBs trip on overload or short circuit; RCCB/ELCB devices trip on leakage current (about 30 mA) and prevent shocks; the earth pin of a three-pin plug is longer and thicker so it connects first.
- Shock severity depends on current through the body; wet skin lowers resistance, so monsoon shocks from tubewell motors are common.

### Semiconductors, batteries and modern electricity

- Doping silicon with phosphorus or arsenic gives n-type, with boron, gallium or indium p-type material; a p–n junction is a diode.
- LEDs emit light when electrons and holes recombine; efficient blue LEDs (Nobel 2014: Akasaki, Amano, Nakamura) made white LED lighting possible.
- The transistor (1947, Bardeen, Brattain and Shockley) works as an amplifier or switch; billions sit on one chip today.
- Lithium-ion batteries (Nobel Chemistry 2019: Goodenough, Whittingham, Yoshino) dominate phones and EVs; India's PLI for Advanced Chemistry Cell batteries (2021) targets 50 GWh of capacity; sodium-ion and solid-state batteries are emerging.
- Fuel cells turn hydrogen into electricity and water; the National Green Hydrogen Mission (January 2023) targets 5 million tonnes of green hydrogen a year by 2030.
- PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana (2024) subsidises rooftop solar for 1 crore households, offering up to 300 free units a month.
- Superconductors show zero resistance and expel magnetic fields (Meissner effect); used in MRI magnets, maglev and particle accelerators.
- Electrostatic precipitators remove fly ash from thermal-plant chimneys; lightning conductors (Benjamin Franklin) channel strikes safely to earth.
- Lightning kills a few thousand Indians a year (NCRB); the Damini app of IITM Pune warns users of nearby strikes.

### Indian power sector — facts with year

- Non-fossil sources crossed half of India's installed capacity in 2025, but coal still generates roughly three-quarters of electricity units.
- Electricity (Rights of Consumers) Rules, 2020 set timelines for new connections, reliable supply and compensation, and enable rooftop net-metering.
- Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (2021) funds smart prepaid meters and loss reduction, targeting AT&C losses of 12–15%.
- UJALA (2015) distributed over 36 crore LED bulbs through EESL; BEE runs star labels under the Energy Conservation Act 2001 (amended 2022 to create a carbon credit trading scheme).
- Budget 2025-26 set up a Nuclear Energy Mission with Rs 20,000 crore for small modular reactors and a goal of 100 GW nuclear capacity by 2047.
- Indigenous 700 MW PHWRs (first at Kakrapar, Gujarat) form the core of India's nuclear expansion.

### Punjab links

- Punjab gives domestic consumers 300 free units a month (from July 2022) and free power to farm tubewells — a large subsidy that shapes PSPCL's finances.
- PSPCL's captive Pachhwara Central coal mine in Jharkhand resumed production in December 2022, improving fuel security for state plants.
- Paddy-season peak demand in Punjab has crossed 16,000 MW in recent years, driven by tubewells and air-conditioning.
- Mohali is Punjab's chip hub: the government Semi-Conductor Laboratory (SCL) and Continental Device India Ltd (CDIL), whose Rs 117 crore expansion for high-power discrete devices (MOSFETs, IGBTs, Schottky diodes in silicon and silicon carbide) was approved under the India Semiconductor Mission in August 2025.
- Punjab Energy Development Agency (PEDA) promotes rooftop solar, canal-top solar and paddy-straw biomass power.
- Monsoon electrocution from tubewell motors and loose wiring is a recurring rural hazard — RCCB/ELCB adoption is the remedy.

## Practice MCQs (12)

**1.** The heating element of an electric iron is usually made of:

(a) Tungsten  
(b) Nichrome  
(c) Copper  
(d) Aluminium

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Nichrome** — Nichrome has high resistivity and resists oxidation at high temperature.

</details>

**2.** Household appliances are connected in parallel because:

(a) The total resistance of the circuit increases  
(b) The same current flows through each appliance  
(c) It reduces the electricity bill of the house  
(d) Each gets full voltage and works independently

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Each gets full voltage and works independently** — Parallel connection keeps voltage the same across each branch.

</details>

**3.** A 1,000 W heater used for 3 hours a day for 30 days consumes:

(a) 90 units  
(b) 30 units  
(c) 9 units  
(d) 900 units

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 90 units** — Energy = 1 kW x 3 h x 30 = 90 kWh = 90 units.

</details>

**4.** Consider: 1. A transformer can step up DC voltage. 2. An electric motor works on Fleming's left-hand rule. Which is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** — Motors use the force on a current in a magnetic field; transformers need changing flux, so they work only with AC.

</details>

**5.** The principle of electromagnetic induction was discovered by:

(a) Hans Christian Oersted  
(b) Michael Faraday  
(c) Georg Simon Ohm  
(d) Alessandro Volta

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Michael Faraday** — Faraday (1831) showed that a changing magnetic field induces current - the basis of generators.

</details>

**6.** A fuse wire should have:

(a) Low melting point and is connected in series  
(b) High melting point and is connected in parallel  
(c) High melting point and is connected in series  
(d) Low resistance and is connected in parallel

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Low melting point and is connected in series** — It must melt quickly on excess current and sit in series with the live wire.

</details>

**7.** Consider the following statements:  
1. When a 60 W and a 100 W bulb (both rated 220 V) are joined in series to the mains, the 60 W bulb glows brighter.  
2. The resistance of a metal wire decreases as its temperature rises.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 1 only** — In series the same current flows, so the higher-resistance 60 W bulb dissipates more power; metal resistance rises with temperature.

</details>

**8.** Consider the following statements:  
1. An RCCB trips when it detects leakage current to earth.  
2. An MCB protects against overload and short circuit.  
3. The earth pin of a three-pin plug is longer and thicker than the others.  
Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only  
(b) 2 and 3 only  
(c) 1 and 3 only  
(d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) 1, 2 and 3** — All three describe standard safety features.

</details>

**9.** The hot resistance of a 220 V, 100 W bulb is about:

(a) 484 ohm  
(b) 22 ohm  
(c) 2.2 ohm  
(d) 4,840 ohm

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 484 ohm** — For a bulb, R = V^2/P = 220^2/100 = 484 ohm at its working temperature (hot). 4,840 ohm is a decimal slip, and the cold resistance is much lower than the hot value.

</details>

**10.** Adding which element to silicon produces an n-type semiconductor?

(a) Phosphorus  
(b) Indium  
(c) Gallium  
(d) Boron

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Phosphorus** — Pentavalent dopants such as phosphorus add free electrons.

</details>

**11.** Electrostatic precipitators in thermal power plants are used to:

(a) Step up generator voltage  
(b) Cool the condenser steam  
(c) Remove fly ash from flue gases  
(d) Raise boiler pressure

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Remove fly ash from flue gases** — Charged plates attract and collect dust particles.

</details>

**12.** Which Indian app gives warnings of lightning strikes?

(a) Mausam  
(b) Damini  
(c) Meghdoot  
(d) Umang

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Damini** — Damini, developed by the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM), Pune, alerts users to lightning near their location. Meghdoot gives agro-weather advice and Mausam gives general weather, so they are not lightning apps.

</details>
