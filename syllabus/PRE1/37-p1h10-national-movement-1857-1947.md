## Short notes

- Early associations: Landholders' Society (1838), British Indian Association (1851), East India Association (London, 1866, Dadabhai Naoroji), Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870), Indian Association (1876, S.N. Banerjea), Madras Mahajan Sabha (1884).
- INC founded on 28 December 1885 at Bombay with A.O. Hume's help; first president W.C. Bonnerjee, 72 delegates. Moderates (1885–1905) used petitions; Naoroji's 'drain theory' exposed economic exploitation.
- Partition of Bengal by Curzon (announced July 1905, effective 16 October 1905) sparked the Swadeshi and Boycott movement; annulled in 1911 when the capital moved to Delhi.
- Muslim League founded at Dacca (30 December 1906); Congress split at Surat (1907) between Moderates and Extremists (Lal-Bal-Pal); Morley-Minto Reforms (1909) gave Muslims separate electorates.
- Revolutionaries: Chapekar brothers (1897), Anushilan Samiti, Khudiram Bose (1908); Ghadar Party (1913, San Francisco; Sohan Singh Bhakna, Lala Har Dayal); Komagata Maru (1914).
- Home Rule Leagues (1916) of Tilak and Annie Besant; Lucknow Pact (1916) united Congress and League and reunited Moderates and Extremists; Montagu Declaration (August 1917).
- Gandhi returned on 9 January 1915; early satyagrahas: Champaran (1917, indigo), Ahmedabad mill strike (1918, his first hunger strike), Kheda (1918, revenue).
- Government of India Act 1919 brought dyarchy in provinces; Rowlatt Act (March 1919) led to the Rowlatt Satyagraha and the Jallianwala Bagh massacre (13 April 1919) under General Dyer.
- Khilafat and Non-Cooperation Movement (launched 1 August 1920, approved at Calcutta and Nagpur in 1920) was withdrawn after Chauri Chaura (5 February 1922).
- Swaraj Party (1923, C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru); Simon Commission (appointed 1927, all-white) boycotted — Lala Lajpat Rai died after a lathi-charge at Lahore (1928); Nehru Report (1928).
- Revolutionary wave: HRA (1924, Kakori 1925), HSRA (1928) of Bhagat Singh and Chandrashekhar Azad; Saunders killed (1928), Assembly bomb (8 April 1929); Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev hanged on 23 March 1931.
- Lahore Congress (December 1929, Jawaharlal Nehru president) adopted Purna Swaraj; 26 January 1930 was observed as Independence Day.
- Civil Disobedience: Dandi March (12 March–6 April 1930); Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931); Gandhi at the Second Round Table Conference (1931); Karachi session (1931) resolution on Fundamental Rights and economic policy.
- Communal Award (1932) gave separate electorates to depressed classes; the Poona Pact (September 1932) between Gandhi and Ambedkar replaced it with reserved seats in joint electorates.
- Government of India Act 1935: provincial autonomy, proposed federation (never formed); Congress formed ministries in most provinces after the 1937 elections and resigned in 1939 over India's entry into WWII.
- 1940s: Lahore Resolution of the Muslim League (23 March 1940), August Offer (1940), Individual Satyagraha (1940, Vinoba Bhave first), Cripps Mission (1942).
- Quit India: resolution of 8 August 1942 at Bombay, 'Do or Die'; INA under Subhas Chandra Bose (Azad Hind government, Singapore, 21 October 1943); INA trials at Red Fort (1945) and RIN mutiny (February 1946).
- Road to freedom: Shimla Conference (1945), Cabinet Mission (1946), Direct Action Day (16 August 1946), Interim Government (2 September 1946), Attlee's statement (20 February 1947), Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947), Indian Independence Act (July 1947), independence on 15 August 1947.

## Punjab angle

- Kuka (Namdhari) movement under Baba Ram Singh opposed British rule and cow slaughter; Kukas were blown from cannons at Malerkotla in 1872. Ajit Singh led 'Pagri Sambhal Jatta' (1907) against the Punjab Colonization Bill.
- Ghadar movement was led mostly by Punjabi emigrants; Kartar Singh Sarabha was hanged in 1915; the Komagata Maru passengers were fired upon at Budge Budge (1914).
- Jallianwala Bagh (Amritsar, 1919) turned Indian opinion against British rule; Udham Singh shot Michael O'Dwyer in London on 13 March 1940 and was hanged that year.
- Akali (Gurdwara Reform) movement — Nankana Sahib (1921), Guru ka Bagh (1922), Jaito morcha — led to the Sikh Gurdwaras Act 1925; Lala Lajpat Rai ('Punjab Kesari') and Bhagat Singh (born at Banga, Lyallpur) are Punjab's icons.

## Deep dive

### INC sessions to know (year — place — president — significance)

- 1885 Bombay — W.C. Bonnerjee (72 delegates); 1886 Calcutta — Dadabhai Naoroji; 1887 Madras — Badruddin Tyabji (first Muslim president); 1888 Allahabad — George Yule (first English president).
- 1893 Lahore — Dadabhai Naoroji; 1896 Calcutta — R.M. Sayani ('Vande Mataram' first sung, by Tagore); 1900 Lahore — N.G. Chandavarkar.
- 1905 Benares — G.K. Gokhale (Swadeshi supported); 1906 Calcutta — Naoroji ('Swaraj' as the goal); 1907 Surat — Rash Behari Ghosh (split); 1909 Lahore — Madan Mohan Malaviya.
- 1911 Calcutta — B.N. Dhar ('Jana Gana Mana' first sung); 1916 Lucknow — A.C. Mazumdar (Lucknow Pact; Moderates–Extremists reunited); 1917 Calcutta — Annie Besant (first woman president).
- 1919 Amritsar — Motilal Nehru (after Jallianwala); 1920 Calcutta special — Lala Lajpat Rai (Non-Cooperation adopted); 1920 Nagpur — C. Vijayaraghavachariar (NCM ratified; linguistic Pradesh Congress Committees).
- 1922 Gaya — C.R. Das (Swarajist split); 1923 Delhi special — Abul Kalam Azad (youngest president); 1924 Belgaum — M.K. Gandhi (his only presidency); 1925 Kanpur — Sarojini Naidu (first Indian woman president).
- 1927 Madras — M.A. Ansari (independence resolution, Simon boycott); 1928 Calcutta — Motilal Nehru (Nehru Report, dominion status ultimatum); 1929 Lahore — Jawaharlal Nehru (Purna Swaraj; 26 January 1930 Independence Day).
- 1931 Karachi — Vallabhbhai Patel (Fundamental Rights and Economic Policy resolution; endorsed Gandhi–Irwin Pact); 1934 Bombay — Rajendra Prasad.
- 1936 Lucknow and Faizpur — Jawaharlal Nehru (Faizpur the first rural session); 1938 Haripura and 1939 Tripuri — Subhas Bose (resigned 1939; Rajendra Prasad took over); 1940 Ramgarh — Abul Kalam Azad; 1946 Meerut — J.B. Kripalani.
- No regular sessions were held 1941–45; Azad remained president through the Quit India and Simla Conference years.

### Viceroys 1858–1947 (name — years — landmark)

- Canning (1856–62) — Indian Councils Act 1861, portfolio system, IPC (1860); Elgin I (1862–63); John Lawrence (1864–69) — Bhutan war, Punjab Tenancy Act 1868; Mayo (1869–72) — financial decentralisation, first (non-synchronous) census 1872, assassinated in the Andamans.
- Northbrook (1872–76); Lytton (1876–80) — Delhi Durbar 1877, Vernacular Press Act and Arms Act 1878, ICS age cut to 19, Second Afghan War; Ripon (1880–84) — VPA repealed, local self-government resolution 1882, Hunter Commission, Ilbert Bill (1883), first synchronous census 1881, Factory Act 1881.
- Dufferin (1884–88) — INC founded, Third Burmese War; Lansdowne (1888–94) — Indian Councils Act 1892, Durand Line (1893); Elgin II (1894–99) — famine and plague, Chapekar brothers' killing of Rand (1897).
- Curzon (1899–1905) — Universities Act 1904, Ancient Monuments Preservation Act 1904, NWFP (1901), Police Commission, Tibet expedition, Partition of Bengal (1905).
- Minto II (1905–10) — Muslim League (1906), Surat split (1907), Morley–Minto reforms (1909); Hardinge II (1910–16) — Delhi Durbar 1911, partition annulled, capital to Delhi, bomb attack (1912), Ghadar and Komagata Maru.
- Chelmsford (1916–21) — Home Rule, Montagu Declaration, Rowlatt Act, Jallianwala Bagh, GoI Act 1919, Non-Cooperation begins; Reading (1921–26) — Chauri Chaura, Moplah rebellion, Rowlatt Act repealed (1922), Kakori case (1925).
- Irwin (1926–31) — Simon Commission, 'Deepavali Declaration' (1929), Dandi March, First RTC, Gandhi–Irwin Pact (March 1931); Willingdon (1931–36) — Second and Third RTC, Communal Award and Poona Pact (1932), GoI Act 1935.
- Linlithgow (1936–43, longest tenure) — 1937 elections, August Offer (1940), Cripps Mission (1942), Quit India, Bengal famine; Wavell (1943–47) — Simla Conference (1945), INA trials, RIN mutiny, Cabinet Mission (1946); Mountbatten (March–August 1947) — 3 June Plan, Independence.

### Constitutional Acts 1861–1947 (Act — key provisions)

- Indian Councils Act 1861 — Indians nominated to the Viceroy's legislative council; portfolio system; ordinance power; legislative powers restored to Bombay and Madras.
- Indian Councils Act 1892 — indirect (recommended) election, right to discuss the budget and ask questions.
- Indian Councils Act 1909 (Morley–Minto) — enlarged councils, separate electorates for Muslims; Satyendra Prasanna Sinha first Indian on the Viceroy's Executive Council.
- Government of India Act 1919 (Montagu–Chelmsford) — dyarchy in provinces (transferred vs reserved subjects), bicameral central legislature, direct elections on limited franchise, communal electorates extended to Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans; Public Service Commission (1926); review after 10 years (Simon).
- Government of India Act 1935 — all-India federation (never formed), provincial autonomy, dyarchy at the centre, three lists, Federal Court (1937), bicameral legislatures in six provinces, Burma separated (1937), Sind and Orissa new provinces (1936).
- Indian Independence Act 1947 (assented 18 July) — two dominions from 15 August 1947, paramountcy lapses, Constituent Assemblies act as legislatures, office of Secretary of State abolished.

### Mass movements — numbers and details that win marks

- Non-Cooperation (1920–22): boycott of councils, courts, schools; national schools (Jamia Millia, Kashi Vidyapith, Gujarat Vidyapith); Tilak Swaraj Fund over Rs 1 crore; called off after Chauri Chaura (5 February 1922).
- Civil Disobedience: Dandi March 12 March–6 April 1930 with 78 satyagrahis (about 385 km); Dharasana salt works raid (Sarojini Naidu); Khudai Khidmatgars (Abdul Ghaffar Khan) at Peshawar — Garhwali soldiers refused to fire (Chandra Singh Garhwali).
- Gandhi–Irwin Pact (5 March 1931) suspended CDM; Gandhi attended the Second RTC alone for the Congress; CDM resumed 1932, withdrawn 1934.
- Individual Satyagraha (October 1940): Vinoba Bhave first, Nehru second — against forced war participation.
- Quit India (8 August 1942, Gowalia Tank, 'Do or Die'): parallel governments at Ballia (Chittu Pandey), Tamluk (Jatiya Sarkar) and Satara (Prati Sarkar, Nana Patil); underground radio by Usha Mehta.
- INA (Azad Hind Fauj): first formed by Captain Mohan Singh (1942), reorganised by Subhas Bose (1943, Singapore; Provisional Government 21 October 1943); Red Fort trials (November 1945) of Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Sahgal and Gurbakhsh Singh Dhillon.
- RIN mutiny (February 1946, Bombay); Cabinet Mission (May 1946) three-tier grouping plan; Direct Action Day (16 August 1946); Interim Government (2 September 1946).

### Punjab in the freedom struggle — detailed chronology

- 1872 — Kuka (Namdhari) rebels blown from guns at Malerkotla; Baba Ram Singh exiled to Rangoon; 1877 — Arya Samaj Lahore; 1881 — The Tribune founded by Dyal Singh Majithia; 1894 — Punjab National Bank (Lahore) with Lajpat Rai's support.
- 1907 — 'Pagri Sambhal Jatta' agitation against the Colonization Bill and canal-rate hike; Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh deported to Mandalay; the bill was vetoed by Minto.
- 1913 — Hindustan Ghadar Party, Astoria/San Francisco (Sohan Singh Bhakna president, Lala Har Dayal); 'Ghadar' paper; 1915 Lahore Conspiracy Case — Kartar Singh Sarabha hanged (16 November 1915).
- 1914 — Komagata Maru (chartered by Gurdit Singh, 376 passengers) turned back from Vancouver; firing at Budge Budge (29 September) killed about 20.
- 1919 — Rowlatt protests; arrest of Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr Satyapal; 13 April Jallianwala Bagh (Dyer); martial law, 'crawling order'; Hunter Committee; Tagore renounced his knighthood; Udham Singh shot O'Dwyer on 13 March 1940 (hanged 31 July 1940).
- 1920–25 — Gurdwara Reform: SGPC (November 1920), Akali Dal (December 1920), Nankana Sahib massacre (20 February 1921), Keys affair (1921–22), Guru ka Bagh (1922), Jaito morcha (1923–25) → Sikh Gurdwaras Act 1925; Babbar Akalis (1921–24) took the violent path.
- 1926 — Naujawan Bharat Sabha (Bhagat Singh, Bhagwati Charan Vohra); 1928 — HSRA at Feroz Shah Kotla; 30 October 1928 — Lajpat Rai injured in the anti-Simon lathi-charge at Lahore (died 17 November); 17 December — Saunders shot.
- 8 April 1929 — Bhagat Singh and B.K. Dutt bomb the Central Legislative Assembly; 23 March 1931 — Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru hanged at Lahore; their memorial is at Hussainiwala (Ferozepur).
- Princely Punjab: Riasti Praja Mandal (1928); Sewa Singh Thikriwala died in Patiala jail (1935); Kirti Kisan Party (1927–28) and Kisan Sabhas spread left politics.
- Unionist Party (Fazl-i-Husain, Chhotu Ram, Sikandar Hayat Khan) ruled Punjab 1937–46; Lahore Resolution (23 March 1940); Partition — Radcliffe Award made public on 17 August 1947.

### Historiography of Indian nationalism (school — argument)

- Nationalist (Tara Chand, R.C. Majumdar, early Bipan Chandra) — a people's struggle against colonial exploitation led by the INC.
- Cambridge school (Anil Seal, John Gallagher, Gordon Johnson) — nationalism as competition among Indian elites for colonial patronage and power.
- Marxist (R. Palme Dutt, A.R. Desai) — bourgeois leadership restraining mass radicalism.
- Subaltern Studies (Ranajit Guha, Shahid Amin's 'Gandhi as Mahatma') — autonomous peasant and worker politics beyond elite control.
- Bipan Chandra's 'Struggle–Truce–Struggle' (S–T–S) — Gandhian strategy alternated mass phases with periods of constructive work; the movement as the 'largest mass movement in history'.
- Use: in Mains, cite at least two schools briefly before giving your balanced view.

### PPSC-style asks and traps

- First session of INC planned at Poona, shifted to Bombay because of a cholera outbreak; Hume was general secretary.
- Annie Besant (1917) = first woman president; Sarojini Naidu (1925) = first Indian woman president; Nellie Sengupta (1933) also presided.
- Fundamental Rights resolution = Karachi 1931 (Patel), not Lahore 1929.
- Jallianwala Bagh firing: General Reginald Dyer (not Michael O'Dwyer, the Lt Governor); Udham Singh killed O'Dwyer, not Dyer.
- Komagata Maru (1914) is separate from the Ghadar rising (February 1915), though linked by passengers and anger.
- Lajpat Rai died after the Simon protest lathi-charge at Lahore (1928) — not at the Jallianwala protests.
- Cripps Mission (1942) offered dominion status after the war; Cabinet Mission (1946) rejected Pakistan as a sovereign state but grouped provinces.

**1.** Who presided over the first session of the Indian National Congress (1885)?

(a) A.O. Hume  
(b) Dadabhai Naoroji  
(c) W.C. Bonnerjee  
(d) Surendranath Banerjea

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) W.C. Bonnerjee** — W.C. Bonnerjee presided over the Bombay session of December 1885.

</details>

**2.** The Purna Swaraj resolution was adopted at the Congress session of:

(a) Lucknow, 1916  
(b) Calcutta, 1928  
(c) Karachi, 1931  
(d) Lahore, 1929

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Lahore, 1929** — The Lahore session under Jawaharlal Nehru declared complete independence as the goal.

</details>

**3.** The Poona Pact (1932) was signed between Gandhi and:

(a) Lord Irwin  
(b) M.A. Jinnah  
(c) B.R. Ambedkar  
(d) Ramsay MacDonald

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) B.R. Ambedkar** — The Poona Pact replaced separate electorates for depressed classes with reserved seats.

</details>

**4.** The Chauri Chaura incident (1922) led Gandhi to withdraw the:

(a) Non-Cooperation Movement  
(b) Quit India Movement  
(c) Individual Satyagraha  
(d) Civil Disobedience Movement

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Non-Cooperation Movement** — After the violence at Chauri Chaura, Gandhi called off Non-Cooperation in February 1922.

</details>

**5.** Consider the statements: 1. The Ghadar Party was founded in San Francisco in 1913. 2. The Morley-Minto Reforms introduced dyarchy in the provinces. Which is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 1 only** — Ghadar was founded in 1913 on the US Pacific coast; dyarchy came with the 1919 Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms.

</details>

**6.** Arrange in chronological order: Rowlatt Satyagraha, Dandi March, Cripps Mission, Cabinet Mission.

(a) Rowlatt Satyagraha, Dandi March, Cabinet Mission, Cripps Mission  
(b) Rowlatt Satyagraha, Dandi March, Cripps Mission, Cabinet Mission  
(c) Rowlatt Satyagraha, Cripps Mission, Dandi March, Cabinet Mission  
(d) Dandi March, Rowlatt Satyagraha, Cripps Mission, Cabinet Mission

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Rowlatt Satyagraha, Dandi March, Cripps Mission, Cabinet Mission** — The order is Rowlatt Satyagraha (1919), Dandi March (1930), Cripps Mission (1942), Cabinet Mission (1946). The tempting error is swapping Cripps and Cabinet Mission, but Cripps came during the 1942 war crisis and the Cabinet Mission after the war.

</details>
