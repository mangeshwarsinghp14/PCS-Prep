## Short notes

- Planning approaches: sectoral planning (by sector like agriculture, industry) and regional planning (to reduce regional imbalance); 'target area' and 'target group' programmes.
- Target area programmes: Command Area Development (1974-75), Drought Prone Areas Programme (Fourth Plan), Desert Development Programme, Hill Area Development Programme (Fifth Plan).
- NCERT case study - Bharmaur tribal region (Chamba, Himachal Pradesh, home of the Gaddis): Integrated Tribal Development Project from 1974 improved roads, schools, health and literacy.
- NCERT case study - Indira Gandhi Canal command area (Rajasthan): canal starts at Harike barrage (Punjab) at the Sutlej-Beas confluence, built from 1958 in two stages; brought irrigation but also waterlogging and salinity.
- Sustainable development: Club of Rome 'Limits to Growth' (1972); Brundtland Report 'Our Common Future' (1987) defined meeting present needs without compromising future generations; Rio Earth Summit (1992); SDGs (2015).
- India's sustainability framework: National Action Plan on Climate Change (2008, eight missions), Mission LiFE (Lifestyle for Environment), Panchamrit (COP26) and updated NDC (2022) - cut emissions intensity by 45% from 2005 levels by 2030; net zero by 2070.
- Roads: India has the world's second-largest road network; Golden Quadrilateral (5,846 km) links Delhi-Mumbai-Chennai-Kolkata; North-South (Srinagar-Kanyakumari) and East-West (Silchar-Porbandar) corridors; Bharatmala (2017); PMGSY (2000) for villages.
- Historic road: Sher Shah Suri's Shahi (Grand Trunk) Road linked Bengal to Peshawar; today largely NH-19/NH-44 route via Punjab.
- Railways: first passenger train Bombay-Thane, 16 April 1853 (34 km); gauges broad (1.676 m), metre (1 m), narrow; Project Unigauge; Eastern (Punjab-Bihar) and Western (Dadri-JNPT) freight corridors; Vande Bharat (2019).
- Waterways: National Waterways Act 2016 declared 111 NWs; NW-1 Ganga (Prayagraj-Haldia), NW-2 Brahmaputra (Sadiya-Dhubri), NW-3 West Coast Canal (Kerala); Sagarmala (2015) for port-led development.
- Ports: major ports include Deendayal (Kandla, tidal port), Mumbai, JNPT (largest container port), Mormugao, New Mangalore, Kochi, Chennai, V.O. Chidambaranar (Tuticorin), Visakhapatnam, Paradip, Kolkata-Haldia.
- Air transport: nationalised 1953; Airports Authority of India (1995); UDAN regional connectivity scheme (2016); India is among the world's largest domestic aviation markets.
- Pipelines: Naharkatia-Nunmati-Barauni crude pipeline (Assam-Bihar), Hazira-Vijaipur-Jagdishpur (HVJ) gas pipeline; National Gas Grid expanding.
- Communication: postal system and telegraph (1850s); telecom boom after 1991; Digital India (2015); UPI (2016); 5G launched 1 Oct 2022; satellites (INSAT/GSAT) support broadcasting and weather; AIR and Doordarshan as public broadcasters.
- International trade: export basket shifted from primary goods to engineering goods, petroleum products, gems and jewellery, pharmaceuticals, chemicals, electronics (smartphones) and services.
- Imports dominated by crude oil and petroleum, electronics, gold, coal, machinery and edible oils; the USA is the largest export destination and China the largest source of imports.
- Foreign Trade Policy 2023 (from 1 April 2023, no end date) targets US$2 trillion exports by 2030; sea ports carry about 95% of India's trade by volume and around 70% by value.

## Punjab angle

- The Indira Gandhi Canal begins at Harike barrage in Punjab (Sutlej-Beas confluence); the Harike wetland is a Ramsar site.
- The Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor starts at Sahnewal near Ludhiana and runs to Sonnagar in Bihar.
- Attari-Wagah is Punjab's land border trade point with Pakistan (Integrated Check Post closed in April 2025 after the Pahalgam attack); the Kartarpur Corridor (2019) links to Gurdwara Darbar Sahib in Pakistan.
- Punjab's international airports: Sri Guru Ram Dass Jee (Amritsar) and Shaheed Bhagat Singh (Mohali).

## Deep dive

### Planning in India — timeline (year — milestone)

- 1938 — National Planning Committee (chaired by Nehru); 1944 — Bombay Plan (industrialists), Gandhian Plan (S.N. Agarwal) and People's Plan (M.N. Roy).
- 15 March 1950 — Planning Commission set up; 1952 — National Development Council; First Plan (1951–56) used the Harrod–Domar model, the Second (1956–61) the Mahalanobis model.
- 1966–69 — plan holiday (three annual plans); 1990–92 — two annual plans; the 12th Plan (2012–17) was the last.
- 1 January 2015 — NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission; plans gave way to vision and strategy documents and the Viksit Bharat @2047 goal.
- Regional programmes: Drought Prone Areas Programme (1973), Desert Development Programme (1977-78), Command Area Development (1974-75), Tribal Sub-Plan (1974-75), Backward Regions Grant Fund (2007).
- Aspirational Districts Programme (January 2018, 112 districts; Punjab's are Moga and Ferozepur) and Aspirational Blocks Programme (January 2023, 500 blocks).
- Sustainable development milestones: Stockholm (1972, UNEP created), Brundtland Report (1987), Rio Earth Summit (1992, Agenda 21), Johannesburg (2002), Rio+20 ('The Future We Want', 2012), SDGs (2015: 17 goals, 169 targets, to 2030).

### Roads and railways — mapping and data

- National highways were renumbered in 2010: north–south routes take odd numbers and east–west routes even numbers; NH-44 (Srinagar–Kanyakumari, through Punjab) is the longest.
- Bharatmala Pariyojana (2017) builds economic corridors and expressways — Delhi–Amritsar–Katra Expressway and the Amritsar–Jamnagar economic corridor touch Punjab.
- Tunnels: Atal Tunnel under Rohtang (9.02 km, 2020), Dr Syama Prasad Mookerjee (Chenani–Nashri) tunnel in J&K, Sela tunnel in Arunachal (2024); Zojila tunnel is under construction.
- Railways: the Udhampur–Srinagar–Baramulla rail link was completed in 2025 with the Chenab bridge (world's highest railway arch bridge, opened 6 June 2025) and Anji Khad (India's first cable-stayed rail bridge).
- New Pamban bridge (opened April 2025) is India's first vertical-lift sea bridge; Konkan Railway (1998) crosses the Western Ghats coast.
- Dedicated Freight Corridors: Eastern (Sahnewal, Ludhiana to Sonnagar, Bihar) and Western (Dadri to JNPA, Mumbai); the Mumbai–Ahmedabad high-speed rail uses Japanese Shinkansen technology.
- Kavach is the indigenous automatic train protection system (PPSC 2025 asked); Vande Bharat trains began in 2019.

### Ports, waterways and corridors — mapping

- Major ports (west to east): Deendayal/Kandla (tidal), Mumbai, JNPA/Nhava Sheva (largest container port), Mormugao (iron ore), New Mangalore, Cochin, V.O. Chidambaranar (Tuticorin), Chennai, Kamarajar/Ennore (first corporatised), Visakhapatnam (deep landlocked harbour), Paradip, Syama Prasad Mookerjee Port Kolkata–Haldia (riverine).
- Vadhavan (Maharashtra) was approved in 2024 as an all-weather deep-draft major port; Vizhinjam (Kerala) was commissioned in 2025 as India's first deep-water transshipment port; a transshipment port is planned at Galathea Bay under the Great Nicobar project.
- National Waterways Act 2016 declared 111 NWs: NW-1 Ganga–Bhagirathi–Hooghly (Haldia–Prayagraj), NW-2 Brahmaputra (Dhubri–Sadiya), NW-3 West Coast Canal (Kerala); the Ganga Vilas cruise (2023) was billed as the world's longest river cruise.
- Regional connectivity: Kaladan multimodal project (Kolkata–Sittwe–Mizoram), Chabahar port in Iran (10-year Indian operating contract signed May 2024), International North–South Transport Corridor (Mumbai–Bandar Abbas–Russia) and the India–Middle East–Europe Corridor (announced at the 2023 G20 in Delhi).
- Sagarmala (2015) promotes port-led development; the Major Port Authorities Act 2021 replaced the Major Port Trusts Act 1963.
- UDAN (2016) subsidises regional flights; Punjab has international airports at Amritsar (Sri Guru Ram Dass Jee) and Mohali (Shaheed Bhagat Singh), plus domestic airports at Adampur, Bathinda and Pathankot.

### International trade — framework and recent events

- Foreign Trade Policy 2023 (from 1 April 2023, no end date) targets US$2 trillion of exports by 2030 and promotes rupee settlement and e-commerce exports.
- Trade agreements: SAFTA (2006), ASEAN FTA (2010, under review), India–Mauritius CECPA (2021), India–UAE CEPA (May 2022), India–Australia ECTA (December 2022), India–EFTA TEPA (signed March 2024, in force 1 October 2025), India–UK CETA (signed July 2025).
- In August 2025 the USA imposed tariffs totalling 50% on many Indian goods (including a penalty linked to Russian oil purchases), pushing India to diversify markets and negotiate a bilateral deal.
- India runs a persistent merchandise trade deficit (oil, electronics, gold) but a large services surplus (IT and business services, global capability centres).
- WTO issues for India: public stockholding for food security (Bali 'peace clause', 2013), fisheries subsidies, e-commerce duties; the 14th Ministerial Conference was held at Yaoundé, Cameroon, in March 2026.
- NCERT terms to define: balance of trade versus balance of payments, terms of trade, entrepôt ports (Singapore, Rotterdam) and gateway ports.

### Communication and digital infrastructure

- India Post: PIN codes (1972, six digits); DIGIPIN (2025) is a 10-character geo-coded digital address.
- Telecommunications Act 2023 replaced the Indian Telegraph Act 1885; TRAI was set up in 1997; 5G services began on 1 October 2022; satellite broadband licences were issued in 2025.
- Navigation: NavIC (IRNSS) regional system and GAGAN satellite-based augmentation for aviation.
- BharatNet is laying optical fibre to gram panchayats; UPI (2016) and the Digital India programme (2015) underpin digital payments.
- Broadcasting: All India Radio (name since 1936, 'Akashvani' from 1957), Doordarshan (1959), Prasar Bharati (1997).
- Space-based communication: INSAT/GSAT series serve television, telemedicine, disaster warning and education.

### Punjab transport and trade file

- The GT Road corridor (NH-44: Shambhu–Rajpura–Ludhiana–Jalandhar–Amritsar–Attari) is Punjab's economic spine, paralleled by the Ambala–Amritsar railway.
- Logistics: Eastern DFC begins at Sahnewal; inland container depots near Ludhiana move hosiery, cycles and parts to ports.
- Border trade: the Attari Integrated Check Post (2012) once handled India–Pakistan and Afghan trade; trade was suspended in 2019 and the ICP closed in April 2025; the Kartarpur Corridor (November 2019) carries pilgrims to Gurdwara Darbar Sahib.
- Punjab's distance from ports raises freight costs for exporters — a key reason it lobbies for freight equalisation and corridor projects.
- Expressways and economic corridors (Delhi–Amritsar–Katra, Amritsar–Jamnagar, Ludhiana–Rupnagar) aim to cut travel times and open new industrial land.
- Amritsar airport's direct flights to the Gulf, Europe and North America serve the diaspora.

### Traps and PPSC patterns

- Kolkata (Syama Prasad Mookerjee Port) is the riverine major port; Kandla is a tidal port.
- NW-1 is Haldia–Prayagraj, not Varanasi–Patna; NW-2 is Dhubri–Sadiya.
- Even-numbered NHs run east–west under the 2010 system.
- NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015, not in 2014.
- PPSC 2018 asked about 'Setu Bharatam' (railway over- and under-bridges); 2021 asked Thal Ghat; 2025 asked Kavach — transport schemes are tested every year.
- Rio+20's outcome document was 'The Future We Want'; the Brundtland Report was 'Our Common Future'.
