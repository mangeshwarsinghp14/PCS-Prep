## Short notes

- Human development means widening people's choices and capabilities, not just income; the concept was put forward by Mahbub ul Haq (first UNDP Human Development Report, 1990) and built on Amartya Sen's capability approach.
- Four pillars of human development: equity, sustainability, productivity and empowerment.
- Approaches: income approach, welfare approach, basic needs approach (ILO), capability approach (Sen).
- Human Development Index: health (life expectancy at birth), education (mean and expected years of schooling), standard of living (GNI per capita in PPP) - combined by geometric mean since 2010.
- Related UNDP indices: Inequality-adjusted HDI, Gender Development Index, Gender Inequality Index, Multidimensional Poverty Index (with Oxford's OPHI, 2010); Bhutan uses Gross National Happiness.
- India is in the 'medium human development' group; HDR 2025 ranked India 130th of 193 countries (HDI 0.685).
- Within India, Kerala leads on most human development indicators; large gaps persist across states, castes, genders and rural-urban areas.
- Settlement = cluster of dwellings; rural settlements depend on primary activities, urban on secondary and tertiary activities.
- Census urban definition: statutory towns (municipality, corporation, cantonment board, notified town area) and census towns - population at least 5,000, density at least 400 per sq km, and 75% or more male main workers in non-farm work.
- Rural settlement types: clustered/compact (northern plains, North-East), semi-clustered (Gujarat plains, parts of Rajasthan), hamleted (middle and lower Ganga plain, Chhattisgarh, Himalayan valleys), dispersed (hills of Meghalaya, Uttarakhand, Himachal, Kerala).
- Hamlets have local names such as panna, para, palli, nagla and dhani.
- Rural settlement patterns: linear (along roads, rivers), rectangular, circular, star-like, T-shaped, cross-shaped, double village.
- Evolution of towns: ancient (Varanasi, Prayagraj, Patna, Madurai), medieval (Delhi, Agra, Hyderabad, Lucknow), modern colonial ports and hill stations (Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata, Shimla) and post-1947 planned towns (Chandigarh, Bhubaneswar, Gandhinagar, Durgapur).
- Population classes of towns: Class I has 1 lakh+; 53 urban agglomerations had over 10 lakh in 2011; Greater Mumbai, Delhi and Kolkata were the megacities (over 1 crore).
- Functional classification: administrative (state capitals), industrial (Jamshedpur), transport/port (Kandla, Kochi), commercial (Kolkata, Saharanpur), mining (Dhanbad, Singrauli), garrison (Ambala, Jalandhar, Mhow), educational (Roorkee), religious (Amritsar, Varanasi, Puri), tourist (Shimla, Udaipur).
- Urban problems: slums, congestion, housing shortage, water and waste management; responses include Smart Cities, AMRUT, PMAY-Urban and Swachh Bharat.

## Punjab angle

- Chandigarh, Punjab's capital, is India's best-known planned city (Le Corbusier, 1950s).
- Punjab's urban share was 37.5% in 2011, above the national 31.2%; Ludhiana is its largest city.
- Amritsar is a religious town (Golden Temple) and Jalandhar a cantonment and industrial town - useful functional examples.

## Deep dive

### HDI mechanics and India's numbers

- HDI goalposts: life expectancy 20–85 years; expected schooling 0–18 years; mean schooling 0–15 years; GNI per capita (PPP) US$100–75,000 on a log scale; dimension indices are combined by geometric mean.
- HDR 2025 ('A matter of choice: People and possibilities in the age of AI') ranked India 130th of 193 with HDI 0.685 — still 'medium human development'; life expectancy about 72 years.
- Iceland topped HDR 2025; South Asian comparison is a favourite Mains angle (Sri Lanka and Bhutan above India).
- Related UNDP measures: Inequality-adjusted HDI (India loses about a third of its HDI to inequality), Gender Development Index, Gender Inequality Index and the Global MPI (with Oxford's OPHI, 2010).
- India's National MPI (NITI Aayog) uses 12 indicators (adds maternal health and bank account); NITI's 2024 paper estimated that about 24.82 crore people escaped multidimensional poverty between 2013-14 and 2022-23 (headcount 29.17% to 11.28%).
- NITI's SDG India Index 2023-24 gave India a composite score of 71, with Kerala and Uttarakhand jointly at the top.

### Thinkers and alternative measures

- Amartya Sen: capability approach ('Development as Freedom', 1999; Nobel 1998) — development as expansion of real freedoms.
- Mahbub ul Haq launched the first Human Development Report (1990) and designed the HDI with Sen.
- Physical Quality of Life Index (Morris D. Morris, 1979): literacy, infant mortality and life expectancy at age one.
- Gross National Happiness — Bhutan (idea of King Jigme Singye Wangchuck, 1970s); the World Happiness Report (UN SDSN, since 2012) is topped by Finland.
- World Bank Human Capital Index (2018) measures expected productivity of a child born today.
- Four pillars (equity, sustainability, productivity, empowerment) should be linked to Indian schemes in answers — e.g., Samagra Shiksha, Ayushman Bharat, SHG-based livelihoods.

### Settlement geography — theories and terms

- Site is the physical ground a settlement stands on; situation is its position relative to surroundings — Amritsar's situation on the GT Road and near the border explains its trade role.
- Central Place Theory (Walter Christaller, 1933): hexagonal market areas and a hierarchy of central places; K=3 (marketing), K=4 (transport), K=7 (administration); August Lösch refined it (1940).
- Rank-size rule (G.K. Zipf, 1949): the nth city has about 1/n of the largest city's population; a 'primate city' (Mark Jefferson, 1939) is disproportionately larger than the next (Bangkok, Dhaka).
- Urban structure models: concentric zone (E.W. Burgess, 1925), sector (Homer Hoyt, 1939) and multiple nuclei (Harris and Ullman, 1945).
- Conurbation (Patrick Geddes, 1915), megalopolis (Jean Gottmann, 1961, for the Boston–Washington corridor) and ecumenopolis (Doxiadis).
- Rurban clusters: Shyama Prasad Mukherji Rurban Mission (2016) developed about 300 clusters with urban amenities; peri-urban sprawl, counter-urbanisation and gentrification are related terms.

### Urbanisation in India — data and missions

- Urban share rose from about 11% (1901) to 17.3% (1951) and 31.16% (2011).
- Census 2011 counted 7,935 towns — 4,041 statutory towns and 3,894 census towns (up from 1,362 census towns in 2001), showing 'in-situ' urbanisation of large villages.
- 53 urban agglomerations had over 10 lakh people in 2011; Greater Mumbai was the largest UA.
- 74th Amendment (1992): nagar panchayat, municipal council, municipal corporation; 12th Schedule lists 18 functions.
- Missions: JNNURM (2005–14); Smart Cities Mission (2015, 100 cities, wound up in 2025); AMRUT (2015) and AMRUT 2.0 (2021); Swachh Bharat Mission-Urban 2.0 (2021); PMAY-Urban (2015) and PMAY-U 2.0 (2024, 1 crore houses); HRIDAY (2015) for 12 heritage cities including Amritsar.
- Punjab's smart cities were Ludhiana, Jalandhar and Amritsar.

### Punjab settlements

- Traditional Punjab villages are compact (nucleated), often on raised mounds; after consolidation of holdings (East Punjab Holdings Act 1948) farmhouses ('deras') spread into the fields, adding a dispersed element.
- Chandigarh: planned by Le Corbusier with Pierre Jeanneret, Maxwell Fry and Jane Drew; sector grid of about 800 m × 1,200 m; the Capitol Complex is part of a UNESCO World Heritage listing (2016).
- Tricity growth: Mohali (SAS Nagar), Panchkula and New Chandigarh form a conurbation spilling across Punjab, Haryana and the UT.
- Functional types: Amritsar (religious, tourism, border trade), Ludhiana (industrial), Jalandhar (sports goods, leather, cantonment), Pathankot (garrison and transport gateway to J&K and Himachal), Bathinda (refinery, thermal power, one of the largest military stations).
- Punjab's urban share (37.5% in 2011) exceeds the national average; the GT Road corridor (Rajpura–Ludhiana–Jalandhar–Amritsar) is its main urban axis.
- Urban problems: unplanned colonies, Buddha Nullah pollution in Ludhiana, solid-waste dumps and urban flooding during heavy monsoon spells.

### Traps and PPSC patterns

- Hoyt = sector model; Burgess = concentric zones — a common swap.
- HDI uses GNI per capita (PPP), not GDP; inequality is adjusted only in the IHDI.
- A census town is not a statutory town — it has no municipality (PPSC 2025 asked census-town criteria).
- Rank-size rule predicts the 3rd city has one-third of the largest city's population.
- MPI ≠ income poverty: it counts deprivations in health, education and living standards.
- India remains in the 'medium' HDI group; do not write 'high'.
