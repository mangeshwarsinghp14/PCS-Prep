## Short notes

- Location factors: raw materials, power, labour, market, transport, capital, water, government policy and agglomeration economies; Alfred Weber's least-cost theory stresses transport costs.
- Classification: by raw material (agro-based, mineral-based, forest-based, marine), by size (large, medium, small/MSME), by ownership (public, private, joint, co-operative), by bulk (heavy and light).
- Iron and steel uses heavy, weight-losing raw materials (iron ore, coking coal, limestone), so plants cluster near the Chhota Nagpur plateau.
- TISCO at Jamshedpur (1907, Sakchi on the Subarnarekha) was India's first major private steel plant; IISCO at Burnpur/Kulti; Visvesvaraya Iron and Steel at Bhadravati (Karnataka).
- SAIL plants: Bhilai, Rourkela, Durgapur, Bokaro, Salem; Visakhapatnam (RINL) is India's first shore-based integrated plant; India has been the world's second-largest crude steel producer since 2018.
- Cotton textiles: first successful modern mill at Mumbai (1854); Mumbai and Ahmedabad were the main early centres; Coimbatore is called the 'Manchester of South India'; industry is now dispersed near markets and cotton areas.
- Jute: concentrated on the Hugli banks (West Bengal) - first mill at Rishra (1855); Partition left most jute-growing areas in East Pakistan.
- Sugar: UP and Maharashtra lead; seasonal, raw cane perishable so mills are near fields; the industry shifted south due to higher sucrose content, longer crushing season and co-operatives.
- Petrochemicals and refining: Mumbai, Vadodara, Jamnagar (world's largest single-site refinery complex), Hazira; products include plastics, synthetic fibres and fertilisers.
- Knowledge-based industry: IT and electronics hubs at Bengaluru ('Silicon Valley of India'), Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai, Delhi-NCR; Software Technology Parks of India (1991).
- Automobiles: clusters at Gurugram-Manesar, Chennai ('Detroit of India'), Pune, Sanand (Gujarat), Pithampur (MP); India became the world's third-largest automobile market in 2022.
- Other industries: cement (India second-largest producer; near limestone in MP, Rajasthan, Andhra), aluminium (Renukoot, Hirakud, Korba), fertilisers (near refineries and gas pipelines).
- NCERT's eight major industrial regions: Mumbai-Pune, Hugli, Bengaluru-Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Chhota Nagpur, Visakhapatnam-Guntur, Gurugram-Delhi-Meerut, Kollam-Thiruvananthapuram.
- Policies: Make in India (2014), Production Linked Incentive schemes (14 sectors), India Semiconductor Mission (2021), SEZ Act 2005, National Industrial Corridor programme (Delhi-Mumbai Industrial Corridor with Dholera, Shendra-Bidkin), revised MSME definitions (2020).
- Issues: low share of manufacturing in GDP, infrastructure and logistics costs, land and labour rigidities, industrial pollution, regional concentration.

## Punjab angle

- Ludhiana: hosiery and woollens, bicycles (India's bicycle hub), auto parts, sewing machines - a leading MSME cluster.
- Jalandhar is India's main sports-goods and hand-tools centre; Mandi Gobindgarh is known as Punjab's 'Steel Town' for its re-rolling mills.
- Guru Gobind Singh Refinery (HMEL) at Bathinda is Punjab's major petroleum refinery; Amritsar and Ludhiana are textile centres.

## Deep dive

### Industrial location theories

- Alfred Weber (1909) least-cost theory: transport, labour and agglomeration costs decide location; the material index (weight of localised raw materials ÷ weight of product) above 1 pulls industry to raw materials, below 1 to the market.
- Isodapanes are lines of equal additional transport cost around the least-cost point; firms move if labour savings exceed the extra transport cost.
- Edgar Hoover stressed terminal versus line-haul costs; August Lösch (1940s) looked for the point of maximum profit rather than least cost.
- D.M. Smith's spatial margins of profitability (1966) and Allan Pred's behavioural matrix (1967) add realism about imperfect information.
- Raw-material oriented: sugar, cement, iron and steel; market oriented: bakeries, soft drinks, furniture; power oriented: aluminium smelting; footloose: electronics, software.
- Agglomeration economies explain clusters such as Ludhiana (cycles and parts), Tiruppur (knitwear) and Bengaluru (software).

### Industry data and rankings

- India has been the second-largest crude steel producer since 2018; the National Steel Policy 2017 aims at 300 million tonnes capacity by 2030-31.
- India is the second-largest cement producer, the third-largest automobile market (since 2022), third by volume in pharmaceuticals and the second-largest mobile-phone manufacturer.
- The eight core industries (coal, crude oil, natural gas, refinery products, fertilisers, steel, cement, electricity) carry about 40% weight in the Index of Industrial Production (base 2011-12); refinery products have the largest weight.
- MSMEs produce close to a third of GDP and about 45% of exports; Udyam registration began in 2020 and investment and turnover limits were raised again in Budget 2025-26.
- Manufacturing's share of GDP has stayed in the mid-teens despite the Make in India goal of 25%.
- Semiconductors: Micron's assembly and test plant at Sanand, the Tata–PSMC fab at Dholera and the first India-made chips in 2025 under the India Semiconductor Mission (2021).

### Industrial policy timeline (year — milestone)

- 1948 — Industrial Policy Resolution; 1956 — IPR (Schedules A, B and C; Mahalanobis model).
- 1991 — New Industrial Policy: delicensing, dilution of MRTP, foreign investment and disinvestment.
- 2005 — SEZ Act; 2011 — National Manufacturing Policy (NIMZs); 2014 — Make in India.
- 2020 — Production Linked Incentive schemes for 14 sectors; 2021 — PM Gati Shakti National Master Plan; 2022 — National Logistics Policy.
- Industrial corridors: Delhi–Mumbai (Dholera, Shendra–Bidkin, Greater Noida, Vikram Udyogpuri), Amritsar–Kolkata, Chennai–Bengaluru and others; in August 2024 the Cabinet approved 12 industrial smart cities including Rajpura–Patiala in Punjab.
- Budget 2025-26 announced a National Manufacturing Mission for small, medium and large industry.

### Industry mapping list (city — industry)

- Aluminium: Hirakud and Angul (NALCO), Korba (BALCO), Renukoot (Hindalco), Jharsuguda (Vedanta).
- Copper smelting: Khetri (Rajasthan), Ghatsila (Jharkhand), Dahej (Gujarat); Tuticorin's Sterlite plant was closed in 2018.
- Fertilisers: Sindri (Jharkhand, 1951 — first public-sector plant), Nangal and Bathinda (National Fertilizers, Punjab), Trombay, Gorakhpur (revived).
- Railway factories: ICF Chennai, Rail Coach Factory Kapurthala (Punjab, 1986), Modern Coach Factory Raebareli, Chittaranjan Locomotive Works, Banaras Locomotive Works (Varanasi), Patiala Locomotive Works (formerly Diesel Loco Modernisation Works).
- Shipyards: Mazagon Dock (Mumbai), Cochin Shipyard, Hindustan Shipyard (Visakhapatnam), GRSE (Kolkata); aircraft: HAL Bengaluru and Nashik.
- Craft clusters: Firozabad (glass bangles), Moradabad (brassware), Bhadohi (carpets), Aligarh (locks), Tiruppur (knitwear), Surat (diamond cutting), Kanpur and Agra (leather), Jalandhar and Meerut (sports goods), Ludhiana (bicycles).

### Punjab industry file

- Punjab's industry is dominated by MSMEs: Ludhiana (hosiery, woollens, bicycles, auto parts, sewing machines), Jalandhar (sports goods, hand tools, leather, rubber), Mandi Gobindgarh (steel re-rolling), Batala (foundry and farm implements), Amritsar (textiles, food processing).
- Large units: HMEL refinery (Bathinda), RCF Kapurthala, Patiala Locomotive Works, NFL Nangal and Bathinda, tractor makers at Mohali and Hoshiarpur, and the Semi-Conductor Laboratory (Mohali) under MeitY.
- Policy: Punjab Industrial and Business Development Policy 2022; 'Invest Punjab' single-window system.
- Logistics advantage: the Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor starts at Sahnewal (Ludhiana) and the Amritsar–Kolkata Industrial Corridor passes through the state; Rajpura–Patiala is an approved industrial smart city.
- Constraints: border location, distance from ports and high freight costs, fragmented MSME base, and power-subsidy burden on state finances.
- Pollution: Ludhiana's dyeing units were moved to common effluent treatment plants to stop discharge into Buddha Nullah.

### Traps and PPSC patterns

- Iron and steel are raw-material oriented because raw materials lose weight; footloose industries are not tied to one raw material.
- Sugar mills shifted south because of higher sucrose content, a longer crushing season and co-operatives.
- Visakhapatnam (RINL) is the first shore-based integrated steel plant; TISCO (1907) is the oldest major private plant.
- Refinery products, not electricity, have the highest weight among core industries.
- PPSC 2021 asked Punjab's GI products (Phulkari) — keep a list of Punjab GI tags with their districts.
- Coimbatore is the 'Manchester of South India'; Ahmedabad was called the 'Manchester of India'.
