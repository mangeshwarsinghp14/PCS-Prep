## Short notes

- Light is an electromagnetic wave travelling at about 3 x 10^8 m/s in vacuum; it needs no medium; sunlight takes about 8 minutes 20 seconds to reach Earth.
- Laws of reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection; incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in one plane.
- Plane mirror image: virtual, erect, same size, laterally inverted, as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.
- Concave (converging) mirror: shaving/make-up mirrors (enlarged erect image when object is within focus), dentist's mirror, reflectors of torches and headlights, solar cookers and furnaces.
- Convex (diverging) mirror: always a virtual, erect, diminished image with a wide field of view - used as rear-view mirrors and at road bends; mirror formula 1/v + 1/u = 1/f, f = R/2.
- Refraction is bending of light due to change of speed between media; refractive index n = c/v; Snell's law: sin i / sin r = constant.
- Effects of refraction: a pool looks shallower, a pencil in water looks bent, stars twinkle (atmospheric refraction), the Sun is visible about 2 minutes before actual sunrise and after actual sunset.
- Total internal reflection occurs from denser to rarer medium beyond the critical angle: optical fibres, the sparkle of diamond, mirages in deserts and on hot roads.
- Convex lens converges (magnifying glass, camera, corrects hypermetropia); concave lens diverges (corrects myopia); power P = 1/f (in metres), unit dioptre; convex positive, concave negative.
- Human eye forms a real, inverted image on the retina; ciliary muscles change lens focal length (accommodation); near point about 25 cm; presbyopia needs bifocals; cataract clouds the lens.
- Dispersion: a prism splits white light into VIBGYOR; violet bends most, red least; a rainbow forms by refraction, dispersion and internal reflection in raindrops and is seen opposite the Sun.
- Scattering: shorter (blue) wavelengths scatter more, so the sky is blue; the Sun looks red at sunrise and sunset; red is used for danger signals as it scatters least; clouds look white as large droplets scatter all colours.
- C.V. Raman discovered the Raman effect (28 February 1928, now National Science Day) and won the Physics Nobel in 1930.
- Sound is a mechanical, longitudinal wave in air and needs a medium (cannot travel in vacuum); it is fastest in solids, slower in liquids, slowest in gases.
- Speed of sound in air is about 343 m/s at 20 C (about 331 m/s at 0 C) and rises with temperature; in water about 1,500 m/s; lightning is seen before thunder is heard.
- Human audible range 20 Hz to 20 kHz; infrasound below 20 Hz (earthquakes, elephants, whales); ultrasound above 20 kHz (bats, dolphins; used in ultrasonography, SONAR, cleaning and detecting flaws in metals).
- Echo is reflected sound heard separately when the reflector is at least about 17 m away (sound persists about 0.1 s); reverberation in halls is reduced with absorbent materials; stethoscopes, megaphones and curved ceilings use multiple reflection.
- Loudness depends on amplitude (measured in decibels), pitch on frequency, quality (timbre) on waveform; SONAR depth = speed x time / 2; the Doppler effect is the apparent change in frequency due to relative motion.

## Deep dive

### Optical phenomena — cause table

- Mirages on hot roads come from refraction and total internal reflection in hot air layers; 'looming' (superior mirage) appears over cold seas.
- Stars twinkle because their point-like light is refracted by shifting air layers; planets, being extended discs, hardly twinkle.
- Primary rainbow: one internal reflection, red outside, about 42 degrees from the anti-solar point; secondary rainbow: two reflections, colours reversed, fainter and at about 51 degrees.
- A 22-degree halo around the Sun or Moon comes from ice crystals in thin cirrostratus clouds.
- Rayleigh scattering (strength varies as 1/wavelength^4) makes the sky blue and sunsets red; the sky looks black from space; larger droplets scatter all colours equally, so clouds look white.
- Interference gives colours to soap bubbles and oil films; diffraction gives rainbow colours on a CD; polarisation is used in glare-cutting sunglasses, 3D cinema and LCD screens.
- The Raman effect (C.V. Raman, 1928) is a change in the colour of light scattered by molecules; Raman spectroscopy now identifies drugs, minerals and adulterants.
- The photoelectric effect (explained by Einstein, Nobel 1921) underlies solar cells, light sensors and automatic doors.

### Optical instruments and the eye

- Compound microscopes use two convex lenses; large astronomical telescopes use mirrors. The James Webb Space Telescope (launched 2021) has a 6.5 m segmented mirror and sits near the Sun–Earth L2 point.
- India's largest optical telescope is the 3.6 m Devasthal Optical Telescope (ARIES, Nainital, 2016); the Giant Metrewave Radio Telescope near Pune is a major radio telescope.
- Periscopes and binoculars use mirrors or prisms (total internal reflection); a kaleidoscope uses multiple reflections.
- Eye defects: myopia (concave lens), hypermetropia (convex), presbyopia (bifocal or progressive), astigmatism (cylindrical lens), cataract (replace lens with an intraocular lens); LASIK reshapes the cornea.
- Persistence of vision (about 1/16 second) makes films (24 frames a second) look continuous.
- The blind spot (where the optic nerve leaves) has no receptors; the fovea (yellow spot) has the most cones and gives the sharpest vision.
- Lasers produce coherent light; uses include LIDAR, barcode scanners, eye surgery and fibre-optic communication. Fibre-optic endoscopes let doctors see inside the body.

### Electromagnetic spectrum — order and uses

- Increasing wavelength: gamma < X-rays < ultraviolet < visible (violet about 400 nm to red about 700 nm) < infrared < microwaves < radio waves.
- Gamma rays: cancer radiotherapy, sterilising medical equipment, food irradiation.
- X-rays (Roentgen, 1895): radiography, CT scans, airport baggage screening.
- Ultraviolet: sterilising water and surfaces, vitamin D synthesis in skin, checking currency notes; the ozone layer absorbs most UV-B and UV-C.
- Infrared: TV remotes, night-vision and thermal cameras (fever screening), heat lamps; the greenhouse effect works by trapping infrared from the Earth's surface.
- Microwaves: ovens (about 2.45 GHz agitate water molecules), radar, mobile and satellite links.
- Radio waves: AM/FM broadcasting; short waves bounce off the ionosphere for long-distance radio. MRI uses a strong magnet and radio waves, with no ionising radiation.

### Sound — applications and numbers

- Speed of sound: about 343 m/s in air at 20 C, about 1,500 m/s in water, about 5,000–6,000 m/s in steel; it rises with temperature and humidity.
- Faster than sound means supersonic (Mach above 1); above Mach 5 is hypersonic; a sonic boom is the shock wave of a supersonic aircraft.
- Decibel scale: whisper about 30 dB, conversation about 60 dB, heavy traffic about 80–90 dB, firecrackers above 120 dB; long exposure above about 85 dB damages hearing.
- Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000: day limits — industrial 75, commercial 65, residential 55, silence zone 50 dB(A); night (10 pm–6 am) limits are 70, 55, 45 and 40 dB(A).
- Doppler effect: police speed guns, Doppler weather radars of IMD, Doppler ultrasound of blood flow, and the redshift of receding galaxies.
- Ultrasound uses: foetal and heart imaging (sex determination is banned under the PCPNDT Act, 1994), lithotripsy to break kidney stones, SONAR, cleaning, flaw detection; bats navigate by echolocation.
- Resonance explains why soldiers break step on bridges and how musical instruments amplify sound; timbre (wave shape) lets us tell a sitar from a flute playing the same note.
- Gol Gumbaz in Bijapur (Vijayapura) has a famous whispering gallery where a whisper carries round the dome by multiple reflection.

### Punjab links

- Dense winter fog and smog over Punjab (December–January) scatter light strongly; visibility can fall below 50 m, disrupting road, rail and flights.
- Stubble-fire smoke raises the aerosol load seen by satellites; Punjab Remote Sensing Centre counts active fires from satellite thermal (infrared) data.
- Instrument landing systems (CAT-III) at north Indian airports such as Amritsar let aircraft land in low visibility.
- Loudspeakers and DJ music at functions are regulated under the Noise Rules 2000, enforced by the Punjab Pollution Control Board and district administration.
- Night-vision and thermal cameras are used along the Punjab border to detect intruders and drones.
- Science City, Kapurthala's digital planetarium and dome theatre demonstrate optics and astronomy for students.

## Practice MCQs (12)

**1.** The blue colour of the clear sky is due to:

(a) Total internal reflection  
(b) Refraction of light  
(c) Dispersion of light  
(d) Scattering of light

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Scattering of light** — Air molecules scatter shorter blue wavelengths much more than red.

</details>

**2.** Vehicles use convex mirrors as rear-view mirrors because they:

(a) Form an inverted image of the traffic  
(b) Give a wider view with an erect image  
(c) Converge light rays to a sharp focus  
(d) Give a magnified view with a real image

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Give a wider view with an erect image** — Convex mirrors always give erect, diminished images covering a wide area.

</details>

**3.** Optical fibres work on the principle of:

(a) Dispersion of white light  
(b) Total internal reflection  
(c) Scattering by dust particles  
(d) Diffraction at narrow slits

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Total internal reflection** — Light is trapped inside the fibre core by repeated total internal reflection.

</details>

**4.** Consider: 1. Sound can travel through vacuum. 2. Sound travels faster in steel than in air. Which is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** — Sound is fastest in solids; being a mechanical wave, it cannot travel through vacuum.

</details>

**5.** Myopia (short-sightedness) is corrected using a:

(a) Cylindrical lens  
(b) Convex lens  
(c) Concave lens  
(d) Bifocal convex lens

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Concave lens** — A diverging concave lens moves the image back onto the retina.

</details>

**6.** Sound waves with frequency above 20,000 Hz are called:

(a) Ultrasonic waves  
(b) Audible waves  
(c) Microwaves  
(d) Infrasonic waves

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Ultrasonic waves** — Ultrasound is used by bats and dolphins and in medical imaging and SONAR.

</details>

**7.** Consider the following statements:  
1. A secondary rainbow has its colours in the reverse order to the primary rainbow.  
2. A rainbow is seen in the same direction as the Sun.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 1 only** — Rainbows appear opposite the Sun; the secondary bow reverses colours.

</details>

**8.** Under the Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000  
1. Industrial areas have a lower limit than silence zones.  
2. Night time means 10 pm to 6 am.  
3. The day-time limit for residential areas is 55 dB(A).  
Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only  
(b) 2 and 3 only  
(c) 1 and 3 only  
(d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 and 3 only** — Industrial limits (75/70) are the highest; silence zones the lowest (50/40).

</details>

**9.** A microwave oven heats food mainly by:

(a) Making water molecules in the food oscillate  
(b) Conduction from a hot metal coil  
(c) Ionising atoms in the food  
(d) Ultraviolet radiation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Making water molecules in the food oscillate** — Microwaves agitate polar water molecules, producing heat.

</details>

**10.** Polaroid sunglasses reduce glare from roads and water by:

(a) Diffraction  
(b) Total internal reflection  
(c) Dispersion  
(d) Polarisation of light

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Polarisation of light** — Reflected glare is partly polarised and is blocked by the filter.

</details>

**11.** Gol Gumbaz in Bijapur is famous for which acoustic effect?

(a) A whispering gallery (repeated sound reflection)  
(b) Doppler shift of sound as visitors move  
(c) Resonance of the hollow iron pillars  
(d) Ultrasonic echolocation by resident bats

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) A whispering gallery (repeated sound reflection)** — Sound reflects repeatedly round the dome.

</details>

**12.** Which of the following has the shortest wavelength?

(a) X-rays  
(b) Microwaves  
(c) Ultraviolet rays  
(d) Gamma rays

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Gamma rays** — Gamma rays sit at the high-energy end of the spectrum.

</details>
