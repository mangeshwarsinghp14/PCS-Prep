## Short notes

- Location: roughly 29°30'N–32°32'N and 73°55'E–76°50'E; area 50,362 sq km, about 1.53% of India's area.
- Neighbours: Pakistan (west, international border of about 553 km), Jammu & Kashmir (north), Himachal Pradesh (north-east), Haryana (south/south-east), Rajasthan (south-west); Chandigarh (UT) is the shared capital.
- Six border districts with Pakistan: Pathankot, Gurdaspur, Amritsar, Tarn Taran, Ferozepur, Fazilka.
- 23 districts: Pathankot and Fazilka created in 2011; Malerkotla became the 23rd district in May 2021.
- Name: Persian 'panj' (five) + 'ab' (water) = land of five rivers (Sutlej, Beas, Ravi, Chenab, Jhelum); Rigvedic 'Sapta Sindhu'; Greek 'Pentapotamia'.
- Physiographic units: (1) Shivalik hills in the north-east; (2) Kandi/foothill undulating belt; (3) vast flat alluvial plain; (4) sandy tracts and dunes in the south-west.
- Kandi belt (Pathankot, Hoshiarpur, Nawanshahr, Rupnagar, Mohali) has seasonal torrents called 'choes' that cause soil erosion and flash floods.
- Cultural-geographical regions: Majha (between Ravi and Beas: Amritsar, Tarn Taran, Gurdaspur, Pathankot); Doaba (Beas–Sutlej: Jalandhar, Kapurthala, Hoshiarpur, Nawanshahr); Malwa (south of Sutlej, the largest region); Puadh (Ropar–Mohali area up to Ghaggar).
- Doabs of undivided Punjab, east to west: Bist (Beas–Sutlej), Bari (Beas–Ravi), Rachna (Ravi–Chenab), Chaj (Chenab–Jhelum), Sindh Sagar (Jhelum–Indus).
- Sutlej (Vedic Shutudri/Shatadru): rises near Mansarovar–Rakas Tal (Tibet), enters India at Shipki La, enters Punjab near Nangal (Rupnagar) below Bhakra; longest river in the state.
- Beas (Vedic Vipasha): rises at Beas Kund near Rohtang, enters Punjab plains near Talwara–Mukerian; the only one of the five rivers that flows wholly within India.
- Beas joins Sutlej at Harike (Tarn Taran–Ferozepur border); the combined Sutlej flows past Ferozepur into Pakistan.
- Ravi (Vedic Parushni/Iravati): enters near Madhopur (Pathankot) and forms part of the Indo-Pak border in Gurdaspur and Amritsar districts.
- Ghaggar: seasonal river from the Shivaliks (Sirmaur, HP), flows through Mohali, Patiala, Sangrur, Mansa; flood-prone; often identified with the Vedic Saraswati (called Hakra in Pakistan).
- Minor streams: Kali Bein (Sultanpur Lodhi, linked with Guru Nanak; cleaned under Sant Balbir Singh Seechewal), Chitti (White) Bein, Chakki (Pathankot, joins Beas), Budha Nallah (Ludhiana, heavily polluted).
- Climate: continental, semi-arid to sub-humid; very hot summers and foggy winters; rainfall falls from over 1,000 mm in the north-east to under 400 mm in the south-west; winter western disturbances help wheat.
- Soils: mostly alluvial (loam to sandy loam); kandi soils in foothills; sandy/desert soils in the south-west; salinity and waterlogging in parts of Muktsar, Fazilka and Faridkot.
- Agro-climatic: Planning Commission Zone VI 'Trans-Gangetic Plains' (with Haryana, Delhi, Chandigarh, north Rajasthan); PAU divides Punjab into five zones — sub-mountain undulating, undulating plain, central plain, western plain and western zone.
- Forest cover is under 4% of area (ISFR), among the lowest in India. State symbols: animal blackbuck, bird baaz (northern goshawk), tree sheesham, aquatic animal Indus river dolphin (declared 2019).
- Six Ramsar sites: Harike (1990), Kanjli and Ropar (2002), Keshopur-Miani, Beas Conservation Reserve and Nangal (2019). Harike is the largest wetland of north India; Abohar sanctuary protects blackbuck with the Bishnoi community.

## Punjab angle

- Malwa holds most districts and assembly seats; Doaba is the NRI/emigration belt; Majha is the border belt.
- South-west Malwa (Bathinda, Mansa, Muktsar, Fazilka) is the cotton and kinnow belt; the Kandi belt suits maize and fruits.

## Deep dive

### Physiography in detail

- Shivalik (Siwalik) hills — the outermost Himalayan range of young Tertiary sandstones and conglomerates, entering Punjab in Pathankot, Hoshiarpur, Rupnagar and SAS Nagar districts.
- Kandi belt — gravelly piedmont below the Shivaliks; deep water table, rain-fed farming, gullied by seasonal choes.
- Sirowal belt — just below the Kandi, where the water table is high and springs seep out; prone to waterlogging.
- Bet (khadar) lands — low floodplains along the Sutlej, Beas and Ravi, renewed by floods; Dhaya/upland (bangar) — older alluvium above the river bluffs.
- Central alluvial plain — one of the flattest and most fertile surfaces in India; gentle slope from north-east to south-west.
- South-western sandy tract — Bathinda, Mansa, Sri Muktsar Sahib and Fazilka; once had sand dunes (tibbas), largely levelled after canal irrigation and the Green Revolution.
- Waterlogged and saline pockets in the south-west (Muktsar, Fazilka, Faridkot) result from canal seepage, poor natural drainage and brackish groundwater.

### Rivers and drainage — quick reference

- Sutlej — enters Punjab at Nangal below the Bhakra dam; headworks at Ropar, Harike and Hussainiwala (Ferozepur); after Harike it forms part of the Indo-Pak boundary before entering Pakistan.
- Beas — enters the plains near Talwara (Pong dam just upstream in Himachal); flows past Mukerian; joins the Sutlej at Harike; it never enters Pakistan.
- Ravi — Ranjit Sagar (Thein) dam and Shahpurkandi dam near Pathankot, Madhopur headworks downstream; forms the boundary in Gurdaspur and Amritsar districts before entering Pakistan.
- Ghaggar — seasonal river from the Shivaliks of Himachal; crosses Mohali, Patiala, Sangrur and Mansa (Sardulgarh) into Haryana and Rajasthan; repeated floods (2023, 2025).
- Minor streams: Chakki (Pathankot–Himachal boundary, joins the Beas), Ujh (from J&K, joins the Ravi), Kiran/Sakki nala (Gurdaspur, feeds Keshopur chhamb), Kali Bein and Chitti Bein (Doaba).
- Choes of Hoshiarpur and Rupnagar — sandy seasonal torrents; the Punjab Land Preservation (Chos) Act, 1900 was an early soil-conservation law.
- Harike — confluence of Beas and Sutlej; barrage of 1952 feeds the Rajasthan (Indira Gandhi) Canal and the Ferozepur and Sirhind feeders.

### Regions, doabs and dialects

- Majha — between Beas and Ravi (Bari Doab): Amritsar, Tarn Taran, Gurdaspur, Pathankot; Majhi is the base of standard Punjabi.
- Doaba — between Beas and Sutlej (Bist Doab): Jalandhar, Kapurthala, Hoshiarpur, SBS Nagar; the NRI belt with the highest SC share.
- Malwa — south of the Sutlej: the largest region with most districts and assembly seats; Malwai dialect; historically the dry 'Jangal Desh' around Bathinda.
- Puadh — the Rupnagar–Mohali tract extending to the Ghaggar and into Haryana; Puadhi dialect.
- Pothohari, Shahpuri and Multani (Saraiki) varieties belong to Pakistani Punjab.

### Climate

- Sub-tropical continental: Köppen Cwa in most of the state, semi-arid BSh in the south-west.
- Seasons: cold season (Nov–Mar), hot season (Apr–June), south-west monsoon (July–Sept), post-monsoon (Oct–Nov).
- The bulk of annual rain falls in July–September; western disturbances bring winter rain that benefits wheat but also hailstorms.
- Rainfall falls from over 1,000 mm in the north-east (Pathankot) to well under 400 mm in the south-west (Fazilka, Bathinda).
- Hazards: summer loo and heat waves (the south-west crosses 45°C), dense winter fog and cold waves, floods in the monsoon, and crop-season air pollution from stubble burning.
- Climate links: paddy transplanting dates are notified zone-wise each June; cotton sowing depends on canal releases and pre-monsoon heat.

### Soils and agro-climatic zones

- Soils: alluvial loams of the central plain; kandi and sub-mountain soils in the north-east; floodplain (bet) soils; sandy and arid-brown soils in the south-west; saline–sodic patches in the south-west.
- Soil-health concerns: low organic carbon, zinc and other micronutrient deficiency, compaction under the paddy–wheat cycle.
- Planning Commission zone: Trans-Gangetic Plains (Zone VI) with Haryana, Delhi, Chandigarh and north Rajasthan.
- PAU's classic five zones: (1) Sub-mountain undulating — Pathankot, Gurdaspur, Hoshiarpur, Rupnagar, Mohali; (2) Undulating plain; (3) Central plain — Amritsar, Tarn Taran, Kapurthala, Jalandhar, Ludhiana, Fatehgarh Sahib, Patiala, Sangrur; (4) Western plain — Ferozepur, Moga, Barnala, Faridkot; (5) Western zone — Muktsar, Fazilka, Bathinda, Mansa.
- Some recent writings add a sixth 'flood-plain region' along the Beas, Sutlej, Ravi and Ghaggar — be ready for 'five or six zones' in options.
- Crop fit: maize, fruits and agroforestry in the Kandi; paddy–wheat in the central plain; cotton–kinnow–wheat in the south-west; sugarcane in Gurdaspur–Doaba.

### Forests, wildlife and wetlands

- ISFR 2023: forest cover 1,846.09 sq km = 3.67% of area (very dense 9.44, moderately dense 784.89, open 1,051.76 sq km); about 58% of this lies outside recorded forest area.
- Wildlife sanctuaries (13 by most counts): Abohar (blackbuck, community-protected by Bishnois), Harike, Bir Moti Bagh, Bir Gurdialpura, Bir Bhunerheri, Bir Mehas, Bir Dosanjh, Bir Bhadson (Patiala area), Bir Aishwan (Sangrur), Bir Chahal (Mansa), Takhni–Rehmapur (Hoshiarpur), Jhajjar–Bacholi (Rupnagar), Kathlaur–Kushlian (Pathankot–Gurdaspur); Nangal is also listed in some counts.
- Community reserves: Lalwan (Hoshiarpur), Keshopur–Chhamb (Gurdaspur), Panniwala–Gumjal–Haripura–Diwankhera (Fazilka), Siswan (Mohali). Conservation reserves: Beas, Ranjit Sagar Dam, Rakh Sarai Amanat Khan, Ropar wetland.
- Ramsar sites (6): Harike (1990), Kanjli (2002), Ropar (2002), Keshopur–Miani, Beas Conservation Reserve and Nangal (all 2019); no new Punjab site has been added since.
- Beas Conservation Reserve shelters the Indus river dolphin (state aquatic animal, 2019); gharials were reintroduced into the Beas in 2017.
- Zoos and safaris: Mahendra Chaudhary Zoological Park, Chhatbir (Mohali) and Tiger Safari, Ludhiana; deer parks at Neelon, Bir Moti Bagh and Bir Talab.
- State symbols: animal blackbuck, bird baaz (northern goshawk), tree shisham (tahli), aquatic animal Indus river dolphin.

### Districts, divisions and administrative units

- 23 districts. Old core: Amritsar, Gurdaspur, Ferozepur, Hoshiarpur, Jalandhar, Ludhiana (British districts); Patiala, Sangrur, Bathinda, Kapurthala (from PEPSU); Rupnagar (1966); Faridkot (1972).
- New districts: Fatehgarh Sahib and Mansa (1992); Moga, Sri Muktsar Sahib and Nawanshahr (1995; renamed SBS Nagar in 2008); SAS Nagar, Tarn Taran and Barnala (2006); Pathankot and Fazilka (27 July 2011); Malerkotla (announced 14 May 2021, notified 2 June 2021).
- Five divisions: Jalandhar (Amritsar, Gurdaspur, Pathankot, Tarn Taran, Kapurthala, Jalandhar, Hoshiarpur); Patiala (Patiala, Ludhiana, Sangrur, Barnala, Fatehgarh Sahib, Malerkotla); Ferozepur (Ferozepur, Fazilka, Sri Muktsar Sahib, Moga); Faridkot (Faridkot, Bathinda, Mansa); Rupnagar (Rupnagar, SAS Nagar, SBS Nagar).
- Sub-units: 97 tehsils and 82 sub-tehsils as per the Statistical Abstract of Punjab 2024 (secondary report).
- A 24th district centred on Anandpur Sahib was proposed in late 2025 but had not been notified in the reports checked — confirm before the exam.
- Largest district by area: Ludhiana (3,767 sq km); smallest: Malerkotla (837 sq km).

### PPSC traps

- The Beas is the only one of the five rivers that flows entirely in India.
- Harike = Beas + Sutlej confluence and Ramsar site; Kanjli is on the Kali Bein (Kapurthala), not on the Sutlej.
- Kandi (dry, gravelly) vs Sirowal (wet, spring line) — opposite moisture conditions.
- Moga and Sri Muktsar Sahib became districts in 1995, not 1992; Malerkotla was notified in June 2021 after the May announcement.
- Ramsar years: Harike 1990; Kanjli and Ropar 2002; the other three in 2019.
- Forest cover (3.67%) is under 4%; with tree cover the green area is still below 7%.

**(a) Beas and Sutlej** — Doaba (the Bist Doab) is the land between the Beas and the Sutlej; Majha lies between the Ravi and the Beas.

(a) Tarn Taran  
(b) Hoshiarpur  
(c) Fazilka  
(d) Pathankot

**5.** Consider the statements:  
1. Kanjli was Punjab's first Ramsar site (1990).  
2. Punjab has six Ramsar sites.  
Which is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** — Statement 1 is wrong: Punjab's first Ramsar site was Harike (1990); Kanjli was designated in 2002. Statement 2 is correct: Harike, Kanjli, Ropar, Keshopur-Miani, Beas Conservation Reserve and Nangal make six Ramsar sites (as of Nov 2026).

**6.** The seasonal Ghaggar river is often identified with which Vedic river?

(a) Vipasha  
(b) Saraswati  
(c) Shatadru  
(d) Parushni

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Saraswati** — Vipasha = Beas, Shatadru = Sutlej, Parushni = Ravi; the Ghaggar–Hakra is linked with the Saraswati.

</details>

**7.** Under the Planning Commission's agro-climatic regions, Punjab falls in the:

(b) Western Dry region

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Trans-Gangetic Plains region** — Zone VI, the Trans-Gangetic Plains, covers Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Chandigarh and north Rajasthan.

**8.** How does annual rainfall vary across Punjab?

(a) It is almost the same everywhere  
(b) It rises from the north-east to the south-west  
(c) It falls from the north-east to the south-west  
(d) It is highest in the central plain

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) It falls from the north-east to the south-west** — The Shivalik foothills get over 1,000 mm, while Fazilka and Abohar get under 400 mm.

**9.** Consider the following statements:  
1. The Beas rises at Beas Kund near the Rohtang Pass.  
2. The Ravi enters the Punjab plains near Madhopur in Pathankot district.

**11.** Consider the following statements:  
1. The Chakki stream joins the Ravi near Pathankot.  
2. The Ujh river, flowing in from Jammu and Kashmir, is a tributary of the Ravi.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** — The Ujh comes down from Jammu and Kashmir and joins the Ravi in the Pathankot–Gurdaspur stretch, so the Ujh statement is correct. The Chakki, which forms part of the Punjab–Himachal boundary near Pathankot, is a tributary of the Beas, not the Ravi.

</details>

**12.** The Majha region of Punjab lies between which pair of rivers?

(a) Sutlej and Ghaggar  
(b) Beas and Sutlej  
(c) Ravi and Beas  
(d) Ravi and Chenab

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Ravi and Beas** — Majha (Amritsar, Tarn Taran, Gurdaspur, Pathankot) lies in the Bari Doab between the Ravi and the Beas. Beas–Sutlej is Doaba, and Ravi–Chenab is the Rachna Doab, now in Pakistan.

</details>

**13.** The Sirowal belt of north-eastern Punjab, lying just below the Kandi belt, is marked by:

(a) Shifting sand dunes and saline soils  
(b) A high water table with seepage springs  
(c) A deep water table and gravelly soils  
(d) Wide floodplains renewed every year

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) A high water table with seepage springs** — Water that sinks into the gravelly Kandi piedmont re-emerges lower down in the Sirowal belt, which therefore has a high water table and waterlogging. A deep water table describes the Kandi itself; sand dunes belong to the south-west.

</details>

**14.** The Kanjli wetland, a Ramsar site since 2002, is formed on which stream?

(a) Budha Nallah  
(b) Chakki  
(c) Kali Bein  
(d) Chitti Bein

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Kali Bein** — Kanjli wetland in Kapurthala district was created by a headworks on the Kali Bein, the stream linked with Guru Nanak at Sultanpur Lodhi. The Budha Nallah is the polluted drain at Ludhiana, and the Chakki is a Beas tributary near Pathankot. (MoEFCC Ramsar site list)

</details>

**15.** The Ghaggar flows through all of the following districts of Punjab EXCEPT:

(a) Bathinda  
(b) Sangrur  
(c) Mansa  
(d) Patiala

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Bathinda** — The Ghaggar enters Punjab near Dera Bassi (SAS Nagar) and flows through Patiala, Sangrur and Mansa (Sardulgarh) before passing into Haryana; Bathinda lies west of its course. (Punjab Pollution Control Board, Ghaggar action plan)

</details>

**16.** In the geography of south-western Punjab, the term 'tibbas' refers to:

(a) Seasonal torrents of the Shivaliks  
(b) Low floodplains along the rivers  
(c) Sand dunes and sandy mounds  
(d) Saline patches in canal areas

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Sand dunes and sandy mounds** — Tibbas are the sand dunes of the Bathinda–Mansa–Muktsar–Fazilka tract, largely levelled after canal irrigation and the Green Revolution. Seasonal hill torrents are called 'choes', and river floodplains are 'bet' lands.

</details>
