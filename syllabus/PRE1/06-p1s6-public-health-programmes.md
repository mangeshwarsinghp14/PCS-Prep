## Short notes

- National Health Mission (NHM, 2013) merges the National Rural Health Mission (2005) and National Urban Health Mission (2013); ASHAs (about one per 1,000 rural population) link communities to services.
- Janani Suraksha Yojana (2005) gives cash incentives for institutional delivery, aimed at poor and SC/ST women; Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (2011) gives free delivery (including C-section), drugs, diet, diagnostics and transport in public facilities.
- Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (2016): free antenatal check-up on the 9th of every month; LaQshya (2017) improves labour rooms; SUMAN (2019) assures zero-cost quality maternal care.
- Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana (2017): Rs 5,000 for the first child; since the 2022 revision under Mission Shakti, Rs 6,000 also for a second child if it is a girl.
- India's MMR fell to 93 per lakh live births (SRS 2019-21); IMR was 27 and U5MR 31 per 1,000 live births (SRS 2021); SDG target for MMR is below 70 by 2030.
- POSHAN Abhiyaan (2018) and Mission Poshan 2.0 target stunting, wasting, anaemia and low birth weight; Anaemia Mukt Bharat uses a 6x6x6 strategy; MAA (2016) promotes breastfeeding.
- RBSK (2013) screens children 0-18 years for the '4 Ds' - defects at birth, diseases, deficiencies, developmental delays; RKSK (2014) covers adolescent health.
- Universal Immunisation Programme began in 1985 (Expanded Programme on Immunisation 1978); Mission Indradhanush (December 2014) targets unvaccinated and partially vaccinated children and pregnant women.
- UIP protects against 12 diseases: TB, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, polio, measles, rubella, hepatitis B, Hib, rotavirus diarrhoea, pneumococcal pneumonia and Japanese encephalitis (in endemic districts).
- U-WIN is the digital registry for immunisation records (modelled on CoWIN used in COVID-19 vaccination).
- Polio: Pulse Polio began 1995; last wild case in Howrah (West Bengal) on 13 January 2011; WHO certified India polio-free on 27 March 2014; IPV added 2015; wild polio persists in Pakistan and Afghanistan.
- TB: RNTCP renamed National TB Elimination Programme (2020); DOTS strategy; Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana gives monthly nutrition support (raised to Rs 1,000 in 2024); PM TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan (2022) enlists Ni-kshay Mitras.
- India set the goal of ending TB by 2025, five years ahead of the SDG 2030 target; BCG vaccine is given at birth.
- HIV/AIDS: first Indian case detected 1986 (Chennai); NACO set up 1992 to run the National AIDS Control Programme; HIV/AIDS (Prevention and Control) Act 2017 bars discrimination; free ART; UNAIDS 95-95-95 targets; World AIDS Day 1 December.
- Elimination targets: lymphatic filariasis (2027), malaria (2030 under National Framework 2016-30), kala-azar and measles-rubella; leprosy remains under NLEP.
- Ayushman Bharat (2018): Health and Wellness Centres (renamed Ayushman Arogya Mandirs in 2023) and PM-JAY with Rs 5 lakh cover per family per year; since October 2024 all citizens aged 70+ are covered regardless of income.
- Other initiatives: National Programme for NCDs (screening for diabetes, hypertension, common cancers), National Mental Health Programme, Tele-MANAS, eSanjeevani telemedicine, Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABHA ID).

## Punjab angle

- Aam Aadmi Clinics, launched in Punjab on 15 August 2022, provide free primary consultations, medicines and tests close to home.
- Mukh Mantri Sehat Yojana: universal cashless cover up to Rs 10 lakh per family (enrolment began 23 September 2025 with a pilot in Barnala and Tarn Taran, then extended statewide).
- Institutional deliveries in Punjab exceed 90% (NFHS-5, 2019-21), but anaemia among women and children and rising NCDs remain concerns.

## Deep dive

### Timeline of India's health programmes (year — event)

- 1946 — Bhore Committee recommends a three-tier primary health care system; 1952 — India becomes the first country with a national family planning programme.
- 1953/1958 — National Malaria Control, then Eradication, Programme; 1955 — leprosy control (NLEP with multi-drug therapy from 1983).
- 1962 — National TB Programme; 1997 — RNTCP with DOTS; 2020 — renamed National TB Elimination Programme.
- 1976 — National Programme for Control of Blindness; 1977 — India declared smallpox-free (last case 1975).
- 1978 — Expanded Programme on Immunisation; 1985 — Universal Immunisation Programme; 1992 — National AIDS Control Programme.
- 1983, 2002, 2017 — National Health Policies; NHP 2017 targeted public health spending of 2.5% of GDP by 2025.
- 2005 — NRHM, ASHAs and JSY; 2008 — RSBY insurance for BPL families (later merged into PM-JAY).
- 2014 — Mission Indradhanush; WHO certifies India polio-free; 2015 — maternal and neonatal tetanus elimination validated.
- 2018 — Ayushman Bharat (Health and Wellness Centres, PM-JAY launched 23 September 2018) and POSHAN Abhiyaan; 2021 — Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission and PM-ABHIM for district health infrastructure.
- 2023 — National Sickle Cell Anaemia Elimination Mission; 2024 — WHO validates India's elimination of trachoma as a public-health problem (October); PM-JAY extended to everyone aged 70+.
- Dec 2024–Mar 2025 — 100-day intensified TB Mukt Bharat campaign in high-burden districts.
- 28 February 2026 — nationwide single-dose HPV vaccination drive for 14-year-old girls launched from Ajmer, using the quadrivalent Gardasil-4 vaccine at government facilities.

### Latest data with year — use in answers

- Maternal mortality ratio: 88 per lakh live births (SRS 2020-22), down from 93 (2019-21); SDG target below 70 by 2030.
- SRS 2023: infant mortality rate 25 per 1,000 live births; total fertility rate 1.9 (Bihar highest at 2.8, Delhi lowest at 1.2); crude birth rate 18.4, crude death rate 6.4.
- WHO Global TB Report 2025: India's TB incidence fell about 21% between 2015 and 2024 (237 to 187 per lakh) and the mortality rate from 28 to 21 per lakh; India still has about a quarter of global cases; treatment coverage reached 92%.
- NFHS-5 (2019-21): about 76% of children 12–23 months fully immunised; institutional births about 89%; stunting about 36%; anaemia in children about 67% and in women 57%.
- India has had no wild poliovirus case since 13 January 2011, but continues IPV and bivalent OPV because the virus persists in Pakistan and Afghanistan.
- India's HIV estimates (2023) put adult prevalence at about 0.2% with roughly 25 lakh people living with HIV.
- National Health Accounts show out-of-pocket spending falling to under 40% of total health expenditure in recent years, though it remains high by global standards.

### Immunisation — schedule, cold chain and new vaccines

- At birth: BCG, OPV-0 and hepatitis B; at 6, 10 and 14 weeks: OPV, pentavalent (DPT + HepB + Hib) and rotavirus; fractional IPV and PCV doses in early infancy.
- At 9–12 months: measles-rubella (MR-1), JE in endemic districts, vitamin A; at 16–24 months: MR-2, DPT and OPV boosters; Td at 10 and 16 years and for pregnant women.
- Additions over time: pentavalent (2011), indigenous rotavirus vaccine Rotavac (2016), PCV (2017), MR campaign (2017).
- HPV: the 2026 drive uses one dose of Gardasil-4 for 14-year-old girls; Serum Institute's indigenous quadrivalent CERVAVAC (2022) is India's own HPV vaccine.
- eVIN (2015) tracks vaccine stocks and storage temperatures in real time; vaccines are kept at +2 to +8 C in ice-lined refrigerators.
- U-WIN, rolled out nationally in 2024, is the digital registry for every vaccination of pregnant women and children up to 16 years.
- AEFI (adverse events following immunisation) surveillance and NTAGI (expert group advising which vaccines to add) are the safety and policy pillars.
- Challenge: India still has a large absolute number of zero-dose children despite high percentage coverage, because of its population size.

### Communicable disease programmes — key features

- NTEP: Ni-kshay portal for case tracking; molecular tests CBNAAT and indigenous Truenat; a shorter all-oral 6-month regimen (BPaLM) for drug-resistant TB adopted in 2024; preventive treatment for contacts; community Ni-kshay Mitras.
- NACP Phase V (2021–26) follows 'test and treat' with free antiretroviral therapy and viral-load testing; prevention of parent-to-child transmission is a priority.
- National Centre for Vector Borne Diseases Control covers malaria, filariasis, dengue, chikungunya, JE and kala-azar; mass drug administration (including triple-drug IDA) targets filariasis.
- Kala-azar: India met its elimination target of under 1 case per 10,000 people at block level in 2023 and is working toward WHO validation.
- Leprosy was eliminated as a public-health problem nationally in 2005 (under 1 per 10,000), but India still reports a large share of global new cases.
- National Viral Hepatitis Control Programme (2018) gives free hepatitis B and C diagnosis and treatment; Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (2004) now runs on the Integrated Health Information Platform.
- National Action Plan for Dog-Mediated Rabies Elimination (2021) aims for zero human rabies deaths by 2030.

### Maternal, child and nutrition schemes — quick matrix

- JSY (2005) = cash for institutional delivery; JSSK (2011) = free services, no cash; PMSMA (2016) = assured antenatal check-up on the 9th of every month, with extended PMSMA for high-risk pregnancies.
- LaQshya (labour rooms), SUMAN (zero-cost assured maternity care), MusQan (child-friendly facilities) are quality initiatives.
- Home-Based Newborn Care by ASHAs, Special Newborn Care Units and Kangaroo Mother Care reduce neonatal deaths.
- National Deworming Day (10 February and 10 August) gives albendazole to children and adolescents aged 1–19 years.
- Anaemia Mukt Bharat '6x6x6': six target groups, six interventions, six institutional mechanisms.
- PM-POSHAN (2021, formerly Mid-Day Meal) and Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0 cover school children, young children and mothers.

### Governance angles and Punjab

- Public health and hospitals are a State subject (List II); the Centre acts through concurrent entries such as population control and family planning, medical education and prevention of inter-state spread of infectious diseases.
- The 15th Finance Commission gave about Rs 70,000 crore of health grants through local bodies; Indian Public Health Standards were revised in 2022.
- Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (renamed from Health and Wellness Centres in 2023) offer an expanded package of 12 primary care services.
- Punjab: Aam Aadmi Clinics (from 15 August 2022; 1,244 by October 2026) provide free consultation, medicines and tests.
- Punjab's Mukh Mantri Sehat Yojana was launched on 22 January 2026 in Mohali, offering every family cashless treatment up to Rs 10 lakh a year for over 2,500 procedures; it builds on AB PM-JAY Mukh Mantri Sehat Bima Yojana (2019, Rs 5 lakh for about 65% of the population).
- PGIMER's 300-bed satellite centre at Sangrur was inaugurated on 25 February 2024; AIIMS Bathinda was dedicated the same day; a 100-bed PGIMER satellite centre is planned at Ferozepur.

**8.** Consider the following statements:

(b) 2 and 3 only  
(c) 1 and 3 only
