## Short notes

- Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in May 1498 via the Cape of Good Hope and was received by the Zamorin; this opened the direct sea route from Europe to India.
- Portuguese: Almeida, first viceroy, followed the 'Blue Water' policy (naval control); Albuquerque captured Goa from Bijapur in 1510; cartaz (pass) system controlled Indian Ocean trade; printing press at Goa (1556).
- Dutch East India Company (VOC, 1602): first factory at Masulipatnam (1605), also Pulicat; mainly focused on Indonesia; defeated by the English at Bedara (1759).
- English East India Company got its charter from Elizabeth I on 31 December 1600; factory at Surat (1613) after Hawkins (1608) and Roe (1615) visited Jahangir.
- Bombay came to the English Crown as dowry of Catherine of Braganza (1661); Fort St George, Madras, was founded by Francis Day (1639–40); Job Charnock founded Calcutta (1690).
- Farrukhsiyar's farman of 1717 gave the Company duty-free trade in Bengal — called the 'Magna Carta' of the Company; its misuse caused conflict with Bengal's nawabs.
- Danes set up at Tranquebar (1620) and Serampore; French East India Company (1664, under Colbert) founded Pondicherry (1674, François Martin).
- Carnatic Wars (Anglo-French): 1st (1746–48), 2nd (1749–54, Dupleix vs Clive), 3rd (1756–63) — French defeated at Wandiwash (1760) by Eyre Coote; Treaty of Paris (1763).
- Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757): Clive defeated Siraj-ud-Daula through Mir Jafar's betrayal; the Company became kingmaker in Bengal.
- Battle of Buxar (22 October 1764): Hector Munro defeated Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula of Awadh and Mughal emperor Shah Alam II; Treaty of Allahabad (1765) gave the Company the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa.
- Dual government in Bengal (1765–72): Company held revenue rights, the nawab held administrative responsibility; ended by Warren Hastings.
- Mysore: four Anglo-Mysore wars against Haider Ali and Tipu Sultan; Treaty of Seringapatam (1792); Tipu died defending Srirangapatna in 1799.
- Marathas: three Anglo-Maratha wars — Treaty of Salbai (1782), Treaty of Bassein (1802, Peshwa accepted subsidiary alliance), Peshwaship abolished after the Third War (1817–18).
- Wellesley's Subsidiary Alliance (1798): the Nizam of Hyderabad was the first to accept; the state kept British troops at its cost and a Resident.
- Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse annexed Satara (1848, first), Jhansi, Nagpur and others; Awadh was annexed in 1856 on grounds of misgovernance.
- Company control was regulated by the Regulating Act 1773 (Governor-General of Bengal, Supreme Court at Calcutta), Pitt's India Act 1784 (Board of Control) and the Charter Acts of 1813, 1833 and 1853.
- Land revenue: Permanent Settlement (Cornwallis, 1793, Bengal), Ryotwari (Munro and Read, Madras and Bombay), Mahalwari (Holt Mackenzie, 1822, North-Western Provinces and later Punjab).

## Punjab angle

- Treaty of Amritsar (1809) between Maharaja Ranjit Singh and Charles Metcalfe fixed the Sutlej as the boundary of Ranjit Singh's power on the east.
- First Anglo-Sikh War (1845–46): battles of Mudki, Ferozeshah, Aliwal and Sobraon; Treaty of Lahore (1846) ceded the Jalandhar Doab and imposed heavy indemnity.
- Second Anglo-Sikh War (1848–49): Chillianwala and Gujrat; Dalhousie annexed Punjab on 29 March 1849 and the Koh-i-Noor passed to the British.
- Punjab was then run by a Board of Administration (Henry and John Lawrence); the Mahalwari-type village settlement was applied in Punjab.

## Deep dive

### Expansion timeline, 1757–1856 (year — event — treaty)

- 1757 — Plassey; 1760 — Mir Qasim replaces Mir Jafar; 22 October 1764 — Buxar: Hector Munro defeats Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula (Awadh) and Shah Alam II.
- 1765 — Treaties of Allahabad: diwani of Bengal, Bihar, Orissa for Rs 26 lakh a year to Shah Alam; Awadh pays Rs 50 lakh and becomes a buffer; 'dual government' in Bengal till 1772; 1770 — Bengal famine.
- Anglo-Mysore Wars: 1767–69 (Treaty of Madras), 1780–84 (Treaty of Mangalore), 1790–92 (Treaty of Seringapatam — half of Mysore lost), 1799 (Tipu killed; Wodeyars restored under subsidiary alliance).
- Anglo-Maratha Wars: 1775–82 (Treaty of Salbai), 1803–05 (after Treaty of Bassein 1802; Assaye, Laswari), 1817–18 (Kirkee, Koregaon; Peshwa pensioned to Bithur).
- 1814–16 — Anglo-Nepal (Gurkha) War, Treaty of Sugauli; Simla hills and Kumaon–Garhwal pass to the Company; Gurkha recruitment begins.
- 1824–26 — First Burmese War (Treaty of Yandabo; Assam, Manipur, Arakan, Tenasserim); 1852 — Lower Burma annexed; 1885 — Upper Burma.
- 1839–42 — First Afghan War (Tripartite Treaty 1838 with Ranjit Singh and Shah Shuja; retreat from Kabul 1842); 1843 — Sind annexed by Charles Napier after Miani and Dabo.
- 1845–46 and 1848–49 — Anglo-Sikh Wars; 29 March 1849 — Punjab annexed; 1856 — Awadh annexed on grounds of misgovernment (Wajid Ali Shah).
- Doctrine of Lapse annexations under Dalhousie: Satara (1848), Jaitpur and Sambalpur (1849), Baghat (1850), Udaipur in Chhattisgarh (1852), Jhansi (1853), Nagpur (1854).

### Governors and Governors-General to 1857 (name — years — landmark)

- Robert Clive — Governor of Bengal 1757–60, 1765–67 — dual government; Mughal grant of diwani; 'Society of Trade'.
- Warren Hastings — Governor 1772, first Governor-General of Bengal 1774–85 — ended dual government, treasury to Calcutta, Rohilla War, Asiatic Society (1784, with William Jones); impeached in Britain (acquitted 1795).
- Lord Cornwallis — 1786–93 — Permanent Settlement (1793), Cornwallis Code, separation of revenue and judicial work, district judges, covenanted civil service; Third Mysore War.
- Sir John Shore — 1793–98 — policy of non-intervention (Battle of Kharda between Marathas and Nizam, 1795).
- Lord Wellesley — 1798–1805 — subsidiary alliance (Hyderabad first, 1798), Fourth Mysore War, Fort William College (1800), Second Maratha War.
- Lord Minto I — 1807–13 — Treaty of Amritsar with Ranjit Singh (1809, Metcalfe); Charter Act 1813.
- Lord Hastings (Moira) — 1813–23 — Gurkha War, Pindari campaign, Third Maratha War; Munro's ryotwari in Madras (1820).
- Lord Amherst — 1823–28 — First Burmese War; Barrackpore mutiny (1824).
- Lord William Bentinck — 1828–35 — sati banned (1829), suppression of thuggee (Sleeman), English Education Act (1835, Macaulay's minute), Mysore (1831) and Coorg (1834) taken; first Governor-General of India from 1833.
- Charles Metcalfe — 1835–36 (acting) — freed the press ('liberator of the Indian press').
- Lord Auckland — 1836–42 — First Afghan War; Lord Ellenborough — 1842–44 — Sind annexed, slavery abolished (Act V of 1843).
- Lord Hardinge I — 1844–48 — First Anglo-Sikh War, treaties of Lahore and Bhyrowal.
- Lord Dalhousie — 1848–56 — Punjab and Lower Burma, lapse, Awadh, first railway (Bombay–Thane, 16 April 1853), telegraph, Post Office Act (1854), Wood's Despatch (1854), Public Works Department.
- Lord Canning — 1856–62 — Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (July 1856), Revolt of 1857; first Viceroy from 1858.

### Parliamentary Acts of the Company era (Act — key provisions)

- Regulating Act 1773 — Governor-General of Bengal with a council of four; Bombay and Madras made subordinate; Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774; Chief Justice Elijah Impey); servants barred from private trade and gifts.
- Amending Act (Act of Settlement) 1781 — limited the Supreme Court's jurisdiction over revenue matters and Company servants' official acts.
- Pitt's India Act 1784 — Board of Control (Crown) over Court of Directors (Company) = 'double government'; council reduced to three; Company territories called 'British possessions in India'.
- Act of 1786 — Governor-General could override his council and also act as Commander-in-Chief (Cornwallis's condition).
- Charter Act 1813 — Indian trade monopoly ended except tea and trade with China; Rs 1 lakh a year for education; Christian missionaries allowed.
- Charter Act 1833 — Governor-General of India (Bentinck first); Company became purely administrative; law member (Macaulay) and Law Commission; Section 87: no Indian barred from office by religion, birth or colour.
- Charter Act 1853 — separated legislative and executive work (Indian Legislative Council of 12); open competition for the civil service; no fixed 20-year renewal.
- Government of India Act 1858 — Crown rule; Secretary of State with a 15-member Council of India; Governor-General also styled Viceroy.

### Land revenue settlements (system — architect — region — features)

- Permanent Settlement (1793) — Cornwallis, with John Shore's inputs — Bengal, Bihar, Orissa, Banaras, north Madras — zamindars as owners; 10/11 of rent to the state fixed for ever; 'sunset law' auctioned defaulting estates.
- Effects: new absentee landlord class, sub-infeudation (patnidars), peasants rack-rented; the state lost later rise in agrarian income.
- Ryotwari — Alexander Read and Thomas Munro (Baramahal 1792; Madras 1820); Bombay (Elphinstone, Wingate and Goldsmid surveys) — settlement directly with cultivators, revised every 20–30 years, rates high.
- Mahalwari — Holt Mackenzie (Regulation VII of 1822), revised by R.M. Bird and James Thomason (Regulation IX of 1833) — North-Western Provinces, Punjab, parts of Central India — settlement with the village/estate (mahal) jointly.
- Punjab after 1849 applied village-community (bhaichara and pattidari) settlements; Punjab Land Alienation Act (1900) later protected 'agricultural tribes' from moneylenders.
- Common outcome everywhere: cash demand, commercialisation of crops (indigo, opium, cotton, jute), rural debt and famines.

### Punjab — from friendship to annexation (date — event)

- 25 April 1809 — Treaty of Amritsar: Sutlej the boundary; the cis-Sutlej states (Patiala, Nabha, Jind, Kaithal etc.) became British-protected.
- 1838 — Tripartite Treaty (British, Ranjit Singh, Shah Shuja); 27 June 1839 — Ranjit Singh dies; Kharak Singh, Nau Nihal Singh (killed by a falling gateway, 1840), Sher Singh (assassinated 1843) follow; Duleep Singh with Rani Jindan as regent.
- First Anglo-Sikh War: Mudki (18 Dec 1845), Ferozeshah (21–22 Dec 1845), Baddowal and Aliwal (Jan 1846), Sobraon (10 Feb 1846); treachery of Lal Singh and Tej Singh is a common theme.
- 9 March 1846 — Treaty of Lahore: Jalandhar Doab ceded, indemnity of Rs 1.5 crore (part paid by ceding Kashmir and hill territory), army limited; 16 March 1846 — Treaty of Amritsar sells Kashmir to Gulab Singh for Rs 75 lakh.
- 16 December 1846 — Treaty of Bhyrowal: British Resident (Henry Lawrence) with a council of regency rules Lahore until Duleep Singh comes of age; Rani Jindan removed.
- April 1848 — Multan revolt of Diwan Mulraj (Vans Agnew and Anderson killed); Second Anglo-Sikh War: Ramnagar (Nov 1848), Chillianwala (13 Jan 1849, heavy British losses), Gujrat (21 Feb 1849, 'battle of the guns').
- 29 March 1849 — Dalhousie annexes Punjab; Board of Administration (Henry and John Lawrence, Charles Mansel); 1853 — John Lawrence Chief Commissioner; 1859 — Punjab becomes a Lieutenant-Governor's province.
- Duleep Singh was sent to Fatehgarh and then to England (1854); the Koh-i-Noor was presented to Queen Victoria (1850).

### Analytical themes — why the British won

- Superior naval power and finance: Bengal revenues after 1765 paid for armies elsewhere ('Bengal as the bridgehead').
- Disciplined sepoy armies trained on European lines, artillery and logistics; Indian rulers' armies divided by factions.
- Diplomacy: subsidiary alliances, buffer states, divide-and-rule among Marathas, Nizam and Mysore.
- Mercantile networks and Indian bankers (Jagat Seths) backed the Company at Plassey.
- Historiography: 'accidental empire' (Seeley, 'in a fit of absence of mind') vs 'sub-imperialism' of men on the spot; P.J. Marshall and C.A. Bayly stress Indian collaborators and the 18th-century context.
- For Punjab, scholars stress the collapse of the Lahore Durbar after 1839 rather than any weakness of the Khalsa army as a fighting force.

### PPSC-style asks and traps

- Hyderabad was the first state to accept the subsidiary alliance (1798); Awadh signed in 1801; Peshwa by the Treaty of Bassein (1802).
- Buxar, not Plassey, made the Company a territorial power; Plassey was won largely by conspiracy.
- Warren Hastings was the first Governor-General of Bengal; Bentinck the first Governor-General of India; Canning the first Viceroy.
- Treaty of Salbai (1782) ended the First Anglo-Maratha War; Treaty of Mangalore (1784) ended the Second Mysore War.
- Chillianwala and Gujrat belong to the Second Anglo-Sikh War; Mudki, Ferozeshah, Aliwal, Sobraon to the First.
- Dual government (Clive, 1765) ≠ 'double government' (Pitt's Act, 1784) — a frequent mix-up.
