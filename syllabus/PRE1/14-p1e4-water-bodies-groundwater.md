## Short notes

- Oceans: Pacific is the largest and deepest (Mariana Trench, Challenger Deep about 11 km); Atlantic is S-shaped with the busiest trade routes; Indian Ocean is the only ocean named after a country; Arctic is the smallest and shallowest.
- Ocean floor relief: continental shelf (shallow, rich fishing and oil), continental slope, abyssal plain, ocean deeps/trenches and mid-ocean ridges (e.g., Mid-Atlantic Ridge).
- Average ocean salinity is about 35 parts per thousand; it is high where evaporation is high and low where rivers, rain or melting ice add fresh water (e.g., Baltic Sea); the Red Sea is among the saltiest seas.
- Very saline inland water bodies: Lake Van (Turkey), Dead Sea and Great Salt Lake (USA).
- Ocean temperature falls with depth; the thermocline separates warm surface water from cold deep water.
- Warm currents: Gulf Stream, North Atlantic Drift, Kuroshio, Brazil; cold currents: Labrador, Canary, California, Humboldt (Peru), Benguela, Oyashio.
- Meeting of warm and cold currents creates rich fishing grounds - Grand Banks off Newfoundland (Gulf Stream and Labrador), off Japan (Kuroshio and Oyashio).
- Tides are caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon and Sun; spring tides at new and full moon, neap tides at the quarter moons; the Bay of Fundy (Canada) has the world's highest tidal range.
- Rivers: Nile is the longest, Amazon carries the most water; in India the Ganga is the longest (about 2,525 km); Himalayan rivers are perennial (snow and rain-fed), peninsular rivers mostly rain-fed and seasonal.
- West-flowing Narmada and Tapi flow through rift valleys and form estuaries; most east-flowing peninsular rivers (Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi) form deltas.
- Indus system: Indus, Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej; Indus Waters Treaty (1960, World Bank-brokered) gave the eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) to India and western rivers to Pakistan; India placed it in abeyance in April 2025.
- Glaciers store most of Earth's fresh water; Siachen is the longest glacier in India's Karakoram region and Gangotri feeds the Bhagirathi; most Himalayan glaciers are retreating.
- Glacial landforms include cirques, moraines and U-shaped valleys; glacial lake outburst floods (GLOFs) are a growing hazard, e.g., South Lhonak lake, Sikkim (October 2023).
- Lakes by origin: tectonic (Baikal - deepest; Caspian - largest by area), crater/impact (Lonar, Maharashtra), lagoons (Chilika - India's largest brackish-water lagoon; Pulicat), oxbow lakes, glacial lakes and man-made reservoirs.
- Wular (J&K) is among India's largest freshwater lakes; Loktak (Manipur) has floating 'phumdis' and the floating Keibul Lamjao National Park.
- Groundwater sits in aquifers (unconfined or confined) below the water table; confined aquifers can feed artesian wells; recharge comes from rain, rivers, canals and irrigation return flow.
- India is the world's largest user of groundwater; CGWB's annual Dynamic Ground Water Resources assessments put national extraction at about 60% of extractable resources, with over-exploited blocks concentrated in the north-west and parts of the south.
- Contaminants: arsenic (Ganga-Brahmaputra plains), fluoride (Rajasthan etc.), nitrate (fertilisers), uranium (parts of Punjab); schemes: Jal Jeevan Mission (2019), Atal Bhujal Yojana (7 states, not Punjab), Jal Shakti Abhiyan - 'Catch the Rain'.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab's groundwater stage of extraction is well above 100% (over 150% in recent CGWB assessments), the highest among states; most of its blocks are over-exploited.
- Punjab Preservation of Subsoil Water Act 2009 bars paddy transplanting before a notified date in June to cut groundwater pumping in peak summer.
- Key water bodies: Sutlej, Beas, Ravi and the seasonal Ghaggar; Harike wetland at the Sutlej-Beas confluence; Bhakra dam (Sutlej, Gobind Sagar reservoir in Himachal), Pong dam (Beas), Ranjit Sagar dam (Ravi); the Sutlej-Yamuna Link canal remains disputed with Haryana.
- Punjab has six Ramsar sites - Harike (1990), Kanjli and Ropar (2002), Nangal, Keshopur-Miani and Beas Conservation Reserve (2019).

Expect questions on ocean currents and fishing grounds, tides, salinity, lake types with examples, Indus treaty allocation and groundwater issues. For Punjab, know rivers, dams, Ramsar sites and the groundwater crisis.

## Deep dive

### Mapping list — Indian rivers (origin — course — key facts)

- Indus: rises near Lake Mansarovar in Tibet, enters India in Ladakh; total length about 2,880 km, about 1,114 km in India; tributaries Zaskar, Shyok, Nubra, Hunza (right) and the Panjnad rivers (left).
- Jhelum: rises at Verinag spring, flows through Wular lake and Srinagar; its tributary Kishanganga (Neelum in PoK) hosts the Kishanganga project.
- Chenab: formed by the Chandra and Bhaga at Tandi (Lahaul); largest Indus tributary in India; projects Salal, Baglihar, Dulhasti, Ratle, Pakal Dul.
- Ravi: rises near Rohtang in the Bara Bhangal area of Himachal, flows past Chamba, enters Punjab at Madhopur (Pathankot); Ranjit Sagar (Thein) and Shahpur Kandi dams.
- Beas: rises at Beas Kund near Rohtang, flows through Kullu and Mandi; Pandoh dam diverts water to the Sutlej via the Beas–Sutlej Link; Pong dam (Maharana Pratap Sagar) at Talwara; joins the Sutlej at Harike; flows wholly within India.
- Sutlej: rises at Rakas Tal near Mansarovar, enters India at Shipki La; antecedent river cutting through the Himalaya; Nathpa Jhakri, Bhakra (Gobind Sagar), Nangal, Ropar and Harike headworks.
- Ghaggar: seasonal river from the Shivaliks, crosses Punjab and Haryana into Rajasthan (Hakra); identified by many scholars with the Vedic Saraswati.
- Ganga: Bhagirathi (Gaumukh, Gangotri glacier) joins Alaknanda at Devprayag; the five prayags on the Alaknanda are Vishnuprayag (Dhauliganga), Nandprayag (Nandakini), Karnaprayag (Pindar), Rudraprayag (Mandakini) and Devprayag (Bhagirathi).
- Brahmaputra: rises from the Chemayungdung glacier as the Yarlung Tsangpo, enters Arunachal as the Siang/Dihang, joined by the Dibang and Lohit; Majuli in Assam is a large river island; China began building a mega hydropower project on the lower Yarlung Tsangpo in July 2025.
- Peninsular: Godavari (Trimbakeshwar, Nashik; longest peninsular river, 'Dakshin Ganga'), Krishna (Mahabaleshwar), Kaveri (Talakaveri, Brahmagiri hills), Mahanadi (Sihawa, Chhattisgarh; Hirakud), Narmada (Amarkantak; rift valley; Sardar Sarovar) and Tapi (Multai, Betul).

### Mapping list — straits, channels and ocean deeps

- India's neighbourhood: Palk Strait (India–Sri Lanka, with Adam's Bridge/Ram Setu), Ten Degree Channel (Andaman–Nicobar), Nine Degree Channel (separates Minicoy from the main Lakshadweep group), Eight Degree Channel (Minicoy–Maldives), Duncan Passage (South Andaman–Little Andaman), Coco Channel (North Andaman–Myanmar's Coco Islands).
- Energy chokepoints: Strait of Hormuz (Iran–Oman; Persian Gulf to Gulf of Oman), Bab-el-Mandeb (Yemen–Djibouti; Red Sea to Gulf of Aden), Strait of Malacca (Malay Peninsula–Sumatra; Indian Ocean to South China Sea).
- Europe and the Mediterranean: Gibraltar (Spain–Morocco), Bosphorus and Dardanelles (Türkiye; Black Sea–Marmara–Aegean), Strait of Dover (England–France), Strait of Messina (Sicily–Italy), Strait of Otranto (Italy–Albania).
- East Asia and Pacific: Taiwan Strait, Korea Strait, Tsugaru (Honshu–Hokkaido), La Pérouse (Hokkaido–Sakhalin), Sunda (Java–Sumatra), Lombok, Torres Strait (Australia–New Guinea), Bass Strait (Australia–Tasmania), Cook Strait (New Zealand's two main islands).
- Americas and Africa: Bering Strait (Asia–North America; Arctic to Pacific), Florida Strait, Strait of Magellan and Drake Passage (South America–Antarctica), Mozambique Channel (Mozambique–Madagascar).
- Deepest trenches: Mariana (Challenger Deep, about 11 km, Pacific), Tonga and Kuril (Pacific), Puerto Rico Trench (deepest in the Atlantic), Java/Sunda Trench (deepest in the Indian Ocean).
- Ocean facts: the Sargasso Sea is the only 'sea' without a coastline; the Great Pacific Garbage Patch sits in the North Pacific gyre; the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (part of the thermohaline 'conveyor belt') is weakening.

### Glaciers, cryosphere and polar research

- Major Indian glaciers: Siachen (Karakoram, about 76 km, feeds the Nubra), Gangotri (feeds the Bhagirathi; retreating), Zemu (Sikkim; largest in the eastern Himalaya), Milam and Pindari (Uttarakhand), Bara Shigri (largest in Himachal, feeds the Chenab), Kolahoi (Kashmir).
- Glacier types: valley (alpine), piedmont (spreading on lowlands), and continental ice sheets — Antarctica holds about 90% of the world's ice.
- The Hindu Kush Himalaya ('Third Pole') holds the largest ice mass outside the polar regions; ICIMOD (2023) warned its glaciers could lose a third to half of their volume by 2100 even at 1.5–2 °C warming.
- Glacial lake outburst floods: the October 2023 South Lhonak lake GLOF in Sikkim destroyed the Teesta-III (Chungthang) dam — the standard modern example.
- Indian research stations: Antarctica — Dakshin Gangotri (1983, now a supply base), Maitri (1989), Bharati (2012); Arctic — Himadri (2008, Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard, Norway); Himalaya — Himansh (2016, Spiti).
- National Centre for Polar and Ocean Research (Goa) runs these stations; the Indian Antarctic Act 2022 brings Indian activities in Antarctica under domestic law.

### Lakes and wetlands — mapping list and Ramsar data

- India: Wular (J&K, large freshwater lake), Dal (Srinagar), Kolleru (Andhra, between the Godavari and Krishna deltas), Loktak (Manipur, phumdis), Sambhar (Rajasthan, largest inland saline lake), Chilika (Odisha, brackish lagoon, Irrawaddy dolphins), Pulicat (AP–TN, Sriharikota barrier), Vembanad (Kerala, longest lake), Pangong Tso and Tso Moriri (Ladakh), Lonar (Maharashtra, meteorite crater in basalt), Renuka (Himachal).
- World: Caspian (largest by area), Superior (largest freshwater by area), Baikal (deepest and oldest, largest freshwater volume), Titicaca (high-altitude navigable), Dead Sea (lowest point on land, about 430 m below sea level), Victoria (source of the White Nile), Tanganyika (long rift lake); the Aral Sea shrank after Soviet irrigation diverted the Amu and Syr Darya.
- Ramsar Convention (1971, Ramsar, Iran; India joined in 1982); Chilika and Keoladeo were India's first sites (1981).
- India had 103 Ramsar sites by October 2026 (latest included Sukhna lake in Chandigarh and Sauj Jheel in Uttar Pradesh), covering about 13.9 lakh hectares; Tamil Nadu has the most (20), followed by Uttar Pradesh (14).
- Montreux Record (sites under threat) for India: Keoladeo National Park and Loktak; Chilika was removed in 2002 after restoration.
- Policy: Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules 2017; National Plan for Conservation of Aquatic Ecosystems (2013, merged lake and wetland schemes); Amrit Dharohar (2023) for community-led Ramsar site management.
- Ramsar COP15 was held at Victoria Falls, Zimbabwe, in July 2025.

### Groundwater — hydrogeology and governance

- Aquifers: unconfined (water table aquifer), confined (between impermeable layers; may be artesian), perched; aquitards slow water, aquicludes block it; porosity is storage, permeability is flow.
- The Indo-Gangetic alluvium holds some of the world's most productive aquifers; hard-rock aquifers of the peninsula store little and fail fast in drought.
- CGWB categories by stage of extraction: safe (under 70%), semi-critical (70–90%), critical (90–100%), over-exploited (over 100%); 'saline' units are unusable without treatment.
- Dynamic Ground Water Resource Assessment 2025: annual recharge 448.52 BCM, extractable 407.75 BCM, extraction 247.22 BCM, national stage 60.63%; of 6,762 units, 73.14% safe, 11.21% semi-critical, 2.97% critical, 10.8% over-exploited, 1.88% saline.
- Punjab: CGWB's 2024 Punjab report put the stage at 156.87% with 115 of 153 units over-exploited; the 2025 figures (about 152%, 110 over-exploited units) show a slight easing as canal irrigation expanded.
- Central Ground Water Authority (constituted in 1997 under the Environment (Protection) Act) regulates extraction through no-objection certificates; NAQUIM (2012) maps aquifers.
- Satellite gravity data (NASA's GRACE mission) first exposed rapid groundwater loss in north-west India around 2009.

### Punjab's water bodies and wetlands

- Harike: barrage (1950s) at the Sutlej–Beas confluence created a large wetland across Tarn Taran, Ferozepur and Kapurthala; it feeds the Indira Gandhi (Rajasthan) canal and the Sirhind and Rajasthan feeders.
- Kanjli: on the Kali Bein near Kapurthala (PPSC 2018 asked its district); the Kali Bein is linked with Guru Nanak at Sultanpur Lodhi.
- Beas Conservation Reserve: about 185 km of the Beas from Talwara to Harike; home of India's only Indus river dolphins; gharials have been reintroduced since 2017.
- Keshopur-Miani (Gurdaspur) is a chain of 'chhambs' (seasonal marshes) managed as a community reserve; Ranjit Sagar Dam and Rakh Sarai Amanat Khan are conservation reserves.
- Reservoirs serving Punjab: Gobind Sagar (Bhakra, Sutlej), Maharana Pratap Sagar (Pong, Beas) and Ranjit Sagar (Ravi); Shahpur Kandi (completed 2024) lets India use more Ravi water.

### Oceans — India-specific facts

- India's Exclusive Economic Zone is about 2.02 million sq km; the coastline, long quoted as about 7,516 km, was re-measured in 2024–25 with a finer-scale method at about 11,098 km — know both figures.
- Deep Ocean Mission (2021) includes Samudrayaan — the crewed submersible Matsya-6000 designed for 6,000 m depth.
- India became the first 'pioneer investor' (1987) with rights to explore polymetallic nodules in the Central Indian Ocean Basin under the International Seabed Authority.
- Coral reefs in India: Gulf of Mannar, Gulf of Kachchh, Lakshadweep (atolls) and Andaman–Nicobar (fringing reefs); NOAA declared the fourth global coral bleaching event in 2024.
- Upwelling off the Somali and Kerala coasts in the monsoon brings nutrients (sardine fishery); the Arabian Sea hosts one of the world's largest oxygen-minimum zones.
- BBNJ ('High Seas') Agreement entered into force on 17 January 2026 after reaching 60 ratifications; India signed in 2024 and was preparing domestic legislation to ratify.

## Practice MCQs (12)

**1.** Under the Indus Waters Treaty (1960), the eastern rivers allotted to India are:

(a) Ravi, Beas and Sutlej  
(b) Indus, Jhelum and Chenab  
(c) Jhelum, Ravi and Beas  
(d) Chenab, Sutlej and Indus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Ravi, Beas and Sutlej** — Eastern rivers went to India and western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) to Pakistan; India placed the treaty in abeyance in April 2025.

</details>

**2.** The Grand Banks fishing ground is formed by the meeting of the:

(a) Gulf Stream and Labrador Current  
(b) Canary and Benguela currents  
(c) Kuroshio and Oyashio  
(d) Humboldt and El Nino current

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Gulf Stream and Labrador Current** — Mixing of warm and cold water off Newfoundland supports plankton and fish.

</details>

**3.** Consider: 1. Spring tides occur at new moon and full moon. 2. Neap tides occur when the Sun, Moon and Earth are in a straight line. Which is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 1 only** — Neap tides occur at the quarter moons when the Sun and Moon pull at right angles.

</details>

**4.** Which is India's largest brackish-water lagoon?

(a) Pulicat  
(b) Wular  
(c) Chilika  
(d) Vembanad

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Chilika** — Chilika on the Odisha coast is the largest brackish-water lagoon in India; Wular is a freshwater lake.

</details>

**5.** The Harike wetland lies at the confluence of:

(a) Ravi and Beas  
(b) Sutlej and Ghaggar  
(c) Sutlej and Beas  
(d) Ravi and Chenab

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Sutlej and Beas** — Harike, a Ramsar site since 1990, is formed by a barrage at the Sutlej-Beas confluence.

</details>

**6.** Which Punjab law bars early paddy transplanting to conserve groundwater?

(a) Punjab Preservation of Subsoil Water Act, 2009  
(b) Punjab Water Resources (Management and Regulation) Act, 2020  
(c) Punjab Land Preservation Act, 1900  
(d) Northern India Canal and Drainage Act, 1873

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Punjab Preservation of Subsoil Water Act, 2009** — The 2009 Act fixes a notified date in June before which paddy nurseries and transplanting are not allowed.

</details>

**7.** Consider the following statements: 1. The Beas river flows entirely within India. 2. The Sutlej enters India through Shipki La. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

</details>

(a) 1 and 2 only  
(b) 2 and 3 only  
(c) 1 and 3 only  
(d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 1 and 2 only** — Wular is in Jammu and Kashmir (Bandipora), not Ladakh.

**9.** Which channel separates the Andaman Islands from the Nicobar Islands?

(a) Ten Degree Channel  
(b) Duncan Passage  
(c) Nine Degree Channel  
(d) Eight Degree Channel

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Ten Degree Channel** — Duncan Passage lies within the Andamans; the Nine and Eight Degree Channels are near Lakshadweep.

</details>

**10.** Devprayag is the confluence of the:

(a) Bhagirathi and Alaknanda  
(b) Alaknanda and Pindar  
(c) Alaknanda and Mandakini  
(d) Yamuna and Tons

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Bhagirathi and Alaknanda** — Below Devprayag the combined stream is called the Ganga.

</details>

**11.** Under CGWB norms, an assessment unit is categorised as 'critical' when its stage of groundwater extraction is:

(a) 70–90%  
(b) 90–100%  
(c) Less than 70%  
(d) More than 100%

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 90–100%** — 70–90% is semi-critical and above 100% is over-exploited.

</details>

**12.** India's Arctic research station Himadri is located in:

(a) Ny-Ålesund, Svalbard (Norway)
