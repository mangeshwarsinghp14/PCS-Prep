## Short notes

- Vedic age is divided into Early (Rig Vedic) c. 1500–1000 BCE and Later Vedic c. 1000–600 BCE.
- The Rig Vedic people settled in Sapta Sindhu, the land of seven rivers: the Indus and its tributaries plus the Saraswati (called naditama, best of rivers).
- Vedic river names: Vitasta (Jhelum), Asikni (Chenab), Parushni (Ravi), Vipas (Beas), Shutudri (Sutlej).
- Rig Veda: oldest text, 10 mandalas and 1028 hymns; mandalas 2–7 are the oldest 'family books'; the 9th is devoted to Soma.
- The 10th mandala has the Purusha Sukta, the first mention of the four varnas; the Gayatri mantra (3rd mandala, to Savitri) is ascribed to Vishvamitra.
- Four Vedas: Rig (hymns), Sama (chants set to melody, root of Indian music), Yajur (sacrificial formulae; Shukla and Krishna recensions), Atharva (charms, spells, folk medicine).
- Each Veda has Brahmanas (ritual), Aranyakas (forest texts) and Upanishads (philosophy, Vedanta); 'Satyameva Jayate' comes from the Mundaka Upanishad.
- Six Vedangas: Shiksha (phonetics), Kalpa (ritual), Vyakarana (grammar), Nirukta (etymology), Chhanda (metre), Jyotisha (astronomy).
- Battle of Ten Kings (Dasharajna) on the Parushni (Ravi): Sudas of the Bharata clan defeated a confederacy of ten chiefs.
- Rig Vedic polity was tribal: rajan as chief; assemblies sabha, samiti, vidatha, gana; women attended sabha and vidatha; bali was a voluntary offering.
- Rig Vedic economy was mainly pastoral; cattle were wealth and 'gavishti' (search for cows) meant war; yava (barley) was the main crop.
- Rig Vedic gods: Indra (most hymns, war god, 'Purandara'), Agni (intermediary), Varuna (upholder of rita, cosmic order), Soma; worship by prayer and sacrifice.
- Later Vedic: centre shifts to the Ganga-Yamuna doab (Kuru-Panchala); iron (krishna ayas) and Painted Grey Ware; settled agriculture; territorial janapadas.
- Later Vedic kingship grew stronger with sacrifices such as Rajasuya, Vajapeya and Ashvamedha; the samiti and sabha lost importance.
- Later Vedic society: varna became rigid and birth-based, gotra and ashrama ideas emerged; Prajapati, Vishnu and Rudra rose in importance.
- Women: Rig Vedic hymns by women such as Ghosha, Apala, Lopamudra; Upanishadic women thinkers Gargi and Maitreyi; overall status declined in the later period.
- Original home of Aryans is debated (Central Asia is the most accepted view); the Boghazkoi inscription (Turkey, c. 14th century BCE) names Indra, Mitra, Varuna and the Nasatyas.

## Punjab angle

- The Rig Vedic heartland, Sapta Sindhu, broadly corresponds to undivided Punjab; most Rig Vedic rivers are Punjab's five rivers.
- The Battle of Ten Kings, the main political event of the Rig Veda, was fought on the Parushni (Ravi).
- The word 'Punjab' (five waters) is Persian and much later; in Vedic times the region was known by its rivers.

## How PPSC asks it

Typical questions: Vedic names of rivers, which Veda or mandala contains what, Upanishad sources of famous phrases, assemblies, and early vs later Vedic contrasts. Memorise river names and the Rig Veda's structure.

## Deep dive

### Chronology and sources (year/phase — development)

- c. 1500–1000 BCE — Early (Rig) Vedic phase in Sapta Sindhu; c. 1000–600 BCE — Later Vedic phase centred on the Ganga–Yamuna doab (Kuru–Panchala).
- c. 1380 BCE — Boghazkoi (Anatolia) treaty between Hittites and Mitanni invokes Indra, Mitra, Varuna and the Nasatyas — external evidence for early Indo-Aryan speakers.
- Avesta (Iranian scripture) shares deities and terms with the Rig Veda (Ahura ~ Asura, Haoma ~ Soma) — evidence of a common Indo-Iranian past.
- Painted Grey Ware (PGW, c. 1200/1000–600 BCE) is linked with Later Vedic settlements: Hastinapur, Atranjikhera, Ahichchhatra, Bhagwanpura (Haryana).
- Iron appears c. 1000 BCE ('krishna ayas' or 'shyama ayas' = black metal in later texts); Atranjikhera (UP) gives early iron tools.
- Max Müller's 1500 BCE date was a working guess; B.G. Tilak ('The Arctic Home in the Vedas', 1903) and others proposed much earlier dates — know the debate exists.
- Rig Veda is a 'shruti' text transmitted orally for centuries; it was written down much later; Sayana (14th c., Vijayanagara) wrote the classic commentary.

### Vedic literature map — what is where

- Rig Veda: 10 mandalas, 1028 hymns (including 11 valakhilya), about 10,600 verses; mandalas 2–7 'family books' of seers (Gritsamada, Vishvamitra, Vamadeva, Atri, Bharadvaja, Vasishtha).
- Mandala 3 — Gayatri (to Savitri, by Vishvamitra); Mandala 7 — Vasishtha and the Battle of Ten Kings; Mandala 9 — Soma; Mandala 10 — Purusha Sukta and Nasadiya Sukta (creation hymn).
- Sama Veda: 1549 (or 1875) verses, almost all from the Rig Veda, set to melody — origin of Indian music; Yajur Veda: Shukla (Vajasaneyi) and Krishna (Taittiriya, Maitrayani) recensions.
- Atharva Veda: charms, healing, marriage and funeral hymns; it calls sabha and samiti the 'two daughters of Prajapati'.
- Brahmanas: Aitareya (Rig), Shatapatha (Shukla Yajur; the largest, tells the story of Videgha Mathava taking fire east to Videha), Panchavimsha (Sama), Gopatha (Atharva).
- Upanishads: 108 traditional (about 13 principal); Chandogya and Brihadaranyaka are oldest; Katha has Nachiketa–Yama dialogue; Mundaka gives 'Satyameva Jayate'; Jabala first lists four ashramas.
- Post-Vedic: six Vedangas, Sutras (Shrauta, Grihya, Dharma, Shulba — Shulba Sutras are the earliest Indian geometry), Upavedas (Ayurveda, Dhanurveda, Gandharvaveda, Shilpaveda).
- Six darshanas: Nyaya (Gautama), Vaisheshika (Kanada, atomism), Sankhya (Kapila), Yoga (Patanjali), Purva Mimamsa (Jaimini), Uttara Mimamsa/Vedanta (Badarayana).

### Rig Vedic vs Later Vedic — comparison table

- Region — Rig: Punjab and Afghanistan (Sapta Sindhu); Later: Ganga–Yamuna doab, Kosala, Videha; Rig mentions the Ganga only once and the Yamuna three times.
- Economy — Rig: pastoral, cattle as wealth (gavishti = war for cows); Later: settled agriculture, rice (vrihi), iron ploughs, 'ploughing with 24 oxen' in Shatapatha.
- Polity — Rig: tribal jana under rajan, assemblies sabha, samiti, vidatha, gana; Later: territorial janapada/rashtra, rituals Rajasuya, Vajapeya, Ashvamedha, and ratnins (royal officials).
- Society — Rig: varna fluid, mentions of 'dasa' and 'dasyu'; Later: four varnas hardened, gotra exogamy, ashramas (three, then four).
- Women — Rig: attended assemblies, women seers Ghosha, Apala, Lopamudra, Vishvavara; Later: excluded from assemblies, status declined (Aitareya Brahmana calls a daughter a source of sorrow).
- Gods — Rig: Indra (250 hymns), Agni (200), Varuna (rita, cosmic order), Soma; Later: Prajapati, Vishnu and Rudra rise; ritual (yajna) dominates.
- Taxes — Rig: bali was voluntary; Later: bali, bhaga, shulka become regular; the 'sangrahitri' (treasurer) and 'bhagadugha' (tax collector) appear among ratnins.
- Metals — Rig: ayas (copper/bronze); Later: iron (shyama ayas) and tin, lead, silver.

### Key terms that PPSC likes (term — meaning)

- Rita — cosmic and moral order guarded by Varuna; Satya and Dharma develop from it.
- Gavishti — 'search for cows', a term for war; gopa/gopati — protector of cows (a title of the king).
- Vish — the people/clan; vishpati — clan chief; kula — family; grama — group of families (not village in the later sense).
- Purohita — chief priest (Vasishtha and Vishvamitra in the Rig Veda); senani — army chief; gramani — head of a grama.
- Nishka — gold ornament later used as a unit of value; krishala — a gold weight; no coins in the Vedic period.
- Vidatha — oldest assembly, with distribution of booty and a role for women; samiti — tribal assembly that could elect the king.
- Panis — rich traders/cattle-lifters disliked in hymns; dasas/dasyus — opponents with 'dark skin' and 'without rites' (interpretations vary).
- Rajasuya — consecration; Vajapeya — chariot race; Ashvamedha — horse sacrifice asserting paramountcy.

### Sapta Sindhu and Punjab — the regional angle

- Rig Vedic rivers and their modern names: Sindhu (Indus), Vitasta (Jhelum), Asikni (Chenab), Parushni (Ravi), Vipasha (Beas), Shutudri (Sutlej), Sarasvati, Drishadvati (often identified with the Chautang), Kubha (Kabul), Gomati (Gomal).
- Nadistuti hymn (Rig Veda 10.75) lists rivers from east to west — a geography lesson in itself.
- Rig Veda 3.33 describes Vishvamitra asking the Vipasha and Shutudri to calm their waters so the Bharatas could cross — a 'Punjab rivers' hymn.
- The Bharata tribe (from which 'Bharatavarsha' later derives) and the Purus were leading Rig Vedic tribes; Kurus formed later by Bharata–Puru merger.
- Kurukshetra (Haryana) — the Later Vedic 'Brahmavarta'/'Kurukshetra' between Sarasvati and Drishadvati — ritual heartland of the Kurus.
- PGW sites in Punjab–Haryana: Bhagwanpura (Kurukshetra), Dadheri, Katpalon, Nagar (Punjab), Ropar Period II — showing Vedic-age settlement continuity in the region.
- Panini (c. 5th–4th century BCE, from Shalatura near Attock) and the learning centre of Taxila keep the Punjab–Gandhara region central to late-Vedic scholarship.

### Aryan question — historiography and debates

- Migration (not 'invasion') model: Indo-European language family (William Jones, 1786) and Indo-Aryan speakers entering from Central Asia in waves c. 2000–1500 BCE.
- Indigenous ('Out of India') views link the Vedic people with the Harappans and the Sarasvati; most linguists and geneticists do not support this.
- Genetic studies (2018–19, including the Rakhigarhi DNA) show a Steppe ancestry component entering South Asia in the 2nd millennium BCE, absent in Harappan samples.
- Material markers argued for Aryans: horse and spoked-wheel chariot (Sinauli, UP, c. 2000–1800 BCE, chariot-like carts — still debated), fire altars, PGW.
- Absence of cities in the Rig Veda vs Harappan urbanism is the strongest cultural contrast; 'pur' means fortified settlement, Indra is 'purandara' (breaker of forts).
- Exam approach: present the evidence (language, texts, archaeology, genetics) and conclude that Vedic culture was a synthesis, not a single 'race'.

### Traps and frequent errors

- Gayatri mantra is in Mandala 3 (not 10); Purusha Sukta is in Mandala 10 (not 9).
- The word 'Hindu' and 'Punjab' are not Vedic; 'Arya' is a cultural/linguistic term, not a racial one.
- Rig Vedic people did not use iron or coins; rice is barely mentioned (vrihi is Later Vedic).
- 'Satyameva Jayate' — Mundaka Upanishad; 'Asato ma sadgamaya' — Brihadaranyaka Upanishad.
- Shulba Sutras (geometry of altars) are part of Kalpa Sutras; they contain a statement equivalent to the Pythagorean theorem.
- The four ashramas are first clearly named in the Jabala Upanishad; the Chandogya mentions only three.
- Indra is the most-praised god (about 250 hymns), Agni second (about 200).
