## Short notes

- Growth: real GDP growth averaged roughly 6-7% a year after 1991; peaks above 9% in 2005-08; slowdown after the 2008 crisis; sharp contraction in 2020-21 due to COVID-19, followed by strong recovery.
- MoSPI released a new GDP series with base year 2022-23 in Feb 2026 (earlier 2011-12); under it, growth for 2025-26 was estimated at about 7.6% (Second Advance Estimates).
- Fiscal deficit = total expenditure minus (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts) - i.e. total borrowing need of the government.
- Revenue deficit = revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts; effective revenue deficit = revenue deficit minus grants for creation of capital assets; primary deficit = fiscal deficit minus interest payments.
- FRBM Act 2003 originally targeted a 3% fiscal deficit and elimination of revenue deficit; N.K. Singh Committee (2017) recommended debt as the main anchor (60% general government: 40% Centre, 20% states).
- Centre's fiscal deficit peaked at about 9.2% of GDP in 2020-21; it was brought down to about 4.4% in 2025-26 and budgeted at 4.3% for 2026-27, with a shift towards a debt-to-GDP target.
- Balance of payments: current account (merchandise trade, services, transfers like remittances, income) plus capital account (FDI, FPI, external loans, banking flows).
- India runs a large merchandise trade deficit offset partly by a services surplus (IT and business services) and remittances - India is the world's largest recipient of remittances.
- 1991 crisis: forex reserves fell to a few weeks of import cover; today reserves are among the world's largest, crossing US$700 billion in 2024.
- Exports of goods and services crossed US$800 billion in 2024-25; services exports are growing faster than merchandise; Foreign Trade Policy 2023 targets US$2 trillion exports by 2030.
- Inflation: high in 1991 and again double-digit CPI in 2009-13; Urjit Patel Committee (2014) led to flexible inflation targeting (2016) - CPI target 4% with a band of 2-6%, set by a six-member Monetary Policy Committee.
- Inflation fell to very low levels in 2025 owing to food price deflation; CPI base year was revised to 2024 (new series released Feb 2026).
- Services: share in GVA rose to over half, led by IT-BPM, telecom, finance, trade and transport; India's growth is described as services-led, unlike the manufacturing-led path of East Asia.
- Structural gap: agriculture still employs the largest share of workers while contributing under one-fifth of GVA; manufacturing share has stayed in the mid-teens.
- Banking: twin balance-sheet problem of the 2010s (bad loans of banks and over-leveraged firms) addressed through IBC 2016, recapitalisation and recovery; bank NPAs fell to multi-year lows by 2024-25.
- Tax: GST (2017) widened the indirect-tax base; GST rationalisation in Sept 2025 moved most goods to two main slabs (5% and 18%) with a 40% rate for luxury and sin goods.
- Commerce and trade policy: shift from protection to openness after 1991, renewed FTAs in the 2020s, and production-linked incentives to raise export competitiveness.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab Economic Survey 2025-26: real GSDP growth 6.1%, agriculture contracted 2.5% due to the 2025 floods; sector shares agriculture 23%, industry 29%, services 48%; per capita income Rs 2,30,523.
- Punjab Budget 2026-27 (presented 8 March 2026 by FM Harpal Singh Cheema): outlay Rs 2,60,437 crore with fiscal deficit around 4.1% of GSDP.
- Punjab's outstanding debt-to-GSDP ratio is among the highest of Indian states, making fiscal consolidation a recurring issue.

## Deep dive

### Latest data dashboard (as of November 2026)

- GDP series: base year moved from 2011-12 to 2022-23 (MoSPI, 27 February 2026); under it real growth was 7.2% (2023-24) and 7.1% (2024-25).
- 2025-26 Provisional Estimates (5 June 2026): real GDP growth 7.7%, real GVA growth 7.9%, nominal GDP about ₹346 lakh crore (nominal growth 8.9%); Q4 growth 7.8%.
- Q1 2026-27 (April–June 2026): real GDP growth 7.8% (6.9% a year earlier); services 10%, industry 8.6%, agriculture 3.6%.
- Per capita Net National Income 2025-26 (Second Advance Estimates, current prices): about ₹2.08 lakh.
- CPI: new series with base 2024 replaced base 2012 (first print January 2026: 2.75%); September 2026 CPI inflation 5.54%, food inflation 7.74% — highest since October 2024.
- RBI (7 October 2026): repo rate raised 25 bp to 5.50% (from 5.25%), stance changed from neutral to 'calibrated tightening'; FY27 projections — CPI 5.2%, GDP 7.1%; first hike since February 2023.
- Union Budget 2026-27 (1 February 2026): fiscal deficit 4.3% of GDP, revenue deficit 1.5%, effective revenue deficit 0.3%, primary deficit 0.7%; Central debt 55.6% of GDP.
- External 2025-26: total exports about US$ 860 billion (goods US$ 442 billion, services US$ 418 billion); overall goods-and-services trade deficit about US$ 119 billion.
- Jobs: PLFS Annual Report 2025 (calendar 2025) unemployment rate 3.1% (usual status, 15+); monthly PLFS (current weekly status) put it at 5.5% in June 2026.

### Growth phases since 1991

- 1991-92: crisis year, growth about 1%; 1992–97: recovery to about 6.5–7% with the Eighth Plan.
- 1997–2003: slowdown to about 5.5% (Asian crisis, droughts, sanctions after 1998 tests, high real interest rates).
- 2003–08: boom close to 9% a year — high savings (over 35% of GDP at peak), investment and global upswing.
- 2008–14: global financial crisis, fiscal stimulus, then high inflation and 'policy paralysis'; growth dipped below 6% in 2012–14.
- 2016–20: demonetisation (2016), GST transition (2017), NBFC stress (IL&FS, 2018); growth slowed to about 4% in 2019-20.
- 2020-21: COVID contraction of about 5.8% (2011-12 series), followed by about 9.7% rebound in 2021-22.
- 2022–27: growth of roughly 7–8% a year led by public capex, services and (in the new series) strong manufacturing.

### Fiscal performance — numbers to quote (Union Budget 2026-27)

- Total expenditure ₹53.47 lakh crore; capital expenditure ₹12.22 lakh crore; effective capital expenditure ₹17.15 lakh crore.
- Revenue receipts ₹35.33 lakh crore (net tax ₹28.67 lakh crore, non-tax ₹6.66 lakh crore); non-debt capital receipts include ₹80,000 crore 'other receipts' (disinvestment).
- Fiscal deficit ₹16.96 lakh crore (4.3%), down from 4.4% (RE 2025-26), 4.8% (2024-25) and 9.2% (2020-21).
- Interest payments ₹14.04 lakh crore — about 40% of revenue receipts, the single biggest spending item.
- Fiscal anchor shifted from deficit to debt: Central debt targeted at 50 ± 1% of GDP by 2030-31.
- 16th Finance Commission (2026–31): states' share of the divisible pool kept at 41%; Centre's fiscal deficit to fall to 3.5% of GDP by 2030-31; states' deficit capped at 3% of GSDP; off-budget borrowing to be brought on budget.
- FRBM history: 2003 Act (3% FD target), repeatedly deferred (2008 crisis, 2020 pandemic); 2018 amendment adopted debt targets after the N.K. Singh review.
- Quality of spending improved: capex rose from about 1.7% of GDP (2019-20) to over 3% in recent budgets.

### Inflation and monetary policy since 1991

- Before 2016 RBI used a 'multiple indicator approach' (1998) and earlier monetary targeting (1985 Chakravarty Committee).
- Double-digit CPI in 2009–13 led to the Urjit Patel Committee (2014) and the Monetary Policy Framework Agreement (February 2015).
- RBI Act amended in 2016: CPI target 4% (±2%) for five-year periods; target retained for 2021–26.
- Policy corridor: SDF (floor) = repo − 25 bp; MSF (ceiling) = repo + 25 bp; after October 2026 repo of 5.50%, SDF is 5.25% and MSF 5.75%.
- Rate cycle 2025: repo cut from 6.50% to 6.25% (February), 6.00% (April), 5.50% (June, with CRR cut from 4% to 3% in stages) and 5.25% (December 2025).
- 2026 turn: food prices, West Asia-linked crude prices and a weaker rupee lifted inflation (August 4.8%, September 5.5%), prompting the October 2026 hike.
- Base effects and food volatility explain most CPI swings; core inflation is steadier and guides the medium-term view.

### External sector and balance of payments

- 2025-26: goods exports US$ 441.8 billion vs goods imports US$ 775.0 billion → merchandise deficit about US$ 333 billion.
- Services exports US$ 418.3 billion vs services imports US$ 204.4 billion → services surplus about US$ 214 billion, which offsets nearly two-thirds of the goods deficit.
- Remittances (current transfers) of over US$ 100 billion a year keep the current account deficit small — India is the largest remittance recipient.
- Capital account: FDI, FPI, ECBs and NRI deposits; net FDI has weakened in recent years as repatriation by investors rose.
- Forex reserves grew from about US$ 1 billion (mid-1991) to well above US$ 600 billion in the 2020s.
- Exchange-rate regime: market-determined since 1993, with RBI intervention to curb volatility; IMF classifies it as a managed float.
- Foreign Trade Policy 2023 (no end date) targets US$ 2 trillion exports by 2030.

### Structure and services

- Services account for over half of GVA and about 30% of employment; IT-BPM and global capability centres drive exports.
- The new GDP series raised the weight of household (unincorporated) enterprises using ASUSE and PLFS data and uses GST and MCA filings more widely.
- Double deflation is now used for manufacturing and agriculture; this changed sector growth rates compared with the old series.
- Agriculture still employs about 43% of workers (PLFS 2025) but produces under a fifth of GVA — the core structural imbalance.
- Manufacturing grew at double digits in 2023-24 and 2025-26 in the new series, but its share remains in the mid-teens.
- Economic Survey 2025-26 frames the next phase as 'strategic resilience' — supply-chain security, AI, energy and critical minerals.

### Punjab's performance (PRS analysis of Budget 2026-27)

- GSDP 2026-27 (current prices) estimated at ₹9,80,635 crore, nominal growth of 10%.
- Outstanding liabilities 45.1% of GSDP at end-2026-27 — among the highest of large states; guarantees a further 2.7% of GSDP.
- Committed expenditure ₹90,335 crore = 72% of revenue receipts (salaries 31%, interest 23%, pensions 18%).
- Power subsidy ₹15,550 crore (12.3% of revenue receipts): agriculture 50%, domestic consumers 37%, industry 13%.
- Own tax revenue ₹70,851 crore (7.2% of GSDP); SGST ₹32,000 crore is about 45% of it.
- Central transfers ₹39,653 crore (31% of revenue receipts); Punjab's share under the 16th Finance Commission rose to 2.00% (1.81% under the 15th, 1.58% under the 14th).
- Revenue deficit 2.2% of GSDP means part of borrowing pays for current spending, not assets.

### Traps and confusions

- Fiscal deficit uses non-debt receipts; borrowing is the financing item, not a receipt in the FD formula.
- Effective revenue deficit is smaller than revenue deficit because capital-asset grants to states are excluded.
- Repo is the rate at which RBI lends; SDF is the floor of the corridor since April 2022 (it replaced fixed reverse repo as the floor).
- New CPI base (2024) and new GDP base (2022-23) are different years — a favourite statement trap.
- Nominal growth is lower than usual when inflation is low (e.g. 2025-26), which hurts tax buoyancy and debt ratios.
- Remittances are in the current account; FDI and NRI deposits are in the capital/financial account.
- Trade deficit (goods only) differs from the goods-and-services deficit and from the current account deficit.
