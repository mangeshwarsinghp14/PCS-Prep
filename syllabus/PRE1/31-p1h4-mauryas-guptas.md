## Short notes

- Chandragupta Maurya (c. 321–297 BCE) overthrew the Nandas with the help of Chanakya (Kautilya), author of the Arthashastra.
- He defeated Seleucus Nicator (c. 305 BCE), gaining territories in the north-west; Megasthenes came as Seleucid envoy and wrote Indica.
- Jain tradition says Chandragupta became a Jain with Bhadrabahu and died by fasting (sallekhana) at Shravanabelagola.
- Bindusara ('Amitraghata') kept ties with the Seleucids (envoy Deimachus) and favoured the Ajivikas.
- Ashoka (c. 268–232 BCE): Kalinga war in his 8th regnal year (c. 261 BCE), described in Major Rock Edict XIII; turned to Dhamma.
- Dhamma: tolerance, non-violence, respect for elders and all sects; Dhamma Mahamattas appointed; missions abroad including to Sri Lanka (Mahendra, Sanghamitra).
- Ashokan edicts are mostly in Prakrit in Brahmi script; Kharosthi in the north-west (Shahbazgarhi, Mansehra); Greek and Aramaic near Kandahar. James Prinsep deciphered Brahmi in 1837.
- Ashoka is named directly only in a few minor edicts such as Maski; elsewhere he is 'Devanampiya Piyadasi'. The Sarnath Lion Capital is India's national emblem.
- Mauryan administration: Arthashastra's saptanga theory; Samaharta (revenue), Sannidhata (treasury); provinces under princes; Megasthenes describes Pataliputra run by six boards of five members.
- Mauryan art: polished monolithic pillars (Chunar sandstone), Barabar caves for Ajivikas, stupas; the empire ended when Pushyamitra Shunga killed Brihadratha (c. 185 BCE).
- Guptas: Chandragupta I (Gupta era from 319–320 CE) married Lichchhavi princess Kumaradevi and took the title Maharajadhiraja.
- Samudragupta's conquests are recorded in the Allahabad Pillar Prashasti composed by Harishena; V.A. Smith called him the 'Napoleon of India'; his veena-playing coins show his love of music.
- Chandragupta II Vikramaditya defeated the Shakas of western India, issued silver coins; Chinese pilgrim Fa-Hien (Faxian) visited; daughter Prabhavatigupta married into the Vakatakas.
- Kumaragupta I is credited with founding Nalanda; Skandagupta repelled the Hunas, and his Junagadh inscription records repair of the Sudarshana lake.
- Literature: Kalidasa (Abhijnanashakuntalam, Meghaduta, Raghuvamsha), Amarasimha's Amarakosha, Vishnu Sharma's Panchatantra; Sanskrit became the court language.
- Science: Aryabhata's Aryabhatiya (499 CE) — earth's rotation, value of pi, place-value; Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita and Panchasiddhantika.
- Art: early structural temples (Dashavatara temple, Deogarh; brick temple at Bhitargaon), Sarnath school Buddha images, Mehrauli iron pillar; abundant gold coins (dinara).
- Gupta decline: Huna invasions (Toramana, Mihirakula), rise of feudatories, land grants (agrahara) to Brahmanas and officials weakening central control.

## Punjab angle

- After Alexander's invasion (Battle of the Hydaspes/Jhelum against Porus, 326 BCE), Chandragupta Maurya expelled the Greek garrisons from Punjab before or while taking Magadha.
- Buddhist tradition says prince Ashoka was sent by Bindusara to suppress a revolt at Taxila, then a Mauryan provincial capital.
- The Huna ruler Mihirakula, who weakened the Guptas, ruled from Sakala (Sialkot) in Punjab.

## How PPSC asks it

Expect questions on edicts and inscriptions (which edict says what, who composed which prashasti), foreign visitors (Megasthenes, Fa-Hien), Gupta scholars and their works, and administrative terms. Memorise inscription–ruler–content pairs.

## Deep dive

### Dynasty tables — Mauryas and Guptas (ruler — dates — what to remember)

- Chandragupta Maurya — c. 321–297 BCE — ended the Nandas with Kautilya; treaty with Seleucus (c. 303 BCE) gave Arachosia, Gedrosia, Paropamisadae; marriage alliance; 500 elephants to Seleucus.
- Bindusara — c. 297–273 BCE — 'Amitraghata'; asked Antiochus I for sweet wine, dried figs and a sophist; Ajivika patron; extended rule into the Deccan.
- Ashoka — accession c. 273, coronation c. 269, rule to c. 232 BCE — Kalinga war in 8th regnal year (c. 261 BCE); Dhamma policy; Third Buddhist Council.
- Later Mauryas — Dasharatha (Nagarjuni caves to Ajivikas), Samprati (Jain patron), … Brihadratha, killed by his general Pushyamitra Shunga c. 185 BCE.
- Samudragupta (c. 335–375) — Allahabad prashasti by Harishena; 'grahana-moksha-anugraha' (capture, release, reinstate) for 12 southern kings; V.A. Smith's 'Napoleon of India'; veena-player and Ashvamedha coins.
- Chandragupta II Vikramaditya (c. 375–415) — defeated the Shaka ruler Rudrasimha III and took Gujarat–Saurashtra; daughter Prabhavatigupta married Vakataka Rudrasena II; Faxian visited (399–414).
- Kumaragupta I (c. 415–455) — 'Mahendraditya'; Nalanda founded in his reign; Kartikeya (peacock) coins; faced the Pushyamitras late in his reign.
- Skandagupta (c. 455–467) — repulsed the Hunas (Bhitari pillar); his governor Parnadatta's son Chakrapalita repaired the Sudarshana lake (Junagadh inscription).
- Decline — Budhagupta, Bhanugupta (Eran inscription, 510 CE), Narasimhagupta Baladitya; Huna rulers Toramana and Mihirakula; Yashodharman of Mandasor defeated Mihirakula (c. 528).

### Mauryan administration — Arthashastra vs Megasthenes

- Arthashastra: 15 books (adhikaranas), saptanga theory — swami (king), amatya, janapada, durga (fort), kosha (treasury), danda (army), mitra (ally).
- 18 tirthas (high functionaries) including mantri, purohita, senapati, yuvaraja, samaharta (revenue collection) and sannidhata (treasury/storehouses).
- Superintendents (adhyakshas): sitadhyaksha (crown lands), akaradhyaksha (mines), lakshanadhyaksha (mint), sulkadhyaksha (tolls), panyadhyaksha (trade), navadhyaksha (shipping), ganikadhyaksha (courtesans).
- Provinces under kumaras/aryaputras: Uttarapatha (Taxila), Avanti (Ujjain), Dakshinapatha (Suvarnagiri), Kalinga (Tosali), Prachya (centre, Pataliputra).
- Ashokan officials in edicts: rajuka (land/justice), pradeshika (district), yukta (accounts), dhamma-mahamatta (from 13th regnal year), stri-adhyaksha-mahamatta (women's welfare).
- Megasthenes ('Indica', surviving in fragments via Strabo, Arrian, Diodorus): seven 'castes' of Indian society; Pataliputra run by 6 boards of 5 members each; army run by 6 boards; agronomoi (district officers) and astynomoi (city officers).
- Megasthenes wrongly says there was no slavery and that Indians did not know writing — use as an example of foreign-source limits.
- Land revenue (bhaga) usually one-sixth; sita = income from crown lands; pindakara = village tax; Sudarshana lake built by Pushyagupta (Chandragupta's governor of Saurashtra).
- Historiography: older view of a highly centralised bureaucratic state (Arthashastra-based); Romila Thapar later described a 'metropolitan core' with 'core' and 'peripheral' regions controlled with varying intensity.

### Ashokan edicts — the complete map

- 14 Major Rock Edicts at Girnar (Gujarat), Kalsi (Dehradun), Shahbazgarhi and Mansehra (Kharosthi, Pakistan), Dhauli and Jaugada (Odisha), Sopara, Yerragudi, Sannati and others.
- At Dhauli and Jaugada, Edicts XI–XIII are replaced by two 'Separate Kalinga Edicts' addressed to officials ('all men are my children').
- RE II — medical care for men and animals; names Cholas, Pandyas, Satiyaputras, Keralaputras and Tambapanni (Sri Lanka) beyond the frontier.
- RE XIII — Kalinga war (150,000 deported, 100,000 killed) and 'dhamma-vijaya' sent to five Greek kings (Antiochus II, Ptolemy II, Antigonus Gonatas, Magas, Alexander).
- 7 Pillar Edicts — Pillar Edict VII (longest) only on the Delhi–Topra pillar; Topra (Yamunanagar, Haryana) and Meerut pillars were moved to Delhi by Firoz Shah Tughlaq.
- Minor Rock Edicts — the personal name 'Ashoka' appears only at Maski (Karnataka, 1915), Gujarra (MP), Nittur and Udegolam (Karnataka); elsewhere 'Devanampiya Piyadasi'.
- Bhabru (Bairat) edict — his faith in Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha; Rummindei pillar — Lumbini's tax reduced to one-eighth; Queen's edict — gifts of Karuvaki, mother of Tivara.
- Kandahar bilingual edict in Greek and Aramaic; Taxila has an Aramaic inscription — the north-west was multilingual.
- Pillar capitals: Sarnath (four lions), Rampurva (bull and lion), Lauriya Nandangarh (single lion), Sankisa (elephant); pillars of Chunar sandstone with a mirror polish.

### Gupta administration, economy and society

- Titles: Maharajadhiraja, Paramabhattaraka, Parameshvara — larger claims than the Mauryas; divine comparison of kings.
- Units: desha/bhukti (province, under uparika) → vishaya (district, under vishayapati) → vithi → grama; kumaramatya as a cadre of high officers.
- Damodarpur copper plates (Bengal) show district boards with nagarashreshthi (guild head), sarthavaha (caravan leader), prathama-kulika (artisan head) and prathama-kayastha (scribe).
- Taxes: bhaga (share of produce), bhoga (periodic supplies), kara, udranga and uparikara (extra levies), hiranya (cash); vishti (forced labour) increasingly mentioned.
- Land grants (agrahara) to Brahmanas with fiscal and administrative rights — R.S. Sharma's 'Indian feudalism' thesis; critics (B.D. Chattopadhyaya) see state formation rather than fragmentation.
- Coins: largest number of gold coins (dinara) of any ancient Indian dynasty, but purity falls under later rulers; silver coins only after the conquest of western India.
- Trade: Roman trade declines after the 3rd century; Byzantine silk route and South-East Asian links grow; guilds (shrenis) act as banks.
- Society: rise of Kayasthas, Chandalas living outside villages (Faxian); first inscriptional evidence of sati at Eran (510 CE); Smritis of Yajnavalkya, Narada, Brihaspati.

### Science, literature and art — 'classical age' file

- Aryabhata (b. 476 CE) — Aryabhatiya (499 CE): earth rotates on its axis, causes of eclipses, value of pi to four decimals; India's first satellite named after him (1975).
- Varahamihira — Pancha Siddhantika, Brihat Samhita (encyclopaedia), Brihat Jataka; Brahmagupta (Brahmasphutasiddhanta, 628 CE, Bhinmal) belongs to the post-Gupta age.
- Kalidasa — plays Abhijnanashakuntalam, Malavikagnimitram (about Agnimitra Shunga), Vikramorvashiyam; poems Raghuvamsha, Kumarasambhava, Meghaduta, Ritusamhara.
- Other works: Vishakhadatta (Mudrarakshasa — Chandragupta Maurya; Devichandraguptam — Ramagupta story), Shudraka (Mrichchhakatika), Amarasimha (Amarakosha), Vishnu Sharma (Panchatantra), Vatsyayana (Kamasutra).
- Temples: Deogarh Dashavatara (earliest shikhara traces), Bhitargaon (brick, Kanpur), Tigawa and Sanchi Temple 17 (flat roof), Nachna-Kuthara Parvati temple.
- Sculpture: Sarnath seated Buddha (dharmachakra mudra), Mathura standing Buddha, Udayagiri (Vidisha) Varaha panel, Sultanganj copper Buddha (now in Birmingham).
- Metallurgy: Mehrauli iron pillar (inscription of 'Chandra', likely Chandragupta II) — rust-resistant for 1600 years.
- Ajanta caves 16, 17 (paintings) date mainly to the Vakataka ruler Harishena's time (5th century) — contemporary with, but not built by, the Guptas.

### Punjab–Haryana in the Mauryan–Gupta age

- Alexander's Punjab campaign (326 BCE): Ambhi of Taxila submitted; Porus fought at the Hydaspes (Jhelum); his army refused to cross the Beas (Hyphasis) — Alexander turned back.
- Taxila was the capital of Uttarapatha; Buddhist legend says prince Ashoka was sent by Bindusara to quell a revolt there, and links Ashoka's son Kunala with the city.
- Yaudheyas — a martial republic of the Sutlej–Yamuna area (Haryana–east Punjab); coins and coin-moulds from Rohtak area ('Yaudheya-gana' coins); defeated by Rudradaman, later listed in the Allahabad prashasti.
- Allahabad prashasti lists frontier republics paying tribute to Samudragupta: Malavas, Arjunayanas, Yaudheyas, Madrakas (Sialkot area), Abhiras and others — a Punjab–Rajasthan belt.
- Hunas: Toramana and Mihirakula ruled from Sakala (Sialkot); Sanghol has yielded coins and seals linked to them; Hiuen Tsang tells of Mihirakula's persecution of Buddhists.
- After the Guptas, the Pushyabhutis of Sthaneshvara (Thanesar, Kurukshetra) rose — Harshavardhana (606–647) began his rule from Haryana.
- Ropar's Gupta-period levels yielded gold coins of Chandragupta and a 'vina-vadini' plaque — local evidence of Gupta reach into the Sutlej valley.

### PPSC-style asks and traps

- Sudarshana lake 'continuity' chain: built by Pushyagupta (Chandragupta Maurya), conduits by Tushaspha (Ashoka), repaired by Rudradaman (c. 150 CE, Junagadh — first long Sanskrit inscription) and by Chakrapalita under Skandagupta (455–56 CE).
- Allahabad pillar carries three layers: Ashoka's edicts, Samudragupta's prashasti and Jahangir's inscription.
- Faxian (Chandragupta II), Hiuen Tsang (Harsha), I-tsing (late 7th century) — match pilgrim to king.
- Samudragupta did not annex the south — he reinstated the defeated kings; he did annex Aryavarta kings by force ('prasabhoddharana').
- Kalinga war is described in RE XIII, not in a pillar edict; Ashoka's name appears in only four minor rock edicts.
- 'Navaratnas' of Vikramaditya is a later legend — Varahamihira and Kalidasa may not be contemporaries of Chandragupta II.
- Gupta Sanskrit inscriptions vs Mauryan Prakrit edicts — language shift is a frequent statement question.
