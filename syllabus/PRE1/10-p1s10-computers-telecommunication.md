## Short notes

- Charles Babbage designed the Analytical Engine and is called the father of the computer; Ada Lovelace wrote the first algorithm for it; Alan Turing laid the theory of computation.
- Generations: 1st vacuum tubes (ENIAC, 1946), 2nd transistors, 3rd integrated circuits, 4th microprocessors (Intel 4004, 1971), 5th AI and parallel processing.
- CPU = ALU (calculations) + control unit + registers; RAM is volatile working memory, ROM non-volatile; cache is fast memory near the CPU; SSDs (flash) are replacing hard disks.
- Data units: bit (0/1), byte = 8 bits, 1 KB = 1,024 bytes; computers use the binary system; ASCII and Unicode encode text (Unicode covers Gurmukhi and other Indian scripts).
- Software: system software (operating systems - Windows, Linux, Android, macOS) and application software; a compiler translates the whole program at once, an interpreter line by line; Linux is open-source (Linus Torvalds); BOSS Linux by C-DAC.
- India's first supercomputer PARAM 8000 (1991) was built by C-DAC under Vijay Bhatkar; the National Supercomputing Mission began in 2015.
- Internet history: ARPANET (1969); TCP/IP protocols; World Wide Web invented by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN (1989); public internet in India launched by VSNL on 15 August 1995.
- IPv4 uses 32-bit addresses, IPv6 128-bit; DNS converts domain names to IP addresses; HTTPS encrypts web traffic.
- Networks: LAN, MAN, WAN; modem (modulator-demodulator), router, switch; cloud computing models IaaS, PaaS, SaaS; government cloud is 'MeghRaj'.
- Emerging technology: AI and machine learning, blockchain, Internet of Things, quantum computing (qubits; National Quantum Mission approved 2023), semiconductors (India Semiconductor Mission 2021).
- Optical fibre communication uses total internal reflection; offers high bandwidth and low loss; BharatNet aims to connect all gram panchayats with optical fibre.
- Satellite communication uses geostationary satellites; NavIC (IRNSS) is India's regional navigation system; GPS is the US global system.
- Mobile generations: 1G analog voice, 2G digital (GSM, SMS), 3G mobile data, 4G LTE, 5G (launched in India on 1 October 2022) with high speed and very low latency; Bharat 6G Vision targets 6G by 2030.
- Short-range wireless: Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, NFC (contactless payments), RFID (FASTag toll collection).
- Cyber threats: virus, worm, trojan, ransomware (WannaCry, 2017), phishing, DDoS; CERT-In is the national nodal agency for cyber incidents.
- Laws and regulators: IT Act 2000 (e-signatures, cyber offences), Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, Telecommunications Act 2023 (replaced Indian Telegraph Act 1885); TRAI (1997) regulates telecom.
- Digital public infrastructure: Digital India (2015), Aadhaar, UPI (NPCI, 2016), DigiLocker, ONDC, CoWIN - together called 'India Stack'.
- Moore's law: the number of transistors on a chip roughly doubles about every two years.

## Punjab angle

- Semi-Conductor Laboratory (SCL), Mohali, under the Ministry of Electronics and IT, is India's government-owned chip fabrication and design facility.
- Mohali has developed as Punjab's IT and electronics hub; Sewa Kendras deliver government services to citizens through an online system.

## Deep dive

### Computer architecture and data — beyond basics

- Von Neumann architecture (1945) stores program and data in the same memory; Harvard architecture keeps them separate.
- Memory hierarchy, fastest to slowest: registers, cache (L1/L2/L3), RAM, SSD/hard disk, cloud or tape; virtual memory uses disk space as an extension of RAM.
- ROM types: PROM (write once), EPROM (erased with ultraviolet light), EEPROM and flash (pen drives, SSDs, firmware).
- Number systems: binary, octal, decimal and hexadecimal (used for colour codes and memory addresses); a nibble is 4 bits; a MAC address is 48 bits.
- AND, OR and NOT are basic logic gates; NAND and NOR are 'universal' because any circuit can be built from either.
- Processors: CISC (x86) vs RISC (ARM, open RISC-V); IIT Madras's SHAKTI processors use RISC-V; ISRO and SCL Mohali's 32-bit Vikram-3201 (180 nm) was presented at Semicon India 2025.
- Supercomputers are rated in FLOPS; AIRAWAT (C-DAC Pune) ranked 75th on the TOP500 list in 2023; three PARAM Rudra supercomputers were dedicated in September 2024 under the National Supercomputing Mission.
- Quantum computers use qubits (superposition, entanglement); India's first full-stack 25-qubit machine 'Indus' by QpiAI (Bengaluru) was launched in April 2025 under the National Quantum Mission.
- Operating systems manage processes, memory, files and devices; the defence ministry adopted the Ubuntu-based Maya OS (2023) for security.

### Internet and networking — layers and protocols

- OSI model has seven layers (physical, data link, network, transport, session, presentation, application); the practical TCP/IP model has four.
- Protocols: HTTP/HTTPS (web), FTP (files), SMTP (sending mail), POP3/IMAP (receiving mail), DNS (names to IP addresses), DHCP (assigns IP addresses), TCP (reliable delivery), UDP (fast, for live video and gaming), VoIP (calls).
- Topologies: star (most LANs), bus, ring, mesh (most robust), tree; devices: hub (broadcasts), switch (forwards by MAC address), router (forwards by IP address), gateway, repeater.
- Media: twisted pair, coaxial, optical fibre (single-mode for long distance), wireless (Wi-Fi standards 802.11), and Li-Fi using light.
- Internet governance: ICANN (domain names and IP allocation), IETF (technical standards), W3C (web standards); NIXI runs the .in registry and Indian internet exchanges.
- BharatNet's amended phase (approved August 2023, about Rs 1.39 lakh crore) aims to connect all gram panchayats with optical fibre.
- Satellite internet providers need a GMPCS licence; Starlink received DoT authorisation in 2025, joining OneWeb and Jio-SES.
- Common confusion: a browser (Chrome) displays pages, a search engine (Google) finds them; the internet is the network, the web is one service on it.

### Telecom technology — generations, spectrum, satellites

- Low frequency bands (such as 700 MHz) travel far and enter buildings; high bands (26 GHz millimetre-wave) carry huge data over short distances.
- 5G has three use-cases: enhanced mobile broadband, ultra-reliable low-latency communication (remote surgery, automation) and massive machine-type communication (IoT); network slicing gives dedicated virtual networks.
- India rolled out 5G from October 2022 at one of the fastest rates worldwide, installing over 4.5 lakh 5G base stations.
- BSNL's nationwide 4G network launched in September 2025 runs on an indigenous stack built by TCS, Tejas Networks and C-DOT, putting India in a small group of countries with their own telecom stack.
- Telecommunications Act 2023 replaced the 1885 Telegraph Act; spectrum is mostly auctioned, but satellite spectrum can be assigned administratively.
- Sanchar Saathi (portal 2023) lets users block lost phones, see connections issued in their name and report fraud calls ('Chakshu').
- Satellite communication: GSAT-N2 (GSAT-20), a Ka-band high-throughput satellite, was launched on SpaceX's Falcon 9 in November 2024; LVM3-M5 launched CMS-03, the heaviest communication satellite launched from India, in November 2025.
- NavIC: the navigation satellite NVS-02 was carried on GSLV-F15 on 29 January 2025 — ISRO's 100th launch from Sriharikota — but a valve fault stopped it reaching its intended orbit.
- Optical fibres work by total internal reflection; Charles Kao won the 2009 Nobel for fibre communication, and Narinder Singh Kapany, born in Moga (Punjab) in 1926, is called the 'father of fibre optics'.

### Cyber security, AI and digital law (2025–26 updates)

- Malware: virus (needs a host file), worm (self-spreading), trojan, spyware, ransomware (WannaCry 2017), keylogger, botnet; social engineering includes phishing, vishing (calls), smishing (SMS) and the 'digital arrest' scam.
- Report cyber fraud on cybercrime.gov.in or helpline 1930, run by the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C, MHA, 2020).
- CERT-In directions (2022) require cyber incidents to be reported within 6 hours; NCIIPC protects critical information infrastructure under Section 70A of the IT Act.
- The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 became operational through the DPDP Rules notified in November 2025, with phased compliance over 12–18 months and a Data Protection Board.
- Encryption: symmetric (AES, one key) vs asymmetric (RSA, public and private keys); digital signatures are legally valid under the IT Act; quantum computers threaten today's public-key encryption.
- IndiaAI Mission (2024, about Rs 10,372 crore) funds compute, datasets, foundation models and safe AI; India hosted the AI Impact Summit in New Delhi in February 2026, where 88 countries and organisations adopted the New Delhi Declaration.
- Blockchain is a distributed tamper-evident ledger; RBI's e-rupee (central bank digital currency) retail pilot began in December 2022.

### Digital public infrastructure — numbers

- UPI recorded an all-time high of 20.70 billion transactions worth Rs 27.28 lakh crore in October 2025, with a single-day record of about 754 million on 18 October 2025.
- Aadhaar is a 12-digit identity number issued by UIDAI under the Aadhaar Act 2016; the Supreme Court upheld it with limits in Puttaswamy (2018).
- Other layers: DigiLocker, eSign, Account Aggregator (RBI framework), ONDC (open commerce), ABHA health IDs, Bhashini (AI translation), GeM, FASTag and DigiYatra.
- During its G20 Presidency (2023) India set up the Global DPI Repository to share such systems with other countries.
- PMGDISHA (2017) aimed to make 6 crore rural people digitally literate; PM-WANI enables public Wi-Fi through local data offices; Common Service Centres deliver e-services in villages.
- India has over 95 crore internet subscribers (TRAI), but gaps by gender, age, region and language persist.

### Punjab links

- Narinder Singh Kapany (born in Moga, 1926) pioneered fibre optics and coined the term.
- Semi-Conductor Laboratory (SCL), Mohali, is India's government-owned chip fab (180 nm CMOS) and co-developed Vikram-3201; a Rs 4,500 crore modernisation over three years was announced in November 2025, with SCL to stay government-run.
- CDIL, Mohali, received India Semiconductor Mission approval in August 2025 to expand power-semiconductor production.
- C-DAC has a centre at Mohali working on e-health and hospital information systems; IIT Ropar hosts a technology innovation hub on agriculture and water (AWaDH) under the cyber-physical systems mission.
- Punjab's Sewa Kendras and doorstep service delivery (helpline 1076, since December 2023) depend on networked databases and e-governance.
- Fake immigration-agent and 'digital arrest' frauds are widespread in Punjab; the 1930 helpline and Punjab Police cyber cells are the first response.

## Practice MCQs (12)

**1.** Which memory loses its contents when power is switched off?

(a) Hard disk  
(b) Flash drive  
(c) ROM  
(d) RAM

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b) Charles Babbage

**7.** Consider the following statements:  
1. NAND and NOR are called universal logic gates.  
2. EPROM chips can be erased using ultraviolet light.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Both 1 and 2** — Both are correct: NAND and NOR gates can build any other logic gate, so they are 'universal', and EPROM is erased by ultraviolet light through its quartz window. EEPROM, by contrast, is erased electrically.

</details>

**8.** Consider the following statements:

2. DHCP translates domain names into IP addresses.  
3. SMTP is used to send e-mail.

(a) 1 and 2 only  
(b) 2 and 3 only  
(c) 1 and 3 only  
(d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) 1 and 3 only** — DNS translates names; DHCP assigns IP addresses.

</details>
