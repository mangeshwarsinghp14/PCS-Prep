## Short notes

- Russia under Tsar Nicholas II (Romanov dynasty) was an autocracy; serfdom was abolished in 1861 but peasants remained poor and land-hungry.
- Late industrialisation created a small but concentrated working class in St Petersburg and Moscow with long hours and poor wages.
- Russian Social Democratic Labour Party (1898) split in 1903 into Bolsheviks (led by Lenin) and Mensheviks; the Socialist Revolutionaries spoke for peasants.
- Defeat in the Russo-Japanese War (1904–05) and 'Bloody Sunday' (January 1905, a march led by Father Gapon fired upon) triggered the 1905 Revolution; the Tsar conceded an elected Duma.
- World War I brought huge military losses, food and fuel shortages and inflation; the influence of Rasputin discredited the court.
- February Revolution (March 1917 by the Western calendar): strikes and women's demonstrations in Petrograd, soldiers joined; Nicholas II abdicated.
- A Provisional Government (Prince Lvov, later Alexander Kerensky) shared power with the Petrograd Soviet — called 'dual power'.
- Lenin returned in April 1917 and issued the April Theses: end the war, land to peasants, banks nationalised, 'All power to the Soviets'; the slogan was 'Peace, Land and Bread'.
- October Revolution (25 October 1917, Julian = 7 November Gregorian): Bolsheviks led by Lenin and Trotsky seized the Winter Palace through the Military Revolutionary Committee.
- Early Soviet decrees: Decree on Peace and Decree on Land; banks and industries nationalised; the elected Constituent Assembly was dissolved in January 1918.
- Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (March 1918) with Germany took Russia out of World War I at the cost of large territories.
- Civil War (1918–21) between the Reds and the Whites (aided by foreign powers); Trotsky built the Red Army; the Tsar and his family were executed in July 1918.
- War Communism during the civil war gave way to Lenin's New Economic Policy (NEP, 1921), allowing small private trade.
- The USSR was formed in December 1922; after Lenin's death (1924) Stalin rose to power and launched Five-Year Plans (from 1928) and forced collectivisation of agriculture.
- Russia switched from the Julian to the Gregorian calendar in 1918, which explains the two sets of dates.
- Impact: first socialist state; Comintern (1919) supported communist parties worldwide; inspired anti-colonial movements; the Communist Party of India was formed at Tashkent (1920) by M.N. Roy and others; India's planning drew on the Soviet model.

## Punjab angle

- Bhagat Singh studied Lenin and socialist writings; the HSRA added 'Socialist' to its name in 1928 under this influence.
- Former Ghadarites influenced by the Soviet revolution started the Kirti journal (1926, Santokh Singh), leading to the Kirti Kisan Party in Punjab in the late 1920s.

## How PPSC asks it

Questions test February vs October revolutions, calendar difference, Lenin's April Theses and slogans, Brest-Litovsk, NEP and the Indian impact. Remember dual dates and who led which government.

## Deep dive

### Long background, 1825–1916 (year — event)

- 1825 — Decembrist revolt of liberal army officers crushed by Nicholas I; first modern revolutionary martyrs.
- 1861 — Alexander II emancipates the serfs; peasants must pay 'redemption payments' for land over 49 years and stay tied to the village commune (mir).
- 1881 — Alexander II assassinated by the populist group People's Will; Alexander III follows a policy of repression and Russification.
- 1890s — Finance minister Sergei Witte drives rapid industrialisation with foreign loans; Trans-Siberian Railway begun (1891); factory workers crowded in St Petersburg and Moscow.
- 1898 and 1901 — Russian Social Democratic Labour Party founded (Minsk, 1898); Socialist Revolutionary (SR) party formed for the peasantry (1901).
- 1902–03 — Lenin's 'What Is to Be Done?' argues for a disciplined party of professional revolutionaries; the 1903 London congress splits Bolsheviks (majority) from Mensheviks (minority).
- 1904–05 — Russo-Japanese War: defeats at Port Arthur and the naval disaster at Tsushima (May 1905).
- 1905 — Bloody Sunday (9 January old style); general strikes; Potemkin mutiny (June); St Petersburg Soviet (Trotsky prominent); October Manifesto promises an elected Duma and civil liberties.
- 1906–11 — Four Dumas (1906–17) have little power; P.A. Stolypin's land reforms let peasants leave the commune, alongside harsh repression ('Stolypin's necktie'); Stolypin assassinated 1911.
- 1912 — Lena goldfields massacre of striking miners revives labour unrest.
- 1914–16 — Russia enters World War I (August 1914); St Petersburg renamed Petrograd; Nicholas II takes personal command (1915) leaving the Empress and Rasputin influential at court; Rasputin murdered by nobles (December 1916).

### 1917–18 step by step (old-style dates; add 13 days for the Western calendar)

- 23 February (8 March) 1917 — International Women's Day bread protests and strikes in Petrograd; within days the garrison mutinies.
- 2 March (15 March) — Nicholas II abdicates; his brother Michael declines the throne; 300 years of Romanov rule end.
- Dual power: Provisional Government (Prince Lvov; Milyukov foreign minister; Kerensky justice) versus the Petrograd Soviet, whose Order No. 1 put army units under soldiers' committees.
- 3 April — Lenin reaches Petrograd's Finland Station after crossing Germany in a 'sealed train'; the April Theses demand no support for the Provisional Government and 'All power to the Soviets'.
- June–July — Kerensky's summer offensive fails; the July Days riots are blamed on the Bolsheviks; Lenin hides in Finland; Kerensky becomes Prime Minister.
- August — General Kornilov's failed march on Petrograd; Bolsheviks are armed to defend the city and win majorities in the Petrograd and Moscow Soviets; Trotsky becomes chairman of the Petrograd Soviet (September).
- 10 October — Bolshevik Central Committee votes for armed insurrection (Zinoviev and Kamenev oppose).
- 24–25 October (6–7 November) — Military Revolutionary Committee seizes key points; the cruiser Aurora fires a signal; the Winter Palace is taken; the Second All-Russian Congress of Soviets approves Soviet power.
- 26 October — Decree on Peace and Decree on Land; Council of People's Commissars (Sovnarkom) with Lenin as chairman, Trotsky for foreign affairs, Stalin for nationalities.
- December 1917 — Cheka (secret police) set up under Felix Dzerzhinsky.
- November 1917–January 1918 — Constituent Assembly elections give the SRs the largest share and Bolsheviks roughly a quarter; the Assembly is dispersed after one day (January 1918).
- March 1918 — Treaty of Brest-Litovsk gives up Poland, the Baltic lands and Ukraine; capital moves to Moscow; the party renames itself the Russian Communist Party (Bolsheviks).
- July 1918 — First Soviet constitution (RSFSR); the royal family is shot at Yekaterinburg.

### Civil war to Stalin, 1918–1938

- 1918–21 — Civil War: Reds against Whites (Kolchak, Denikin, Yudenich, Wrangel) plus foreign intervention (Britain, France, USA, Japan) and the Czech Legion; Reds win through central position, Trotsky's Red Army and White disunity.
- War Communism — grain requisitioning, nationalisation of all industry, ban on private trade; it caused famine and peasant revolts.
- March 1921 — Kronstadt sailors' revolt crushed; Tenth Party Congress bans factions and adopts the New Economic Policy (tax in kind, private small trade and farming).
- 1922 — Treaty of Rapallo with Germany (April) ends diplomatic isolation; USSR formed (30 December) by Russia, Ukraine, Belorussia and Transcaucasia.
- 1922–24 — Stalin becomes General Secretary (1922); Lenin's 'Testament' warns against him; Lenin dies 21 January 1924; Petrograd renamed Leningrad.
- Succession struggle: Trotsky's 'permanent revolution' versus Stalin's 'socialism in one country'; Trotsky expelled (1927), exiled (1929), murdered in Mexico (1940).
- 1928 — First Five-Year Plan (heavy industry: steel, coal, power; Magnitogorsk, Dnieper dam).
- 1929–33 — Forced collectivisation into kolkhozes; 'liquidation of the kulaks as a class'; famine of 1932–33 (in Ukraine called the Holodomor).
- 1936–38 — 'Stalin Constitution' (1936) on paper; Great Purge and Moscow show trials (Zinoviev, Kamenev, Bukharin) in practice.

### Parties, ideas and terms

- Bolsheviks — Lenin; centralised vanguard party, alliance of workers and poor peasants, armed seizure of power.
- Mensheviks — Martov, Plekhanov; broad mass party, expected a long bourgeois-democratic stage before socialism.
- Socialist Revolutionaries (SRs) — Viktor Chernov; peasant socialism and land to those who till it; Left SRs briefly joined Sovnarkom.
- Kadets (Constitutional Democrats) — Milyukov; liberal constitutional monarchy, later republic; dominated the first Provisional Government.
- Soviet — elected council of workers' and soldiers' deputies; first formed in 1905.
- Dictatorship of the proletariat, democratic centralism, vanguard party — core Leninist terms often asked in Mains.
- Kulak — better-off peasant; kolkhoz — collective farm; sovkhoz — state farm; Gosplan — state planning body (1921).
- Comintern (Third International, 1919, Moscow) — directed communist parties abroad; dissolved 1943.

### Impact on India and Punjab

- Communist Party of India founded at Tashkent on 17 October 1920 by M.N. Roy, Abani Mukherji and others; M.N. Roy debated Lenin on colonial strategy at the Comintern's Second Congress (1920).
- Government replied with conspiracy cases: Peshawar (1922–23), Kanpur Bolshevik (1924) and Meerut (1929–33), which jailed trade-union and communist leaders.
- Punjab: former Ghadarites turned leftward; Santokh Singh started the Punjabi monthly 'Kirti' (1926); the Kirti Kisan Party was formed (1928) with leaders such as Sohan Singh Josh.
- Naujawan Bharat Sabha (Lahore, 1926) and the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (renamed 1928) show the socialist turn of Bhagat Singh and his comrades; Bhagat Singh also wrote for 'Kirti'.
- Jawaharlal Nehru visited Moscow for the tenth anniversary (1927) and Rabindranath Tagore in 1930 ('Letters from Russia'); both admired Soviet education and planning.
- Planning idea: Soviet Five-Year Plans influenced the National Planning Committee (1938, Nehru chair) and the Planning Commission (1950); the Mahalanobis model of the Second Plan stressed heavy industry.
- Global: inspired anti-colonial movements, led Western states to fear communism (Red Scare), and set up the ideological split that became the Cold War.

### Historiography

- Soviet official view — a proletarian revolution led by a party that expressed the will of the masses.
- Liberal/'totalitarian' school (Richard Pipes, 'The Russian Revolution', 1990) — October was a coup by a small conspiratorial party.
- Social historians (Sheila Fitzpatrick, Alexander Rabinowitch) — the Bolsheviks had real support in Petrograd factories and garrisons in autumn 1917.
- Orlando Figes, 'A People's Tragedy' (1996) — the revolution as a popular upheaval that ended in tragedy.
- John Reed, 'Ten Days That Shook the World' (1919) — sympathetic American eyewitness account of October; Lenin wrote a short introduction.
- Exam use: 'Was October a revolution or a coup?' — show both views, then judge by the land seizures and soldier desertions that made Bolshevik rule possible.

### Traps and quick facts

- Calendar: Russia used the Julian calendar until February 1918, so 'February' and 'October' revolutions fall in March and November by the Western calendar.
- Kerensky was a Socialist Revolutionary (Trudovik), not a Bolshevik; he led the Provisional Government from July 1917.
- The Duma came from 1905; the Soviets also began in 1905 — both before 1917.
- 'Peace, Land and Bread' is the Bolshevik slogan; 'All power to the Soviets' comes from the April Theses.
- Brest-Litovsk (March 1918) was with Germany and its allies; it was annulled after Germany's defeat in November 1918.
- NEP was introduced in 1921 under Lenin, not by Stalin; Stalin ended it with the first Five-Year Plan (1928).
- USSR formed in December 1922, not 1917; it dissolved in December 1991.

**3.** 'Bloody Sunday' (1905) in St Petersburg involved a peaceful march led by:

(a) Rasputin  
(b) Father Gapon  
(c) Plekhanov  
(d) Kerensky

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Father Gapon** — Troops fired on Father Gapon's petition march, sparking the 1905 Revolution.

**4.** Consider the statements:  
1. The October Revolution is so named because it took place in October according to the Gregorian calendar.  
2. Tsar Nicholas II abdicated before Lenin returned to Russia in April 1917.  
Which is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** — Statement 1 is wrong: the revolution took place on 25 October by the Julian calendar then used in Russia, which is 7 November by the Gregorian calendar. Statement 2 is correct: Nicholas II abdicated in March 1917, before Lenin returned to Russia in April 1917.

</details>

**5.** Lenin introduced the New Economic Policy (NEP) in:

(a) 1928  
(b) 1924  
(c) 1917  
(d) 1921

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) 1921** — NEP replaced War Communism in 1921; Five-Year Plans began in 1928 under Stalin.

</details>

**6.** The Bolshevik slogan in 1917 was:

(a) Blood and Iron  
(b) No taxation without representation  
(c) Peace, Land and Bread  
(d) Liberty, Equality, Fraternity

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Peace, Land and Bread** — 'Peace, Land and Bread' captured the demands of soldiers, peasants and workers.

</details>

**7.** After the 1903 split of the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party, the Menshevik faction was led by:

(a) Alexander Kerensky  
(b) Leon Trotsky  
(c) Julius Martov  
(d) Georgi Plekhanov

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Julius Martov** — At the 1903 London congress Lenin's group won a vote and took the name Bolsheviks ('majority'), while Julius Martov led the Mensheviks ('minority'). Plekhanov, the 'father of Russian Marxism', sided with the Mensheviks later; Kerensky was not a Social Democrat.

</details>

**8.** Who headed the Russian Provisional Government when the Bolsheviks seized power in October 1917?

(a) Pavel Milyukov  
(b) Alexander Kerensky  
(c) Prince Georgy Lvov  
(d) Grigori Zinoviev

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Alexander Kerensky** — Kerensky replaced Prince Lvov as head of the Provisional Government in July 1917 and fled when the Winter Palace fell. Milyukov was its first foreign minister, and Zinoviev was a Bolshevik.

</details>

**9.** P.A. Stolypin's reforms in Russia (1906–11) mainly sought to:

(a) Let peasants quit the commune and own their farms  
(b) Nationalise land and hand it over to the Soviets  
(c) Give the Duma full control over ministers  
(d) Collectivise agriculture into state farms

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Let peasants quit the commune and own their farms** — Stolypin wanted a class of prosperous independent peasants loyal to the Tsar, so he let peasants leave the mir and consolidate their strips, while crushing dissent harshly. Collectivisation came much later under Stalin (from 1929).

</details>

**10.** Consider the following statements:  
1. Serfdom in Russia was abolished by Tsar Alexander II in 1861.  
2. The October Manifesto of 1905 promised an elected legislature (Duma).  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Both 1 and 2** — Both are correct: the 1861 Emancipation freed the serfs but burdened them with redemption payments, and Nicholas II's October Manifesto, issued during the 1905 Revolution, promised civil liberties and an elected Duma, which in practice had little power.

</details>

**11.** Consider the following statements:  
1. The Union of Soviet Socialist Republics was formally created in 1924, after Lenin's death.  
2. The first Soviet Five-Year Plan began in 1928.  
3. Lenin's New Economic Policy replaced War Communism.  
Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only  
(b) 2 and 3 only  
(c) 1 and 3 only  
(d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 and 3 only** — The USSR was formed in December 1922, while Lenin was alive; he died in January 1924. Stalin's first Five-Year Plan began in 1928, and the NEP (1921) replaced the harsh War Communism of the Civil War years.

</details>

**12.** Who organised the Red Army during the Russian Civil War (1918–21)?

(a) Joseph Stalin  
(b) Nikolai Bukharin  
(c) Leon Trotsky  
(d) Felix Dzerzhinsky

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Leon Trotsky** — As Commissar for War, Trotsky built the Red Army from the Red Guards and former Tsarist officers and led it to victory over the Whites. Dzerzhinsky headed the Cheka (secret police), and Bukharin was a party theorist.

</details>
