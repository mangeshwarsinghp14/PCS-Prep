## Short notes

- WWI (1914–18): Allies (Britain, France, Russia; Italy from 1915, USA from 1917, Japan) vs Central Powers (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Empire, Bulgaria).
- Long-term causes: militarism and naval race, alliance system (Triple Alliance 1882; Triple Entente 1907), imperial rivalry, and nationalism (Pan-Slavism, Balkan crises).
- Immediate cause: Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria was assassinated at Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 by Gavrilo Princip; Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia on 28 July 1914.
- Western Front saw trench warfare (Marne 1914, Verdun and Somme 1916); new weapons included machine guns, poison gas, tanks, aircraft and submarines; Gallipoli (1915) failed for the Allies.
- USA entered in April 1917 after unrestricted German submarine warfare (Lusitania sunk, 1915) and the Zimmermann telegram; Russia left after the Bolshevik revolution (1918).
- Armistice on 11 November 1918. Treaty of Versailles (28 June 1919) blamed Germany (war guilt clause, Article 231), imposed reparations, returned Alsace-Lorraine to France, demilitarised the Rhineland and took German colonies.
- Other treaties: Saint-Germain (Austria), Trianon (Hungary), Neuilly (Bulgaria), Sevres (Ottoman Empire, later replaced by Lausanne 1923). League of Nations (1920, Geneva) and ILO (1919) were set up.
- India in WWI: over a million Indians served abroad; Khudadad Khan became the first Indian to win the Victoria Cross (1914); India Gate commemorates Indian soldiers who died.
- WWII causes: harsh Versailles terms, Great Depression (1929), rise of Fascism (Mussolini, 1922) and Nazism (Hitler, chancellor 1933), failure of the League, Japanese aggression (Manchuria, 1931) and appeasement (Munich Agreement, 1938).
- WWII (1939–45): Axis (Germany, Italy, Japan — Tripartite Pact 1940) vs Allies (Britain, France, USSR from 1941, USA from December 1941, China).
- Start: after the Nazi-Soviet Pact (August 1939), Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939; Britain and France declared war on 3 September.
- Key events: fall of France and Dunkirk evacuation (1940), Battle of Britain (1940), German invasion of USSR — Operation Barbarossa (June 1941), Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor (7 December 1941).
- Turning points: Midway (June 1942) in the Pacific, El Alamein (1942) in North Africa, Stalingrad (1942–43) on the Eastern Front; D-Day landings in Normandy on 6 June 1944.
- End: Hitler's suicide (30 April 1945); Germany surrendered (VE Day, 8 May 1945); atomic bombs on Hiroshima (6 August) and Nagasaki (9 August); Japan surrendered (announced 15 August, signed 2 September 1945).
- The Holocaust killed about six million Jews; Nazi leaders were tried at Nuremberg.
- Wartime diplomacy: Atlantic Charter (1941), Tehran (1943), Yalta (February 1945) and Potsdam (July 1945) conferences; Bretton Woods (1944) created the IMF and World Bank.
- Results: United Nations founded (Charter signed June 1945, in force 24 October 1945); USA and USSR became superpowers; Cold War; rapid decolonisation in Asia and Africa.
- India in WWII: Viceroy Linlithgow declared India at war without consulting Indians; Congress ministries resigned (1939); Cripps Mission, Quit India and INA followed; about 2.5 million Indians served; Bengal famine (1943).

## Punjab angle

- Punjab supplied the largest share of Indian Army soldiers in both World Wars because of the British 'martial races' recruitment policy.
- Khudadad Khan, the first Indian Victoria Cross winner (1914), came from Chakwal in undivided Punjab.
- Ghadarites tried to use WWI to start an uprising in Punjab (planned for February 1915), which was crushed and followed by the Lahore Conspiracy Case trials.
- Wartime recruitment pressure, price rise and returning soldiers fed the discontent in Punjab that surfaced during the Rowlatt agitation of 1919.

## How PPSC asks it

Expect causes, alliance membership, treaties and their terms, turning-point battles, dates of start and end, and India's role. Memorise a two-column timeline (WWI and WWII) and the post-war institutions.

## Deep dive

### Road to 1914 and the July Crisis (year — event)

- 1879/1882 — Dual Alliance (Germany–Austria-Hungary), widened into the Triple Alliance with Italy (1882).
- 1894 — Franco-Russian Alliance; 1904 Entente Cordiale (Britain–France); 1907 Anglo-Russian Convention completes the Triple Entente.
- 1898–1912 — Anglo-German naval race (Tirpitz's Navy Laws; Britain's Dreadnought, 1906).
- 1905 and 1911 — Moroccan crises (Tangier, Agadir) push Britain closer to France.
- 1908 — Austria annexes Bosnia-Herzegovina, angering Serbia and Russia; 1912–13 Balkan Wars enlarge Serbia.
- 28 June 1914 — Archduke Franz Ferdinand shot in Sarajevo by Gavrilo Princip (linked to the Serbian 'Black Hand').
- 23 July — Austrian ultimatum to Serbia; 28 July — Austria declares war; 30 July — Russian general mobilisation.
- 1–4 August — Germany declares war on Russia (1 Aug) and France (3 Aug); the Schlieffen Plan sends German armies through neutral Belgium; Britain declares war on 4 August, citing the 1839 guarantee of Belgian neutrality.
- Ottoman Empire joins the Central Powers (October–November 1914); Bulgaria (October 1915); Italy switches to the Allies by the secret Treaty of London (April 1915).

### World War I — fronts and turning points

- Western Front: German advance stopped at the Marne (September 1914); 'race to the sea' and trench deadlock from Switzerland to the Channel.
- First chlorine gas attack at Second Ypres (April 1915); first tanks at the Somme (September 1916).
- Verdun (February–December 1916) and the Somme (July–November 1916) — battles of attrition with over a million casualties combined at each.
- Eastern Front: German victory at Tannenberg (August 1914); Russia's Brusilov Offensive (1916) is its last big success.
- Gallipoli (April 1915–January 1916) — failed Allied attempt to knock out the Ottomans; Mustafa Kemal rose to fame defending it.
- Mesopotamia: Indian Army force surrenders at Kut-al-Amara (April 1916); Baghdad taken (March 1917).
- Sea: Battle of Jutland (May 1916) — indecisive; Germany's unrestricted submarine warfare (resumed February 1917) and the Zimmermann telegram bring the USA in (6 April 1917).
- 1917–18 — Russia exits (Brest-Litovsk, March 1918); German Spring Offensive (March 1918) fails; Allied Hundred Days Offensive (from August 1918).
- Middle East: Indian cavalry (Jodhpur and Mysore Lancers) capture Haifa on 23 September 1918 — remembered at Teen Murti, New Delhi; Balfour Declaration (November 1917) promises a Jewish national home in Palestine.
- Kaiser Wilhelm II abdicates (9 November 1918); armistice signed in a railway carriage at Compiègne, in force 11 a.m., 11 November 1918.

### Peace settlement and the inter-war system

- Paris Peace Conference opened January 1919; the 'Big Four' were Wilson (USA), Lloyd George (UK), Clemenceau (France), Orlando (Italy).
- Wilson's Fourteen Points (January 1918): open diplomacy, freedom of the seas, arms reduction, self-determination, and a League of Nations (Point 14).
- Versailles (28 June 1919, Hall of Mirrors): German army capped at 100,000, no air force or submarines, Rhineland demilitarised, Saar under the League for 15 years, Danzig a free city, Polish Corridor; reparations later fixed at 132 billion gold marks (1921).
- League Covenant forms Part I of each peace treaty; League began work in January 1920 at Geneva; the US Senate rejected Versailles, so America never joined.
- India signed the Treaty of Versailles and was an original member of the League of Nations even though it was not self-governing.
- Mandates: former German colonies and Ottoman lands given to Britain (Iraq, Palestine, Transjordan) and France (Syria, Lebanon).
- Repair attempts: Dawes Plan (1924) and Young Plan (1929) on reparations; Locarno Treaties (1925); Kellogg–Briand Pact outlawing war (1928).
- Keynes, 'The Economic Consequences of the Peace' (1919), called the reparations a 'Carthaginian peace' — a classic debate point.

### India and Punjab in World War I

- Over one million Indians served overseas (France, Mesopotamia, Egypt, Palestine, East Africa, Gallipoli); about 74,000 died.
- Undivided Punjab supplied about 4.8 lakh men — roughly one-third of all Indian recruits — according to M.S. Leigh's official account 'The Punjab and the War' (1922).
- Lieutenant-Governor Michael O'Dwyer's recruitment quotas, war loans and press controls built up the anger that burst out in Punjab in 1919 (Rowlatt satyagraha, Jallianwala Bagh).
- Ghadar Party (founded 1913, San Francisco) planned a mutiny for 21 February 1915; it was betrayed; Lahore Conspiracy Case trials followed and Kartar Singh Sarabha was hanged (16 November 1915).
- Komagata Maru (Japanese ship carrying mostly Punjabi passengers, turned back from Vancouver) — police firing at Budge Budge near Calcutta on 29 September 1914.
- Khudadad Khan (129th Baluchis) won the first Victoria Cross awarded to an Indian soldier, near Ypres (October 1914); Garhwal Rifles' Darwan Singh Negi and Gabar Singh Negi also won the VC.
- Political fall-out: Lucknow Pact (1916), Home Rule Leagues (1916), Montagu's Declaration (20 August 1917) promising 'responsible government', and the Rowlatt Act (1919).
- India Gate (All India War Memorial) by Edwin Lutyens — foundation 1921, completed 1931 — names Indian soldiers who died in WWI and the Third Anglo-Afghan War (1919).

### Causes of World War II — the 1930s slide

- Great Depression after the Wall Street Crash (October 1929): mass unemployment in Germany helped the Nazis rise from 2.6% of the vote (1928) to the largest party (July 1932).
- Mussolini's March on Rome (October 1922); Hitler becomes Chancellor (30 January 1933); Enabling Act (March 1933) makes him dictator; Germany leaves the League (October 1933).
- Japan invades Manchuria (1931), leaves the League (1933), attacks China proper (1937, Nanjing massacre).
- Italy invades Abyssinia (1935–36); League sanctions fail — the League's credibility collapses.
- Germany reintroduces conscription (1935) and remilitarises the Rhineland (March 1936); Rome–Berlin Axis (1936); Anti-Comintern Pact (Germany–Japan, 1936; Italy joins 1937).
- Spanish Civil War (1936–39): Germany and Italy back Franco (bombing of Guernica, 1937); the USSR backs the Republic.
- Anschluss with Austria (March 1938); Munich Agreement (29–30 September 1938) gives Sudetenland to Germany — peak of appeasement (Chamberlain, Daladier).
- Germany occupies the rest of Czechoslovakia (March 1939); Britain and France guarantee Poland.
- Nazi–Soviet Non-Aggression Pact (23 August 1939) with a secret protocol dividing Poland and the Baltic; Germany invades Poland on 1 September 1939.

### World War II — chronology of turning points

- 1939–40 — 'Phoney War'; Soviet–Finnish Winter War; Germany takes Denmark and Norway (April 1940), invades the Low Countries and France (May 1940); Churchill becomes PM (10 May 1940).
- 1940 — Dunkirk evacuation (May–June); France signs armistice (22 June), Vichy regime; Battle of Britain (July–October); Tripartite Pact (September).
- 1941 — Lend-Lease Act (March); Operation Barbarossa (22 June); Atlantic Charter (14 August, Roosevelt–Churchill); Pearl Harbor (7 December) brings the USA into the war.
- 1942 — Fall of Singapore (15 February; tens of thousands of Indian soldiers captured, many later joined the INA); Midway (June); El Alamein (October–November); Stalingrad begins (August).
- 1943 — German Sixth Army surrenders at Stalingrad (2 February); Kursk tank battle (July); Allies invade Sicily and Italy, Mussolini falls (July), Italy surrenders (September); Tehran Conference (November–December).
- 1944 — Imphal–Kohima (March–July) halts the Japanese–INA thrust into India; D-Day in Normandy (6 June); Bretton Woods Conference creates the IMF and World Bank (July).
- 1945 — Yalta Conference (February); Hitler's suicide (30 April); German surrender, VE Day (8 May); UN Charter signed at San Francisco (26 June); Potsdam Conference (July–August).
- Atomic bombs on Hiroshima (6 August) and Nagasaki (9 August); USSR declares war on Japan (8 August); Japan announces surrender (15 August) and signs it aboard USS Missouri (2 September 1945).
- Aftermath: about 6 million Jews killed in the Holocaust; Nuremberg trials (1945–46); Tokyo trials, where Indian judge Radhabinod Pal wrote a famous dissent; United Nations in force 24 October 1945.

### India and Punjab in World War II

- Viceroy Linlithgow declared India at war on 3 September 1939 without consulting Indian leaders; Congress ministries resigned (October–November 1939).
- Sequence: August Offer (1940) → Individual Satyagraha (October 1940; Vinoba Bhave first satyagrahi) → Cripps Mission (March 1942) → Quit India (8 August 1942).
- The Indian Army grew to about 2.5 million — the largest volunteer army in history; Punjab again provided a large share of soldiers.
- Punjab's Unionist Premier Sikandar Hayat Khan backed the war effort; the Sikandar–Jinnah Pact (1937) had linked the Unionists to the Muslim League in national politics.
- First INA formed by Captain Mohan Singh (1942) from POWs taken at Singapore; Subhas Chandra Bose took charge in 1943 and proclaimed the Azad Hind government at Singapore (21 October 1943).
- Red Fort INA trials (from November 1945) of Shah Nawaz Khan, Prem Kumar Sahgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon united Indian opinion; followed by the Royal Indian Navy mutiny (February 1946).
- Bengal famine (1943) killed an estimated 2–3 million people; wartime inflation and shortages hit Punjab's towns as well.
- The war left Britain weakened and in debt to India (sterling balances), speeding the transfer of power in 1947.

### Traps and frequently asked pairs

- WWI began 28 July 1914 (Austria on Serbia); Britain entered on 4 August 1914; WWII began 1 September 1939 (Germany on Poland); Britain and France declared war on 3 September.
- The USA joined WWI in April 1917 but WWII only after Pearl Harbor (December 1941); the USSR joined the Allies only after Barbarossa (June 1941).
- Atlantic Charter (1941) — Roosevelt and Churchill; Churchill said its self-determination clause did not apply to India.
- Yalta (February 1945) — Roosevelt, Churchill, Stalin; Potsdam (July–August 1945) — Truman, Stalin, Churchill then Attlee.
- League of Nations HQ Geneva (1920–46); UN HQ New York; UN Charter signed 26 June, in force 24 October 1945 (UN Day).
- Operation names: Barbarossa (invasion of USSR), Overlord (Normandy), Sea Lion (planned invasion of Britain, cancelled), Dynamo (Dunkirk evacuation).
- Haifa Day (23 September) and the Battle of Saragarhi (1897) are often confused — Saragarhi is not a World War battle.

**7.** The post-World War I peace treaty with the Ottoman Empire, later replaced by the Treaty of Lausanne (1923), was the Treaty of:

(a) Neuilly  
(b) Sèvres  
(c) Saint-Germain  
(d) Trianon

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Sèvres** — The Treaty of Sèvres (1920) carved up Ottoman lands; Mustafa Kemal's nationalists rejected it and won the better terms of Lausanne. Trianon was with Hungary, Neuilly with Bulgaria and Saint-Germain with Austria.

</details>

**8.** The 'war guilt' clause of the Treaty of Versailles, which held Germany and its allies responsible for the war, is:

(a) Article 16  
(b) Article 10  
(c) Article 22  
(d) Article 231

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Article 231** — Article 231 became the legal basis for reparations and a lasting German grievance. Article 10 of the League Covenant (part of the same treaty) dealt with collective security, Article 16 with sanctions and Article 22 with mandates.

</details>

**9.** Which battle of June 1942 is regarded as the turning point of the Pacific war against Japan?

(a) Midway  
(b) Guadalcanal  
(c) Coral Sea  
(d) Iwo Jima

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Midway** — At Midway the US Navy sank four Japanese aircraft carriers, ending Japan's naval superiority six months after Pearl Harbor. Coral Sea (May 1942) came just before it, and Iwo Jima was fought in 1945.

</details>

**10.** Consider the following statements:  
1. The Zimmermann Telegram (1917) proposed a German alliance with Mexico against the United States.  
2. The sinking of the Lusitania in 1915 led the USA to declare war on Germany within weeks.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 1 only** — The Zimmermann Telegram offered Mexico lost US territory in return for joining Germany and helped push the USA into the war. The USA declared war only in April 1917, nearly two years after the Lusitania sinking of May 1915, after Germany resumed unrestricted submarine warfare.

</details>

**11.** Consider the following statements:  
1. The Munich Agreement (1938) allowed Germany to annex the Sudetenland.  
2. The Battle of Britain (1940) was fought mainly in the air.  
3. The D-Day landings of 6 June 1944 took place in Normandy.  
Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only  
(b) 2 and 3 only  
(c) 1 and 3 only  
(d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) 1, 2 and 3** — All three are correct: Munich was the high point of appeasement, the RAF defeated the Luftwaffe in the Battle of Britain and so stopped a German invasion, and Operation Overlord opened the second front in Normandy.

</details>

**12.** Consider the following statements:  
1. The League of Nations had its headquarters at Geneva.  
2. The United States never became a member of the League of Nations.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Both 1 and 2** — Both are correct: the League (1920) sat at Geneva, and although President Wilson proposed it, the US Senate refused to ratify the Treaty of Versailles, so the USA never joined. Its absence was a key reason for the League's weakness.

</details>
