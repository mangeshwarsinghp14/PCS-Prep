# Key challenges & responses

## Short notes

- Agriculture employs nearly half the workforce but contributes under one-fifth of GVA; about 86% of holdings are small and marginal (Agriculture Census 2015-16) with average operational holding 1.08 ha.
- Farm challenges: monsoon dependence, low productivity, fragmented holdings, groundwater depletion, weak markets and storage, climate risk.
- Responses: MSP for 22 mandated crops (plus FRP for sugarcane) on CACP advice; PM-KISAN (2019) Rs 6,000 a year in three instalments; PM Fasal Bima Yojana (2016); e-NAM (2016); Soil Health Card (2015); PM Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (2015).
- Food security: NFSA 2013 covers up to 75% rural and 50% urban population - 5 kg grain per person a month, 35 kg per Antyodaya household; grains free under PMGKAY since 2023 (extended five years from Jan 2024).
- Industrialisation: Make in India (2014), Production Linked Incentive schemes in 14 sectors (2020-21), India Semiconductor Mission, industrial corridors, MSME definitions revised (2020); manufacturing share stuck in the mid-teens of GDP.
- Poverty: Tendulkar method estimated 21.9% poverty in 2011-12; NITI Aayog's multidimensional poverty index says about 24.82 crore people escaped multidimensional poverty between 2013-14 and 2022-23.
- Employment: high informality (most workers in informal jobs), low female participation, educated youth unemployment; PLFS is the main official survey.
- Rural employment: MGNREGA (2005) gave 100 days of guaranteed wage work; it was replaced by the VB-G RAM G Act, 2025, which raises the guarantee to 125 days.
- Livelihood and skills: DAY-NRLM (2011, self-help groups), PMEGP (2008), PM Kaushal Vikas Yojana (2015), PM Vishwakarma (2023); four Labour Codes in force from 21 Nov 2025.
- Rural infrastructure: PMGSY (2000) all-weather roads; PMAY-Gramin (2016, earlier Indira Awaas Yojana 1985); Jal Jeevan Mission (2019) tap water to every rural home; Saubhagya (2017) electrification; BharatNet broadband.
- Urban infrastructure: urbanisation 31.1% (Census 2011); Smart Cities Mission, AMRUT and PMAY-Urban (all 2015); Swachh Bharat Mission (2014); PM Gati Shakti (2021) for integrated infrastructure planning.
- Health: National Health Mission (NRHM 2005); Ayushman Bharat (2018) - Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (health and wellness centres) and PM-JAY cover of Rs 5 lakh per family per year, extended in 2024 to all citizens aged 70+.
- National Health Policy 2017 targeted public health spending of 2.5% of GDP; out-of-pocket spending has fallen but remains high.
- Education: RTE Act 2009; Samagra Shiksha (2018); PM POSHAN (mid-day meal, 1995); NEP 2020 - 5+3+3+4 structure, 50% GER in higher education by 2035, public spending target of 6% of GDP.
- Social-sector concerns: learning outcomes (ASER), malnutrition and stunting (NFHS-5), health workforce gaps, and need for better state capacity.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab's wheat-paddy cycle has caused severe groundwater depletion and stubble burning; crop diversification is the state's main agricultural challenge.
- Agriculture contracted 2.5% in Punjab in 2025-26 because of the 2025 floods (Punjab Economic Survey 2025-26).
- Punjab's Aam Aadmi Clinics (from 2022) provide free primary care; the Mukh Mantri Mawan Dhian Satikar Yojana gives Rs 1,000 a month to women aged 18+ (Rs 1,500 to SC women).

## How PPSC asks it

Questions are scheme-heavy: launch years, coverage and benefits (NFSA, PM-KISAN, PM-JAY, JJM, NEP targets) and poverty methodologies. Keep a scheme table and update for replaced or renamed schemes.

## Deep dive

### Agriculture and food security — new responses (2024–26)

- Digital Agriculture Mission (September 2024, ₹2,817 crore): AgriStack farmer IDs, digital crop survey and geo-referenced land records.
- National Mission on Natural Farming (November 2024, ₹2,481 crore) and National Mission on Edible Oils – Oilseeds (October 2024, 2024-25 to 2030-31).
- PM Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana (approved July 2025; ₹24,000 crore a year): convergence of farm schemes in 100 low-productivity districts for six years.
- Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses (approved October 2025; ₹11,440 crore; 2025-26 to 2030-31): procurement of tur, urad and masur at MSP and seed support to cut imports.
- Budget 2026-27: Bharat-VISTAAR, a multilingual AI advisory tool linked to AgriStack; record ₹2,761 crore for fisheries under PMMSY; support for high-value crops (sandalwood, walnuts, almonds).
- MSP 2026-27 kharif: common paddy ₹2,441/quintal (+₹72); rabi 2027-28 (approved September 2026): wheat ₹2,610 (+₹25, a small rise), mustard ₹6,613, masur ₹7,390.
- Budget 2026-27 allocations: PMGKAY food subsidy ₹2.27 lakh crore; urea subsidy ₹1.17 lakh crore; nutrient-based subsidy ₹54,000 crore; PM-KISAN ₹63,500 crore.
- Food security has moved from calories to nutrition: fortified rice through PDS, millets promotion, POSHAN 2.0 (₹23,100 crore in 2026-27).
- Structural challenges remain: small holdings, groundwater stress in the north-west, climate shocks (2025 floods in Punjab), weak price discovery outside wheat and paddy.

### Industrialisation — challenge and policy response

- Challenge: manufacturing stuck at about 15–17% of GDP; low labour-intensive exports; high logistics costs; small firm size ('missing middle').
- PLI schemes (2020-21) in 14 sectors with about ₹1.97 lakh crore outlay: mobiles and electronics show the clearest gains.
- India Semiconductor Mission (December 2021) supports fabs and ATMP units; Budget 2026-27 announced ISM 2.0.
- Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme outlay raised to ₹40,000 crore in Budget 2026-27.
- MSME definitions revised again in Budget 2025-26 (investment limits raised 2.5 times, turnover limits doubled); Budget 2026-27 created a ₹10,000 crore SME Growth Fund to build 'champion' firms and plans to modernise 200 legacy clusters.
- Budget 2026-27 also announced Biopharma SHAKTI (₹10,000 crore), a container-manufacturing scheme (₹10,000 crore) and three chemical parks.
- Enablers: Labour Codes in force (November 2025), GST rationalisation (September 2025), PM Gati Shakti and National Logistics Policy (2022).

### Poverty, inequality and employment — latest evidence

- NITI Aayog: multidimensional poverty fell from 29.17% (2013-14) to 11.28% (2022-23) — about 24.8 crore people exited.
- World Bank (June 2025): at the revised $3-a-day line, extreme poverty fell from 27.1% in 2011-12 to 5.3% in 2022-23, based on the household consumption survey (HCES 2022-23).
- PLFS Annual Report 2025 (usual status, 15+): LFPR 59.3% (female 40.0%), unemployment 3.1% (rural 2.4%, urban 4.8%), youth (15–29) unemployment 9.9% (urban 13.6%).
- Quality of work: 56.2% self-employed, 23.6% regular wage/salaried, 20.2% casual (2025); agriculture's share of jobs fell to 43.0% from 44.8% in 2024.
- PLFS sample was redesigned from January 2025 and monthly CWS estimates began; June 2026 CWS unemployment rate was 5.5%, female LFPR 32.7%.
- VB-G RAM G Act 2025 replaced MGNREGA: 125 days per rural household, Centre–State sharing 60:40 (90:10 for NE and Himalayan states), up to 60 no-work days in peak farm season; ₹95,692 crore in Budget 2026-27.
- PM Viksit Bharat Rozgar Yojana (from 1 August 2025, about ₹99,446 crore): incentives to first-time employees and employers for new formal jobs; ₹20,083 crore in 2026-27.
- Concerns: low female participation in urban areas, educated youth unemployment, informality and stagnant real wages for casual workers.

### Rural and urban infrastructure

- Jal Jeevan Mission extended to 2028; Budget 2026-27 allocates ₹67,670 crore.
- PMGSY-IV (2024-25 to 2028-29) to connect about 25,000 unconnected habitations; PMGSY gets ₹19,000 crore in 2026-27.
- PMAY-Gramin: two crore more houses targeted for 2024–29; PMAY-Urban 2.0 (2024) targets one crore urban houses.
- Budget 2025-26 announced a ₹1 lakh crore Urban Challenge Fund; Budget 2026-27 proposes ₹5,000 crore per 'City Economic Region' over five years and a ₹100 crore incentive for cities issuing municipal bonds above ₹1,000 crore.
- Economic Survey 2025-26 devotes a chapter to urbanisation ('making India's cities work'): land use, transit, municipal finance.
- Rural connectivity: BharatNet for gram panchayats; PM-WANI public Wi-Fi.
- Challenge: weak municipal revenues (property tax under 0.2% of GDP), delays in Census-based urban data (Census 2027 pending).

### Social sector — health and education

- Health spending: Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY ₹9,500 crore and PM-ABHIM ₹4,200 crore in Budget 2026-27; PM-JAY extended to all citizens aged 70+ (2024).
- National Health Accounts: out-of-pocket spending fell from 64.2% of total health expenditure in 2013-14 to 39.4% in 2021-22.
- Budget 2026-27: a second NIMHANS-type mental health institute in North India; training of 1.5 lakh geriatric caregivers.
- Education: Samagra Shiksha ₹42,100 crore and PM POSHAN ₹12,750 crore in 2026-27; NEP 2020 targets 50% higher-education GER by 2035.
- Budget 2026-27 also plans AVGC content labs in 15,000 schools and 500 colleges and a girls' STEM hostel in every district.
- Learning gaps (ASER, NAS/PARAKH) and stunting (NFHS-5: 35.5% of under-5 children) are the main quality challenges.
- Economic Survey 2025-26 chapter on education and health asks 'what works' — focus on outcomes, teachers and primary care.

### Punjab — challenges and responses

- Food security role: Punjab procured 12.3 million tonnes of wheat in rabi 2026 — over a third of the all-India total of about 33 million tonnes; Centre set a 180 lakh tonne paddy target for Punjab's 2026 kharif season.
- Farm challenge: wheat–paddy monoculture, falling water tables, stubble burning and the 2025 floods (agriculture −2.5% in 2025-26).
- Response: 10-year horticulture programme with JICA aiming to raise fruit and vegetable area by 300% by 2035 (Budget 2026-27).
- Welfare: Mukh Mantri Mawan Dhian Satikar Yojana gets ₹9,300 crore and Meri Rasoi ration kits ₹900 crore in 2026-27 (PRS).
- Health and education: Mukh Mantri Sehat Yojana for universal health cover (₹2,000 crore) and Sikhiya Kranti 2.0 (₹3,500 crore) in 2026-27.
- Industry: Industrial and Business Development Policy 2026 targets food processing, textiles, sports goods and border/Kandi areas.
- Water: 'Nal Jal Mitra' trains one person per gram panchayat to run village water supply.
- Social challenges: drug abuse, youth emigration and debt-laden public finances (committed spending 72% of revenue receipts).

### How PPSC/UPSC test this topic

- Scheme–ministry–year matching (e.g. PMGSY 2000, JJM 2019, PMAY-U 2.0 2024, VB-G RAM G 2025).
- Statement questions on funding patterns (60:40, 90:10) and entitlements (125 days, 5 kg grain, ₹5 lakh cover).
- Data questions from PLFS: LFPR vs WPR vs unemployment rate; usual status vs current weekly status.
- Poverty methodology: Lakdawala, Tendulkar, Rangarajan, NITI's MPI (health, education, living standards; 12 indicators).
- Renamed or replaced schemes are favourite traps (MGNREGA → VB-G RAM G; health and wellness centres → Ayushman Arogya Mandirs).
- Mains: 'challenge — response — gap — way forward' format with one Punjab example scores well.

## Practice MCQs (12)

**1.** The National Food Security Act 2013 covers up to:

(a) 50% of rural and 75% of urban population  
(b) 67% of rural and 33% of urban population  
(c) 75% of rural and 50% of urban population  
(d) 100% of rural and 50% of urban population

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) 75% of rural and 50% of urban population** — Overall coverage is about two-thirds of the population.

</details>

**2.** Under PM-KISAN, eligible farmer families receive:

(a) Rs 10,000 a year in two instalments  
(b) Rs 12,000 a year in four instalments  
(c) Rs 5,000 a year in one instalment  
(d) Rs 6,000 a year in three instalments

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Rs 6,000 a year in three instalments** — Three equal instalments of Rs 2,000 are paid by DBT.

</details>

**3.** Consider the statements:  
1. NEP 2020 targets a Gross Enrolment Ratio of 50% in higher education by 2030.  
2. PM-JAY provides health cover of Rs 5 lakh per year to each member of an eligible family separately, not on a family-floater basis.  
Which is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Neither 1 nor 2** — Statement 1 is wrong: NEP 2020's 50% GER target for higher education is for 2035 (2030 is the target for 100% GER in school education). Statement 2 is wrong: PM-JAY gives Rs 5 lakh per family per year on a family-floater basis. Figures as of Nov 2026; scheme limits can be revised.

</details>

**4.** The main aim of the Jal Jeevan Mission is to:

(a) A functional tap connection to every rural home  
(b) Recharge groundwater in over-exploited blocks  
(c) Clean and rejuvenate the river Ganga basin  
(d) Build check dams in drought-prone districts

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) A functional tap connection to every rural home** — JJM (2019) targets tap water to every rural home.

</details>

**5.** Which 2025 law replaced MGNREGA and raised guaranteed rural work to 125 days?

(a) PM Garib Kalyan Rozgar Act, 2025  
(b) VB-G RAM G Act, 2025  
(c) Deendayal Antyodaya Rozgar Act, 2025  
(d) National Rural Livelihood Guarantee Act, 2025

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) VB-G RAM G Act, 2025** — The VB-G RAM G Act received assent in Dec 2025, replacing MGNREGA.

</details>

**6.** According to Agriculture Census 2015-16, small and marginal holdings make up about:

(a) 95% of all holdings  
(b) 50% of all holdings  
(c) 66% of all holdings  
(d) 86% of all holdings

</details>
