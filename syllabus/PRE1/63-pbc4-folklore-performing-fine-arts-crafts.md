## Short notes

- Bhangra: vigorous men's harvest dance (Baisakhi) to the dhol; Giddha: women's dance with boliyan and clapping; Malwai Giddha is performed by men.
- Jhumar: slow, graceful dance from the Sandal Bar region (now Pakistan); Luddi: victory dance; Sammi: women's dance of the Sandal Bar; Kikli: girls' spinning game-dance; Jaago: wedding-night procession with a decorated pitcher (gagar) of lamps.
- Gatka: Sikh martial art with sticks and swords, displayed at Hola Mohalla and nagar kirtans.
- Folk instruments: dhol, tumbi (one-string, popularised by Lal Chand Yamla Jatt), algoza (double flute), chimta, sarangi and dhad (dhadi ballads), bugchu, kato, ghara, dholki.
- Folk song forms: boliyan, tappe, mahiya, jugni (popularised by Alam Lohar), ghodiyan and suhag (wedding songs), sithniyan (teasing wedding songs), alahniyan (laments), dhola, var (ballad).
- Folk theatre: Naqal (Bhand–Naqqal comic acts), Swang, Rasdhari performances.
- Folk tales: Heer–Ranjha (Jhang/Takht Hazara), Sohni–Mahiwal (Chenab, Gujrat), Mirza–Sahiban, Sassi–Punnu, Puran Bhagat, Raja Rasalu, Dulla Bhatti, Jeona Morh, Jagga.
- Fairs: Maghi Mela at Sri Muktsar Sahib (January; 40 Muktas, Battle of Khidrana 1705); Hola Mohalla at Anandpur Sahib (day after Holi, started by Guru Gobind Singh); Baisakhi at Talwandi Sabo.
- More fairs: Shaheedi Jor Mela, Fatehgarh Sahib (December, younger Sahibzadas); Chhapar Mela (Ludhiana district, Gugga Pir); Jarag Mela (Ludhiana district); Roshni Mela, Jagraon; Baba Sodal Mela, Jalandhar; Sheikh Farid Aagman Purb, Faridkot.
- Harballabh Sangeet Sammelan at Devi Talab Mandir, Jalandhar (since 1875) is among the oldest classical music festivals in India; Kila Raipur Rural Olympics (Ludhiana) began in 1933.
- Festivals: Lohri (13 Jan), Maghi, Baisakhi (Khalsa foundation 1699), Teeyan (women's monsoon festival in Sawan), Bandi Chhor Divas, Gurpurabs.
- Phulkari: darn-stitch embroidery in silk floss on the back of coarse khaddar; 'Bagh' when the whole cloth is covered; got a GI tag (2010-11) for Punjab, Haryana and Rajasthan.
- Thathera craft of Jandiala Guru (Amritsar), traditional brass and copper utensil making, was inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2014.
- Other crafts: Punjabi jutti, parandas, durries, khes, pakhi (hand fans), peerhi (stools), Hoshiarpur wood inlay, Kartarpur furniture, Jalandhar sports goods.
- Music: Gurmat Sangeet (kirtan in prescribed ragas, rababi tradition from Bhai Mardana); Patiala gharana (Ali Baksh and Fateh Ali; Bade Ghulam Ali Khan); Sham Chaurasi gharana (Hoshiarpur).
- Folk singers: Surinder Kaur ('Nightingale of Punjab'), Kuldeep Manak (kaliyan), Asa Singh Mastana, Gurmeet Bawa (long 'hek'), Gurdas Maan, Hans Raj Hans.
- Painting and sculpture: Sobha Singh (Andretta; portraits of the Gurus), Amrita Sher-Gil (father Umrao Singh Sher-Gil), Satish Gujral (born Jhelum), Kirpal Singh (Sikh history paintings), Nek Chand (Rock Garden, Chandigarh).
- Architecture: Harmandir Sahib (gold-plated under Ranjit Singh); Qila Mubarak and Sheesh Mahal, Patiala; Moorish Mosque and Jagatjit Palace, Kapurthala; Virasat-e-Khalsa, Anandpur Sahib (Moshe Safdie, 2011); Partition Museum, Amritsar.

## Punjab angle

- PPSC often asks fair–place matching and craft identification; Phulkari and the Thathera UNESCO listing are frequent targets.

Expect matching of fairs to places, dances to region/gender, instruments to artists, and crafts to towns. Memorise the Maghi, Hola Mohalla, Jor Mela and Chhapar pairs and the UNESCO/GI listings.

## Deep dive

### Fairs and festivals — calendar view (month — fair — place — why)

- January: Lohri (13 Jan; bonfire songs about Dulla Bhatti); Maghi Mela at Sri Muktsar Sahib (Chali Mukte of Khidrana, 1705; also a stage for political conferences).
- February: Kila Raipur 'Rural Olympics' (Ludhiana, held since 1933); Basant kite-flying, especially in Patiala and Amritsar.
- February–March: Hola Mohalla at Anandpur Sahib, the day after Holi — Nihang displays of horsemanship and gatka.
- 13/14 April: Baisakhi — Khalsa foundation day (1699); big gatherings at Talwandi Sabo and Anandpur; also the Jallianwala Bagh anniversary.
- July–August (Sawan–Bhadon): Teeyan (women's monsoon festival with giddha and swings); Chhapar Mela (Ludhiana district) for Gugga Pir.
- September: Baba Sheikh Farid Aagman Purb, Faridkot; Baba Sodal Mela, Jalandhar (Anant Chaudas).
- October–November: Mela Gadri Babeyan Da at Desh Bhagat Yadgar Hall, Jalandhar (the 34th edition opened on 30 Oct 2025); Diwali/Bandi Chhor Divas at Amritsar; Guru Nanak Gurpurab.
- December: Shaheedi Jor Mela at Fatehgarh Sahib (younger Sahibzadas) and at Chamkaur Sahib (elder Sahibzadas); Harballabh Sangeet Sammelan, Jalandhar (since 1875).
- Other local fairs: Roshni Mela, Jagraon (Peer Baba Mohkam Din); Jarag Mela (Sheetla Mata); Ram Tirath fair (Amritsar, Kartik).

### Dances and performing traditions — who, where, what marks them

- Bhangra — men; harvest dance rooted in Majha and Sialkot–Gujranwala; dhol beat, boliyan, high-energy jumps.
- Giddha — women; clapping circle with boliyan; Teeyan and weddings. Malwai Giddha — men of Malwa with props such as sapp, kato, chimta and tumbi.
- Jhumar — men; slow circular dance of the Sandal Bar (now Pakistan); Sammi — women's dance of the same Bar region.
- Luddi — victory dance with one hand behind the back; Dhamal — Sufi-shrine dance with raised arms; Kikli — girls in pairs, crossed hands, spinning.
- Jaago — wedding-night procession with a decorated gagar of lamps; Julli — dervish-style dance associated with Sufi shrines.
- Folk theatre: Naqqals and Bhands (comic duets), Swang (sung drama), Rasdhari (Krishna plays), Ramlila; ballad traditions of Dhadi (dhad and sarangi; Sohan Singh Seetal) and Kavishri (unaccompanied Malwa singing).

### Music — gharanas, Gurmat Sangeet and instruments

- Patiala gharana — founded at the Patiala court by Ali Bakhsh 'Jarnail' and Fateh Ali 'Karnail' (19th c.); Bade Ghulam Ali Khan is its most famous voice.
- Sham Chaurasi gharana (Hoshiarpur) — Salamat Ali and Nazakat Ali Khan, who moved to Pakistan; Talwandi gharana — dhrupad.
- Gurmat Sangeet: kirtan in prescribed ragas; the rababi tradition began with Bhai Mardana's rabab; instruments traditionally linked with the Gurus include the saranda, taus and dilruba.
- Folk instruments: tumbi (one string), algoza (paired flutes), dhol, dholki, chimta, sarangi, dhad, bugchu, kato (squirrel clapper), ghara (pot), iktara, been.
- Folk song genres: boliyan, tappe, mahiya, jugni, dhola, kaliyan (Kuldeep Manak), ghodiyan and suhag (wedding), sithniyan (teasing songs), alahniyan (mourning songs).
- Singers often matched in MCQs: Lal Chand Yamla Jatt (tumbi), Surinder Kaur (Padma Shri 2006), Prakash Kaur, Asa Singh Mastana, Kuldeep Manak, Gurdas Maan, Hans Raj Hans (Padma Shri 2008).

### Crafts, textiles and GI

- Phulkari: darn stitch worked from the back of hand-spun khaddar with untwisted silk floss (pat); colours red, maroon, golden-yellow.
- Types: Bagh (whole cloth covered); Chope (border work, gifted by the maternal grandmother at a wedding); Sainchi (figurative scenes, Malwa — Bathinda/Faridkot); Thirma (on white khaddar); Darshan Dwar (gate motif, for gurdwara offering); Bawan Bagh (52 motifs).
- Phulkari received GI registration (2011) jointly for Punjab, Haryana and Rajasthan; Basmati rice (GI 2016) is shared by Punjab with other Indo-Gangetic states; Punjab began the GI process for Amritsari kulcha in 2025.
- Thathera craft of Jandiala Guru (Amritsar): hand-beaten brass, copper and kansa (bell-metal) utensils; inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2014 (9.COM).
- Punjabi jutti with tilla (metal-thread) embroidery — Patiala, Muktsar and Malerkotla; parandas (tasselled braids) and Patiala salwar.
- Durrie and khes weaving on village looms; peerhi (low stools), manji/charpai weaving, pakhi (hand fans), chhikku (baskets).
- Hoshiarpur wood inlay (once ivory, now bone and plastic) and Kartarpur (Jalandhar) furniture; Jalandhar sports goods and Amritsar papad-warian are craft-industries.

### Painting, architecture and archaeological heritage

- Sikh court painting at Lahore (19th c.) drew on Pahari (Guler–Kangra) artists; Golden Temple interiors use mohrakashi (fresco), gach (stucco) and jaratkari (stone inlay).
- Qila Mubarak, Patiala (founded by Baba Ala Singh, 1763) — Sheesh Mahal and murals; Qila Mubarak, Bathinda — where Razia Sultan was held (1240).
- Gobindgarh Fort and Ram Bagh Summer Palace (Maharaja Ranjit Singh Panorama), Amritsar.
- Kapurthala: Jagatjit Palace (French-style, now Sainik School) and the Moorish Mosque modelled on the Koutoubia mosque of Marrakesh.
- Virasat-e-Khalsa, Anandpur Sahib — museum of Sikh history designed by Moshe Safdie, opened in Nov 2011.
- Rupnagar (Ropar) — Harappan site excavated by Y.D. Sharma (1952–55), the first Harappan site excavated after Independence; Sanghol (Fatehgarh Sahib) — Harappan to Kushana levels, with Kushana sculptures of the Mathura school and a site museum.
- Modern: Sobha Singh (Andretta), Kirpal Singh (Sikh battle paintings), Amrita Sher-Gil; Nek Chand's Rock Garden and Le Corbusier's Capitol Complex (UNESCO World Heritage, 2016) in Chandigarh.

### Folklore — tales, heroes and collectors

- Love legends and their locales: Heer–Ranjha (Jhang/Takht Hazara), Sohni–Mahiwal (Gujrat, river Chenab), Mirza–Sahiban (Danabad/Khiva), Sassi–Punnu (Bhambore to Kech), Puran Bhagat and Raja Rasalu (Sialkot).
- Outlaw-heroes of ballads: Dulla Bhatti (Akbar's time), Jeona Morh, Jagga Jatt, Sucha Soorma.
- Folk deities and saints of the village: Gugga Pir (snake-saint), Sakhi Sarwar, Khwaja Khizr, Sheetla Mata, Baba Sodal.
- Folk wisdom: akhaan (proverbs) and bujharatan (riddles) — often tested in Punjabi-language papers.
- Colonial collectors: R.C. Temple (Legends of the Panjab, 1884–1900), Flora Annie Steel (Tales of the Punjab, 1894), Charles Swynnerton (Romantic Tales from the Panjab, 1903).
- Indian collectors: Devinder Satyarthi (folk-song gatherer, Giddha) and M.S. Randhawa (folk songs and Kangra painting studies).

### PPSC patterns and traps

- Hola Mohalla (Anandpur) ≠ Holi; Maghi (Muktsar) ≠ Lohri (the day before).
- Jhumar and Sammi belong to the Sandal Bar (Pakistan); Malwai Giddha is danced by men.
- UNESCO ICH = Thathera (2014); GI = Phulkari (2011). Do not swap.
- Harballabh (1875) is a classical music festival, not a fair for a saint.
- Ropar was excavated in the 1950s; Sanghol's famous Kushana sculptures came to light in the 1980s.
- Patiala gharana (khayal, thumri) vs Sham Chaurasi (Hoshiarpur) — both Punjab gharanas.

**1.** Phulkari is a traditional Punjabi form of:

(a) Embroidery  
(b) Durrie weaving  
(c) Pottery  
(d) Wall painting

**2.** Which craft of Jandiala Guru was inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2014?

(a) Terracotta pottery for village use  
(b) Brass and copper utensil making  
(c) Wood inlay work on furniture  
(d) Phulkari needlework and embroidery

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Brass and copper utensil making** — The Thatheras of Jandiala Guru (Amritsar) make utensils by hand-beating brass and copper.

**3.** Hola Mohalla is celebrated at:

(a) Sri Muktsar Sahib  
(b) Talwandi Sabo  
(c) Anandpur Sahib  
(d) Fatehgarh Sahib

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Anandpur Sahib** — Guru Gobind Singh started it at Anandpur Sahib as a display of martial skills.

**4.** The Maghi Mela at Sri Muktsar Sahib commemorates:

(a) The martyrdom of the younger Sahibzadas  
(b) The forty Muktas of the Battle of Khidrana  
(c) The founding of the Khalsa  
(d) The martyrdom of Guru Tegh Bahadur

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) The forty Muktas of the Battle of Khidrana** — It marks the 1705 battle at Khidrana, renamed Muktsar.

</details>

**5.** The Harballabh Sangeet Sammelan is held at:

(a) Jalandhar  
(b) Ludhiana  
(c) Patiala  
(d) Amritsar

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Jalandhar** — It is held at Devi Talab Mandir, Jalandhar, and dates to 1875.

</details>

**6.** Consider the statements:  
1. Giddha is traditionally a men's dance.  
2. Jhumar originated in the Sandal Bar region.  
Which is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** — Giddha is traditionally a women's dance; Bhangra and Malwai Giddha are men's forms.

**7.** The tumbi is most closely associated with which folk singer?

(a) Asa Singh Mastana  
(b) Surinder Kaur  
(c) Lal Chand Yamla Jatt  
(d) Gurdas Maan

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Lal Chand Yamla Jatt** — Yamla Jatt made the one-string tumbi famous.

</details>

**8.** The Kila Raipur Rural Olympics are held in which district?

(a) Sangrur  
(b) Moga  
(c) Ludhiana  
(d) Patiala

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Ludhiana** — They have been held at Kila Raipur, near Ludhiana, since 1933.

</details>

**9.** Sammi is a traditional folk dance performed by:

(a) Women of the Doaba region  
(b) Men of the Pothohar plateau  
(c) Men of the Malwa region  
(d) Women of the Sandal Bar

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Women of the Sandal Bar** — Sammi is the women's dance of the Sandal Bar, the area between the Ravi and Chenab now in Pakistan, and Jhumar is the men's dance of the same area. Malwai Giddha is the men's dance of Malwa.

</details>

**10.** In the decoration of the Harmandir Sahib, the term 'mohrakashi' refers to:

(a) Gilded stucco relief in gypsum  
(b) Fresco painting on wet plaster  
(c) Inlay of coloured stones in marble  
(d) Carved and painted wooden panels

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Fresco painting on wet plaster** — Mohrakashi is the fresco work on the walls; jaratkari is stone inlay in marble and gach (or tukri) is stucco work with gilding. All three were used in the 19th-century decoration of the Golden Temple.

</details>

**11.** The renowned vocalist Bade Ghulam Ali Khan belonged to which gharana of Hindustani music?

(a) Gwalior  
(b) Patiala  
(c) Kirana  
(d) Sham Chaurasi

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Patiala** — Bade Ghulam Ali Khan (born at Kasur) is the best-known voice of the Patiala gharana, founded at the Patiala court by Ali Bakhsh and Fateh Ali. Sham Chaurasi (Hoshiarpur) is the other Punjab gharana.

</details>

**12.** The Moorish Mosque at Kapurthala, built for Maharaja Jagatjit Singh, was modelled on a mosque in:

(a) Marrakesh, Morocco  
(b) Cordoba, Spain  
(c) Cairo, Egypt  
(d) Istanbul, Turkey

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Marrakesh, Morocco** — The mosque (completed c.1930) was designed by the French architect M. Manteaux on the lines of the Koutoubia Mosque of Marrakesh. Kapurthala's Jagatjit Palace, by contrast, was modelled on Versailles.

</details>

**13.** The Roshni Mela, held at the shrine of Peer Baba Mohkam Din in the month of Phagan, takes place at:

(a) Sri Muktsar Sahib  
(b) Malerkotla  
(c) Sirhind  
(d) Jagraon

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Jagraon** — The three-day Roshni (lights) mela at Jagraon in Ludhiana district draws Hindus, Sikhs and Muslims, who light lamps at the dargah. Malerkotla's chief shrine is that of Haider Shaikh and Sirhind's is the Rauza Sharif.

</details>

**14.** Consider the following statements:  
1. Kikli is performed by girls in pairs who hold crossed hands and spin.  
2. Luddi is a dance traditionally performed to celebrate a victory.  
3. Malwai Giddha is traditionally performed by women.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only  
(b) 2 and 3 only  
(c) 1 and 3 only  
(d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 1 and 2 only** — Kikli is a girls' pair dance and Luddi a victory dance, so statements 1 and 2 are correct. Malwai Giddha, despite the name, is danced by men of Malwa with props such as the sapp, chimta and tumbi; ordinary Giddha is the women's dance.

</details>

**15.** Consider the following statements:  
1. In a 'Bagh' phulkari, the base cloth is almost entirely covered by the embroidery.  
2. A 'Chope' was traditionally embroidered by the maternal grandmother as a gift for the bride.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Both 1 and 2** — Both are correct. The Bagh (garden) covers the whole khaddar so the base hardly shows, while the Chope, worked on both sides with the same pattern, is the nani's wedding gift. Ordinary phulkaris leave much of the base cloth showing.

</details>

**16.** Consider the following statements:  
1. The archaeological site of Sanghol is in Fatehgarh Sahib district.  
2. The Virasat-e-Khalsa museum is at Anandpur Sahib.  
3. Qila Mubarak at Patiala was founded by Baba Ala Singh.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only  
(b) 2 and 3 only  
(c) 1 and 3 only  
(d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) 1, 2 and 3** — All three are correct. Sanghol has Harappan to Kushana levels and a site museum; Virasat-e-Khalsa, designed by Moshe Safdie, opened at Anandpur Sahib in 2011; and Ala Singh built Qila Mubarak in 1763. Do not confuse Patiala's Qila Mubarak with the Bathinda fort of the same name.

</details>
