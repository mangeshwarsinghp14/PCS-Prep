## Short notes

- Census 2011: India's population 121.09 crore - about 17.5% of world population on 2.4% of world land area.
- India overtook China as the most populous country in 2023 (UN World Population Prospects estimate).
- Density (2011): 382 persons per sq km; highest state Bihar (1,106), lowest Arunachal Pradesh (17); Delhi has the highest density overall.
- Distribution depends on physical factors (relief, climate, water, soils), socio-economic factors (agriculture, industry, urbanisation) and history; dense in the northern plains and coastal deltas, sparse in Himalaya, Thar and the North-East hills.
- Most populous state: Uttar Pradesh (about 16.5% of India); least populous: Sikkim.
- Phases of growth: 1901-21 stagnant (1911-21 the only decade of negative growth; 1921 is the 'year of great divide'); 1921-51 steady growth; 1951-81 population explosion; after 1981 high but steadily declining growth.
- Decadal growth 2001-11: 17.7%, the lowest since Independence.
- Sex ratio 2011: 943 females per 1,000 males (Kerala highest at 1,084, Haryana lowest among states at 879); child sex ratio (0-6) 919.
- Literacy 2011: 74.04% (male 82.1%, female 65.5%); Kerala highest, Bihar lowest; urban population 31.16%.
- Demographic transition: India is in the late expanding stage; Total Fertility Rate fell to 2.0 (NFHS-5, 2019-21), below the replacement level of 2.1.
- Demographic dividend: a large working-age (15-64) population lowers the dependency ratio, but gains depend on jobs, skills and health.
- Next census: Census 2027 with reference date 1 March 2027 (1 Oct 2026 for snow-bound areas) - India's first digital census and the first since 1931 to enumerate caste.
- Migration types: internal (intra-state, inter-state) and international; streams - rural-rural (largest, mostly women moving after marriage), rural-urban, urban-urban, urban-rural.
- Census 2011 recorded about 45.6 crore migrants by place of last residence (around 37% of population).
- Causes: push factors (poverty, unemployment, low wages, disasters, conflict) and pull factors (jobs, higher wages, education, health, amenities); marriage is the main reason for female migration, work for male migration.
- Major out-migration states: Uttar Pradesh and Bihar; major destinations: Maharashtra, Delhi, Gujarat, Haryana.
- Consequences: economic (remittances, labour supply to cities), demographic (skewed sex ratio at source and destination), social (mixing of cultures, spread of ideas), environmental (overcrowding, slums, pressure on services), brain drain abroad.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab (Census 2011): population about 2.77 crore, density 551 per sq km, decadal growth 13.9%, urban share 37.5%.
- Punjab's sex ratio was 895 and child sex ratio 846 (2011) - among the lowest, linked to son preference.
- Punjab literacy 75.8% (2011); Scheduled Castes form 31.9% of the population, the highest share among states.
- Punjab sees heavy international emigration (especially Doaba to Canada, UK, Australia) and large inflow of migrant farm and industrial workers from UP and Bihar.

## Deep dive

### Population theories and measures

- Malthus (1798): population grows geometrically, food arithmetically; 'positive checks' (famine, war, epidemics — asked by PPSC 2018) and 'preventive checks' (late marriage, moral restraint).
- Neo-Malthusians (Paul Ehrlich, 'The Population Bomb', 1968; IPAT: Impact = Population × Affluence × Technology) versus Ester Boserup (1965), who argued that population pressure drives agricultural intensification.
- Demographic transition theory (Warren Thompson 1929, Frank Notestein 1945): high stationary → early expanding → late expanding → low stationary; some add a fifth 'declining' stage (Japan, Italy).
- Optimum population (Edwin Cannan) is the size at which output per head is highest; carrying capacity is the maximum an environment can sustain.
- Measures: crude birth and death rates (per 1,000), infant mortality rate (per 1,000 live births), maternal mortality ratio (per 1,00,000 live births), total fertility rate, net reproduction rate (1 = exact replacement), dependency ratio.
- Population pyramids: expansive (broad base — high fertility), constrictive (narrow base — ageing), stationary (near-vertical sides).
- UNFPA places India's demographic-dividend window roughly from 2005–06 to 2055–56, after which ageing accelerates.

### Census history and Census 2027

- 1872 — first (non-synchronous) census under Lord Mayo; 1881 — first synchronous census (W.C. Plowden); 1951 — first after Independence under the Census Act 1948; 2011 — 15th census.
- Census 2021 was postponed because of COVID-19; Census 2027 will be the 16th census and the 8th since Independence.
- Phase 1, Houselisting and Housing Census, ran between 1 April and 30 September 2026 (each state chose a 30-day window); Phase 2, Population Enumeration, is due in February 2027.
- Reference date: 00:00 hours on 1 March 2027; for snow-bound areas of Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh, Himachal and Uttarakhand it is 1 October 2026.
- It is India's first digital census (mobile app, self-enumeration option) and the first since 1931 to record caste for all communities.
- Other data sources: Sample Registration System (annual fertility and mortality), NFHS (IIPS Mumbai), Civil Registration System under the Registration of Births and Deaths Act 1969 (2023 amendment made the digital birth certificate a single proof of date of birth).

### World population — current data

- UN World Population Prospects 2024: world population about 8.2 billion in 2024, likely to peak around 10.3 billion in the mid-2080s.
- World population crossed 8 billion on 15 November 2022 (UN); World Population Day is 11 July (since 1989).
- India overtook China as the most populous country in 2023; UN projections put India's population at about 1.45 billion in 2024, peaking at about 1.7 billion in the early 2060s.
- China's population has been falling since 2022; Japan, Italy and South Korea are ageing fastest.
- About 90% of humanity lives in the Northern Hemisphere and about 60% in Asia.
- Densest countries: Monaco and Singapore (city-states), Bangladesh among large countries; Mongolia is among the least dense.

### India's population composition — key data

- Religion (2011): Hindu 79.8%, Muslim 14.2%, Christian 2.3%, Sikh 1.7%, Buddhist 0.7%, Jain 0.4%.
- Scheduled Castes 16.6% and Scheduled Tribes 8.6% of India's population (2011); Punjab has the highest SC share (31.9%), while Punjab, Haryana, Chandigarh, Delhi and Puducherry have no notified STs.
- Age structure (2011): about 31% aged 0–14, 60% aged 15–59 and 8.6% aged 60+.
- Census 2011 recorded agricultural labourers outnumbering cultivators for the first time.
- NFHS-5 (2019–21): total fertility rate 2.0, below replacement; sex ratio of the total population 1,020 females per 1,000 males, the first time above 1,000 in NFHS; sex ratio at birth 929.
- Punjab NFHS-5: TFR about 1.6 — among the lowest in India, which with heavy emigration means a fast-ageing countryside.

### Migration — theory, data and policy

- Ravenstein's 'laws of migration' (1885): most moves are short-distance, migration proceeds in steps, and women dominate short-distance moves.
- Everett Lee (1966): push factors, pull factors, intervening obstacles and personal factors; Zelinsky's mobility transition (1971) links migration to the demographic transition.
- The Census measures migration by place of birth and by place of last residence; marriage dominates female migration and work dominates male migration.
- PLFS 2020-21 put India's migration rate at 28.9%; the Economic Survey 2016-17 estimated about 9 million inter-state work migrants a year (2011–16) using railway data.
- India has the world's largest diaspora and is the largest recipient of remittances (well over US$125 billion a year in 2023–24), mainly from the Gulf and the USA.
- Policy tools: One Nation One Ration Card (portability of food rations), e-Shram portal (August 2021) for unorganised workers; the Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act 1979 is subsumed in the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code 2020.
- Consequences to write in answers: remittance economies, 'left-behind' elderly and women, brain drain versus brain gain, urban informal settlements, and distress or climate-induced migration.

### Punjab population and migration file

- Census 2011: about 2.77 crore people, density 551 per sq km, decadal growth 13.9%, urban share 37.5%, sex ratio 895, child sex ratio 846, literacy 75.8%, SC share 31.9%.
- Doaba (Jalandhar, Hoshiarpur, Kapurthala, Nawanshahr) is the traditional 'NRI belt'; the study-visa route (IELTS) spread emigration to Malwa and Majha after 2010.
- Canada's 2024 caps on study permits and India–Canada tensions reduced student outflows; in February 2025 US deportation flights carrying Indian migrants landed at Amritsar, exposing irregular 'donkey route' migration.
- In-migration: lakhs of workers from Uttar Pradesh and Bihar sustain paddy transplanting, Ludhiana's industry and urban construction.
- Low fertility plus emigration means rising old-age dependency in villages — relevant for health and pension planning by an SDM.
- Punjab's child sex ratio (846 in 2011) was among the worst, linked with son preference; enforcement of the PCPNDT Act and 'Beti Bachao Beti Padhao' are the policy responses.

### Traps and PPSC patterns

- India measures sex ratio as females per 1,000 males; many international sources use males per 100 females — read the question (PPSC 2025 asked sex-ratio statements).
- 1921 is the 'year of the great divide'; 1911–21 is the only decade of negative growth.
- Rural-to-rural is the largest migration stream (mostly women after marriage), not rural-to-urban.
- TFR is per woman, not per couple; replacement level is 2.1 because some children do not survive.
- Census 2027's reference date is 1 March 2027, not 2026 — Phase 1 alone happened in 2026.
- PPSC 2018 asked Malthus's 'positive checks' — expect theory questions as well as data.

**1.** According to Census 2011, India's population density was:

(a) 325 persons per sq km  
(b) 412 persons per sq km  
(c) 362 persons per sq km  
(d) 382 persons per sq km

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) 382 persons per sq km** — Density rose from 325 in 2001 to 382 in 2011.

</details>

**2.** Which year is called the 'year of great divide' in India's demographic history?

(a) 1981  
(b) 1921  
(c) 1911  
(d) 1951

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 1921** — After 1921 population grew continuously; 1911-21 was the only decade of decline.

</details>

**3.** Which state had the highest population density in Census 2011?

(a) Kerala  
(b) Bihar  
(c) West Bengal  
(d) Uttar Pradesh

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Bihar** — Bihar had 1,106 persons per sq km (Census 2011), the highest among states. West Bengal (1,029) is next; Delhi and Chandigarh are higher but are Union Territories, not states.

</details>

**4.** Consider the statements:  
1. Rural-to-urban is the largest internal migration stream.  
2. Marriage is the most common reason for female migration in India.  
Which is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** — The rural-to-rural stream is the largest, dominated by women migrating after marriage.

</details>

**5.** Which state recorded the lowest sex ratio in Census 2011?

(a) Punjab  
(b) Jammu and Kashmir  
(c) Haryana  
(d) Rajasthan

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Haryana** — Haryana had 879 females per 1,000 males, the lowest among states in Census 2011. Punjab (895) is the tempting answer but was higher; the Union Territory of Delhi (868) was lower still.

</details>

**6.** India's child sex ratio (0-6 years) in Census 2011 was:

(a) 919  
(b) 914  
(c) 927  
(d) 943

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 919** — It fell from 927 in 2001 to 919 in 2011; 943 is the overall sex ratio.

</details>

**7.** Consider the following statements:  
1. The first synchronous census of India was held in 1872.  
2. Census 2027 uses 1 March 2027 as its reference date for most of the country.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** — Statement 1 is wrong: the 1872 census was India's first but was not synchronous; the first synchronous census was held in 1881 under Lord Ripon. Statement 2 is correct: Census 2027 uses 1 March 2027 as its reference date, with 1 October 2026 for snow-bound areas.

</details>

**8.** Consider the following statements: 1. Punjab and Haryana have no notified Scheduled Tribes. 2. Punjab has the highest share of Scheduled Castes among states (Census 2011). 3. NFHS-5 found India's total fertility rate to be below replacement level. Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only  
(b) 2 and 3 only  
(c) 1 and 3 only  
(d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) 1, 2 and 3** — All three are correct: no STs in Punjab or Haryana, Punjab's SC share 31.9%, NFHS-5 TFR 2.0.

</details>

**9.** Who argued that population pressure stimulates agricultural intensification, challenging Malthus?

(a) Thomas Malthus  
(b) Ester Boserup  
(c) Warren Thompson

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Ester Boserup** — Boserup's 'The Conditions of Agricultural Growth' (1965) made this argument.

**10.** The 'laws of migration' (1885) were formulated by:

(a) E.G. Ravenstein  
(b) Everett Lee  
(c) Wilbur Zelinsky  
(d) Friedrich Ratzel

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) E.G. Ravenstein** — Lee gave the push–pull model in 1966; Zelinsky the mobility transition in 1971.

</details>

**11.** A Net Reproduction Rate of 1 means that:

(a) Mothers are exactly replaced by daughters  
(b) The population doubles in every generation  
(c) Women face zero mortality until age 49  
(d) The total fertility rate is exactly one child

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Mothers are exactly replaced by daughters** — An NRR of 1 corresponds to replacement-level fertility.

</details>

**12.** Which region of Punjab is traditionally known as the 'NRI belt' for its high overseas emigration?

(a) Puadh  
(b) Malwa  
(c) Majha  
(d) Doaba

**(d) Doaba** — Doaba districts such as Jalandhar and Hoshiarpur have the oldest emigration links.
