## Short notes

- Background: League of Nations (1920) failed to stop WWII; Atlantic Charter (1941), Declaration by United Nations (1 Jan 1942), Dumbarton Oaks (1944) and Yalta (1945) prepared the UN.
- UN Charter signed at San Francisco on 26 June 1945; UN came into existence on 24 Oct 1945 (UN Day); India was a founding member; headquarters New York; 193 member states.
- Six principal organs: General Assembly, Security Council, Economic and Social Council (ECOSOC), Trusteeship Council, International Court of Justice, Secretariat.
- General Assembly: all members, one vote each; 'important questions' need a two-thirds majority; the 1950 'Uniting for Peace' resolution lets it act when the Security Council is deadlocked.
- Security Council: 15 members - 5 permanent with veto (USA, UK, France, Russia, China) and 10 non-permanent elected by the GA for 2-year terms; decisions need 9 votes, and substantive decisions also need no negative vote (veto) by any P5 member.
- India has served as a non-permanent UNSC member eight times, most recently 2021-22.
- ECOSOC: 54 members elected for 3-year terms; coordinates specialised agencies, functional and regional commissions.
- Trusteeship Council suspended operations on 1 Nov 1994 after Palau, the last trust territory, became independent.
- International Court of Justice: The Hague (only principal organ outside New York); 15 judges with 9-year terms; Indian judge Dalveer Bhandari; India won the Kulbhushan Jadhav case (2019).
- Secretariat is headed by the Secretary-General, appointed by the GA on the Security Council's recommendation for 5 years; Antonio Guterres's second term ends 31 Dec 2026, so a successor is being chosen in 2026.
- Specialised agencies (autonomous, linked through ECOSOC): FAO (Rome), WHO (Geneva), ILO (Geneva, 1919, tripartite), UNESCO (Paris), IMF and World Bank (Washington), ICAO (Montreal), IMO (London), ITU (Geneva), UPU (Bern), WIPO and WMO (Geneva), IFAD (Rome), UNIDO (Vienna).
- Funds and programmes are not specialised agencies: UNICEF (1946, New York), UNDP (publishes Human Development Report), UNEP and UN-Habitat (Nairobi), UNHCR (Geneva), WFP (Rome), UNFPA.
- IAEA (Vienna) is an autonomous related organisation reporting to the GA and UNSC; WTO is also a related organisation, not a specialised agency.
- Nobel Peace Prizes: ILO (1969), UN peacekeeping forces (1988), UN and Kofi Annan (2001), WFP (2020).
- Peacekeeping began with UNTSO (1948); India is historically one of the largest troop contributors to UN peace operations.
- Agenda 2030 (adopted 2015): 17 Sustainable Development Goals and 169 targets, succeeding the Millennium Development Goals (2000-2015).
- UNSC reform: G4 (India, Brazil, Germany, Japan) seek permanent seats; opposed by the 'Uniting for Consensus' group (incl. Italy, Pakistan); African position is the Ezulwini Consensus.

## Punjab angle

- The Capitol Complex of Chandigarh (Punjab's capital, designed by Le Corbusier) is part of a UNESCO World Heritage serial site inscribed in 2016.

## Deep dive

### Charter articles to know (Article — content)

- Art 1 purposes; Art 2(4) bans threat or use of force against any state; Art 2(7) bars UN intervention in domestic jurisdiction (except Chapter VII enforcement).
- Art 4 — admission by the General Assembly on the Security Council's recommendation; Art 6 — expulsion (never used).
- Art 19 — a member in arrears equal to two full years of contributions can lose its GA vote.
- Art 23 — composition of the Security Council; Art 24 — primary responsibility for peace; Art 25 — members must carry out its decisions; Art 27 — voting (veto).
- Chapter VI (Arts 33–38) — peaceful settlement; Chapter VII (Arts 39–51) — enforcement: Art 41 non-military sanctions, Art 42 military action, Art 51 inherent right of self-defence.
- Chapter VIII — regional arrangements (e.g. AU, OAS); Arts 53 and 107 — "enemy state" clauses (now obsolete).
- Art 96 — GA and SC may seek advisory opinions from the ICJ (e.g. ICJ opinion on states' climate obligations, July 2025).
- Art 97 — Secretary-General is the chief administrative officer; Art 99 — SG may bring any threat to peace to the Council (invoked by Guterres over Gaza, Dec 2023).
- Arts 108–109 — amendment needs two-thirds of the GA and ratification by two-thirds of members including all five permanent members.
- Charter amended: 1965 (Council 11→15, ECOSOC 18→27), 1968 (Art 109), 1973 (ECOSOC 27→54).
- Chapter XI — non-self-governing territories; Chapters XII–XIII — trusteeship system.

### Timeline (year — event)

- 1919/1920 — League of Nations; India (then British-ruled) was an original member — a rare case of a non-self-governing member.
- 26 June 1945 — India signed the Charter (delegation led by A. Ramaswami Mudaliar); 51 original members.
- 1946 — first GA at Westminster Central Hall, London; Trygve Lie first SG; India raised treatment of Indians in South Africa — the UN's first human-rights/anti-racism item.
- 1948 — UDHR adopted (Hansa Mehta helped change "all men" to "all human beings"); Genocide Convention; India referred Kashmir to the Council (1 Jan 1948) — UNCIP, then UNMOGIP (1949), still present.
- 1950 — Uniting for Peace resolution (Korea); 1956 — UNEF I, the first armed peacekeeping force (Suez).
- 1960 — Declaration on Granting Independence to Colonial Countries (Res. 1514); 1971 — Res. 2758 seated the People's Republic of China.
- 1992 — An Agenda for Peace (Boutros-Ghali); 2005 World Summit — Responsibility to Protect, Peacebuilding Commission; 2006 — Human Rights Council (47 members) replaced the Commission.
- 2015 — Agenda 2030 and the Paris Agreement; 22 Sept 2024 — Pact for the Future with the Global Digital Compact and Declaration on Future Generations.
- 2025 — UN80 year; the UN80 Initiative (March 2025) sought savings amid budget cuts; US withdrawal from WHO took effect in Jan 2026.
- 2026 — selection of the 10th Secretary-General: Security Council straw polls began July 2026 (Costa Rica's Rebeca Grynspan led early polls); the new SG takes office on 1 Jan 2027.

### Secretaries-General (name — country — term)

- Trygve Lie — Norway — 1946–52 (resigned).
- Dag Hammarskjold — Sweden — 1953–61; died in an air crash in Northern Rhodesia during the Congo crisis; posthumous Nobel Peace Prize 1961.
- U Thant — Burma — 1961–71; first Asian SG.
- Kurt Waldheim — Austria — 1972–81.
- Javier Perez de Cuellar — Peru — 1982–91.
- Boutros Boutros-Ghali — Egypt — 1992–96; only SG limited to one term (US veto).
- Kofi Annan — Ghana — 1997–2006; Nobel Peace Prize with the UN, 2001.
- Ban Ki-moon — South Korea — 2007–16 (Shashi Tharoor was runner-up in 2006).
- Antonio Guterres — Portugal — 2017–2026. No woman has yet held the post.
- Vijaya Lakshmi Pandit (1953) was the first woman President of the GA; Annalena Baerbock (Germany) presided over the 80th session (2025–26).

### India and the UN — beyond the basics

- UNSC terms: 1950–51, 1967–68, 1972–73, 1977–78, 1984–85, 1991–92, 2011–12, 2021–22; India lost the 1996 contest to Japan; it is a candidate for 2028–29.
- Peacekeeping firsts: Korea (1953, Gen. K.S. Thimayya chaired the Neutral Nations Repatriation Commission); Congo ONUC (1960–64, Capt. G.S. Salaria — Param Vir Chakra); first all-women Formed Police Unit to Liberia (2007).
- Indian judges at the ICJ: B.N. Rau, Nagendra Singh (President 1985–88), R.S. Pathak, Dalveer Bhandari (since 2012).
- India-led observances: International Day of Non-Violence (2 Oct, 2007), International Yoga Day (21 June, resolution Dec 2014), International Year of Millets (2023).
- India proposed the Comprehensive Convention on International Terrorism (1996) — still not adopted because states disagree on defining terrorism.
- UNSC 1267 sanctions committee listings (e.g. Masood Azhar, May 2019) are key to India's counter-terror diplomacy.
- Punjab link: the Indus basin's eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) and UNESCO — Chandigarh's Capitol Complex (2016); Sri Harmandir Sahib has been on India's UNESCO tentative list since 2004.
- Drug trafficking via the Golden Crescent affects Punjab; UNODC's World Drug Report is the standard global source.

### Agencies, HQs and reports — trap list

- Vienna: IAEA, UNODC, UNIDO, CTBTO Preparatory Commission. Geneva: WHO, ILO, ITU, WIPO, WMO, UNHCR, UNCTAD, Human Rights Council, IOM. Rome: FAO, IFAD, WFP. Nairobi: UNEP, UN-Habitat. Bonn: UNFCCC and UNCCD secretariats. Madrid: UN Tourism (UNWTO). Tokyo: UN University.
- Oldest bodies now in the UN system: ITU (1865) and UPU (1874); ILO (1919) is the oldest that began with the League era and became the first specialised agency (1946).
- International Criminal Court (Rome Statute 1998, The Hague) and the Permanent Court of Arbitration (1899) are NOT UN organs; India is not a party to the Rome Statute.
- Reports: World Investment Report (UNCTAD), Emissions Gap Report and Global Environment Outlook (UNEP), State of World Population (UNFPA), World Drug Report (UNODC), Human Development Report (UNDP), World Happiness Report (UN SDSN-linked, not an agency).
- UN Women (2010) merged four bodies; UNRWA (1949) serves Palestine refugees; UNICEF began as an emergency fund in 1946.
- Security Council presidency rotates monthly in English alphabetical order; India presided in Aug 2021 and Dec 2022.
