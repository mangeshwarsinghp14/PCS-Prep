## Short notes

- Punjab Economic Survey 2025-26: real GSDP growth 6.1%; agriculture −2.5% (2025 floods); sector shares agri 23% / industry 29% / services 48%; per capita income ₹2,30,523.
- Long-run trend: Punjab led states in per capita income in the Green Revolution decades but grew more slowly after the 1990s and has been overtaken by several states.
- Punjab Budget 2026-27 (presented 8 March 2026 by FM Harpal Singh Cheema): total outlay ₹2,60,437 crore.
- Budget 2026-27 (PRS): expenditure excluding debt repayment ₹1,66,711 crore; receipts excluding borrowings ₹1,26,740 crore; GSDP estimated at ₹9,80,635 crore.
- Deficits 2026-27 BE: fiscal deficit 4.1% of GSDP (₹39,971 crore); revenue deficit 2.2% of GSDP (₹21,955 crore), meaning borrowing funds day-to-day spending.
- Debt: outstanding liabilities estimated at 45.1% of GSDP by end-2026-27, among the highest of any state; heavy interest burden.
- Committed expenditure (2026-27 BE) ≈ 72% of revenue receipts: salaries ₹39,115 crore (31%), interest ₹28,755 crore (23%), pensions ₹22,465 crore (18%).
- Subsidies: power subsidy ₹15,550 crore (≈12% of revenue receipts) — free power to farm tubewells (since 1997) and 300 free units a month for households (from July 2022).
- Own tax revenue 2026-27 BE ₹70,851 crore (≈7.2% of GSDP); main sources are SGST, state excise, stamp duty and VAT on fuel.
- Major 2026-27 allocations: Mukh Mantri Mawan Dhian Satikar Yojana ₹9,300 crore (₹1,000/month to women 18+, ₹1,500 to SC women); Sikhiya Kranti 2.0 ₹3,500 crore; Mukh Mantri Sehat Yojana ₹2,000 crore.
- NITI Aayog Fiscal Health Index 2025 (Jan 2025): Punjab ranked last among 18 major states, with weak debt and expenditure-quality scores.
- Fiscal rules: Punjab FRBM Act 2003; the Centre caps state borrowing (Article 293) and has cut limits over off-budget borrowing and the power sector.
- Finance Commission: 15th FC share of Punjab in the divisible pool 1.81%; 16th FC (2026–31, chair Arvind Panagariya) raised it to 2.00%, kept the states' vertical share at 41% and discontinued revenue-deficit grants.
- 16th FC criteria weights: income distance 42.5%, population (2011) 17.5%, demographic performance 10%, area 10%, forest 10%, contribution to GDP 10% (new).
- Centre–state fiscal issues: end of GST compensation (June 2022) hit Punjab, a consuming and agrarian state; the Centre withheld the Rural Development Fund (fee on central procurement) after 2021; disputes over scheme branding and fund releases.
- Other issues: pension liability (the state notified a return to the Old Pension Scheme in Nov 2022); low capital expenditure; borrowing to repay old debt; need for tax reform and power-subsidy targeting.
- Development challenges: agrarian stagnation, low industrial investment, youth emigration and drugs; flood recovery after 2025.
- Key bodies: Punjab State Finance Commission (for local bodies), Economic & Statistical Organisation Punjab (prepares the survey), Planning Department (Annual Plan/District plans).

## Punjab angle

- Use 45.1% debt–GSDP, 72% committed expenditure and last place in the FHI 2025 as hard data in fiscal-stress answers.

## Deep dive

### Budget 2026-27 — the full arithmetic (PRS, 19 Mar 2026)

- Total expenditure ₹2,60,437 crore = net expenditure ₹1,66,711 crore + debt repayment ₹93,726 crore (of which ₹80,000 crore is short-term Ways and Means Advances from the RBI, repaid within the year).
- Net expenditure split: revenue expenditure ₹1,48,146 crore (+3%), capital outlay ₹18,381 crore (+76% over 2025-26 RE), loans ₹184 crore.
- Financing: receipts excluding borrowings ₹1,26,740 crore; net borrowings ₹38,471 crore; central 50-year interest-free capex loans ₹6,799 crore (0.7% of GSDP) are outside the borrowing ceiling.
- Revenue receipts ₹1,26,190 crore: own revenue 69% (own tax ₹70,851 crore, own non-tax ₹15,687 crore) and Centre 31% (tax devolution ₹30,464 crore, +21%; grants ₹9,189 crore, −9%).
- Own tax sources: SGST ₹32,000 crore (45% of own tax), state excise ₹12,800 crore, stamps and registration ₹9,000 crore (+29%), VAT ₹8,200 crore, vehicle tax ₹4,700 crore, electricity duty ₹3,516 crore.
- Non-tax revenue includes ₹3,800 crore budgeted from sale of land and property (₹8,300 crore at the 2025-26 RE stage).
- Primary deficit 1.1% of GSDP; GSDP 2026-27 projected at ₹9,80,635 crore (+10% nominal).
- Outstanding government guarantees ₹23,654 crore (2.7% of GSDP) on 31 Mar 2026 — contingent liabilities not counted in debt.

### Slippages and trends

- 2024-25 actuals vs budget: fiscal deficit 4.7% of GSDP (budgeted 3.8%); revenue deficit 3.9% (budgeted 2.9%); own non-tax revenue 44% below estimate; capital outlay 8% below.
- 2025-26 revised estimates: fiscal deficit 4.2% (budgeted 3.8%); revenue deficit 3.0% (budgeted 2.7%); outstanding liabilities 45.2% of GSDP.
- Debt path in the Medium Term Fiscal Policy: 45.1% (2026-27 BE) → 44.4% (2027-28) → 43.0% (2028-29), projections only.
- Near-continuous use of WMA and overdraft: between 2014-15 and 2023-24 Punjab used them for 221–344 days a year (CAG, cited by PRS); 12 states used no WMA in 2023-24.
- Real growth: GSDP +6.1% in 2025-26 against India's GDP +7.4%; per capita income ₹2,30,523 vs India ₹2,19,575.

### Where the money goes — sector shares vs other states (PRS)

- Education 12.9% of expenditure (states' average 14.5%); allocation ₹21,503 crore incl. ₹3,500 crore for Sikhiya Kranti 2.0 and ₹1,435 crore for Samagra Shiksha.
- Health 4.7% (average 6.2%); ₹7,787 crore incl. ₹2,000 crore for the Mukh Mantri Sehat Yojana (universal health cover).
- Rural development 1.7% (average 4.9%); MGNREGA ₹615 crore and PMAY-Gramin ₹800 crore.
- Agriculture 9.2% (average 5.7%) — mostly the ₹7,715 crore farm power subsidy; police 5.5% (average 4.0%); energy 3.5% (average 5.3%).
- Social welfare and nutrition jumps 66% to ₹18,775 crore — driven by the ₹9,300 crore Mawan Dhian Satikar Yojana cash transfer and ₹6,132 crore social-security pensions.
- Power subsidy 2026-27: ₹15,550 crore = agriculture ₹7,715 crore (50%), domestic ₹5,771 crore (37%), industry ₹2,064 crore (13%); it grew at about 12% a year between 2018-19 and 2025-26.
- A 16th Finance Commission study found 75% of households in Punjab's highest consumption bracket get free electricity — the second-highest share among states studied.

### CAG's audit view (State Finances 2023-24, Report No. 1 of 2025, tabled Mar 2026)

- Total debt and liabilities ₹3.46 lakh crore = 43.72% of GSDP (FRBM ceiling 46.81%); 44.27% including off-budget borrowings.
- Debt grew at 9.16% a year (CAGR) from 2019-20 to 2023-24; CAG warned that growth alone may not stabilise the debt ratio.
- Revenue deficit 3.79% of GSDP against a 3.52% target; fiscal deficit 4.45% against 4.60%.
- Revenue expenditure rose from ₹75,860 crore (2019-20) to ₹1,17,407 crore (2023-24); committed spending took over 75% of revenue.
- Capital expenditure only ₹4,743 crore — 3.88% of total spending; borrowed funds went mostly to current spending and repaying old debt.
- Subsidies rose to about 16% of revenue, 92–99% of it for power; the share of central grants in revenue receipts fell from 23.68% to 16.03%.
- Government's counter-claim: the debt–GSDP ratio fell from 48.25% (2022) to about 44.5% (2026).

### Centre–state finance and the 16th Finance Commission

- Punjab's share of the divisible pool: 1.58% (14th FC) → 1.81% (15th FC) → 2.00% (16th FC, 2026–31); vertical share unchanged at 41%; report tabled 1 Feb 2026 (chair Arvind Panagariya).
- 16th FC grants for Punjab (2026–31): rural local bodies ₹8,486 crore, urban local bodies ₹7,834 crore, disaster management ₹2,477 crore; special wastewater grants up to ₹5,000 crore each for Ludhiana and Amritsar.
- Revenue-deficit, sector-specific and state-specific grants were discontinued — a loss for Punjab, for which the 15th FC had recommended ₹25,968 crore of revenue-deficit grants (these ended in 2024-25).
- Fiscal-deficit limit for states recommended at 3% of GSDP for 2026–31 (central capex loans excluded).
- Long-running grievances: end of GST compensation (2022), withholding of the Rural Development Fund on central procurement, cuts in borrowing limits for off-budget and power-sector liabilities, and the demand to waive militancy-era central loans.
- Constitutional hooks: Article 280 (Finance Commission), 293(3) (Centre's consent for state borrowing while indebted to the Centre), 243-I and 243-Y (State Finance Commission for local bodies).

### State public sector enterprises — a fiscal risk

- 49 SPSEs as of March 2023, of which 16 were inactive; state investment in the 33 working ones was ₹49,920 crore — 99% in power (45%) and agriculture and allied (54%).
- 18 working SPSEs (55%) made losses; the biggest loss-makers were PSPCL, PUNGRAIN (Punjab State Grains Procurement Corporation) and Punjab Agro Foodgrains Corporation.
- Net worth of 14 loss-making SPSEs had been completely eroded by accumulated losses (CAG Report No. 2 of 2024).
- The 16th Finance Commission advised states to review inactive and loss-making SPSEs.
- Guarantees to SPSE borrowings (₹23,654 crore in 2026) can turn into debt if the companies default.
- Grain-holding costs (interest on cash-credit limits, storage losses) are a recurring drain on procurement agencies.

### Growth story and exam framing

- Punjab topped per capita income among major states until the early 1990s; slowdown causes — agrarian saturation, the militancy decade, industrial flight after 2003, fiscal stress, border location.
- Revenue deficit means borrowing to pay salaries, pensions, interest and subsidies; fiscal deficit is total borrowing need; primary deficit removes interest to show current-year imbalance.
- Committed expenditure above 70% of revenue receipts leaves little room for capital spending — use the 72% (2026-27) and 85% (2024-25 actual) figures.
- Reform menu: targeted power subsidy (DBT or caps for large farmers), property-tax and stamp-duty reform, SPSE restructuring, capital-expenditure ring-fencing, better GST compliance.
- Pension: return to the Old Pension Scheme (notified Nov 2022) adds future liabilities.
- Quote sources with year: PRS (Mar 2026), CAG (Report No. 1 of 2025), NITI Fiscal Health Index 2025 (data year 2022-23; Punjab grouped with the 'aspirational' weakest states).
