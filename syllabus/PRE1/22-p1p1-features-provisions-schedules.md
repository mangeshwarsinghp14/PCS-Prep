## Short notes

- Constituent Assembly formed under the Cabinet Mission Plan (1946); first met 9 Dec 1946; Rajendra Prasad was President; Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution on 13 Dec 1946.
- Drafting Committee set up 29 Aug 1947 with B.R. Ambedkar as chairman; Constitution adopted 26 Nov 1949 (Constitution Day) and came fully into force 26 Jan 1950.
- Original Constitution: 395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules; today it has 25 Parts, 12 Schedules and well over 450 Articles - the lengthiest written constitution in the world.
- Salient features: blend of rigidity and flexibility; federal system with unitary bias ('Union of States', Art 1); parliamentary government; integrated and independent judiciary; synthesis of parliamentary sovereignty and judicial review.
- Other features: Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles and Fundamental Duties; secular state; universal adult franchise; single citizenship; independent bodies (ECI, CAG, UPSC); emergency provisions; three-tier government after the 73rd/74th Amendments.
- Government of India Act 1935 supplied the bulk: federal scheme, office of Governor, judiciary, public service commissions, emergency provisions and administrative details.
- Borrowed from UK: parliamentary system, rule of law, single citizenship, cabinet system, writs, bicameralism; from USA: Fundamental Rights, judicial review, independent judiciary, impeachment of President, removal of judges, Vice-President.
- From Ireland: Directive Principles, nomination of Rajya Sabha members, method of presidential election; from Canada: federation with strong Centre, residuary powers with Centre, Centre appointing Governors, advisory jurisdiction of SC.
- From Australia: Concurrent List, freedom of trade and commerce, joint sitting; Weimar Germany: suspension of FRs in emergency; USSR: Fundamental Duties and ideal of justice; France: republic and liberty-equality-fraternity; South Africa: amendment procedure; Japan: 'procedure established by law'.
- Basic structure doctrine: Kesavananda Bharati (1973, 13-judge bench, 7:6) - Parliament can amend any part but not destroy the basic structure; Preamble held part of the Constitution (Berubari 1960 had said otherwise).
- Schedules 1-4: (1) States and UTs; (2) salaries and allowances of President, Governors, Speaker, judges, CAG etc.; (3) oaths and affirmations; (4) allocation of Rajya Sabha seats.
- Schedules 5-6: (5) administration of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes; (6) tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram (autonomous district councils).
- Schedule 7: Union, State and Concurrent Lists (originally 97, 66 and 47 entries); the 42nd Amendment shifted five subjects - education, forests, weights and measures, protection of wild animals and birds, administration of justice - from State to Concurrent List.
- Schedule 8: 22 languages - originally 14; Sindhi added 1967 (21st Amdt), Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali 1992 (71st), Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali 2003 (92nd).
- Schedule 9 (1st Amendment 1951) shields land-reform laws; I.R. Coelho (2007): laws placed there after 24 April 1973 can be reviewed if they violate the basic structure.
- Schedules 10-12: (10) anti-defection (52nd Amdt 1985); (11) 29 subjects for Panchayats (73rd); (12) 18 subjects for Municipalities (74th).
- Key Article ranges: 1-4 Union and territory; 12-35 FRs; 36-51 DPSP; 51A duties; 52-151 Union; 152-237 States; 243 Panchayats and Municipalities; 245-263 Centre-State relations; 280 Finance Commission; 324 ECI; 352/356/360 emergencies; 368 amendment.
- Article 370 (temporary provisions for J&K) was made inoperative in Aug 2019 and the move was upheld by the Supreme Court in Dec 2023.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab was reorganised under Article 3 by the Punjab Reorganisation Act 1966 - Haryana created, hill areas transferred to Himachal Pradesh, Chandigarh made a UT.
- Punjabi was among the 14 languages in the original Eighth Schedule.
- Chandigarh is administered under Article 239; since 1984 the Governor of Punjab also serves as the Administrator of Chandigarh.

## How PPSC asks it

PPSC repeatedly asks 'source of borrowed feature' matches, which Schedule contains what, and Constituent Assembly dates. Memorise the 12 Schedules and the language-addition amendments.

## Deep dive

### Article-wise map I — Parts I to XI (Part — Articles — subject)

- Part I — Arts 1–4 — Union and its territory (Art 3 new states/boundary changes by simple law).
- Part II — Arts 5–11 — Citizenship.
- Part III — Arts 12–35 — Fundamental Rights.
- Part IV — Arts 36–51 — Directive Principles; Part IVA — Art 51A — Fundamental Duties.
- Part V — Arts 52–151 — The Union (executive 52–78, Parliament 79–122, President's legislative power 123, Union judiciary 124–147, CAG 148–151).
- Part VI — Arts 152–237 — The States (Governor, state legislature, High Courts, subordinate courts); Art 152 earlier excluded J&K.
- Part VII — originally Part B states; repealed by the 7th Amendment (1956).
- Part VIII — Arts 239–242 — Union Territories (Art 239AA Delhi, 239AB failure of machinery in Delhi).
- Part IX — Arts 243–243-O — Panchayats; Part IXA — Arts 243P–243ZG — Municipalities; Part IXB — Arts 243ZH–243ZT — Co-operative societies (97th Amendment).
- Part X — Arts 244–244A — Scheduled and Tribal Areas.
- Part XI — Arts 245–263 — Relations between Union and States (legislative 245–255, administrative 256–263).

### Article-wise map II — Parts XII to XXII

- Part XII — Arts 264–300A — Finance, property, contracts and suits (266 Consolidated Fund, 267 Contingency Fund, 280 Finance Commission, 300A right to property).
- Part XIII — Arts 301–307 — Freedom of trade, commerce and intercourse within India.
- Part XIV — Arts 308–323 — Services under Union and States (311 safeguards for civil servants, 312 All India Services, 315–323 Public Service Commissions).
- Part XIVA — Arts 323A–323B — Tribunals (42nd Amendment).
- Part XV — Arts 324–329 — Elections (Art 329A was repealed by the 44th Amendment).
- Part XVI — Arts 330–342A — Special provisions for SCs, STs, OBCs, Anglo-Indians (338 NCSC, 338A NCST, 338B NCBC, 342A SEBC lists).
- Part XVII — Arts 343–351 — Official language (343 Hindi in Devanagari, 350A mother-tongue instruction, 350B linguistic minorities officer, 351 development of Hindi).
- Part XVIII — Arts 352–360 — Emergency provisions.
- Part XIX — Arts 361–367 — Miscellaneous (361 immunity of President and Governors, 365 effect of non-compliance with Union directions).
- Part XX — Art 368 — Amendment.
- Part XXI — Arts 369–392 — Temporary, transitional and special provisions (370 J&K, 371–371J special provisions for states).
- Part XXII — Arts 393–395 — Short title, commencement, authoritative Hindi text (394A, added 1987), repeals (395 repealed the Indian Independence Act 1947 and the GoI Act 1935).

### Schedules — finer details beyond the summary

- First Schedule: 28 states and 8 UTs (after Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu merged in Jan 2020).
- Second Schedule: Parts A–E list salaries/allowances (President, Governors; Speakers and Chairmen; SC and HC judges; CAG).
- Third Schedule has oaths of Union and state ministers, MPs, MLAs, judges and the CAG — but the President's (Art 60), Vice-President's (Art 69) and Governor's (Art 159) oaths are in the Articles themselves.
- Fourth Schedule: Rajya Sabha seats — Uttar Pradesh 31 (highest); Punjab 7.
- Fifth Schedule: Scheduled Areas in 10 states (AP, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, HP, Jharkhand, MP, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan); Tribes Advisory Council of up to 20 members, three-fourths ST MLAs.
- Sixth Schedule: 10 autonomous district councils — Assam 3, Meghalaya 3, Mizoram 3, Tripura 1; Governor can create/alter districts.
- Seventh Schedule today: Union List about 100 subjects (numbered up to 97 with lettered entries), State List about 61, Concurrent List about 52.
- Eighth Schedule links to Arts 344(1) and 351; Punjabi has been in it since 1950.
- Tenth Schedule para 4: merger exception if two-thirds of the legislature party agree; para 6: Speaker/Chairman decides.
- Twelfth Schedule begins with "urban planning including town planning" and ends with "regulation of slaughter houses and tanneries"; includes fire services, urban forestry, burials and cremation grounds, cattle pounds.

### Making of the Constitution — people and committees

- Sachchidananda Sinha was temporary President (9 Dec 1946); Rajendra Prasad permanent President; H.C. Mookerjee and V.T. Krishnamachari Vice-Presidents.
- Drafting Committee (7): B.R. Ambedkar (chair), N. Gopalaswamy Ayyangar, Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar, K.M. Munshi, Mohammad Saadullah, N. Madhava Rau (replaced B.L. Mitter), T.T. Krishnamachari (replaced D.P. Khaitan, who died in 1948).
- B.N. Rau was the Constitutional Adviser; S.N. Mukherjee the chief draftsman.
- Committees: Union Powers and Union Constitution (Nehru); Provincial Constitution, and Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights and Minorities (Patel); Rules of Procedure and Steering (Rajendra Prasad).
- Sub-committees: Fundamental Rights (J.B. Kripalani), Minorities (H.C. Mookerjee), North-East tribal areas (Gopinath Bardoloi), Excluded areas (A.V. Thakkar).
- After Partition the Assembly had 299 members; 11 sessions over about 165 days; members signed on 24 Jan 1950, the same day Rajendra Prasad was elected President and Jana Gana Mana adopted as national anthem.
- Calligraphy by Prem Behari Narain Raizada; illustrations by Nandalal Bose and his students (Santiniketan).
- Punjab members included Sardar Hukam Singh (later Lok Sabha Speaker, 1962–67), Bhupinder Singh Maan, Gurmukh Singh Musafir, Bakshi Tek Chand and Pandit Thakur Das Bhargava — whose amendment inserted "reasonable" before "restrictions" in what became Art 19.
- Descriptions: "lawyers' paradise" (Ivor Jennings); "first and foremost a social document" (Granville Austin); Preamble as "identity card" (N.A. Palkhivala) and "political horoscope" (K.M. Munshi).

### High-yield single Articles (Article — subject)

- Art 13 — laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights void; "law" includes ordinances, rules, customs (not constitutional amendments, after the 24th Amendment).
- Arts 31A–31C — saving of certain laws (agrarian reform; 31C protects laws for Art 39(b)–(c)).
- Art 39(b) — Property Owners Association (Nov 2024, 9 judges): not every private resource is a "material resource of the community".
- Art 72/161 — pardon powers; Art 78 — PM's duty to inform the President; Art 88 — ministers and Attorney General may speak in either House.
- Art 131 — original jurisdiction in Centre–state disputes; Art 143 — advisory jurisdiction (used for the Nov 2025 Governor-assent reference).
- Art 200/201 — assent to state Bills; Art 213 — Governor's ordinances; Art 231 — common High Court for two or more states (Punjab, Haryana and Chandigarh share one).
- Art 239 — UT administration (Chandigarh run by the Punjab Governor as Administrator); Art 240 — President's regulation power for certain UTs.
- Art 262 — Parliament may bar courts in inter-state water disputes; Art 263 — Inter-State Council.
- Art 300A — property; Art 311 — dismissal safeguards; Art 335 — efficiency in services vs reservation.
- Art 355 — Union's duty to protect states; Art 356 — President's Rule; Art 360 — financial emergency (never used).
- Art 361 — no criminal proceedings against the President or Governor during term; Art 365 — failure to follow Union directions.
- Art 371 to 371J — special provisions for Maharashtra/Gujarat, Nagaland, Assam, Manipur, AP/Telangana, Sikkim, Mizoram, Arunachal, Goa and Karnataka (Hyderabad-Karnataka, 371J, 2012).

### Punjab specifics

- PEPSU was a Part B state (1950–56) headed by a Rajpramukh (the Maharaja of Patiala); it merged into Punjab on 1 Nov 1956.
- Punjab's Rajya Sabha share (7) is fixed in the Fourth Schedule; Lok Sabha 13 seats under Art 81 and the Delimitation Order.
- Art 231 common High Court: the Punjab and Haryana High Court at Chandigarh also serves the UT.
- Art 3 was used for the Punjab Reorganisation Act 1966; the 18th Amendment (1966) clarified that "State" in Art 3 includes a UT and that forming a new state includes uniting part of a state or UT to another — enabling the Punjabi Suba.
- Chandigarh under Arts 239/240: in Nov 2025 a proposal to place Chandigarh under Art 240 (as a Constitution 131st Amendment Bill) was listed and then dropped after protests across Punjab parties.
- The Constitution's Punjabi translation is published by the Legislative Department (official-language regional series).
- Art 25 Explanation I (kirpan) and Explanation II (Sikhs grouped with Hindus for reform laws) are Punjab-specific provisions often asked in Mains.

### Traps

- Art 1 calls India a "Union of States"; the word "federation" is not used.
- Original Constitution: 395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules; today about 470 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules — Article numbers were kept by inserting lettered Articles.
- Part VII no longer exists but the Parts are not renumbered.
- Hindi authoritative text is Art 394A (58th Amendment, 1987).
- Fundamental Duties are a single Article (51A) in Part IVA.
- Commonwealth membership does not limit sovereignty (London Declaration, 1949).
- Preamble amended only once — the 42nd Amendment (1976).

(a) Canada
