## Short notes

- Basic life processes: nutrition, respiration, transportation (circulation) and excretion, coordinated by nervous and hormonal systems.
- Autotrophic nutrition (photosynthesis): 6CO2 + 6H2O gives glucose + 6O2 using sunlight and chlorophyll; heterotrophs may be holozoic, saprophytic (fungi) or parasitic.
- Digestion: salivary amylase starts starch digestion in the mouth; stomach secretes HCl, pepsin (proteins) and mucus; bile from the liver emulsifies fats (has no enzyme; stored in gall bladder).
- Pancreatic juice has trypsin (proteins), lipase (fats) and amylase; the small intestine completes digestion and absorbs food through finger-like villi; the large intestine absorbs water.
- Liver is the largest gland; small intestine is the longest part of the gut.
- Aerobic respiration in mitochondria breaks glucose into CO2, water and ATP; anaerobic respiration gives lactic acid in muscles (cramps) and ethanol + CO2 in yeast (fermentation).
- Gas exchange happens in lung alveoli; haemoglobin in RBCs carries oxygen; the diaphragm drives breathing.
- Human heart has four chambers and double circulation; arteries carry blood away from the heart (pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood), veins have valves; William Harvey described circulation.
- Normal adult blood pressure about 120/80 mm Hg; RBCs live about 120 days and are made in red bone marrow; WBCs fight infection; platelets help clotting.
- ABO blood groups were discovered by Karl Landsteiner; O negative is the universal donor and AB positive the universal recipient (for red cells); Rh incompatibility can affect a second Rh+ baby of an Rh- mother.
- Excretion: urea is made in the liver and filtered by the kidneys; the nephron is the kidney's functional unit; dialysis is used in kidney failure.
- Brain: cerebrum (thinking, memory, voluntary actions), cerebellum (balance, posture), medulla oblongata (involuntary actions like heartbeat, breathing); reflex actions are controlled by the spinal cord.
- Hormones: insulin (pancreas; lack causes diabetes), thyroxine (needs iodine; lack causes goitre), growth hormone (pituitary - the 'master gland'), adrenaline (emergency response).
- Adult skeleton has 206 bones; femur is the longest, stapes (in the middle ear) the smallest.
- Energy values: carbohydrates and proteins about 4 kcal/g, fats about 9 kcal/g; roughage (dietary fibre) aids bowel movement.
- Fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K; water-soluble B-complex and C. Deficiencies: A - night blindness, B1 - beriberi, B3 - pellagra, B12 - pernicious anaemia, C - scurvy, D - rickets/osteomalacia, K - poor clotting.
- Vitamin D is made in skin exposed to sunlight; vitamin K is partly made by gut bacteria.
- Minerals: iron (anaemia), iodine (goitre), calcium (bones, teeth). Protein deficiency causes kwashiorkor; combined protein and calorie deficiency causes marasmus.

- Punjab carries a high burden of diet-related lifestyle conditions such as diabetes, obesity and hypertension, linked to calorie-dense diets and low activity.

## Deep dive

### Digestion and absorption — enzyme and hormone table beyond NCERT

- Mouth: salivary amylase (ptyalin) turns starch into maltose; lysozyme in saliva kills some bacteria.
- Stomach: HCl activates pepsinogen to pepsin; rennin curdles milk in infants; the stomach also makes intrinsic factor, needed to absorb vitamin B12.
- Gut hormones: gastrin raises stomach acid; secretin and cholecystokinin (CCK) from the duodenum trigger pancreatic juice and bile release.
- Pancreas: trypsinogen is activated to trypsin by enterokinase; pancreatic amylase, lipase and nucleases finish the job with intestinal maltase, sucrase and lactase.
- Lactose intolerance results from low lactase; coeliac disease is an immune reaction to gluten (wheat protein).
- Absorbed sugars and amino acids go to the liver through the hepatic portal vein; fats enter lacteals (lymph) as chylomicrons.
- Liver roles: stores glycogen, iron and vitamins A, D, B12; makes urea (ornithine cycle), bile, albumin and fibrinogen; detoxifies drugs and alcohol.
- Gut bacteria in the large intestine make vitamin K and some B vitamins; the gut microbiome shapes immunity and metabolism.
- Most peptic ulcers are caused by Helicobacter pylori (Marshall and Warren, Nobel 2005), not by stress alone.

### Circulation, respiration and excretion — numbers and applications

- Resting heart rate about 72 per minute; cardiac output about 5 litres per minute; the SA node is the natural pacemaker and an ECG records its electrical activity.
- An adult has about 5–6 litres of blood (plasma about 55%); WHO anaemia cut-offs for haemoglobin are 13 g/dL for men, 12 for non-pregnant women and 11 for pregnant women and children 6–59 months.
- The lymphatic system returns tissue fluid and carries fats; the spleen removes old red cells.
- Carbon monoxide binds haemoglobin over 200 times more strongly than oxygen — the reason coal heaters (angithi) in closed rooms kill people in winter.
- At high altitude the body makes more red cells (acclimatisation); sudden ascent can cause mountain sickness.
- Each kidney has about a million nephrons; together they filter about 180 litres a day but excrete only 1–1.5 litres of urine. ADH (vasopressin) controls water reabsorption — its lack causes diabetes insipidus.
- Kidney stones are mostly calcium oxalate; the Pradhan Mantri National Dialysis Programme (2016) offers free dialysis in district hospitals.
- Organ donation is governed by the Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act, 1994; NOTTO coordinates national allocation.

### Control, coordination and hormones (applications)

- Neurotransmitters: acetylcholine (muscle action), dopamine (its loss causes Parkinson's disease), serotonin (mood); myelin speeds nerve impulses.
- The hypothalamus links nerves and hormones and controls temperature, hunger and thirst; the pituitary releases GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, prolactin, oxytocin and ADH.
- Excess growth hormone in childhood causes gigantism, in adults acromegaly; deficiency in childhood causes dwarfism.
- Thyroid: low thyroxine in children causes cretinism, excess causes Graves' disease; calcitonin lowers and parathyroid hormone raises blood calcium.
- Adrenal cortex makes cortisol (stress, anti-inflammatory) and aldosterone (sodium balance); the adrenal medulla makes adrenaline for 'fight or flight'.
- Islets of Langerhans: beta cells make insulin, alpha cells glucagon; insulin was isolated by Banting and Best in 1921.
- The pineal gland secretes melatonin, which sets the sleep–wake (circadian) rhythm — the subject of the 2017 Nobel in Medicine.
- Rods need vitamin A (rhodopsin) for dim-light vision, cones give colour; the semicircular canals of the inner ear sense balance, which is why mismatched signals cause motion sickness.

### Nutrition science and Indian guidelines

- ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2024) give 17 guidelines and estimate that over half of India's disease burden is linked to unhealthy diets; they advise limiting added sugar, salt (WHO: under 5 g a day) and ultra-processed food.
- Adults need roughly 0.8 g of protein per kg body weight per day; 'hidden hunger' means micronutrient deficiency despite enough calories.
- Minerals: zinc (immunity, growth; ORS plus zinc for childhood diarrhoea), potassium and sodium (nerve signals), fluoride (teeth), selenium (antioxidant), cobalt (part of vitamin B12), magnesium (centre of chlorophyll).
- Folic acid (B9) before and in early pregnancy prevents neural-tube defects; vitamin A deficiency is a leading preventable cause of childhood blindness, so under-fives get six-monthly vitamin A doses.
- FSSAI fortification standards (2018) cover wheat flour, rice, edible oil, milk and double-fortified salt; the Centre approved supply of fortified rice through PDS and other schemes until 2028 (October 2024).
- FSSAI limits industrial trans fat to 2% of total fat in oils and fats (from 2022).
- 2023 was the International Year of Millets after an India-sponsored UN resolution; millets are marketed as 'Shree Anna'.
- NFHS-5 (2019-21): 24% of women and about 23% of men were overweight or obese (BMI 25 or more); Asian Indians face metabolic risk at lower BMI.

### Life processes in plants (often clubbed with this topic)

- C3 plants (wheat, rice) lose energy to photorespiration in heat; C4 plants (maize, sugarcane, sorghum) concentrate CO2 using Kranz anatomy and are more efficient in hot sun.
- Water rises in xylem mainly by transpiration pull (cohesion–tension); phloem carries sugars from source leaves to sinks such as grains and roots.
- Auxin causes cell elongation and rooting (synthetic 2,4-D kills broad-leaved weeds); gibberellin elongates stems and enlarges seedless grapes; cytokinin promotes cell division and delays ageing.
- Abscisic acid closes stomata under drought and keeps seeds dormant; ethylene ripens fruit.
- Nitrogen fixation: Rhizobium in legume nodules (enzyme nitrogenase, protected by leghaemoglobin), free-living Azotobacter, and cyanobacteria such as Anabaena living in the fern Azolla in paddy fields.
- Photoperiodism: rice and soybean are short-day plants, wheat a long-day plant; vernalisation (cold exposure) triggers flowering in winter cereals.

### Punjab links

- NFHS-5 (2019-21) recorded anaemia in about 71% of Punjab's children aged 6–59 months and nearly 59% of women, both higher than in NFHS-4.
- About two in five women in Punjab were overweight or obese in NFHS-5, among the highest shares in India — a lifestyle-nutrition warning.
- Punjab has among the highest per-person milk availability in India, yet anaemia persists — showing that calories and protein do not guarantee iron and micronutrient sufficiency.
- PGIMER Chandigarh runs one of north India's busiest deceased-donor organ transplant and dialysis services, a referral point for Punjab patients.
- Coal-heater (angithi) deaths from carbon monoxide recur in Punjab winters — a practical example for respiration questions.
- Rice–wheat monocropping and polished grain diets reduce dietary diversity; millet and pulse promotion in Punjab ties into crop diversification.

## Practice MCQs (12)

**1.** Which vitamin deficiency causes scurvy?

(a) Vitamin D  
(b) Vitamin A  
(c) Vitamin C  
(d) Vitamin B1

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Vitamin C** — Scurvy (bleeding gums, poor wound healing) results from lack of ascorbic acid.

**2.** Bile, which helps in digestion of fats, is produced by the:

(a) Pancreas  
(b) Stomach  
(c) Liver  
(d) Gall bladder

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Liver** — Bile is made in the liver and only stored in the gall bladder; it emulsifies fats and has no enzymes.

</details>

**3.** Which part of the brain maintains posture and balance of the body?

(a) Medulla oblongata  
(b) Hypothalamus  
(c) Cerebrum  
(d) Cerebellum

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Cerebellum** — The cerebellum coordinates voluntary movement, posture and balance.

</details>

**4.** Consider: 1. Veins carry blood away from the heart. 2. The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood. Which is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** — Arteries carry blood away from the heart (the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs); veins return blood to the heart.

</details>

**5.** Muscle cramps after strenuous exercise are caused by accumulation of:

(a) Pyruvic acid  
(b) Urea  
(c) Lactic acid  
(d) Ethanol

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Lactic acid** — Under oxygen shortage, muscles respire anaerobically and produce lactic acid.

</details>

**6.** Which of the following is a water-soluble vitamin?

(a) Vitamin B12  
(b) Vitamin D  
(c) Vitamin A  
(d) Vitamin K

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Vitamin B12** — B-complex and C are water-soluble; A, D, E and K are fat-soluble.

</details>

**7.** Consider the following statements:  
1. Digested fats are absorbed into the lacteals of intestinal villi.  
2. Glucose absorbed from the intestine reaches the liver first through the hepatic portal vein.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Both 1 and 2** — Fats go into lymph via lacteals; sugars go to the liver via the portal vein.

</details>

**8.** Consider the following pairs (hormone — disorder due to deficiency):  
1. ADH — diabetes insipidus  
2. Insulin — diabetes mellitus  
3. Thyroxine in children — cretinism  
Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

(a) 1 and 2 only  
(b) 2 and 3 only  
(c) 1 and 3 only  
(d) 1, 2 and 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) 1, 2 and 3** — All three are standard deficiency disorders.

</details>

**9.** Most peptic ulcers are caused by:

(a) Vitamin C deficiency  
(b) Excess bile secretion  
(c) Infection with Helicobacter pylori  
(d) Lack of salivary amylase

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Infection with Helicobacter pylori** — Marshall and Warren proved the bacterial cause (Nobel 2005).

</details>

**10.** Which gland secretes melatonin, which regulates the sleep–wake cycle?

(a) Parathyroid  
(b) Adrenal medulla  
(c) Thymus  
(d) Pineal gland

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Pineal gland** — The pineal gland releases melatonin in darkness.

</details>

**11.** Burning coal in a closed room is dangerous mainly because carbon monoxide:

(a) Blocks the windpipe physically  
(b) Destroys red blood cells directly  
(c) Turns the blood strongly acidic at once  
(d) Binds haemoglobin far more strongly than oxygen

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Binds haemoglobin far more strongly than oxygen** — Carbon monoxide binds haemoglobin about 200+ times more strongly than oxygen, forming carboxyhaemoglobin that cannot carry oxygen. It does not destroy red cells or block the windpipe, which are the wrong options.

</details>

**12.** Which of the following is a C4 plant?

(a) Wheat  
(b) Rice  
(c) Maize  
(d) Mustard

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Maize** — Maize, sugarcane and sorghum are C4; wheat, rice and mustard are C3.

</details>
