## Short notes

- Punjabi is written in Gurmukhi (in India), standardised under Guru Angad Dev; Shahmukhi (Perso-Arabic) is used in Pakistan.
- Baba Farid (12th–13th c.) is regarded as the first major Punjabi poet; his shabads and saloks are in the Guru Granth Sahib.
- Guru Nanak's works include Japji Sahib, Asa di Var, Babarvani (on Babur's invasion) and Barah Maha (in Raag Tukhari); the Guru Granth Sahib is the core of medieval Punjabi literature.
- Bhai Gurdas (Guru Arjan's scribe) wrote 40 Vaaran in Punjabi — called the key to the Guru Granth Sahib — and Kabit Savaiye in Braj.
- Janamsakhis (life stories of Guru Nanak — Puratan, Bhai Bala, Meharban traditions) are the earliest Punjabi prose.
- Sufi poetry: Shah Hussain (kafis), Sultan Bahu (siharfi), Bulleh Shah (kafis, Kasur) — all from undivided Punjab.
- Qissa (romance) tradition: Damodar's Heer (Akbar's time, earliest), Waris Shah's Heer (1766, the classic), Peelu's Mirza Sahiban, Hashim Shah's Sassi Punnu, Qadir Yar's Puran Bhagat.
- Shah Muhammad's Jangnama (Jang Singhan te Firangian) describes the First Anglo-Sikh War; var, siharfi, baramaha, kafi and jangnama are classic Punjabi genres.
- Bhai Vir Singh (1872–1957), father of modern Punjabi literature: Sundari (1898, first Punjabi novel), the epic Rana Surat Singh, Khalsa Samachar (1899); his Mere Saiyan Jiyo won the first Punjabi Sahitya Akademi Award (1955).
- Bhai Kahn Singh Nabha compiled the Mahan Kosh, an encyclopaedia of Sikh literature (published 1930); Prof. Puran Singh pioneered free verse (Khulle Maidan).
- Poets: Dhani Ram Chatrik (pioneer of modern Punjabi poetry and Gurmukhi typography), Prof. Mohan Singh (Sawe Pattar; Sahitya Akademi 1959 for Wadda Vela), Shiv Kumar Batalvi (Loona — Sahitya Akademi 1967, youngest recipient, called 'Birha da Sultan').
- Amrita Pritam (1919–2005): Sunehure (Sahitya Akademi 1956, first woman recipient), Jnanpith for Kagaz te Canvas (1981), novel Pinjar, autobiography Rasidi Ticket, and a famous Partition elegy addressed to Waris Shah.
- Gurdial Singh (1933–2016): novels Marhi da Diva (1964) and Anhe Ghore da Daan; Jnanpith Award 1999. He and Amrita Pritam are the only Punjabi Jnanpith winners.
- Nanak Singh, novelist (Pavitra Papi, Chitta Lahu; Sahitya Akademi 1961 for Ik Mian Do Talwaran); Jaswant Singh Kanwal (Lahu di Lo); Kulwant Singh Virk, short stories.
- Gurbakhsh Singh Preetlari: edited Preet Lari magazine (1933) and founded Preet Nagar between Amritsar and Lahore; Sant Singh Sekhon and Kartar Singh Duggal were major writers.
- Drama: Norah Richards, the 'mother of Punjabi theatre'; Ishwar Chander Nanda's Subhadra, an early modern Punjabi play; Balwant Gargi (Loha Kut); Ajmer Singh Aulakh (rural plays).
- Revolutionary poetry: Pash (Avtar Singh Sandhu, killed 1988); Sant Ram Udasi. Surjit Patar (1945–2024): Hanere vich Sulagdi Varnmala (Sahitya Akademi 1993), Padma Shri 2012.
- Dalip Kaur Tiwana: Eho Hamara Jivana (Sahitya Akademi 1971), Padma Shri 2004 (returned in 2015). Institutions: Punjabi Sahit Akademi Ludhiana (1954), Punjabi University Patiala (1962).

## Punjab angle

- Punjabi University Patiala was set up in 1962 to develop the Punjabi language; the Bhasha Vibhag (Languages Department) gives the state's literary awards (Shiromani Sahitkar).

## How PPSC asks it

Author–work–award matching dominates: first novel, Jnanpith winners, Sahitya Akademi firsts, qissa authors, Jangnama. Memorise pairs and years rather than literary analysis.

## Deep dive

### Periods and genres — the skeleton of Punjabi literary history

- Early phase (before 1500): Nath-jogi sayings (Gorakhnath tradition) and the saloks of Baba Farid — the oldest securely dated Punjabi verse.
- Guru period (1500–1700): bani of the Gurus, Bhai Gurdas's Vaaran, Janamsakhis (first prose), goshtis (dialogues) and paramarth (commentary).
- Sufi and qissa period (1600–1850): kafi, siharfi, baramaha, qissa romances, var and jangnama.
- Singh Sabha / modern period (1870–1947): print culture, novel, essay, drama, journalism; Bhai Vir Singh to Puran Singh and Mohan Singh.
- After 1947: progressive (pragativadi) poetry, experimentalism (prayogvad, 1960s), Naxalite/jujharu poetry (1970s), Dalit writing, Partition and diaspora literature.
- Var — heroic ballad sung to dhadi music (Guru Nanak's Asa di Var; Guru Gobind Singh's Chandi di Var; Najabat's Var of Nadir Shah).
- Siharfi — acrostic poem following the alphabet (Sultan Bahu); Baramaha — poem on the twelve months (Guru Nanak's in Raag Tukhari, Guru Arjan's in Majh).
- Kafi — short lyrical Sufi song with a refrain (Shah Hussain, Bulleh Shah); Jangnama — war poem (Shah Muhammad on the First Anglo-Sikh War).

### Sufi and qissa poets — name, period, work

- Baba Farid (1173–1266): 112 saloks and 4 shabads included in the Guru Granth Sahib.
- Shah Hussain (1538–1599), Lahore: kafis; Mela Chiraghan at his shrine; companion Madho Lal.
- Sultan Bahu (1630–1691), Jhang: Qadiri Sufi; siharfi with the refrain 'Hu'.
- Bulleh Shah (1680–1757), Kasur: kafis attacking orthodoxy and caste; disciple of Shah Inayat Qadiri.
- Damodar Gulati — earliest Heer (Akbar's time); Waris Shah — Heer (1766), written at Malka Hans; the definitive qissa.
- Peelu and Hafiz Barkhurdar — Mirza–Sahiban; Hashim Shah — Sassi–Punnu (Ranjit Singh's era); Fazal Shah — Sohni–Mahiwal; Qadir Yar — Puran Bhagat and Raja Rasalu.
- Mian Muhammad Bakhsh — Saif-ul-Muluk (1863), the best-loved epic of the Pothohar region.
- Shah Muhammad — Jangnama (Jang Singhan te Firangian), an eyewitness-era lament on the fall of the Sikh kingdom (1845–46).

### Modern writers — work, award and identifying tag

- Bhai Vir Singh (1872–1957): novels Sundari (1898), Bijay Singh, Satwant Kaur; epic Rana Surat Singh; first Punjabi Sahitya Akademi award (1955, Mere Saiyan Jio); Padma Bhushan 1956.
- Bhai Kahn Singh Nabha: Mahan Kosh (1930), the encyclopaedia of Sikh literature; Ham Hindu Nahin (1898).
- Prof. Puran Singh: free verse — Khulle Maidan, Khulle Ghund; Dhani Ram Chatrik: Chandan Wari, Kesar Kiari.
- Prof. Mohan Singh: Sawe Pattar (1936); Sahitya Akademi 1959 for Wadda Vela; edited Panj Darya.
- Amrita Pritam (1919–2005): Sahitya Akademi 1956 (Sunehade); Jnanpith 1981 (Kagaz te Canvas); Padma Vibhushan 2004; novel Pinjar (1950); edited the magazine Nagmani.
- Shiv Kumar Batalvi (1936–73): verse-play Loona (1965), Sahitya Akademi 1967 — youngest recipient.
- Harbhajan Singh: Sahitya Akademi 1969 and Saraswati Samman 1994 (Rukh te Rishi).
- Kartar Singh Duggal (short stories; Sahitya Akademi 1965; Padma Bhushan 1988) and Kulwant Singh Virk (Sahitya Akademi 1968).
- Dalip Kaur Tiwana: Eho Hamara Jivana (Sahitya Akademi 1971); Saraswati Samman 2001 (Katha Kaho Urvashi).
- Gurdial Singh (1933–2016): Marhi da Deeva (1964), Anhe Ghore da Daan, Parsa; Sahitya Akademi 1975 (Adh Chanani Raat); Padma Shri 1998; Jnanpith 1999.
- Surjit Patar (1945–2024): Sahitya Akademi 1993; Saraswati Samman 2009 (Lafzan di Dargah); Padma Shri 2012, returned in 2020 in support of the farmers' protest.

### Award tables to memorise (verified to 2025)

- Jnanpith in Punjabi: only Amrita Pritam (1981) and Gurdial Singh (1999); the 60th Jnanpith (2025) went to Tamil poet Vairamuthu, so the Punjabi count remains two.
- Saraswati Samman in Punjabi: Harbhajan Singh (1994), Dalip Kaur Tiwana (2001), Surjit Patar (2009).
- Sahitya Akademi (Punjabi) firsts: Bhai Vir Singh 1955 (first), Amrita Pritam 1956 (first woman); no Punjabi award was given in 1957, 1958, 1960 and 1966.
- Recent Sahitya Akademi (Punjabi): 2015 Jaswinder Singh (Maat Lok, novel); 2016 Swarajbir (Maseya di Raat, play); 2017 Nachhattar (Slow Down, novel); 2018 Mohanjit (Kone da Sooraj, poetry).
- 2019 Kirpal Kazak (Antheen, stories); 2020 Gurdev Singh Rupana (Aam Khass, stories); 2021 Khalid Hussain (Sullan da Salan, stories); 2022 Sukhjit (Main Aynghosh Nahin, stories).
- 2023 Swarnjit Savi (Mann di Chip, poetry); 2024 Paul Kaur (Sun Gunvanta Sun Budhivanta, poetry); 2025 Jinder (Safety Kit, short stories; announced 16 March 2026).
- Other Sahitya Akademi winners often asked: Jaswant Singh Kanwal (1997, Taushali di Hanso), Ram Sarup Ankhi (1987, Kothe Kharak Singh), Ajeet Cour (1985, Khana Badosh — autobiography), Ajmer Singh Aulakh (2006, play), Waryam Singh Sandhu (2000, Chauthi Koot).
- Yuva Puraskar 2025 (Punjabi): Mandeep Aulakh, a GNDU alumna, for a poetry collection (Tribune report).

### Drama, prose and the Punjabi press

- Ishwar Chander Nanda — father of modern Punjabi drama (Dulhan, 1913; Subhadra, 1920); Norah Richards, an Irishwoman at Dyal Singh College Lahore and later Andretta, is called the mother of Punjabi theatre.
- Harcharan Singh and Balwant Gargi (Loha Kut, 1944; Sahitya Akademi 1962 for the theatre history Rangmanch) took drama to the stage; Gursharan Singh ('Bhai Manna Singh') built a people's theatre movement in villages.
- Later dramatists: Ajmer Singh Aulakh (rural plays), Atamjit (Sahitya Akademi 2009), Swarajbir (2016), Charan Dass Sidhu (2003).
- Prose and memoir: Gurbakhsh Singh Preetlari's essays and the Preet Lari monthly (1933); Balraj Sahni's travelogues; Amrita Pritam's Rasidi Ticket; Ajeet Cour's Khana Badosh.
- Press: Khalsa Akhbar (Lahore, 1886, Giani Ditt Singh), Khalsa Samachar (1899, Bhai Vir Singh), Preet Lari (1933), Nagmani (Amrita Pritam).
- Institutions: Punjabi Sahit Akademi, Ludhiana (1954); Punjabi University, Patiala (1962); Bhasha Vibhag (Languages Department) awards the state's Shiromani Sahitkar honours.

### Later movements — Partition, protest, Dalit and diaspora writing

- Partition writing: Amrita Pritam's elegy to Waris Shah (1947) and novel Pinjar (1950); Kartar Singh Duggal's Partition stories; Bhisham Sahni's Tamas (Hindi) is the cross-language reference.
- Progressive poets (1930s–50s): Mohan Singh, Amrita Pritam, Bawa Balwant — social realism and Marxist influence.
- Naxalite/jujharu poetry (1970s): Pash (Loh Katha, 1970; killed 1988), Lal Singh Dil, Sant Ram Udasi.
- Dalit literature: Lal Singh Dil, Gurdas Ram Alam, Balbir Madhopuri's autobiography Changiya Rukh.
- Diaspora writing: Punjabi poets and storytellers of Canada and the UK (e.g., Sadhu Binning, Ajmer Rode, Amarjit Chandan) form a recognised stream.
- Pakistani Punjabi (Shahmukhi) literature continues the Sufi and qissa line; Waris Shah, Bulleh Shah and Baba Farid are shared heritage.

### Traps and quick checks

- Mohan Singh's Sahitya Akademi was for Wadda Vela (1959), not Sawe Pattar.
- Nanak Singh's award is listed by Sahitya Akademi as 1961 (some guides print 1962).
- Gurdial Singh's Sahitya Akademi was for Adh Chanani Raat (1975); the Jnanpith (1999) honoured his whole work.
- Batalvi's Loona is a verse-play (poetic drama), not a novel.
- Sundari (1898) is the first Punjabi novel; Damodar's Heer is the earliest Heer, Waris Shah's (1766) the classic one.
- Shah Muhammad wrote the Jangnama; Shah Hussain wrote kafis — do not mix the two Shahs.
