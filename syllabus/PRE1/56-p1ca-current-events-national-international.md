## Short notes

- Weight: about 10–12 of 100 GS questions in PPSC Prelims 2025 were current affairs; many 'static' questions are also triggered by news.
- Window: PPSC usually asks events from roughly the 12–18 months before the exam; revise older items only where they connect to static topics.
- Punjab first: state budget and Economic Survey, new schemes, Acts passed by the Vidhan Sabha, river-water and canal disputes, agriculture and procurement, awards and sports.
- National polity: new Acts, constitutional amendments and bills, major Supreme Court judgments and Presidential References, Finance Commission and Election Commission decisions.
- Economy: RBI policy rates, GDP releases (new 2022-23 base series from Feb 2026), inflation (new CPI base 2024), Union Budget numbers, new schemes and indices.
- Reports and indices: note the publisher, India's rank and Punjab's rank (e.g. Global Hunger Index, HDR, NITI indices, PARAKH survey).
- Environment: new Ramsar sites (India 103 by Oct 2026), climate summits, pollution and stubble-burning data, protected areas.
- Science and tech: ISRO launches, defence tests, genome-edited crops (India's first two rice varieties released May 2025), health programmes.
- International: summits India hosts or chairs (BRICS 2026 in New Delhi), trade deals (India–EU FTA), UN events, neighbourhood relations.
- Recent landmarks to know: Indus Waters Treaty placed in abeyance by India (April 2025); Kartarpur Corridor suspended (May 2025); 16th Finance Commission (chair Arvind Panagariya) covers 2026–31 with states' share kept at 41%.
- Supreme Court opinion on the Presidential Reference (Nov 2025): courts cannot fix timelines for Governors on Bills and there is no 'deemed assent'.
- Method: one source daily (The Tribune for Punjab + one national paper or PIB), a 5-minute note in the app, and a monthly compilation revision.
- Make one line per event: what, who, where, number, and the static topic it links to — this app links each item to its syllabus topic for you.
- Turn every news item into a likely MCQ: a 'which of the following is correct' statement or a match-the-pair; practise those weekly.
- For Mains, keep a 'Punjab data' page: budget size, GSDP growth, groundwater extraction, procurement, flood impact — examiners reward current figures with their year.
- Avoid trivia overload: skip celebrity, minor appointments and one-day controversies unless they connect to the syllabus.

## Punjab angle

- Track Punjab Vidhan Sabha sessions — every new Act (e.g. Punjab Protection of Trees Act 2026) is a likely question.
- Note Punjab's rank whenever a national index is released; PPSC often asks the state's position.
- Water (SYL, BBMB, Bhakra/Pong storage), stubble burning and paddy procurement return every year — keep running data.

## How PPSC asks it

PPSC asks current affairs as direct facts (who/where/which rank) and as statement-based questions linking the news to static topics; Punjab-specific news is over-represented.

## Deep dive

### The three-layer system (daily → monthly → yearly)

- Daily (60–75 min): one Punjab paper (The Tribune or Punjabi Tribune) + one national paper (The Hindu or Indian Express); read headlines, then only items that fit a syllabus node; skip crime, celebrity and party-political noise.
- Daily output: 5–10 one-line notes, each tagged with a syllabus node id (e.g. g3e8 for Punjab economy, g2g5 for RTI and probity), never paragraph copies of articles.
- Weekly (2 hours, Sunday): merge the week's notes into topic pages; check numbers against the primary source (PIB release, ministry site, PRS, RBI).
- Monthly (half a day): read one monthly compilation or Yojana/Kurukshetra issue; attempt 50 MCQs on that month; update the Punjab tracker sheet.
- Yearly anchors: Union Budget and Economic Survey (Jan–Feb), Punjab Budget and Economic Survey (March), RBI policy cycles, Census/NFHS/PLFS releases, major index releases.
- Rule of three: an event deserves a note if it is (1) new law/scheme/institution, (2) a number or rank that may be asked, or (3) linked to a static topic you already study.
- Time window: start serious CA notes about 18 months before Prelims; keep older material only where it explains a continuing issue (e.g. water disputes).

### Source map by category

- Punjab: The Tribune and Punjabi Tribune (state news), Punjab Vidhan Sabha proceedings, Punjab Budget speech, Economic Survey of Punjab, PRS state budget analysis, Department of Information & Public Relations releases.
- National polity and law: PIB, PRS bill summaries and Monthly Policy Review, Supreme Court judgments as reported in The Hindu/Indian Express, Gazette notifications for dates of commencement.
- Economy: Economic Survey, Union Budget at a glance, RBI Monetary Policy statements and Annual Report, MoSPI releases (GDP, CPI, PLFS), Finance Commission report.
- International relations: MEA bilateral briefs and joint statements; UN and summit communiqués; read one explainer per major summit India hosts or attends.
- Environment and ecology: Down To Earth, MoEFCC/PIB releases, Ramsar and UNESCO lists, IPCC and UNEP report summaries.
- Science and technology: ISRO, DRDO, ICMR and DST releases on PIB; 'Science' pages of a national daily.
- Reports and indices: always open the publisher's own summary to note publisher, India's rank/score and Punjab's position.
- Avoid: forwarded social-media 'facts', anonymous PDFs and YouTube thumbnails as primary sources; verify every number before it enters notes.

### Note template (one card per item)

- Title — what it is (Act/Bill/Scheme/Index/Report/Judgment/Event).
- Who — ministry, body or court; for indices, the publishing organisation.
- When — date announced vs date passed vs date notified/in force (these differ and are a favourite trap).
- What — 3–5 key features with numbers (amount, target, coverage, age limits, ranks).
- Static link — the syllabus node and article/Act it connects to (e.g. Article 200 for Governor's assent; Schedule VII entry for a new law).
- Punjab angle — Punjab's rank, share, district, or a parallel state scheme.
- Likely question — write one MCQ statement (true/false) and one 10-mark Mains question on it.
- Source — the primary link you checked.

### Punjab tracker — standing numbers to keep updated (verify each year)

- Punjab Budget 2026-27 (presented 8 March 2026): total expenditure Rs 2,60,437 crore; fiscal deficit target 4.1% of GSDP; revenue deficit 2.2% of GSDP (PRS analysis).
- Punjab GSDP 2026-27 projected at Rs 9,80,635 crore (current prices); real growth estimated 6.1% in 2025-26 with agriculture at –2.5% after the 2025 floods.
- Outstanding liabilities about 45% of GSDP (2026-27 BE); committed spending (salaries, pensions, interest) about 72% of revenue receipts; power subsidy Rs 15,550 crore.
- NITI Aayog Fiscal Health Index 2025 (January 2025): Punjab ranked last among the 18 major states; Odisha first.
- 16th Finance Commission (2026–31): states' share of the divisible pool kept at 41%; it recommends a 3% of GSDP fiscal deficit limit for states.
- Transparency International CPI 2025 (released February 2026): India 91st of 182 countries.
- Recurring Punjab files: SYL canal and Ravi–Beas waters, BBMB, stubble burning counts, paddy and wheat procurement, groundwater, drugs ('Yudh Nashian Virudh'), emigration and deportations, border security, floods and embankments.
- Every new Punjab Act (from the Vidhan Sabha) and every new state scheme with an amount and beneficiary group goes on this sheet.

### How PPSC frames current-affairs questions

- Statement-based MCQs ('Which of the statements given above is/are correct?') — test detail: who chairs, which year, which ministry, which law.
- Match the following — report/index with publisher, scheme with ministry, place in news with state or country.
- Firsts and records — first state/district to do something, first Indian to win an award; note the exact category.
- Punjab-specific — new Acts, budget numbers, schemes, awards to Punjab personalities, sports results, heritage sites.
- Static-through-news — a news event used to ask the underlying static fact (e.g. a Governor-Bill dispute leading to a question on Article 200).
- Mains GS — current issues appear as 'discuss/critically examine' questions on Punjab's economy, water, agriculture, governance and security.
- Elimination: extreme words (only, all, never) are usually wrong; ranks and percentages are tested with near-miss options, so learn the exact figure or leave it.

### Revision cycle and the final 100 days

- Spaced repetition: revise each month's notes after 7 days, 30 days and 90 days.
- Consolidate by theme, not by month, in the last 4 months: polity, economy, Punjab, IR, environment, S&T, awards/sports.
- Last 100 days: one yearly compilation + your own Punjab tracker; solve two full current-affairs mocks a week.
- Make a one-page 'numbers sheet' (budget, growth, ranks, schemes) and read it daily in the last fortnight.
- Stop adding new sources in the last month; only update your existing notes.
- For Mains, write one 150-word answer a week on a current Punjab issue, using numbers from your tracker sheet.

### Traps to avoid

- Announced ≠ approved ≠ passed ≠ notified ≠ in force; a Bill passed by the Vidhan Sabha still needs the Governor's assent.
- Calendar year vs financial year (April–March) vs crop year (July–June) vs academic year.
- Rank vs score in indices; the number of countries/states covered changes between editions.
- Base year changes (GDP, CPI series) make old and new numbers non-comparable.
- Union scheme vs Punjab scheme with a similar name; centrally sponsored vs central sector schemes.
- Reports by NITI Aayog vs RBI vs MoSPI vs private bodies — the publisher itself is often the question.
- Do not memorise opinion columns; use them only for arguments in Mains and essay.

## Practice MCQs (12)

**1.** The 16th Finance Commission, chaired by Arvind Panagariya, covers which award period?

(a) 2026–31  
(b) 2027–32  
(c) 2025–30  
(d) 2021–26

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 2026–31** — The 16th FC award covers 2026–27 to 2030–31; the 15th FC covered 2021–26.

</details>

**2.** In its November 2025 opinion on the Presidential Reference, the Supreme Court held that:

(a) Governors may withhold assent permanently without returning the Bill  
(b) Courts cannot fix Bill timelines, and 'deemed assent' is invalid  
(c) A Bill is deemed assented if the Governor does not act in one month  
(d) Governors must decide on Bills within three months

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Courts cannot fix Bill timelines, and 'deemed assent' is invalid** — The Court ruled out judicially fixed timelines and the idea of deemed assent.

</details>

**3.** India placed the Indus Waters Treaty with Pakistan in abeyance in:

(a) April 2025  
(b) October 2026  
(c) August 2019  
(d) January 2023

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) April 2025** — India put the 1960 treaty in abeyance in April 2025 after the Pahalgam attack.

</details>

**4.** India's first genome-edited rice varieties, released by ICAR in May 2025, are:

(a) Swarna Sub-1 and Improved Samba Mahsuri  
(b) DRR Dhan 100 (Kamala) and Pusa DST Rice 1  
(c) Pusa Basmati 1121 and Pusa Basmati 1509  
(d) Golden Rice and a Bt basmati variety

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) DRR Dhan 100 (Kamala) and Pusa DST Rice 1** — ICAR released DRR Dhan 100 (Kamala) and Pusa DST Rice 1 in May 2025.

</details>

**5.** Which wetland in Chandigarh became a Ramsar site in October 2026, taking India's total to 103?

(a) Sukhna Lake  
(b) Ropar Wetland  
(c) Kanjli Wetland  
(d) Harike Wetland

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Sukhna Lake** — Sukhna Lake (with Sauj Jheel, UP) was added in October 2026; Harike, Kanjli and Ropar were already Ramsar sites in Punjab.

</details>

**6.** 'Maratha Military Landscapes', inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2025, relates to:

(a) Forts in Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu  
(b) Chola-era temples of Tamil Nadu  
(c) Mughal gardens of the Kashmir valley  
(d) Rock-cut caves of Ajanta and Ellora

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Forts in Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu** — Twelve Maratha-era forts were inscribed as one serial property in July 2025.

</details>

**7.** The Nobel Peace Prize 2026 was awarded to:

(a) Michelle Bachelet  
(b) Mary Robinson  
(c) Navi Pillay  
(d) Louise Arbour

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Navi Pillay** — The Norwegian Nobel Committee honoured South African jurist Navi Pillay, of Indian (Tamil) descent, for promoting peace and international law; she presided over the Rwanda tribunal and was UN High Commissioner for Human Rights (2008–14). **8.** Consider the following statements about the 2026 Asian Games:

**9.** Consider the following statements about the Global Hunger Index 2026:  
1. The index is published by the World Food Programme.  
2. India was ranked 100th of 122 countries, in the 'serious' category.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** — The GHI is produced by European NGOs and research partners led by Welthungerhilfe, not by the WFP. In the 2026 edition India ranked 100th of 122 with a score of 24.1 ('serious'), and its child-wasting rate was among the highest in the world. (GHI 2026, Oct 2026 — as of Nov 2026)

</details>

**10.** Kerala was officially renamed 'Keralam' in August 2026 through a law passed by Parliament under which Article of the Constitution?

(a) Article 368  
(b) Article 4  
(c) Article 1  
(d) Article 3

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Article 3** — Article 3 lets Parliament form new states and alter the area, boundaries or name of a state, after referring the Bill to the state legislature for its views. Article 4 only says such laws are not constitutional amendments under Article 368. The Kerala (Alteration of Name) Act, 2026 was notified in late August 2026. (Onmanorama/The News Minute, Aug 2026 — as of Nov 2026)

</details>
