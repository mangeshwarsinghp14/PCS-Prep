## Short notes

- Census 2011 population: 2,77,43,338 (about 2.29% of India); males 1.46 crore, females 1.31 crore.
- Sex ratio 895 (India 943); child sex ratio (0–6) 846 (India 919), among the lowest in India.
- Density 551 per sq km (India 382); decadal growth 2001–11 was 13.89% (India 17.7%).
- Literacy 75.84% (male 80.44%, female 70.73%), close to the national 74.04%.
- Urban population 37.5%, rural 62.5%; Ludhiana is the largest city and most populous district.
- Scheduled Castes are 31.94% of the population, the highest share of any state; Shaheed Bhagat Singh Nagar district has the highest SC share (about 42.5%).
- Religion (2011): Sikh 57.7%, Hindu 38.5%, Muslim 1.9% (concentrated in Malerkotla), Christian 1.3%.
- District extremes (2011): Hoshiarpur has the highest literacy (84.6%) and highest sex ratio (961); Mansa the lowest literacy (61.8%); Bathinda the lowest sex ratio (868); Barnala was the least populous.
- Reserved seats: 34 of 117 assembly seats are SC-reserved; 4 of 13 Lok Sabha seats are SC-reserved (Jalandhar, Hoshiarpur, Faridkot, Fatehgarh Sahib).
- Punjabi in Gurmukhi script is the official language under the Punjab Official Language Act 1967.
- NFHS-5 (2019-21): total fertility rate 1.6 (below replacement level 2.1); sex ratio of total population 938; sex ratio at birth 904.
- NFHS-5: infant mortality about 28 per 1,000; institutional births 94.3%; stunting of under-5 children 24.5%; anaemia among women aged 15–49 is 58.7%.
- NITI Aayog National MPI 2023: 4.75% of Punjab's population multidimensionally poor (NFHS-5 basis), among the five lowest states.
- SDG India Index 2023-24 (NITI): Punjab scored 76, in the 'Front Runner' category (India 71; Kerala and Uttarakhand top with 79).
- Per capita income (Punjab Economic Survey 2025-26): ₹2,30,523; Punjab has slipped from the top ranks it held in the 1970s–90s.
- Migration: heavy emigration abroad (Doaba, and now Malwa students on study visas) alongside in-migration of farm and industrial labour from UP and Bihar.
- Demographic issues: ageing population, low fertility, skewed child sex ratio (improving after PC-PNDT enforcement), drug abuse among youth, and rural–urban gaps.
- Human development indicators to know: HDI covers health, education and income; Punjab does well on income and institutional delivery, but anaemia and the sex ratio lag.

## Punjab angle

- Compare Punjab with Kerala and Haryana on sex ratio and TFR; PPSC often asks for district-level Census toppers.
- After 2011, Pathankot, Fazilka and Malerkotla became districts; Census 2011 tables show their figures under the parent districts.

## How PPSC asks it

Mostly direct data questions: Census 2011 sex ratio, child sex ratio, literacy, SC share, district toppers and bottoms, and recent NFHS/NITI index figures. Memorise the numbers with their year.

## Deep dive

### Census 2011 — extra layers beyond the headline numbers

- Rank: 15th most populous state in 2011 (16th after Telangana was carved out of Andhra Pradesh in 2014); about 1.53% of India's area but 2.29% of its people.
- Decadal growth slowed from about 20.1% (1991–2001) to 13.89% (2001–11) — among the slowest of the major states.
- Sex ratio trend: 882 (1991) → 876 (2001) → 895 (2011). Child sex ratio: 798 in 2001 (the lowest in India then) → 846 in 2011.
- Literacy rose from about 69.7% (2001) to 75.84% (2011); the gender gap narrowed but persists.
- Most populous district: Ludhiana (34.88 lakh); least populous in 2011: Barnala (5.96 lakh); Malerkotla, carved out in 2021, has about 4.5 lakh.
- Scheduled Castes 31.94% (highest of any state); Punjab has no notified Scheduled Tribe population.
- Census 2011 shows Pathankot, Fazilka and Malerkotla inside their parent districts (Gurdaspur, Ferozepur, Sangrur) — use parent figures when asked about 2011.

### NFHS-5 (2019-21) — health, nutrition and household indicators

- Total fertility rate 1.6, unchanged from NFHS-4 and well below replacement (2.1); infant mortality about 28 per 1,000 live births.
- Literacy (NFHS definition): 79% of women and 87% of men aged 15–49; 15% of women have never attended school.
- Women's work: about three-quarters of women aged 15–49 were not employed in the previous 12 months; 82% of women have a bank account they use themselves.
- Marriage: median age at first marriage 23.0 years (women 25–29); 9% of women aged 20–24 married before 18 (8% in NFHS-4).
- Nutrition: children under five — stunted 25%, wasted 11%, underweight 17%; anaemia among women 59% (up five points since NFHS-4); 23% of men anaemic.
- Lifestyle disease: 41% of women and 32% of men overweight or obese; hypertension in 19% of women and 27% of men (15–49).
- Households: all have electricity; 78% live in pucca houses; 67% have piped water in dwelling/yard; 77% use clean cooking fuel; 97% have access to a toilet.
- Son preference remains visible in desired family composition and in the sex ratio at birth (904 in NFHS-5).

### Income, poverty and development indices

- Per capita income 2025-26: ₹2,30,523 (current prices, +6.4%) against India's ₹2,19,575 — Punjab is now only about 5% above the national average (PRS, Mar 2026).
- Punjab led the major states in per capita income through the Green Revolution decades until the early 1990s and has since been overtaken by several states.
- SDG India Index 2023-24: score 76 (Front Runner), above the national 71 but behind the top states (79).
- Working-age population share was 72.6% in 2023 (Punjab Economic Survey 2025-26) — a demographic window that is closing as the state ages.
- Services employ about 41% of Punjab's workforce (PLFS 2023-24) against about 30% nationally.

### Migration and diaspora

- International migration: Doaba villages have long sent workers to the UK, Canada and the Gulf; since the 2010s, Malwa youth migrate on student visas — the 'IELTS economy'.
- Policy shocks: Canada's 2024 caps on study permits and the US deportation flights of 2025 (the first US military plane landed at Amritsar on 5 Feb 2025) hit Punjabi migrants.
- Remittances support housing, consumption and land prices, but emigration also drains skilled youth and leaves an ageing rural population.
- In-migration: labour from Uttar Pradesh and Bihar fills paddy transplanting, industry, construction and services jobs.
- Partition (1947) caused the largest population exchange in the region's history; refugees from West Punjab reshaped towns such as Ludhiana, Jalandhar and Amritsar.
- Institutions for the diaspora: Department of NRI Affairs, NRI Sabha Punjab and dedicated NRI police stations.

### Social issues with data hooks

- Drugs: the Punjab Opioid Dependence Survey (2015) estimated about 2.3 lakh opioid-dependent persons; the state's 'Yudh Nashian Virudh' campaign began in March 2025.
- Farm distress: a three-university survey (PAU, Punjabi University, GNDU) counted over 16,000 farmer and farm-labourer suicides in 2000–2015.
- Gender: the child sex ratio improved after strict PC-PNDT enforcement, but female labour force participation remains among the lowest in India.
- Ageing: low fertility and youth emigration are raising the old-age dependency ratio, pressing on pensions and health care.
- Caste: high SC share with low land ownership — the one-third share of panchayat land reserved for SC lease-holders has sparked struggles in Malwa villages.
- Health: high non-communicable disease burden (obesity, hypertension, diabetes) and the cancer concern in the Malwa cotton belt.

### What changes next — Census 2027 and data sources

- Census 2027 will be India's first digital census and will record caste; house-listing ran from April to September 2026 and population enumeration is due in February 2027 (reference date 1 March 2027).
- Its results will update the SC share (relevant to 34 SC-reserved assembly seats and 4 Lok Sabha seats) and feed the next delimitation.
- Annual sources between censuses: Sample Registration System (fertility, mortality), PLFS (jobs), NFHS (health), Economic Survey of Punjab (income), Statistical Abstract of Punjab (district data).
- NFHS-6 (fieldwork 2023-24) results will replace NFHS-5 figures once released — note the survey round in every answer.
- Always state the source and year: Census sex ratio (895, 2011) and NFHS sex ratio (938, 2019-21) measure different things.
- District-level dashboards: the NITI State Nutrition Profile for Punjab (2022) gives district-wise nutrition indicators.

### PPSC traps

- Sex ratio (895) vs child sex ratio (846) vs sex ratio at birth (904, NFHS-5) — three different measures.
- Punjab has the highest SC percentage, but Uttar Pradesh has the largest SC number.
- No ST population in Punjab — options claiming a small ST share are wrong.
- Hoshiarpur tops both literacy and sex ratio among districts (2011); Mansa is lowest in literacy; Bathinda lowest in sex ratio.
- Malerkotla is the only Muslim-majority district; Muslims are 1.93% of the state.
- MPI 2023 (4.75%) uses NFHS-5 data, not Census data.

**16.** Consider the following statements:

</details>
