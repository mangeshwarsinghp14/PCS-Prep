## Short notes

- Power: Punjab State Electricity Board (1959) was split on 16 April 2010 into PSPCL (generation and distribution) and PSTCL (transmission).
- PSPCL runs Ropar and Lehra Mohabbat thermal plants and hydel projects; it bought the GVK Goindwal Sahib plant (2024); its captive coal mine is Pachhwara Central (Jharkhand).
- PEDA (Punjab Energy Development Agency, 1991) is the nodal agency for renewable energy (solar, biomass, compressed biogas from paddy straw).
- Industrial PSUs: Punjab Financial Corporation (1953), Punjab Small Industries and Export Corporation (PSIEC), Punjab State Industrial Development Corporation (PSIDC, 1966), Punjab Agro Industries Corporation.
- Punjab Tractors Ltd (Swaraj brand), promoted by PSIDC in 1970, was a PSU success story; it was later sold to Mahindra & Mahindra (2007).
- Food and procurement: PUNGRAIN (Food & Civil Supplies Department), PUNSUP, Punjab State Warehousing Corporation, MARKFED and the FCI procure wheat and paddy for the central pool.
- Punjab Mandi Board (under the Punjab Agricultural Produce Markets Act 1961) regulates mandis, collects market fee and the Rural Development Fund, and builds link roads.
- Transport: PEPSU Road Transport Corporation (PRTC, Patiala), Punjab Roadways and PUNBUS; free bus travel for women in government buses (from 2021).
- Urban and infrastructure: PUDA (1995), GMADA (Mohali region), GLADA (Ludhiana), Punjab Infrastructure Development Board (PIDB), Punjab Water Supply & Sewerage Board.
- Cooperative law: Punjab Cooperative Societies Act 1961; Registrar of Cooperative Societies supervises; Primary Agricultural Cooperative Societies (PACS) give short-term crop loans and inputs.
- Credit cooperatives: Punjab State Cooperative Bank (apex), District Central Cooperative Banks and PACS (three-tier short-term structure); Punjab State Cooperative Agricultural Development Bank for long-term credit.
- MARKFED (Punjab State Cooperative Supply and Marketing Federation, 1954): fertilisers, procurement, food processing; consumer brand 'Sohna'.
- MILKFED (Punjab State Cooperative Milk Producers' Federation, 1973): three-tier Anand-pattern dairy cooperative (village society → district milk union → federation); brand Verka.
- SUGARFED: federation of cooperative sugar mills (e.g., Batala, Morinda, Nawanshahr, Gurdaspur, Bhogpur); many mills face losses and modernisation needs.
- Other cooperatives: HOUSEFED (cooperative housing), Punjab State Cooperative Bank, labour and construction cooperatives; Centre's Ministry of Cooperation (2021) promotes multipurpose PACS.
- Knowledge institutions (state public sector): PAU (1962), Punjabi University Patiala (1962), GNDU Amritsar (1969), IKG Punjab Technical University (1997), GADVASU (2005-06), Baba Farid University of Health Sciences, Faridkot.
- Issues: PSU losses (PSPCL subsidy dues, sugar mills, PRTC), the need for professional management, cooperative bank NPAs, political interference; reforms include restructuring, digitisation and proposals to merge district cooperative banks into the state cooperative bank.

## Punjab angle

- Verka (MILKFED) and MARKFED are cited as successful cooperative models; contrast them with loss-making cooperative sugar mills.

## Deep dive

### The SPSE landscape (CAG and PRS data)

- As of March 2023 Punjab had 49 State Public Sector Enterprises, 16 of them inactive (CAG State Finances Audit Report, Report No. 2 of 2024).
- Investment in the 33 working SPSEs was ₹49,920 crore (equity plus long-term loans) — about 54% in agriculture and allied and 45% in power.
- 18 working SPSEs made losses; PSPCL, PUNGRAIN and Punjab Agro Foodgrains Corporation accounted for most of them; 14 had fully eroded net worth.
- Sector map: power (PSPCL, PSTCL); food and agriculture (PUNGRAIN, Punjab Agro Industries Corporation, Punjab Agro Foodgrains Corporation, Punjab State Warehousing Corporation, Punjab State Container and Warehousing Corporation — CONWARE).
- Industry and finance: PSIDC (1966), PSIEC, Punjab Financial Corporation (1953), Punjab Infotech; infrastructure: PIDB, PUDA and regional development authorities (GMADA, GLADA, PDA, ADA, BDA, JDA).
- Transport: PEPSU Road Transport Corporation (PRTC, Patiala, 1956), Punjab Roadways, and PUNBUS (Punjab State Bus Stand Management Company).
- Government guarantees to SPSE loans stood at ₹23,654 crore (2.7% of GSDP) on 31 March 2026 — a contingent fiscal risk.

### Power-sector institutions and reform

- PSEB (1959) was unbundled on 16 April 2010 into PSPCL (generation and distribution) and PSTCL (transmission).
- Regulator: Punjab State Electricity Regulatory Commission (1999) fixes tariffs; the subsidy for free farm power and domestic units is paid by the state to PSPCL.
- UDAY (2016): Punjab took over about ₹15,600 crore of PSPCL's debt; subsidy arrears remained a recurrent problem.
- Asset moves: captive coal from Pachhwara Central (Jharkhand) restarted in 2022; the 540 MW Goindwal Sahib plant was bought from GVK in Jan 2024.
- PEDA (1991) is the state's renewable-energy nodal agency; PSPCL faces Renewable Purchase Obligation targets.
- Smart-metering and loss reduction are funded under the central RDSS scheme; agricultural feeders are being separated for better supply management.

### Cooperative movement — history and structure

- Origins: Co-operative Credit Societies Act 1904 and the 1912 Act; colonial Punjab built one of India's strongest cooperative credit networks to fight moneylender debt.
- Colonial Registrars of Cooperative Societies in Punjab included H. Calvert and Malcolm Darling, whose books shaped thinking on rural credit.
- Law today: Punjab Cooperative Societies Act 1961, administered by the Registrar of Cooperative Societies under the Department of Cooperation.
- Punjab has pursued a two-tier structure by merging the District Central Cooperative Banks into the State Cooperative Bank — a proposal slowed by regulatory and legal hurdles.
- Apex federations: MARKFED (1954; brand Sohna), MILKFED (1973; brand Verka), SUGARFED (cooperative sugar mills), HOUSEFED (housing), Punjab State Cooperative Bank.
- National links: Ministry of Cooperation (July 2021), computerisation of PACS, model bye-laws for multipurpose PACS, Multi-State Cooperative Societies (Amendment) Act 2023, National Cooperative Policy (July 2025).

### MILKFED, MARKFED and SUGARFED — cases for answers

- MILKFED follows the Anand pattern: village dairy cooperative societies → district milk unions → state federation; it procures, processes and sells under the Verka brand.
- The Verka brand is named after Verka village (Amritsar), site of the first plant; MILKFED took its larger role in 1983, when the plants of the Punjab Dairy Development Corporation were handed to the cooperative sector under Operation Flood; its plants handle about 30 lakh litres a day (MILKFED).
- MARKFED supplies fertiliser and inputs, procures wheat and paddy for the central pool, and runs food-processing units; Sohna is its consumer brand.
- SUGARFED's cooperative mills (e.g., Batala, Bhogpur, Morinda, Nawanshahr, Gurdaspur) suffer from old machinery, low recovery and payment arrears to cane growers.
- Success factors for MILKFED/MARKFED: assured market, professional management, value addition; weaknesses of SUGARFED: political pricing, low capacity use.
- Use Verka (success) and the cooperative sugar mills (stress) as a contrast pair in 10–15 mark answers.

### Other public institutions worth knowing

- Punjab Pollution Control Board (1975) — stubble, industrial effluent and river-pollution regulation.
- Punjab Water Regulation and Development Authority (2020) — groundwater extraction charges for non-farm users.
- Punjab Health Systems Corporation (1996) — runs district and sub-divisional hospitals.
- Punjab State Finance Commission — devolution to panchayats and urban local bodies (Articles 243-I and 243-Y).

### Issues and reform options

- Losses concentrate in power and food procurement: subsidy delays, grain storage losses and interest on cash-credit limits.
- The 16th Finance Commission recommended that states review inactive and loss-making SPSEs — close, merge or professionalise.
- Cooperative banks: crop-loan waivers (2017 waiver up to ₹2 lakh for small and marginal farmers) and repayment culture affect NPAs; PACS need diversification into storage, inputs and services.
- Governance: independent boards, performance contracts (MoUs), timely audited accounts and listing of viable units are standard reform suggestions.
- Positive models: Verka's market share, Punjab Tractors' early success (later privatised, 2007), and PSPCL's coal-block revival.
- Link to fiscal health: SPSE losses and guarantees feed the state's debt — connect this node with public finance in Mains answers.
