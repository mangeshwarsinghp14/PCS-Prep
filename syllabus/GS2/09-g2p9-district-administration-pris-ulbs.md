## Short notes

- Evolution: Mughal faujdar/amalguzar; Company era — Warren Hastings created Collector (1772); Cornwallis (1793) separated revenue and judicial functions; later Collector-Magistrate combined.
- Non-regulation provinces (e.g. Punjab after 1849 annexation) used the Deputy Commissioner combining revenue, magisterial and some judicial powers — origin of the 'DC' title.
- Post-1947: DC/Collector as revenue head, District Magistrate (law and order), development coordinator, election officer (DEO), disaster manager (chair DDMA under DM Act 2005), crisis manager.
- Sub-division: SDM as executive magistrate under BNSS 2023 (e.g. Sec 163, formerly Sec 144 CrPC), revenue officer, registration and land records supervisor, local election ERO/RO.
- Challenges: overload, frequent transfers, political interference, coordination with elected local bodies, police-magistracy relations, commissionerate system shifting magisterial powers to police in cities.
- Committees: 2nd ARC 15th Report (State and District Administration) — DC to be district's coordinator with devolution to PRIs; 2nd ARC 6th Report (Local Governance).
- Panchayati Raj evolution: Balwantrai Mehta (1957) three-tier; Ashok Mehta (1977–78) two-tier; G.V.K. Rao (1985); L.M. Singhvi (1986) constitutional status.
- 73rd Amendment (in force 24 April 1993 — National Panchayati Raj Day): Part IX, Art 243–243O; Gram Sabha; three tiers (intermediate optional for states under 20 lakh population).
- Mandatory: direct elections, five-year term, elections within six months of dissolution, one-third seats for women (many states incl. Punjab provide 50%), SC/ST reservation, SEC, SFC.
- PESA 1996 extends Panchayati Raj to Fifth Schedule areas with stronger Gram Sabha powers.
- 74th Amendment (Part IXA, Art 243P–243ZG): nagar panchayat, municipal council, municipal corporation; ward committees (3 lakh+), District Planning Committee (243ZD), Metropolitan Planning Committee (243ZE).
- Issues: inadequate devolution of 3Fs, proxy representatives ('sarpanch-pati'), weak Gram Sabhas, parastatal dominance in cities, low property-tax collections, irregular elections.
- Initiatives: e-Gram Swaraj, SVAMITVA (drone-based rural property cards), Gram Panchayat Development Plans (People's Plan Campaign), Rashtriya Gram Swaraj Abhiyan, AMRUT, Smart Cities Mission, 15th/16th FC grants to local bodies.
- Good practices: Kerala's people's planning (1996) with large untied funds; ward-level participatory budgeting in Pune.
- Way forward: activity mapping, own revenue, cadre of panchayat officials, social audits, women leaders' training, digital accounting.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab was a non-regulation province after 1849; the DC model and Punjab Land Revenue Act 1887 still shape revenue administration (patwari, kanungo, tehsildar, SDM, DC).
- Punjab has 23 districts (Malerkotla created 2021); police commissionerates operate in major cities like Ludhiana, Amritsar and Jalandhar.
- Punjab Panchayati Raj Act 1994: gram panchayat, panchayat samiti, zila parishad; 50% reservation for women in PRIs and ULBs.
- Gram panchayat elections were held in October 2024 on a non-party basis across over 13,000 panchayats; panchayat shamlat (common) land lease income is a key own source.
- Urban: Punjab Municipal Act 1911 and Punjab Municipal Corporation Act 1976; urbanisation 37.5% (Census 2011); Ludhiana, Jalandhar and Amritsar are Smart Cities.
- 'Bhagwant Mann Sarkar Tuhade Dwar' doorstep delivery (helpline 1076) and Sewa Kendras reduce citizens' dependence on district offices.

## How PPSC asks it

High-priority for an SDM aspirant: role of DC/SDM in changing times, PRIs' effectiveness, women's reservation outcomes, municipal finance. Examiners reward evolution with dates, committee names and Punjab-specific administrative detail.

## Deep dive

### Evolution of district administration (year — milestone)

- 1772 — Warren Hastings created the Collector; 1793 — Cornwallis separated revenue and judicial work (summary).
- 1829 — Bentinck created Commissioners of Revenue and Circuit — origin of the Divisional Commissioner.
- 1861 — Police Act: district Superintendent of Police under the general control of the District Magistrate.
- 18 May 1882 — Lord Ripon's resolution on local self-government (Ripon called the "father of local self-government").
- 1907–09 — Royal Commission on Decentralisation (Hobhouse) recommended village panchayats and sub-district boards.
- 1952–53 — Community Development and the Block Development Officer: the Collector became a development coordinator.
- 1980 — IRDP and District Rural Development Agencies; DRDAs later merged with Zila Parishads in many states (2022).
- 2005 — Disaster Management Act: DM/DC chairs the District Disaster Management Authority (Sec 25).
- 2018 — Aspirational Districts Programme (112 districts); 2023 — Aspirational Blocks Programme (500 blocks).

### District Collector/DC — functions and legal sources

- Revenue: land records, mutation, recovery of land revenue and public dues as arrears of land revenue, land acquisition (RFCTLARR Act 2013).
- Magisterial: executive magistrate under BNSS 2023 (orders like the old Sec 144 CrPC are now under Sec 163 BNSS), arms licences (Arms Act 1959), jail inspection.
- Elections: District Election Officer (Sec 13AA RPA 1950); often Returning Officer for the Lok Sabha seat.
- Disaster: chair of the DDMA; crisis manager in floods, fires and epidemics.
- Development: convergence of schemes (PMAY-G, MGNREGA, Jal Jeevan), district planning, Aspirational District dashboards.
- Regulatory: excise, mines and minerals, registration of documents (often as Registrar), food security grievance redressal.
- Social justice: District Vigilance and Monitoring Committee under the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act.
- Census: Principal Census Officer for the district (Census 2027).

### Punjab — district and revenue administration

- Five revenue divisions (Patiala, Jalandhar, Ferozepur, Rupnagar, Faridkot) and 23 districts — Fazilka and Pathankot created in 2011, Malerkotla in 2021.
- Hierarchy: Patwari (patwar circle) → Kanungo → Naib Tehsildar → Tehsildar → SDM → Deputy Commissioner (Collector) → Divisional Commissioner → Financial Commissioner (Revenue).
- Laws: Punjab Land Revenue Act 1887, Punjab Tenancy Act 1887, Punjab Land Records Manual; records — jamabandi (record of rights), girdawari (crop inspection), intkal (mutation), shajra (village map).
- Lal lakir (village abadi) properties had no title records; SVAMITVA and Punjab's "Mera Ghar Mere Naam" scheme provide property cards.
- E-governance: online jamabandi through the Punjab Land Records Society, e-registration, Sewa Kendras for citizen services, doorstep delivery helpline 1076.
- Police commissionerates operate in major cities (Ludhiana, Amritsar, Jalandhar); districts elsewhere have SSPs.
- Disaster role tested in the 2023 and 2025 floods; in 2025 all 23 districts were declared flood-affected.

### Urban local government — Part IXA map and urban issues

- 243Q — nagar panchayat (transitional area), municipal council (smaller urban area), municipal corporation (larger urban area); industrial townships may be exempted.
- 243R — composition (nominated experts without vote); 243S — wards committees in municipalities with 3 lakh or more population.
- 243T — reservation (including one-third for women); 243U — five-year term; 243W — powers (Twelfth Schedule); 243X — taxes and grants.
- 243Y — Finance Commission review; 243Z — audit; 243ZA — State Election Commission; 243ZD — District Planning Committee; 243ZE — Metropolitan Planning Committee (10 lakh+).
- Urban missions: JNNURM (2005), AMRUT (2015) and AMRUT 2.0 (2021), Smart Cities Mission (2015–March 2025), SBM-Urban 2.0, PMAY-Urban 2.0 (2024), PM e-Bus Sewa (2023).
- Finance: property tax is the main own source; municipal bonds revived by Pune (2017); 15th FC made property-tax floor rates a grant condition.
- 2nd ARC 6th Report: city governments, mayor-in-council, a municipal cadre and an urban local body ombudsman.

### Punjab — urban and rural local bodies

- Laws: Punjab Municipal Act 1911 (councils and nagar panchayats), Punjab Municipal Corporation Act 1976, Punjab Regional and Town Planning and Development Act 1995 (development authorities such as GMADA).
- Dec 2024 polls: five municipal corporations (Amritsar, Jalandhar, Ludhiana, Patiala, Phagwara) and several councils/nagar panchayats.
- Smart Cities: Ludhiana, Jalandhar and Amritsar; Punjab Municipal Infrastructure Development Company manages projects.
- Canal-based 24x7 water supply for Amritsar and Ludhiana is financed by the World Bank and AIIB (Punjab Municipal Services Improvement Project).
- Grievances through the Local Government department's mSeva portal; services through Sewa Kendras.
- Rural: gram panchayat polls (Oct 2024, no party symbols), Zila Parishad/Samiti polls (Dec 2025, on party symbols); the 2026 Bill creates a Panchayat Development Secretary cadre.
- Issues: shamlat land encroachment, factional village politics, cash incentives for "unanimous" panchayats criticised as weakening contests.

### Mains — issues and way forward

- DC overload: regulatory, developmental and crisis roles together; 2nd ARC 15th Report suggested separating regulatory functions and making the district council the development body.
- Political interference and frequent transfers (T.S.R. Subramanian, 2013 — fixed tenure and civil services boards).
- Commissionerate system shifts magisterial powers to police in cities — efficiency vs accountability debate.
- Local bodies need the 3Fs, their own cadre, GIS-based property tax, and social audits.
- Aspirational Districts model (convergence, collaboration, competition) shows data-driven district governance.
- Punjab focus: border districts (drugs, drones), flood resilience, groundwater crisis — all managed at the district level.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** Trace the evolution of district administration in India. Is the Deputy Commissioner still the 'kingpin' of district administration? (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: district as basic unit since Mughal and British times.
- Evolution: Collector 1772, Cornwallis 1793, non-regulation DC (Punjab), post-1947 development role.
- Present roles: revenue, magistracy, development, elections, disasters, welfare delivery.
- Erosion: PRIs/ULBs, commissionerates, specialised agencies, digital delivery.
- Continuing centrality: coordination, crisis (floods 2025, COVID), elections.
- Reforms: 2nd ARC 15th report, tenure stability, delegation to SDMs.
- Conclusion: from 'kingpin' to 'coordinator and facilitator'.

</details>

**2.** "Three decades after the 73rd Amendment, panchayats remain agents of the state rather than institutions of self-government." Critically examine with examples from Punjab. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: vision of Art 40 and 243G.
- Achievements: regular elections, lakhs of women representatives, SC/ST inclusion, GPDPs.
- Shortfalls: 3Fs deficit, bureaucratic control (BDPO), tied grants, proxy representation, weak Gram Sabha.
- Punjab: 1994 Act, 50% women quota, 2024 non-party elections, shamlat land income, factionalism.
- Good practice: Kerala.
- Way forward: activity mapping, own revenue, capacity, social audit.
- Conclusion: devolution must move from law to practice.

</details>

**3.** Discuss the challenges faced by Urban Local Bodies in India and suggest measures to strengthen municipal finances. (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: 74th Amendment; rapid urbanisation.
- Challenges: low own revenue, parastatals, weak DPC/MPC, capacity gaps.
- Measures: property tax reform with GIS, user charges, municipal bonds, SFC compliance, 16th FC grants.
- Punjab: 37.5% urban, Smart City projects in Ludhiana, Jalandhar, Amritsar.
- Conclusion: empowered mayors and accountable councils.

</details>
