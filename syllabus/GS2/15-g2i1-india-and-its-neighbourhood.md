## Short notes

- Frameworks: Neighbourhood First policy, Act East (2014), SAGAR (2015) expanded as MAHASAGAR (2025, announced in Mauritius), Gujral Doctrine (non-reciprocal generosity to smaller neighbours).
- Pakistan: cross-border terrorism core issue; after the Pahalgam attack (22 April 2025) India placed the Indus Waters Treaty (1960) in abeyance, closed the Attari ICP and downgraded ties; Operation Sindoor (7 May 2025) struck terror infrastructure; hostilities ceased 10 May 2025.
- China: boundary dispute along LAC; Galwan clash (June 2020); October 2024 understanding on patrolling in Depsang and Demchok; PM attended SCO summit in Tianjin (2025); trade deficit and supply-chain dependence persist.
- China factor: CPEC through PoK, Belt and Road in neighbourhood, Chinese presence in Sri Lanka (Hambantota), Maldives, Nepal; India counters with connectivity, credit lines and capacity building.
- Bangladesh: Land Boundary Agreement 2015 (100th Amendment) and maritime settlement (2014) were high points; after Sheikh Hasina's ouster (Aug 2024) ties strained under the interim government; Ganga Water Treaty (1996) expires in 2026; Teesta sharing unresolved.
- Nepal: open border under 1950 Treaty; 2015 blockade perception; 2020 map dispute over Kalapani-Lipulekh-Limpiyadhura; hydropower trade growing; political churn after September 2025 youth-led protests.
- Bhutan: closest partner; hydropower cooperation; Gelephu Mindfulness City and cross-border rail links; India's largest development assistance recipient.
- Sri Lanka: India's roughly $4 billion support during 2022 economic crisis; issues — fishermen and Katchatheevu, Tamil reconciliation (13th Amendment), Chinese research vessels; deepening defence and energy ties under President Anura Kumara Dissanayake.
- Maldives: 'India Out' campaign and troop replacement by civilians (2024), followed by a reset with currency swap and PM's visit (2025); Greater Malé Connectivity Project.
- Myanmar: 2021 coup; Kaladan Multi-modal project and Trilateral Highway; refugee flows into Mizoram/Manipur; Operation Brahma relief after March 2025 earthquake; move to fence border and end Free Movement Regime.
- Afghanistan: humanitarian aid (wheat, medicines) via Chabahar; pragmatic engagement with Taliban, including Foreign Minister Muttaqi's 2025 visit and decision to upgrade India's mission in Kabul.
- Regional bodies: SAARC stalled (no summit since 2014); BIMSTEC prioritised (Charter in force 2024); BBIN Motor Vehicles Agreement; Colombo Security Conclave for maritime security.
- Tools: Lines of Credit, grants, capacity building (ITEC), energy grids (cross-border electricity trade), vaccine diplomacy, HADR as first responder.
- Challenges: asymmetry and 'big brother' perception, domestic politics of neighbours, slow project delivery, China's chequebook diplomacy, cross-border migration and insurgency.
- Way forward: faster project execution, people-to-people ties, trade facilitation (ICPs), connectivity, respect for neighbours' sovereignty, sub-regional cooperation.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab shares about 553 km of international border with Pakistan; drone-dropped drugs and arms from across the border are a major security concern.
- Attari–Wagah: the Integrated Check Post at Attari (Amritsar) was closed in April 2025, hurting traders, transporters and Afghan dry-fruit trade linked to Punjab.
- Kartarpur Corridor (opened Nov 2019 for Guru Nanak's 550th Prakash Purab) was suspended amid the May 2025 hostilities — a Punjab-specific people-to-people link.
- Indus system: eastern rivers Ravi, Beas and Sutlej allocated to India under IWT; abeyance raises questions on better use of eastern-river water in Punjab.
- BSF jurisdiction extended to 50 km in Punjab (Oct 2021) for border security; border districts face displacement of farming (fenced land beyond barbed wire).

## How PPSC asks it

Examiners want country-specific analysis with recent developments (IWT abeyance, Bangladesh transition, China thaw) and a balanced way forward. Punjab angles (border, Attari, Kartarpur, drones) earn extra credit in PPSC.

## Deep dive

### Neighbourhood timeline 2025–26 (year — event)

- 23 April 2025 — after the Pahalgam attack, India holds the Indus Waters Treaty 'in abeyance until Pakistan credibly and irrevocably abjures its support for cross-border terrorism' (wording as recorded by the Court of Arbitration).
- 7 May 2025 — Operation Sindoor; the Kartarpur corridor is suspended from the Indian side the same day; it was still closed at its sixth anniversary in November 2025 and the Centre told Parliament in 2026 that the security situation compels it to stay suspended (Tribune, September 2026), even though the bilateral agreement had been renewed in 2024 for five years.
- 8 August 2025 — the Court of Arbitration (PCA, The Hague) gives its Award on General Interpretation of the IWT; India boycotts the proceedings and calls the court 'illegally constituted'.
- September 2025 — Gen-Z protests topple the Oli government in Nepal; Sushila Karki heads an interim government.
- 12 February 2026 — Bangladesh election: the BNP wins a two-thirds majority; Tarique Rahman is sworn in as Prime Minister on 17 February 2026.
- 5 March 2026 — Nepal election: the Rastriya Swatantra Party wins a landslide; Balendra 'Balen' Shah, former Kathmandu mayor, is sworn in on 27 March 2026 as Nepal's 47th and youngest Prime Minister.
- 15 May 2026 — the Court of Arbitration's maximum-pondage award; on 16 May the MEA rejects it as 'null and void'.
- 31 August 2026 — Award on Treaty Status: the Court unanimously holds that the IWT 'remains fully in force' because neither the treaty nor the law of treaties allows India to suspend it unilaterally. A separate order bars India from concreting the Ratle dam wall and power intake above set levels until 90 days after the Neutral Expert's final decision (expected around July 2027). India did not take part.
- 2026 — the Ganga Water Treaty (signed December 1996, 30-year term) is due to expire in December 2026; as of mid-2026 formal negotiations had not begun, and the Lok Sabha Committee on External Affairs urged the government to start talks 'at the earliest'.

### Indus Waters Treaty — legal anatomy (with Punjab links)

- Signed 19 September 1960 at Karachi by Nehru and Ayub Khan, with the World Bank as a signatory for limited purposes.
- Eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej; about 33 MAF) go to India for unrestricted use. Western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab; about 135 MAF) go to Pakistan, but India may use them for domestic, non-consumptive and limited agricultural purposes and for run-of-river hydropower under Annexure D design limits.
- Dispute ladder (Article IX): a 'question' goes to the Permanent Indus Commission; a 'difference' (technical) goes to a Neutral Expert (Annexure F); a 'dispute' goes to a Court of Arbitration (Annexure G). Pakistan's use of the court and India's preference for the Neutral Expert ran in parallel after the World Bank lifted its 2016 'pause' in 2022.
- India issued notices seeking modification of the treaty under Article XII(3) in January 2023 and again in 2024, before declaring abeyance in 2025.
- Contested projects: Kishenganga (330 MW, Jhelum basin, commissioned 2018) and Ratle (850 MW, Chenab). Others in the pipeline: Pakal Dul (1,000 MW), Kiru, Kwar, and a revived Tulbul navigation project at Wular Lake.
- Punjab link: the Shahpurkandi dam on the Ravi (completed 2024) lets Punjab and J&K use water that used to flow across the border; there are proposals to make fuller use of eastern-river water (Ujh project, a second Ravi–Beas link).
- Humanitarian practice: during the August 2025 floods India passed flood warnings to Pakistan through diplomatic channels even with the treaty in abeyance — a point worth making in an ethics-of-IR answer.
- Exam angles: Does 'abeyance' exist in international law? (The court said the term has no technical meaning and treated it as a claim of suspension or termination.) Is the treaty a 'standalone' arrangement, or is it tied to terrorism as a fundamental change of circumstances?

### China — beyond the LAC

- Agreements on the boundary: 1993 Agreement on maintaining peace and tranquillity along the LAC, 1996 confidence-building measures, 2005 Political Parameters and Guiding Principles, 2013 Border Defence Cooperation Agreement.
- Flashpoints: Depsang (2013), Chumar (2014), Doklam (2017), Galwan (15 June 2020); informal summits at Wuhan (2018) and Mamallapuram (2019).
- Thaw: October 2024 patrolling arrangement for Depsang and Demchok; Modi–Xi meeting at Kazan (October 2024); Special Representatives' talks resumed; Kailash Mansarovar Yatra resumed in 2025; direct flights restarted in October 2025; PM at the SCO summit in Tianjin (2025).
- Water: in July 2025 China began building a mega-dam on the lower Yarlung Tsangpo (Medog), estimated at about 60 GW. India is planning the Siang Upper Multipurpose Project in Arunachal Pradesh as a storage buffer. India and China have no water-sharing treaty, only memoranda on sharing hydrological data.
- Economy: India's trade deficit with China was about US$99 billion in 2024-25. China curbed exports of rare-earth magnets and specialty fertilisers (2025), which exposed supply-chain dependence. India uses Press Note 3 (2020) screening of FDI from land-border countries.
- Neighbourhood competition: CPEC through PoK, Hambantota (99-year lease, 2017), Chinese research vessels in the Indian Ocean, and Chinese financing of a Teesta river project offered to Bangladesh.
- Books for depth: Nirupama Rao, 'The Fractured Himalaya' (2021), on the history of the boundary; Shivshankar Menon, 'Choices' (2016), chapter on the 2005 border agreement.

### Country-by-country deep points (2025–26)

- Bangladesh — Sheikh Hasina has lived in India since August 2024; Bangladesh's International Crimes Tribunal sentenced her to death in absentia (17 November 2025) and Dhaka sought her extradition. India withdrew the transshipment facility for Bangladeshi exports (April 2025) and restricted some land-port imports (May 2025). The new BNP government links better ties to renewing the Ganga treaty; the Teesta remains unresolved.
- Nepal — the open border and Gorkha recruitment (Agnipath stalled recruitment of Nepali Gorkhas); objection to India–China trade via Lipulekh (August 2025); hydropower exports to India and Bangladesh through Indian grids. Balen Shah's government (2026) is an opening for a reset.
- Bhutan — hydropower is the backbone (Chukha, Tala, Mangdechhu; Punatsangchhu-II 1,020 MW inaugurated by PM Modi and the King in November 2025). Planned rail links Kokrajhar–Gelephu and Banarhat–Samtse; India supports the Gelephu Mindfulness City.
- Sri Lanka — first India–Sri Lanka defence cooperation MoU (April 2025), Sampur solar project, Trincomalee energy hub; continuing issues: fishermen and Katchatheevu (ceded under the 1974 agreement, with fishing rights clarified in 1976), and the 13th Amendment for Tamils.
- Maldives — reset after 'India Out': PM Modi was chief guest at the 60th Independence Day (July 2025), with a ₹4,850 crore line of credit and FTA talks; India also supports the Greater Malé Connectivity Project and the Hanimaadhoo airport.
- Myanmar — the junta held phased elections (December 2025–January 2026); India keeps the Kaladan (Sittwe port operational since 2023) and Trilateral Highway projects alive while handling refugees in Mizoram and Manipur.
- Afghanistan — Foreign Minister Muttaqi visited New Delhi in October 2025, and India upgraded its technical mission in Kabul to an embassy; aid had flowed via Chabahar, but the US revoked its Chabahar sanctions waiver from 29 September 2025; a temporary extension for India lapsed on 26 April 2026.
- Pakistan — after Operation Sindoor, India's stated doctrine: a major terror attack will be treated as an act of war; no distinction between terrorists and their state sponsors; no tolerance of nuclear blackmail. Trade, visas and the Attari ICP remain shut.

### Doctrines and personalities (name — role — why it matters)

- Jawaharlal Nehru — Panchsheel (1954) and non-alignment — basis of the early China policy.
- Indira Gandhi — 'Indira doctrine' treating South Asia as India's security sphere; 1971 Bangladesh war; Simla Agreement (1972) with Bhutto.
- I.K. Gujral — Gujral Doctrine (1996): non-reciprocity towards smaller neighbours, non-interference, respect for territorial integrity, peaceful settlement — produced the 1996 Ganga treaty.
- Atal Bihari Vajpayee — Lahore bus yatra (1999), then Kargil; the Agra summit (2001).
- Manmohan Singh — vision of a connected neighbourhood ('breakfast in Amritsar, lunch in Lahore, dinner in Kabul'), and the 2011 Bangladesh land-boundary protocol.
- Narendra Modi — Neighbourhood First (2014 swearing-in with SAARC leaders), SAGAR (2015) and MAHASAGAR (2025), the 2015 Land Boundary Agreement, Operation Sindoor (2025).
- S. Jaishankar — 'The India Way' (2020): strategic autonomy recast as multi-alignment; 'Why Bharat Matters' (2024).
- Shivshankar Menon and Nirupama Rao — former Foreign Secretaries whose books explain the reasoning behind the China and Pakistan policies.

### Punjab and the neighbourhood — exam-ready points

- Attari ICP (opened 2012): Pakistan trade collapsed after India imposed 200% duty following Pulwama (2019); the crossing was closed entirely in April 2025. Afghan dry-fruit trade and Amritsar's transporters and porters were hit hardest.
- Kartarpur Corridor: agreement signed 24 October 2019, opened November 2019 for Guru Nanak's 550th Prakash Purab; Pakistan charges a US$20 service fee; renewed in October 2024 for five years; suspended on 7 May 2025. It was still suspended in September 2026 (MHA: security concerns).
- Pilgrim jathas to Nankana Sahib and other shrines continue under the 1974 Protocol on Visits to Religious Shrines, through Attari with visas, when the two governments allow it.
- Border security: the BSF's jurisdiction in Punjab was extended to 50 km in 2021; narco-drones; farmers' land lies beyond the fence. All of these tie security to agrarian livelihoods.
- Water: Punjab's stake is in the eastern rivers and the Bhakra–Beas system; interstate disputes (SYL canal, BBMB control) shape how India can use the water it has rights to under the IWT.
- Diaspora and soft power: West Punjab–East Punjab cultural links (shared language, Sufi shrines, Sikh heritage sites in Pakistan) can support people-to-people diplomacy when ties allow.

### Common confusions and traps

- Eastern rivers = Ravi, Beas, Sutlej (India); western = Indus, Jhelum, Chenab (Pakistan). The Neutral Expert handles technical 'differences'; the Court of Arbitration handles 'disputes'.
- The Ganga treaty is about Farakka flows in the lean season (1 January–31 May); the Teesta has no treaty (a 2011 draft was stalled by West Bengal).
- Kalapani–Lipulekh–Limpiyadhura is the Nepal dispute (Nepal's 2020 map amendment); Susta is a smaller Nepal dispute; Katchatheevu is the Sri Lanka issue.
- The BIMSTEC Charter entered into force in 2024; SAARC has held no summit since Kathmandu (2014), and the 2016 Islamabad summit was cancelled.
- SAGAR became MAHASAGAR (Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions), announced in Mauritius in March 2025.
- The Gujral Doctrine dates from 1996–97, not the 1980s; the Simla Agreement is 1972, the Lahore Declaration 1999.

**3.** Assess the significance of the Kartarpur Corridor and the Attari–Wagah border for Punjab and India–Pakistan relations. (150 words)

- Kartarpur: visa-free pilgrimage since 2019; soft-power symbol.
- Attari: ICP for trade, Afghan imports, livelihoods in Amritsar.
