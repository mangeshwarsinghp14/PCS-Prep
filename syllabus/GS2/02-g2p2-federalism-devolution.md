## Short notes

- Distribution of powers: Seventh Schedule — Union List, State List, Concurrent List (42nd Amendment moved education, forests, weights and measures, wildlife, administration of justice to Concurrent).
- Residuary powers with Union (Art 248); Parliament can legislate on State List under Art 249 (Rajya Sabha resolution), 250 (Emergency), 252 (consent of two or more states), 253 (treaties).
- Administrative relations: Art 256–257 (states to comply with Union laws/directions), Art 365 (failure to comply), All India Services (Art 312), Governors appointed by President.
- Financial relations: Art 268–293; Art 270 sharing of central taxes; Art 275 grants-in-aid; Art 282 discretionary grants; Art 293 state borrowing needs Centre's consent if indebted to it.
- Finance Commission (Art 280): 15th FC (N.K. Singh) set states' share of divisible pool at 41% for 2021–26.
- 16th FC (chair Arvind Panagariya, award 2026–31, report tabled with Budget 2026-27) kept vertical share at 41%; weights: income distance 42.5%, population (2011) 17.5%, demographic performance 10%, area 10%, forest and ecology 10%, new 'contribution to GDP' 10%.
- 16th FC discontinued revenue deficit grants and state-specific grants, a concern for fiscally stressed states such as Punjab.
- Cesses and surcharges are outside the divisible pool; their rising share is a major federal grievance.
- GST (101st Amendment 2016): Art 246A concurrent taxing power; GST Council Art 279A; Mohit Minerals (2022) held Council recommendations persuasive, not binding; compensation to states ended June 2022.
- Cooperative federalism institutions: Inter-State Council (Art 263; set up 1990 on Sarkaria advice), Zonal Councils (States Reorganisation Act 1956), NITI Aayog (2015) Governing Council.
- Commissions: Sarkaria (1983–88) on Centre-state relations and Governor's role; Punchhi (2007–10) recommended fixed Governor tenure and localised emergency.
- Art 356 misuse curbed by S.R. Bommai (1994): floor test, judicial review of proclamation, Assembly kept in suspended animation until Parliament approves.
- Governor and assent: State of Punjab v Principal Secretary to Governor (2023) — Governor cannot sit on Bills indefinitely; withholding requires return to House. Presidential Reference opinion (20 Nov 2025): courts cannot fix timelines or 'deemed assent', but prolonged unexplained inaction is open to limited review.
- Devolution to local levels: 73rd/74th Amendments — Eleventh Schedule (29 subjects) for panchayats, Twelfth Schedule (18 subjects) for municipalities; Art 243-I/243Y State Finance Commissions every five years.
- Challenges at local level: weak '3Fs' (funds, functions, functionaries), delayed SFCs, low own-source revenue (property tax), parallel bodies (DRDAs, parastatals), state control via officers.
- Contemporary issues: One Nation One Election (129th Amendment Bill, 2024, with JPC), delimitation freeze till after first Census post-2026, language and NEP disputes, agencies (CBI consent withdrawal), centrally sponsored scheme conditionalities.
- Asymmetric federalism: Art 370 (abrogated 2019), Art 371 special provisions, Fifth and Sixth Schedules.

## Punjab angle

- SC in State of Punjab v Principal Secretary to Governor of Punjab (Nov 2023) upheld Assembly session validity and ruled Governors must act on Bills — a Punjab-origin landmark on federalism.
- Feb 2023: SC held the Punjab Governor is bound by Cabinet advice to summon the Budget session.
- Water and territory disputes: SYL canal (SC 2016 opinion against Punjab Termination of Agreements Act 2004), Chandigarh's status, BBMB control (2025 water-release dispute with Haryana).
- BSF jurisdiction extended from 15 to 50 km along Punjab's border (Oct 2021); Punjab Assembly opposed it as encroachment on 'police and public order' (State List).
- Fiscal stress: Punjab's debt-GSDP ratio is among the highest of states (above 40%); end of GST compensation and revenue deficit grants hurts; Budget 2026-27 fiscal deficit ~4.1% of GSDP.
- Punjab Panchayati Raj Act 1994 and municipal laws implement 73rd/74th Amendments; 2020 Assembly resolution and Bills against central farm laws show agriculture-federalism friction.

## Deep dive

### Legislative relations — Articles and doctrines

- Art 245 territorial extent; Art 246 three Lists; Art 246A GST (concurrent); Art 248 residuary; Art 254 repugnancy (state law prevails if it got Presidential assent, unless Parliament later overrides).
- Art 249 (Rajya Sabha two-thirds resolution, one year at a time), Art 250 (during Emergency), Art 252 (two or more states consent — e.g. Wild Life Protection Act 1972, Water Act 1974, Urban Land Ceiling Act 1976, Transplantation of Human Organs Act 1994), Art 253 (treaties).
- Doctrines: pith and substance (Prafulla Kumar Mukherjee, Privy Council 1947), colourable legislation (K.C. Gajapati Narayan Deo, 1953), harmonious construction, territorial nexus, incidental and ancillary powers.
- Art 200/201 — reservation of state Bills; Art 304(b) — state restrictions on trade need prior Presidential sanction.
- State of UP v Lalta Prasad Vaish (Oct 2024, 9 judges, 8:1) — states can regulate "industrial alcohol" as part of "intoxicating liquors".
- Mineral Area Development Authority (July 2024) — states can tax mineral-bearing land; a fiscal gain for mineral states.
- Farm laws 2020 were made under Concurrent entry 33 (trade in foodstuffs) though agriculture is State entry 14 — a classic federal dispute; the laws were repealed in Nov 2021.

### Fiscal federalism — structure, data and disputes

- Vertical imbalance: Centre raises about 60% of revenue but states spend about 60% of general government expenditure.
- Cesses and surcharges (Art 270 exclusion) are outside the divisible pool; their rising share since the 2010s reduces states' effective share below the Finance Commission figure.
- GST: the 56th Council meeting (3 Sept 2025) adopted two main slabs (5% and 18%) plus a 40% rate for luxury/sin goods from 22 Sept 2025; the compensation cess is being wound up after loan repayment.
- GST Appellate Tribunal began functioning in 2025; Mohit Minerals (2022) held Council recommendations persuasive, not binding.
- Borrowing: Art 293(3) requires Central consent for states indebted to it; the Centre counts off-budget borrowings of state entities since 2022; Kerala's Art 131 suit was referred to a Constitution Bench (April 2024).
- Centrally sponsored schemes: the NITI sub-group of CMs (2015) grouped them as core of the core, core and optional; matching shares (60:40, 90:10 for NE and hill states).
- NITI Aayog's Fiscal Health Index (2025) placed Odisha at the top and Punjab at the bottom among major states.
- Asymmetric fiscal federalism: Art 275 grants, Art 282 discretionary grants, special category legacy.

### Administrative, institutional and emergency relations

- Arts 256–257 (compliance and directions), 258 (Centre may entrust functions to states), 258A (states to Centre), 261 (full faith and credit), 262 (water disputes), 263 (Inter-State Council).
- Inter-State Council set up in 1990 on Sarkaria's advice; it has met only rarely (11th meeting in 2016).
- Zonal Councils (States Reorganisation Act 1956) chaired by the Union Home Minister; the Northern Zonal Council covers Punjab, Haryana, Himachal, Rajasthan, Delhi, J&K, Ladakh and Chandigarh.
- Governor: B.P. Singhal (2010) — no arbitrary removal; Nabam Rebia (2016) — discretion limited; Punchhi Commission proposed fixed five-year tenure and an impeachment-like removal process.
- Central agencies: CBI needs state "general consent" (DSPE Act Sec 6); Punjab withdrew general consent in Nov 2020; ED and NIA have their own jurisdiction.
- BSF jurisdiction raised to 50 km in Punjab (Oct 2021) — challenged by Punjab under Art 131.
- S.R. Bommai (1994) remains the main check on Art 356; use has fallen sharply since the 1990s.

### Devolution to local levels — challenges and reforms

- Activity mapping of the 29 and 18 subjects is incomplete in most states; functions are transferred without funds or staff.
- State Finance Commissions are often late or their reports not acted upon; the 15th FC made audited accounts and property-tax floor rates conditions for grants.
- Parastatals (development authorities, water boards) dominate city functions; DRDAs and line departments limit rural bodies.
- 2nd ARC 6th Report: separate local-body cadres, district councils, a Local Body Ombudsman, stronger Gram Sabha audits.
- Good practice: Kerala's plan devolution; Karnataka's high devolution score; social audits in Telangana and Meghalaya (Meghalaya passed a social audit law in 2017).
- Punjab: new Panchayat Development Secretary cadre bill (Aug 2026) aims at stronger panchayat administration; panchayats rely on shamlat land income.

### Punjab — federal flashpoints (issue — year — status)

- Chandigarh: shared capital and UT since 1966; promised to Punjab in 1970 and 1985 but not transferred; central service rules applied to Chandigarh employees (March 2022) led to a Punjab Assembly resolution (1 April 2022) demanding transfer.
- Nov 2025: proposal to bring Chandigarh under Art 240 (President's regulation power) was listed and then dropped after all Punjab parties objected.
- Panjab University (an inter-state body corporate under Sec 72 of the Punjab Reorganisation Act 1966): a Central notification (late Oct 2025) trimming the senate and abolishing the graduates' constituency was withdrawn within days after protests (Nov 2025).
- River waters: SYL canal — Punjab Termination of Agreements Act 2004 held invalid in the Presidential reference opinion (10 Nov 2016); in Aug 2026 the Attorney General told the SC that Punjab and Haryana were moving closer to a settlement.
- BBMB (Sec 79 of the 1966 Act): 2022 rule change dropped the convention of permanent members from Punjab and Haryana; in 2025 the Board's decision to release extra water to Haryana led to a Punjab Assembly special session and a High Court order (6 May 2025) directing compliance; Central forces were approved for Bhakra–Nangal security.
- Rural Development Fund: the Centre withheld large sums over Punjab's use of the cess; Punjab sued under Art 131 (2023).
- Fiscal stress: debt above 40% of GSDP; 2025 floods hit all 23 districts and the Centre announced Rs 1,600 crore relief (Sept 2025).
- Governor disputes (2023): budget session and Bills — settled by the SC; the Nov 2025 reference opinion now governs assent.

### Answer-writing aids

- Concepts: cooperative federalism (Austin), competitive federalism (NITI indices), "holding-together" federation (Alfred Stepan), "bargaining federalism" (Morris-Jones).
- Use indicators: share of states in divisible pool (41%), cess share, GST compensation end, CSS conditionalities, Art 356 counts before/after Bommai.
- Quote: Bommai — states are not mere appendages of the Centre; Rajamannar and Anandpur Sahib for autonomy demands.
- Balance: national security and market integration need a strong Centre; diversity and accountability need strong states and local bodies.
- Way forward: revive Inter-State Council, statutory GST dispute mechanism, cap on cesses (Fifteenth FC concern), timelines for Governors by convention after the 2025 opinion.
- Punjab case studies give every answer a state flavour: SYL, BBMB, Chandigarh, PU, BSF jurisdiction, RDF.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** "Fiscal federalism in India is tilting towards the Centre." Examine with reference to cesses and surcharges, GST and the recommendations of the 16th Finance Commission. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: constitutional scheme of tax sharing (Art 270, 275, 280).
- Centripetal trends: rising cesses/surcharges outside divisible pool; end of GST compensation (2022); Council dominated by Centre's vote weight; tied CSS funds.
- 16th FC: share unchanged at 41%; GDP-contribution criterion rewards richer states; revenue deficit grants dropped — stress for states like Punjab, Kerala.
- Counterpoints: 41% is historically high; GST improved compliance; Mohit Minerals affirmed states' voice; special assistance for capital investment loans.
- Way forward: cap on cesses, GST dispute mechanism, states' own revenue effort, institutionalised Inter-State Council.
- Conclusion: fiscal trust central to cooperative federalism.

</details>

**2.** Discuss the role of the Governor as a link between the Union and the States. In light of recent Supreme Court pronouncements, suggest reforms to prevent friction. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: Art 153–162, 200, 356; Governor as constitutional head and Centre's appointee.
- Friction points: delay on Bills, summoning sessions, university Chancellor role, reports under Art 356.
- Judgments: Bommai 1994; Nabam Rebia 2016; Punjab Governor cases (Feb and Nov 2023); TN Governor case (Apr 2025); Presidential Reference opinion (Nov 2025) — no judicial timelines, no deemed assent.
- Commission views: Sarkaria (eminent outsider, consult CM), Punchhi (fixed term, removal by Assembly resolution).
- Reforms: conventions/timelines via legislation, Inter-State Council guidelines, apolitical appointments.
- Conclusion: Governor as 'friend, philosopher, guide', not an agent.

</details>

**3.** Despite the 73rd and 74th Amendments, local bodies remain financially dependent. Analyse the reasons and suggest measures, with examples from Punjab. (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: Art 243G, 243W, 243H/243X, SFCs.
- Reasons: limited tax assignment, poor property tax collection, irregular SFCs, untied vs tied grants, state control.
- Punjab: dependence on state transfers; panchayat common land lease income as a key own source.
- Measures: GIS-based property tax, municipal bonds, timely SFC awards, activity mapping, capacity building.
- Conclusion: fiscal autonomy is the core of the 3Fs.

</details>
