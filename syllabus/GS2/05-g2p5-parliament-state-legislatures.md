## Short notes

- Parliament (Art 79–122): President, Lok Sabha, Rajya Sabha. Lok Sabha elected seats 543; Rajya Sabha max 250 (238 elected + 12 nominated under Art 80).
- 104th Amendment (2019) ended nomination of Anglo-Indians to Lok Sabha and state assemblies; extended SC/ST reservation to 2030.
- State legislatures (Art 168–212): Legislative Councils exist only in a few states; created/abolished by Parliament on Assembly resolution with special majority (Art 169).
- Sessions: gap between two sessions not more than six months (Art 85/174); quorum one-tenth (Art 100/189); usual three sessions — Budget, Monsoon, Winter.
- Business: Question Hour, Zero Hour (Indian innovation, 1960s), calling attention, adjournment motion, no-confidence motion (Rule 198 LS; needs 50 members), cut motions.
- Bills: ordinary, money (Art 110; Speaker's certificate final subject to review per Rojer Mathew 2019 referral), financial, Constitution amendment; joint sitting Art 108 held three times (1961, 1978, 2002).
- Rajya Sabha has 14 days on Money Bills and only recommendatory power; Art 249 and 312 give it special powers.
- Committees: PAC (chaired by Opposition member by convention), Estimates, COPU, 24 Departmentally Related Standing Committees (since 1993), Joint Parliamentary Committees, Select Committees.
- Privileges: Art 105/194 — freedom of speech in House, immunity for votes; not codified; Sita Soren (2024, 7 judges) overruled P.V. Narasimha Rao (1998): no immunity for bribery for vote/speech.
- Anti-defection: Tenth Schedule (52nd Amendment 1985; 91st 2003 removed 'split'); Kihoto Hollohan (1992) upheld it with judicial review; Keisham Meghachandra (2020) urged Speaker to decide in ~3 months.
- Ordinances (Art 123/213): Krishna Kumar Singh (2017) held re-promulgation without legislature is fraud on Constitution.
- Issues: declining sittings and debate time, Bills passed without committee scrutiny, disruptions, use of Money Bill route, frequent suspension of MPs, vacancy of Deputy Speaker post in 17th Lok Sabha.
- Other issues: weak Opposition role, whip system limiting deliberation, low women's representation (106th Amendment yet to apply), criminalisation.
- Reforms: minimum sitting days, mandatory committee referral, Opposition days, codified privileges, independent Speaker conventions (UK model), PRS-type research support.
- Financial control: budget (Art 112 Annual Financial Statement), Appropriation Bill (Art 114), Finance Bill, vote on account (Art 116), CAG reports to PAC.
- Digital: Digital Sansad and e-Vidhan (NeVA) for paperless legislatures.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab Vidhan Sabha has 117 members (unicameral); the Punjab Legislative Council was abolished in 1970.
- Punjab sends 13 members to Lok Sabha and 7 to Rajya Sabha.
- Special sessions: Oct 2020 Bills against central farm laws; 2025 resolutions on BBMB water releases — examples of state legislature in federal contestation.
- 2023 dispute over adjourned (not prorogued) Budget session reconvened by Speaker; SC (Nov 2023) held such sittings valid.

## Deep dive

### Parliament — current facts and data

- 17th Lok Sabha (2019–24) had about 274 sittings — the lowest for a full-term House — and only a small share of Bills (around 16%) went to committees.
- 18th Lok Sabha (from June 2024): Speaker Om Birla re-elected; Rahul Gandhi became the first recognised Leader of Opposition since 2014; the Deputy Speaker post stayed vacant, as in the whole 17th Lok Sabha.
- Women in the 18th Lok Sabha: 74 (about 13.6%).
- Vice-President Jagdeep Dhankhar resigned on 21 July 2025; C.P. Radhakrishnan was elected and became Rajya Sabha Chairman in Sept 2025.
- New Parliament building inaugurated on 28 May 2023; the special session there (Sept 2023) passed the women's reservation Bill.
- Record suspension of 146 MPs from both Houses in Dec 2023.
- Waqf (Amendment) Bill 2024 was examined by a JPC (chair Jagdambika Pal) and enacted in April 2025 — a recent example of JPC scrutiny.

### Procedure — finer points

- Joint sittings: 1961 (Dowry Prohibition Bill), 1978 (Banking Service Commission Repeal Bill), 2002 (Prevention of Terrorism Bill).
- Only 14 private members' Bills have become law; the last was in 1970; the Rajya Sabha passed Tiruchi Siva's transgender rights Bill (2015) — the first private member's Bill it passed in about 45 years.
- Budget presented on 1 February since 2017, with the Railway Budget merged into it; guillotine applies to undiscussed demands.
- Cut motions: policy cut (reduce to Re 1), economy cut (specific amount), token cut (Rs 100).
- Devices: starred (oral) and unstarred questions, short-notice questions, half-an-hour discussion, short-duration discussion (Rule 193), calling attention (Rule 197), adjournment motion (Rule 56, 50 members), no-confidence motion (Rule 198, 50 members).
- Rajya Sabha Rule 267 (suspension of listed business) is often invoked by the Opposition and rarely admitted.
- Art 117 financial Bills need Presidential recommendation; Money Bill certification by the Speaker (Art 110(3)) under review after Rojer Mathew (2019).

### Committees

- PAC: 22 members (15 Lok Sabha + 7 Rajya Sabha), since 1921; chaired by an Opposition MP since 1967; examines CAG reports.
- Estimates Committee: 30 members, all from the Lok Sabha (1950); Committee on Public Undertakings: 22 members (1964).
- 24 Departmentally Related Standing Committees since 1993 (16 serviced by the Lok Sabha, 8 by the Rajya Sabha), each with 31 members (21 + 10), one-year term.
- JPCs: Bofors (1987), Harshad Mehta securities scam (1992), Ketan Parekh (2001), soft drinks pesticides (2003), 2G (2011), Waqf Bill (2024–25), ONOE Bills (2024–), 130th Amendment Bills (2025–).
- Ethics Committees: Rajya Sabha (1997), Lok Sabha (2000); Mahua Moitra was expelled in Dec 2023 on its recommendation in the cash-for-query matter.
- Committee on Subordinate Legislation scrutinises rules made under delegated powers.

### Privileges, discipline and anti-defection — cases

- Raja Ram Pal (2007) — Houses can expel members, subject to limited judicial review.
- Sita Soren (2024, 7 judges) — no immunity for bribery for a vote or speech (overruled P.V. Narasimha Rao, 1998).
- Ashish Shelar (2022) — one-year suspension of 12 Maharashtra MLAs beyond the session was unconstitutional.
- Kihoto Hollohan (1992), Keisham Meghachandra (2020, decide within about three months), Subhash Desai (2023, Speaker must decide in reasonable time).
- Nabam Rebia (2016) — a Speaker facing a removal notice should not decide disqualification petitions; referred to a 7-judge bench in 2023.
- Privileges remain uncodified (Arts 105, 194); codification is a standing reform demand.

### State legislatures and the Punjab Vidhan Sabha

- Legislative Councils exist in six states: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Bihar and Uttar Pradesh (J&K's ended in 2019).
- Punjab Vidhan Sabha: 117 members; 16th Assembly since March 2022; Speaker Kultar Singh Sandhwan; Leader of Opposition Partap Singh Bajwa (Congress).
- Special sessions: Oct 2020 (state amendment Bills against the central farm laws), 1 April 2022 (resolution on Chandigarh), June 2023 (Sikh Gurdwaras (Amendment) Bill on free telecast of Gurbani; universities chancellor Bill), May 2025 (BBMB water resolution).
- July 2025: the Punjab Prevention of Offences against Holy Scriptures Bill 2025 (life imprisonment for sacrilege) was referred to a 15-member select committee for wider consultation.
- Aug 2026: the Punjab Panchayati Raj (Amendment) Bill 2026 (Panchayat Development Secretary cadre) was published for the Assembly.
- State assemblies sit few days a year on average (PRS data); Punjab's sessions are typically short, with heavy use of special sessions.
- Assembly committees include Public Accounts, Estimates, Public Undertakings, Subordinate Legislation, Privileges and Petitions.

### Issues and reforms for Mains

- Fewer sittings, less committee scrutiny, use of the Money Bill route (Aadhaar 2016), guillotined budgets, disruptions and suspensions.
- NCRWC (2002): minimum 120 days for the Lok Sabha and 100 for the Rajya Sabha; 50 days for larger state assemblies.
- Ideas: Opposition days (UK model), an independent Speaker who leaves the party, mandatory committee referral, research support like PRS, a fixed parliamentary calendar.
- Deputy Speaker vacancy (Art 93 says the House "shall" choose one) — a constitutional convention under strain.
- Women's representation stalled: the 2026 delimitation Bill's defeat delays the 106th Amendment.
- Digital Sansad and NeVA (National e-Vidhan) improve transparency of proceedings.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** "Parliament is increasingly becoming a forum for formalities rather than deliberation." Critically examine the decline in parliamentary functioning and suggest reforms. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: Parliament as deliberative heart of democracy.
- Evidence: fewer sittings, Bills without committee scrutiny, disruptions, guillotine of budget demands, suspensions.
- Causes: adversarial politics, whip, executive dominance, Money Bill route.
- Counterview: high legislative output, active Question Hour in some sessions, digitisation.
- Reforms: 100-day sitting floor, mandatory committee referral, Opposition days, Speaker neutrality.
- Conclusion: quality of law depends on quality of debate.

</details>

**2.** Evaluate the working of the Tenth Schedule. Should the power to decide disqualification be taken away from the Speaker? (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: 52nd and 91st Amendments; aim of stability.
- Achievements: curbed 'Aaya Ram Gaya Ram'.
- Problems: Speaker's partisanship and delays, mass resignations to bypass, merger clause, curbs on dissent.
- Judgments: Kihoto Hollohan, Keisham Meghachandra, Subhash Desai (2023).
- Proposals: Election Commission/President decide (Dinesh Goswami, Law Commission 170th), time limit, limit to confidence/money votes.
- Conclusion: protect both stability and conscience.

</details>

**3.** What are parliamentary privileges? Discuss the need for their codification. (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: Art 105/194; individual and collective privileges.
- Purpose: independent functioning.
- Issues: undefined scope, conflict with free press and Art 19/21, contempt powers.
- Sita Soren 2024: bribery not protected.
- Codification pros/cons; Kerala/assembly examples.
- Conclusion: limited codification with judicial review.

</details>
