## Short notes

- Election Commission (Art 324): CEC and ECs; Chief Election Commissioner and Other ECs (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act 2023 — selection committee of PM, Leader of Opposition, and a Union Minister nominated by PM (replacing interim Anoop Baranwal 2023 committee that included CJI).
- CEC removable only like an SC judge; ECs removable on CEC's recommendation; powers: superintendence of rolls and elections, MCC, party recognition and symbols (Symbols Order 1968).
- UPSC (Art 315–323): members appointed by President, six-year term or 65 years; removal by President after SC inquiry for misbehaviour (Art 317); advisory role, annual report to Parliament.
- State PSC: members appointed by Governor; term six years or 62 years; removable only by President.
- CAG (Art 148–151): appointed by President; six years or 65; removal like SC judge; DPC Act 1971; audits Union, states, PSUs; reports to President/Governor, examined by PAC.
- Finance Commission (Art 280): constituted every five years; Finance Commission (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1951 sets qualifications.
- GST Council (Art 279A): Union FM chair; Centre one-third vote weight, states two-thirds; decisions by three-fourths of weighted votes.
- National Commission for SCs (Art 338), for STs (Art 338A, 89th Amendment 2003), for Backward Classes (Art 338B, 102nd Amendment 2018, constitutional status).
- Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities (Art 350B); Attorney General (Art 76) — right to audience in all courts, can participate in Parliament without vote; Advocate General (Art 165).
- State Election Commission (Art 243K) and State Finance Commission (Art 243-I/243Y) for local bodies.
- Inter-State Council (Art 263) is constitutionally provided but created by Presidential order (1990).
- Issues: executive dominance in appointments (ECI Act 2023 debate), post-retirement appointments, vacancies, delays in NCSC/NCST reports, CAG audit coverage and timeliness, PSC exam leaks.
- Reforms suggested: collegium-like broad-based selection, fixed tenures, cooling-off periods, financial autonomy (charged expenditure), statutory backing for MCC.
- Distinguish constitutional (named in Constitution) vs statutory (Act of Parliament) vs executive (resolution, e.g. NITI Aayog) bodies — a frequent exam trap.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab Public Service Commission (Art 315) conducts PCS; its 2002 'cash-for-jobs' scam under then chairman led to arrests and is a standard probity case study.
- Punjab State Election Commission (Art 243K) conducted gram panchayat elections in October 2024.
- Punjab has the highest proportion of Scheduled Castes among states (31.9%, Census 2011); NCSC and the statutory Punjab State Commission for SCs handle grievances.
- Advocate General of Punjab represents the state in major federal litigation (Governor's assent case, SYL, BBMB).
- CAG audit reports on Punjab finances regularly flag rising debt, off-budget borrowings and power subsidy burden.

## Deep dive

### Appointment, tenure and removal — comparison table

- CEC and ECs (Art 324): appointed by the President on a selection committee's recommendation under the 2023 Act; six years or 65; CEC removable like an SC judge, ECs on the CEC's recommendation.
- CAG (Art 148): appointed by the President; six years or 65; removable like an SC judge; salary charged on the Consolidated Fund; not eligible for further government office.
- UPSC (Arts 315–323): chair and members appointed by the President; six years or 65; removal by the President after an SC inquiry for misbehaviour (Art 317) or for insolvency, outside paid work, infirmity.
- State PSC: appointed by the Governor; six years or 62; removable only by the President. A Joint State PSC is created by an Act of Parliament on request of states — it is statutory, not constitutional.
- Attorney General (Art 76): holds office during the President's pleasure; right of audience in all courts and to speak in Parliament without vote (Art 88). Advocate General (Art 165) is the state counterpart.
- State Election Commissioner (Art 243K): appointed by the Governor, removable like an HC judge.
- NCSC (338), NCST (338A), NCBC (338B): have civil-court powers in inquiries; reports go to the President and are laid in Parliament.

### Recent developments 2023–2026

- CEC and Other ECs Act 2023: selection committee of PM, Leader of Opposition and a Union minister; search committee under the Law Minister; Gyanesh Kumar became CEC in Feb 2025 (the LoP dissented); challenges to the Act are pending.
- CAG K. Sanjay Murthy (from Nov 2024); UPSC Chairman Ajay Kumar (from May 2025).
- GST Council's 56th meeting (Sept 2025) reworked rates; GST Appellate Tribunal began functioning in 2025.
- 16th Finance Commission (chair Arvind Panagariya) report for 2026–31 tabled with Budget 2026–27.
- UPSC tightened identity checks (Aadhaar-based authentication) after the 2024 Puja Khedkar case.
- Lokpal (statutory) chaired by Justice A.M. Khanwilkar since 2024.

### Constitutional vs statutory vs executive bodies

- Constitutional: ECI, UPSC, SPSC, Finance Commission, GST Council, NCSC, NCST, NCBC, CAG, AG, Advocate General, Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities, SEC, SFC.
- Statutory: NHRC (1993), CVC (2003 Act; earlier an executive body from 1964 on the Santhanam Committee's advice), CIC (RTI 2005; 2019 amendment let the Centre fix tenure and salary), Lokpal (2013 Act; first chair P.C. Ghose, 2019), NCW (1990), NCM (1992), NCPCR (2005).
- Executive: NITI Aayog (resolution of 1 Jan 2015); CBI derives powers from the Delhi Special Police Establishment Act 1946 but was set up by a 1963 resolution.
- Exam trap: NCBC was statutory (1993) before becoming constitutional (102nd Amendment, 2018).
- Exam trap: the Special Officer for Linguistic Minorities (Art 350B) was added by the 7th Amendment (1956).

### Case law and issues

- State of UP v Manbodhan Lal Srivastava (1957) — consultation with the UPSC under Art 320(3) is directory, not mandatory.
- Vineet Narain (1997) — CVC given statutory footing and supervision over CBI; T.N. Seshan (1995) — ECI as multi-member body.
- Anoop Baranwal (2023) — interim CJI-inclusive selection panel until Parliament legislated; the 2023 Act replaced the CJI with a Union minister.
- CAG's "presumptive loss" estimates (2G, 2010; coal blocks, 2012) sparked debate on audit limits; CAG audits of PPPs and local bodies remain contested.
- ECI's expenditure is not charged on the Consolidated Fund — making it "charged" is a long-pending reform demand.
- Delays: NCSC/NCST reports and action-taken reports often reach Parliament years late.

### Punjab bodies

- Punjab State Election Commission (Art 243K) — conducted gram panchayat polls (Oct 2024), ULB polls (Dec 2024) and Zila Parishad/Samiti polls (Dec 2025).
- Punjab State Finance Commission (Art 243-I/243Y) recommends devolution to PRIs and ULBs.
- Punjab State Commission for Scheduled Castes (statutory, 2004 Act), Punjab State Human Rights Commission (1997), Punjab State Information Commission (RTI 2005), Punjab Right to Service Commission (2011 Act), Punjab Lokpal (state law).

### Mains frames

- Independence rests on four legs: appointment process, security of tenure, removal procedure, and financial autonomy (charged expenditure).
- Executive-dominated selection (ECI 2023 Act) vs collegium-type broad panels (NJAC debate, Lokpal selection committee).
- Accountability without capture: annual reports to Parliament, PAC scrutiny of CAG reports, parliamentary questions.
- Cooling-off periods: CAG and UPSC members barred from later government posts — extend the bar to ECs and others.
- Quote Ambedkar on the CAG as "the most important officer" under the Constitution.
- Punjab angle: the PPSC scandal (2002) shows why transparent selection, video-recorded interviews and online result disclosure matter for state commissions.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** "The independence of constitutional bodies is the safeguard of constitutional democracy." Discuss with reference to the Election Commission and the CAG. (250 words)

*Like a real PPSC question: [Mains 2021 · GS2 Q7](../../past-papers/mains_2021_GS2.md#q7)*

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: 'fourth branch' institutions.
- ECI: Art 324; removal safeguards; Anoop Baranwal 2023; 2023 Act's committee composition debate.
- CAG: Art 148; Ambedkar's 'most important officer'; role in 2G/coal audits; limited follow-up via PAC.

**2.** Examine the role of Public Service Commissions in ensuring a merit-based civil service. What reforms are needed to restore credibility of state PSCs? (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: Art 315–323; advisory role.
- Functions: recruitment, disciplinary consultation, promotion principles.
- Problems: paper leaks, delays, litigation, appointment of partisan members, PPSC 2002 scam.
- Positive examples: UPSC's credibility, online systems.
- Reforms: transparent appointments, Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act 2024 model, technology, fixed calendars.
- Conclusion: merit system as basis of good governance.

</details>

**3.** Distinguish between the National Commission for Scheduled Castes and the National Commission for Backward Classes in terms of status and functions. (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: Art 338 vs 338B.
- NCSC: investigate safeguards, inquire complaints, civil court powers, reports to President.
- NCBC: 102nd Amendment gave constitutional status; 105th Amendment (2021) restored states' power to identify SEBCs.
- Common: civil court powers, consultation on policy.
- Conclusion: need for timely reports and action-taken memoranda.

</details>
