## Short notes

- Bilateral trade agreements: India–UAE CEPA (2022), India–Australia ECTA (2022), India–Mauritius CECPA (2021), India–EFTA TEPA (signed 2024; includes investment commitment), India–UK CETA (signed July 2025).
- US trade tensions: US imposed 25% reciprocal tariffs plus an additional 25% penalty linked to Russian oil purchases (August 2025), affecting labour-intensive exports.
- RCEP: India withdrew in 2019 over trade deficit with China, dairy and agriculture concerns; joined IPEF (2022) pillars except trade.
- Quad (India, US, Japan, Australia): revived 2017; focuses on maritime security, critical technologies, vaccines, HADR; Leaders' summits since 2021.
- SCO: India full member since 2017; counter-terrorism (RATS) and Central Asia connectivity; India declined to endorse BRI language.
- BRICS: expanded in 2024 (Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE) and Indonesia in 2025; New Development Bank; India chairs BRICS in 2026 — emphasis on reformed multilateralism and Global South.
- G20: India's 2023 presidency — African Union admitted as permanent member, New Delhi Leaders' Declaration, Global Biofuels Alliance, IMEC (India–Middle East–Europe Economic Corridor) announced.
- I2U2 (India, Israel, UAE, US) for food security and clean energy; IMEC for trade connectivity bypassing chokepoints.
- Regional: SAARC (1985; dormant since 2016), BIMSTEC (1997; Charter in force 2024), IORA, Mekong Ganga Cooperation (2000), Colombo Security Conclave, BBIN.
- Global agreements: WTO agreements (TRIPS, AoA — Peace Clause for food stocks), Paris Agreement (India's updated NDC 2022), International Solar Alliance (India–France, 2015), CDRI (2019).
- Strategic partnerships and foundational defence pacts with US: LEMOA (2016), COMCASA (2018), BECA (2020); iCET/TRUST initiative on critical technologies.
- Russia: Special and Privileged Strategic Partnership; S-400 deliveries; discounted crude since 2022; INSTC corridor.
- Groupings affecting India's interests: OPEC+ output decisions, China-led BRI, AUKUS, EU's CBAM and deforestation regulation, US-China tech controls.
- Strategic autonomy: India balances memberships in Quad and SCO/BRICS — 'multi-alignment'.
- Challenges: overlapping memberships, FTA utilisation rates low, non-tariff barriers, standards compliance, protectionist pressures.
- Way forward: complete pending FTAs (EU, others), improve export competitiveness, use BRICS 2026 chair for Global South agenda, strengthen BIMSTEC.

## Punjab angle

- RCEP withdrawal was welcomed by Punjab's dairy farmers, who feared competition from New Zealand and Australian milk products.
- FTAs with UK, EFTA and UAE open opportunities for Ludhiana hosiery/textiles, Jalandhar sports goods, cycle parts and basmati rice exporters.
- US tariff hikes (2025) pose risks for Punjab's export-oriented MSMEs in textiles, auto components and hand tools.
- WTO Peace Clause invoked by India for rice (2020) protects MSP procurement in Punjab's wheat-paddy system.
- International Solar Alliance goals align with Punjab's solar pumps (PM-KUSUM) and rooftop solar push.

## Deep dive

### 2026 scorecard — groupings and agreements (verified)

- BRICS — 18th summit, 12–13 September 2026, Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, under India's chairship and in the 20th year of BRICS. Theme: 'Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability'. The New Delhi Declaration covers reformed multilateralism and a bigger voice for the Global South, resilient supply chains, digital cooperation, and climate and energy transition.
- India–EU FTA — talks concluded on 27 January 2026 at the India–EU summit in New Delhi (called the 'mother of all deals'). Next steps: legal scrubbing, translation, signature, and approval by the European Parliament and Council; in July 2026 both sides agreed to sign before the end of 2026, with roll-out expected in early 2027.
- India–UK CETA — signed July 2025; entered into force on 15 July 2026 together with the Double Contribution Convention (social-security relief for posted workers). About 99% of Indian exports by tariff lines get duty-free access.
- India–US — a framework for an interim trade agreement was announced on 7 February 2026. The US 'reciprocal' tariff on India was cut to 18%, the extra 25% penalty linked to Russian oil was removed, and generic medicines got zero duty.
- India–Canada — PM Mark Carney visited New Delhi on 2 March 2026: a 10-year uranium supply deal (about US$2.6 billion), cooperation on critical minerals, and a target to finish a comprehensive economic partnership agreement (CEPA) by the end of 2026.
- SCO — 26th Heads of State summit, Bishkek, 31 August–1 September 2026, for the SCO's 25th anniversary; PM Modi named India's priorities as Security, Connectivity and Opportunity, and asked for 'no double standards' on terrorism.
- Quad — India has chaired since 2024 but held no leaders' summit in 2025; in May 2026 reports said India would pass hosting to Australia. Foreign ministers still met in New Delhi.
- WTO MC14 (Yaoundé, Cameroon, March 2026) — ended in deadlock; members could not agree to extend the moratorium on customs duties on electronic transmissions (in place since 1998).

### Trade agreements — anatomy and India's FTA strategy

- Integration ladder: preferential trade agreement → free trade agreement → comprehensive agreement (CECA/CEPA, adding services and investment) → customs union → common market → economic union. WTO legal basis: GATT Article XXIV (goods), GATS Article V (services), and the Enabling Clause for developing countries.
- India's older FTAs: Sri Lanka (1998), Singapore CECA (2005), SAFTA (2006), ASEAN goods agreement (2009, under review since 2023), Korea CEPA (2009), Japan and Malaysia (2011). Industry complained that these widened deficits and that firms used their preferences poorly.
- New-generation deals since 2021: Mauritius CECPA (2021), UAE CEPA (in force May 2022), Australia ECTA (December 2022), EFTA TEPA (signed March 2024, in force October 2025, with a commitment of US$100 billion investment over 15 years), UK CETA (signed July 2025, in force 15 July 2026), and the EU FTA (concluded 2026).
- New chapters India now accepts: services and professional mobility (Mode 4), digital trade, government procurement (first opened in the UK CETA), sustainability and gender; investment protection and geographical indications are negotiated separately with the EU.
- India's red lines: dairy and sensitive farm goods are kept out of these deals; no TRIPS-plus data exclusivity that would hurt generic medicines; policy space for MSP procurement.
- EU-specific frictions: the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (definitive phase from 1 January 2026, covering steel, aluminium, cement, fertiliser and more), the EU Deforestation Regulation, and India's request for 'data adequacy' status.
- The gap between headline and use: rules-of-origin paperwork, non-tariff barriers (standards, certification) and low awareness among MSMEs keep FTA usage low. The fix: export facilitation desks, a common origin-certification platform, and district export hubs.

### Strategic and economic groupings — structure snapshots

- Quad: began as the 2004 tsunami core group, first met in 2007, revived in 2017, leaders' meetings since 2021. Initiatives: Indo-Pacific Partnership for Maritime Domain Awareness (2022), critical and emerging technology, the Quad Cancer Moonshot (2024), and coast-guard cooperation at sea.
- SCO: founded 2001 in Shanghai; India and Pakistan joined in 2017 and Iran in 2023, and Belarus became the 10th member in 2024. RATS (anti-terror structure) is in Tashkent. India does not endorse BRI language and refused to sign the June 2025 defence ministers' statement because it left out Pahalgam.
- BRICS: BRIC foreign ministers first met in 2006; first summit at Yekaterinburg in 2009; South Africa joined in 2010. New Development Bank (2014 Fortaleza agreement; HQ Shanghai; first president K.V. Kamath; now led by Dilma Rousseff) and the Contingent Reserve Arrangement (US$100 billion). Expanded in 2024 (Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE) and 2025 (Indonesia); a 'partner country' tier was created at Kazan in 2024.
- G20: finance-minister level from 1999, leaders' summits from 2008. India's 2023 presidency brought in the African Union; South Africa hosted in 2025 and the US holds the 2026 presidency.
- BIMSTEC: Bangkok Declaration 1997; seven members; secretariat in Dhaka; Charter in force in 2024; the 6th summit (Bangkok, April 2025) adopted the Bangkok Vision 2030; India leads the security pillar.
- Indian Ocean: IORA (1997, 23 members), Colombo Security Conclave (2020; India, Sri Lanka, Maldives, Mauritius and later Bangladesh), and Mekong–Ganga Cooperation (2000).
- Corridors: INSTC (2000 agreement among India, Iran, Russia); Chabahar (10-year operating contract signed May 2024 between IPGL and Iran's Ports and Maritime Organization); IMEC (announced September 2023); I2U2 (India, Israel, UAE, US).

### Defence and strategic agreements India has signed

- With the US: GSOMIA (2002), LEMOA (2016, logistics), COMCASA (2018, secure communications), Industrial Security Annex (2019), BECA (2020, geospatial), Security of Supply Arrangement (2024), and a 10-year defence framework renewed in 2025.
- With Russia: Strategic Partnership (2000), upgraded to 'Special and Privileged' (2010); S-400 deliveries, BrahMos joint venture, AK-203 rifles made at Amethi.
- With France: Horizon 2047 roadmap (2023); 26 Rafale-M jets for the Navy (contract April 2025); Scorpène submarines built at Mazagon.
- With Japan: the 2+2 dialogue and the Acquisition and Cross-Servicing Agreement (2020); with Australia: the Mutual Logistics Support Arrangement (2020).
- Technology regimes India joined: MTCR (2016), Wassenaar Arrangement (2017), Australia Group (2018); NSG membership is still blocked (India got an NSG waiver in 2008).
- Multi-alignment in one line: India is in the Quad and SCO/BRICS at the same time, buys Russian oil and arms while deepening US technology ties, and treats each issue on its merits.

### Punjab's stake in agreements and groupings

- Basmati: India applied in 2018 for EU 'protected geographical indication' status (application published 2021), and Pakistan contests it. The India–EU FTA was concluded on 27 January 2026, and how it treats geographical indications matters to Punjab and Haryana basmati growers.
- UK and EU deals cut duties for Ludhiana knitwear and hosiery, Jalandhar sports goods and leather, hand tools and auto parts — MSME clusters where duty margins decide orders.
- Dairy stays out of these deals, which protects Punjab's dairy cooperatives (Verka) and small producers — remember why India left RCEP in 2019.
- US tariffs (2025) and the 2026 interim framework affect Punjab's engineering goods and textiles; the cut to 18% eases but does not remove the cost disadvantage against competitors.
- WTO rules on public stockholding and the 'peace clause' protect MSP procurement of Punjab's wheat and paddy; the MC14 deadlock means there is still no permanent solution.
- Connectivity groupings (INSTC, Chabahar) are India's way around a closed Pakistan route, the one Punjab's Attari land port could otherwise have served.

### PYQ patterns and traps

- Typical asks: 'Relevance of the Quad / SCO / BRICS for India', 'Impact of FTAs on India's trade deficit', 'Why did India stay out of RCEP?', 'India's G20 presidency legacy'.
- Frame: origin and membership → India's objectives → achievements → limits and contradictions → way forward. Add one 2026 fact (BRICS New Delhi Declaration, India–EU FTA, UK CETA in force).
- Trap: EFTA = Switzerland, Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein — not EU members.
- Trap: RCEP (signed 2020, in force 2022) has 15 members; India withdrew in November 2019 at the Bangkok summit.
- Trap: India joined IPEF's supply-chain, clean-economy and fair-economy pillars but stayed out of the trade pillar.
- Trap: G4 (India, Brazil, Germany, Japan) seek UNSC permanent seats; 'Uniting for Consensus' (Italy, Pakistan and others) opposes new permanent members.
- Trap: the NDB's headquarters is in Shanghai, but its first president was Indian (K.V. Kamath); the CRA is not a bank.

**1.** "India's simultaneous membership of the Quad and BRICS reflects its strategic autonomy rather than contradiction." Discuss with reference to India's BRICS chairship in 2026. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: multi-alignment and strategic autonomy.
- Quad: maritime security, tech, Indo-Pacific balance.
- BRICS: Global South voice, NDB, reform of global governance, expanded membership.

- BRICS 2026 chair: priorities — reformed multilateralism, development finance, climate, counter-terrorism.

**2.** India has signed a series of free trade agreements since 2021. Analyse the opportunities and challenges they present, especially for MSMEs and agriculture. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: shift from RCEP exit to bilateral FTAs.
- Agreements: UAE CEPA, Australia ECTA, EFTA TEPA, UK CETA.
- Opportunities: market access for labour-intensive goods, services mobility, investment.
- Challenges: low utilisation, rules of origin, standards, competition for sensitive sectors.
- Punjab: textiles, sports goods, basmati; dairy safeguards.
- Way forward: FTA awareness, quality infrastructure, export credit.
- Conclusion: FTAs need domestic competitiveness.

</details>

**3.** Why has BIMSTEC gained prominence over SAARC in India's regional strategy? (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: both regional groupings.
- SAARC paralysis due to India–Pakistan tensions.
- BIMSTEC: links South and Southeast Asia, Act East, Bay of Bengal security, Charter in force 2024.
- Challenges: slow FTA, limited secretariat capacity, Myanmar situation.
- Conclusion: BIMSTEC as practical regionalism.

</details>
