## Short notes

- Policy cycle: agenda setting, formulation, adoption, implementation, monitoring and evaluation; Indian failures commonly occur at implementation.
- Design issues: top-down 'one size fits all' schemes, poor targeting (inclusion/exclusion errors), multiplicity of overlapping centrally sponsored schemes, weak baseline data, inadequate consultation.
- Implementation issues: leakages, capacity gaps at block/district level, delayed fund flows, corruption, lack of convergence across departments, weak grievance redressal.
- Tools for better delivery: DBT (from 2013) using JAM trinity, PFMS, outcome budgeting, NITI Aayog's Development Monitoring and Evaluation Office (DMEO), Aspirational Districts Programme (2018) with ranking-based competition.
- Social audit (MGNREGA Sec 17) and community monitoring improve accountability.
- NGOs: service delivery, advocacy, innovation, watchdog; National Policy on the Voluntary Sector 2007; NGO Darpan portal for registration with NITI Aayog.
- FCRA 2010 amended 2020: ban on transfer of foreign contribution to other NGOs, administrative expense cap reduced to 20%, mandatory FCRA account at SBI New Delhi main branch; licences of several NGOs cancelled.
- Issues with NGOs: accountability, foreign funding concerns, dependence on government grants, 'briefcase NGOs', limited reach in remote areas.
- SHGs: NABARD's SHG-Bank Linkage Programme (1992); DAY-NRLM (2011) with community institutions; Lakhpati Didi target of 3 crore women; Kudumbashree (Kerala, 1998) as model.
- SHG benefits: financial inclusion, women's empowerment, social capital, convergence with PRIs; challenges: low-value livelihoods, market linkage, over-indebtedness, regional imbalance (strong in South, weaker in North).
- Donors and charities: World Bank, ADB, bilateral donors (JICA), philanthropic foundations; CSR under Sec 135 Companies Act 2013 (2% of average net profit for companies above thresholds).
- Cooperatives: Ministry of Cooperation (2021), Multi-State Cooperative Societies (Amendment) Act 2023; Amul as success model.
- Institutional stakeholders: PRIs/ULBs, academic institutions, think tanks, media, courts (PIL-driven entitlements, e.g. Right to Food case).
- Partnership models: PPPs, social impact bonds, convergence of CSR with government schemes.
- Good practices: 'Jan Bhagidari' approach in Swachh Bharat, Jal Jeevan Mission's village water committees, Kerala's People's Plan.
- Way forward: evidence-based policy, pilots before scale-up, decentralised planning, outcome-linked funding, transparent NGO regulation that does not stifle civil society.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab State Rural Livelihoods Mission (PSRLM) implements DAY-NRLM; SHG density in Punjab is lower than in southern states.
- Civil society strength: All India Pingalwara Charitable Society (founded by Bhagat Puran Singh, Amritsar), gurdwara langar and kar seva, Khalsa Aid in disaster relief including the 2025 floods.
- State interventions: Aam Aadmi Clinics (from Aug 2022), Schools of Eminence, 300 free power units, Mukh Mantri Mawan Dhian Satikar Yojana (₹1,000/month to women 18+, ₹1,500 to SC women).
- 'Yudh Nashian Virudh' anti-drug campaign (from March 2025) combines enforcement, de-addiction and community participation — a policy-implementation case study.
- Crop diversification and stubble management (in-situ machinery subsidies, direct seeded rice incentives) illustrate design vs farmer-behaviour gaps.

## How PPSC asks it

Questions ask about design-implementation gaps in welfare schemes, role of NGOs/SHGs/CSR, and FCRA debates. Reward goes to examples of success and failure, data-backed reasoning and practical reforms.

## Deep dive

### Policy-design toolkit examiners reward

- Theory of change / logical framework: map inputs → activities → outputs → outcomes → impact, with indicators and assumptions at each level; it shows where a scheme breaks down.
- Targeting choices: universal (e.g. mid-day meal) vs targeted (e.g. PMAY). Targeted schemes make inclusion errors (non-poor get in) and exclusion errors (poor left out); self-targeting designs such as MGNREGA's manual work at minimum wage reduce both.
- Instrument choice: cash vs in-kind (PDS grain vs DBT — Chandigarh and Puducherry piloted cash for food in 2015); conditional (Janani Suraksha Yojana) vs unconditional (PM-KISAN ₹6,000 a year) transfers.
- Behavioural design: Economic Survey 2018-19 devoted a chapter to 'nudges' (social norms in Swachh Bharat, defaults, salience); use it for behaviour-change questions.
- Evidence-based policy: randomised evaluations (the 2019 Economics Nobel to Banerjee, Duflo, Kremer), pilots before scale-up, third-party evaluation, and public release of evaluation reports.
- Fiscal federalism in design: the Sub-Group of Chief Ministers (2015) split centrally sponsored schemes into 'core of the core' (social protection, MGNREGA), core, and optional; the standard funding ratio is 60:40 (90:10 for North-Eastern and Himalayan states).
- Outcome focus: the Output-Outcome Monitoring Framework run by NITI Aayog's DMEO (since 2018) ties budget heads to measurable indicators; schemes are appraised for continuation at the start of each Finance Commission cycle.
- Design failures to cite: one-size-fits-all norms (unit costs that ignore hilly or border terrain), overlapping schemes, weak baseline data, ignoring state capacity, no exit or sunset clause.

### What evaluations of major schemes have found (cite with source)

- MGNREGA — the 2nd ARC 2nd Report 'Unlocking Human Capital: Entitlements and Governance' (2006) urged gram-panchayat planning, social audits and ICT. Swaraj Abhiyan (2016): the Supreme Court enforced compensation for wage delays (0.05% of unpaid wages per day). Later problems: late payments, shrinking budgets, exclusions under Aadhaar-based payment (made mandatory from January 2024).
- PDS — the Shanta Kumar Committee (2015) estimated that about 46% of PDS grain leaked (NSSO 2011-12 data). It proposed narrowing NFSA coverage to about 40% and moving gradually to cash. After ONORC portability and ePoS machines, leakage estimates fell in most states.
- Ujjwala (PMUY) — the CAG performance audit (2019) found low refill rates (an average of about 3.7 cylinders a year among beneficiaries). Lesson: access is not the same as sustained use, so affordability design matters.
- Swachh Bharat (Gramin) — WHO (2018) estimated about 3 lakh diarrhoeal deaths averted between 2014 and 2019. Independent surveys found sustained use and maintenance of toilets uneven, which led to the ODF-Plus phase.
- Aspirational Districts — a UNDP appraisal (2021) credited the 'convergence, collaboration, competition' model and real-time ranking with faster gains in health and nutrition indicators. Critics point to dashboards pushing 'teaching to the indicator'.
- DBT — the government claims large savings from removing duplicate and fake beneficiaries. Independent researchers warn of exclusion through Aadhaar-seeding failures and urge offline grievance channels.
- NITI Aayog's DMEO evaluated all centrally sponsored schemes in 2020-21, grouped into umbrella programmes. It recommended consolidation, outcome indicators and better data systems. It also runs Unified Citizen Service Satisfaction studies.
- Structure for using evaluation evidence in answers: claim → source (CAG, DMEO, NSSO/NFHS, independent study) → number → design lesson.

### NGOs and civil society — law, money, accountability

- Legal forms: public trust (Indian Trusts Act 1882 or state public trust laws), society (Societies Registration Act 1860), or Section 8 company (Companies Act 2013, not-for-profit).
- Tax: income-tax exemption needs registration under Section 12A/12AB and donor deduction needs Section 80G; from 2021 all charities had to re-register, with renewal every five years.
- FCRA 2010 (replaced the 1976 Act). The 2020 amendments also require Aadhaar or passport details of office-bearers, and allow the government to suspend a registration for up to 360 days during inquiry.
- CSR (Companies Act 2013, Section 135) applies where net worth is ₹500 crore or more, turnover ₹1,000 crore or more, or net profit ₹5 crore or more. Unspent CSR must go to an 'unspent CSR account' or a Schedule VII fund (2019 amendment). Implementing agencies must register on Form CSR-1 (2021).
- NGO accountability: in a 2017 PIL (M.L. Sharma) the Supreme Court noted that a large share of NGOs did not file accounts and ordered audits of CAPART-funded bodies. Thalappalam Co-operative Bank (2013) set the test for when a body is 'substantially financed' by government and so falls under RTI.
- Civil society roles with examples: data and accountability (Pratham's ASER since 2005, ADR in the 2024 electoral bonds case), service delivery (SEWA, Goonj), transparency movements (MKSS's jan sunwais led to RTI), legal empowerment.
- Punjab examples: Sant Balbir Singh Seechewal's kar-seva clean-up of the Kali Bein rivulet (from 2000) is a model of community river restoration; Kheti Virasat Mission (Jaito) promotes natural farming; gurdwara networks and Khalsa Aid in relief work; Pingalwara in care for destitute people.
- Balanced view for answers: NGOs add reach and innovation, but need transparency (accounts, governance, conflict-of-interest rules). The state should regulate their activity without throttling legitimate dissent.

### SHGs, cooperatives and community institutions — deeper

- DAY-NRLM architecture: SHG (10–20 women) → Village Organisation → Cluster Level Federation, supported by community resource persons. SHGs receive a Revolving Fund and a Community Investment Fund, and interest subvention on bank loans.
- Lakhpati Didi: the aim is an annual household income of at least ₹1 lakh for 3 crore SHG women. Namo Drone Didi (2023) plans 15,000 drones for SHGs to provide agri-drone services.
- State models: Kudumbashree (Kerala, 1998) is linked to panchayats; JEEViKA (Bihar, 2007, World Bank-supported) runs 'Didi ki Rasoi' canteens; Andhra Pradesh's SERP/Velugu pioneered federations.
- Constraints: low-value livelihoods, weak market linkage, multiple loans leading to over-indebtedness, elite capture within groups, and a much thinner SHG presence in northern states, including Punjab.
- Cooperatives: the 97th Amendment (2011) added Part IXB, but in Rajendra Shah (2021) the Supreme Court held that Part IXB applies only to multi-state cooperatives because state cooperatives are a State List subject. The Multi-State Cooperative Societies (Amendment) Act 2023 followed.
- Cooperative reforms after 2021: model bye-laws for PACS, computerisation of PACS, a plan for decentralised grain storage in the cooperative sector, the Tribhuvan Sahkari University Act 2025, and a National Cooperation Policy (2025).
- Punjab: Milkfed (Verka brand, 1973) is the leading cooperative success; the PACS network and the Punjab State Cooperative Bank finance farm credit; PSRLM implements NRLM.
- Social capital: the 2nd ARC 9th Report 'Social Capital — A Shared Destiny' (2008) recommended an accreditation system for NGOs, light-touch regulation, and partnership frameworks for SHGs and cooperatives.

### Punjab case studies — design vs implementation

- Free farm power (since 1997): it secured food output but encouraged paddy over-extraction. CGWB assessments show Punjab drawing well over 100% of annual groundwater recharge — the highest stage of extraction in India. A textbook case of perverse incentives.
- Punjab Preservation of Subsoil Water Act 2009: barred paddy transplanting before a notified mid-June date. It saved water, but it squeezed the gap between paddy harvest and wheat sowing, which fed stubble burning — an unintended consequence.
- Direct Seeded Rice incentive (₹1,500 per acre, 2022): adoption stayed below targets because of weed problems, uneven fields and the need for verification. Lesson: incentives must match agronomic reality and extension support.
- Fiscal frame (PRS, Punjab Budget 2026-27): committed expenditure (salaries, pensions, interest) is 72% of revenue receipts; power subsidies are ₹15,550 crore (12.3% of revenue receipts); outstanding liabilities are about 45.1% of GSDP; fiscal deficit 4.1% of GSDP.
- New welfare commitments in 2026-27 (PRS): Mukh Mantri Mawan Dhian Satikar Yojana (₹9,300 crore), Mukh Mantri Sehat Yojana for universal health cover (₹2,000 crore), Sikhiya Kranti 2.0 (₹3,500 crore), Meri Rasoi ration kits (₹900 crore). These are useful for a 'welfare vs freebies' debate.
- Freebies debate: in S. Subramaniam Balaji (2013) the Supreme Court said poll promises are not 'corrupt practices'; in 2022 the court referred the question to a larger bench. A good answer separates merit goods (health, education, nutrition) from untargeted subsidies.
- Land Pooling Policy 2025 (withdrawn August 2025 after farmer protests) and the 'Yudh Nashian Virudh' anti-drug campaign (2025) are both cases on stakeholder consultation and trust.

### PPSC answer pointers and common traps

- Separate 'policy failure' (a wrong theory of change) from 'implementation failure' (a right design with weak delivery); examiners reward this distinction.
- Always add a stakeholder map: state, PRIs/ULBs, NGOs, SHGs, CSR donors, beneficiaries, frontline workers (ASHA, anganwadi, patwari).
- Trap: the SHG–Bank Linkage Programme began as a NABARD pilot in 1992; NRLM came in June 2011 (renamed DAY-NRLM in 2016).
- Trap: NGO Darpan is NITI Aayog's portal (unique ID for grants); FCRA is run by the Ministry of Home Affairs.
- Trap: CSR spending through a company's own foundation is allowed only if the foundation is registered (CSR-1); spending on employees only, or on political parties, does not count.
- Trap: the 60:40 Centre–state ratio applies to most CSS; MGNREGA wages are 100% centrally funded (materials 75:25).
- Way-forward checklist: pilot → evaluate → scale; convergence at district level; social audit; grievance redress; data privacy; sunset reviews; community ownership (O&M committees).

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** "Good policies fail in India not in design but in delivery." Examine with examples and suggest measures to bridge the implementation gap. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: policy cycle and implementation deficit.
- Design flaws: targeting errors, centralised templates, fragmented CSS.
- Delivery issues: capacity, leakages, convergence, fund delays.
- Successes: DBT savings, Aspirational Districts, Jal Jeevan Mission.
- Punjab: anti-drug campaign, stubble management lessons.
- Measures: evidence-based pilots, DMEO evaluations, social audits, decentralised flexibility.
- Conclusion: delivery capacity as part of design.

</details>

**2.** Discuss the role of Self-Help Groups in women's empowerment and rural development. Why has the SHG movement been weaker in north-western states like Punjab? (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: SHG-BLP 1992, DAY-NRLM.
- Contributions: credit access, savings, enterprise, voice in Gram Sabha, Kudumbashree model.
- Limitations: subsistence enterprises, market linkage, debt.
- Punjab factors: agrarian economy with large farms, lower female labour participation, social norms, late NRLM roll-out, migration.
- Way forward: farmer producer organisations, dairy/food processing clusters, digital markets.
- Conclusion: SHGs as building blocks of inclusive growth.

</details>

**3.** Evaluate the impact of the 2020 amendments to the FCRA on India's civil society. (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: FCRA 2010 objectives — national security, transparency.
- 2020 changes: no sub-granting, 20% admin cap, SBI account, Aadhaar.
- Positive: traceability, curbs misuse.
- Concerns: small grassroots NGOs hit, research/advocacy sector strained, discretion in licence cancellation.
- Way forward: risk-based regulation, appeal mechanism.
- Conclusion: balance security with democratic space.

</details>
