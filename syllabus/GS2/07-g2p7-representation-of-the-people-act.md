## Short notes

- RPA 1950: allocation of seats in Parliament and legislatures, delimitation of constituencies, qualifications of voters, preparation of electoral rolls, Chief Electoral Officer, DEO, ERO.
- RPA 1950 Sec 16: disqualification from registration (non-citizen, unsound mind declared by court, disqualified for corrupt practice); Sec 20 'ordinarily resident', with special provisions for service voters.
- Election Laws (Amendment) Act 2021: four qualifying dates (1 Jan, 1 Apr, 1 Jul, 1 Oct), voluntary Aadhaar linking, gender-neutral 'spouse' for service voters.
- RPA 1951: conduct of elections, qualifications and disqualifications of members, corrupt practices, election disputes, registration of political parties.
- Sec 8: disqualification on conviction for specified offences/sentence of 2+ years, for term plus six years; Sec 8(4) protection for sitting members struck down in Lily Thomas (2013).
- Sec 10A: failure to lodge election expenses — disqualification for 3 years; Sec 77: account of election expenses within limits set by rules.
- Sec 29A: registration of parties with ECI; Sec 29B/29C: contributions and reporting of donations above ₹20,000.
- Sec 33(7): a candidate can contest from at most two constituencies (since 1996 amendment); Sec 33A and Form 26 affidavit on criminal antecedents, assets, education.
- Sec 61A: EVMs (inserted 1988); VVPAT ordered in Subramanian Swamy (2013); ADR (2024) SC rejected return to paper ballots.
- Sec 62(5): persons in prison or police custody cannot vote (except preventive detention); Sec 60: postal ballot categories.
- Sec 123: corrupt practices — bribery, undue influence, appeal on religion/caste/community/language (Abhiram Singh 2017: covers voters' religion too), booth capturing; Sec 125A false affidavit.
- Sec 126: ban on public meetings/ads in 48 hours before close of poll ('silence period'); Sec 126A: exit-poll restrictions.
- Sec 80–100: election petitions to High Court within 45 days; trial to be concluded within six months (directory).
- Sec 151A: by-elections within six months of vacancy unless remainder of term is less than one year.
- Key judgments: PUCL (2013) NOTA; Public Interest Foundation (2018) — publicise criminal records; Rambabu Singh Thakur (2020) — parties must explain selecting such candidates; ADR (Feb 2024) struck down electoral bond scheme.
- Reform agenda: decriminalisation (disqualify on framing of charges — Law Commission 244th Report), state funding (Indrajit Gupta Committee 1998), simultaneous elections (Kovind Committee 2024), remote voting for migrants, statutory MCC.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab: 13 Lok Sabha and 117 Assembly constituencies; delimitation based on 2001 Census (Delimitation Act 2002) frozen till first Census after 2026.
- Punjab has a sizeable number of service voters (serving armed forces personnel) who may vote by post or proxy (classified service voters) under RPA provisions.
- Drug and liquor seizures during Model Code periods are a recurring Punjab concern, linking RPA's corrupt-practice provisions to the state's drug problem.
- Large NRI population: overseas electors can register under Sec 20A RPA 1950 but must vote in person — a live issue for Punjab's diaspora.

## Deep dive

### RPA 1950 — section map

- Secs 3–7 — allocation of seats in the Lok Sabha, assemblies and councils (First to Fourth Schedules of the Act).
- Sec 8A — delimitation in the North-East states (used for Assam in 2023).
- Secs 13A–13C — Chief Electoral Officer, District Election Officer, Electoral Registration Officer and assistants.
- Sec 14 — qualifying dates (four a year since 2021); Sec 15 — one roll per constituency; Secs 17–18 — no one on more than one roll.
- Sec 19 — conditions of registration (18 years and ordinarily resident); Sec 20 — "ordinarily resident", including service voters and their spouses.
- Sec 20A — overseas electors (added 2010) — must vote in person at their polling station.
- Sec 21 — preparation and revision of rolls; Sec 21(3) allows a special revision — the legal basis of the 2025–26 SIR.
- Secs 22–23 — correction and inclusion; Sec 23(4)–(6) — voluntary Aadhaar linking (2021).
- Sec 24 — appeals against ERO decisions to the DEO and then the CEO; Sec 27 — graduates' and teachers' constituencies for councils.

### RPA 1951 — key sections beyond disqualification

- Secs 3–6 — qualifications for Rajya Sabha, Lok Sabha, assemblies and councils (e.g. a Lok Sabha candidate must be an elector in some constituency).
- Sec 9 — dismissal for corruption or disloyalty disqualifies for five years; Sec 9A — government contracts; Sec 10 — offices in government companies.
- Sec 11 — ECI may remove or reduce a disqualification (it reduced Sikkim CM Prem Singh Tamang's disqualification period in 2019).
- Sec 33A — affidavit on pending cases; Sec 34 — deposits; Sec 36 — scrutiny; Sec 52 — poll countermanded only on death of a recognised party's candidate.
- Sec 58 — repoll; Sec 58A — adjournment for booth capturing; Sec 61A — EVMs (inserted 1988).
- Sec 62(5) — a person in prison cannot vote (except in preventive detention), but an undertrial can still contest — Amritpal Singh won Khadoor Sahib in 2024 while detained.
- Sec 70 — a person elected to both Houses or two seats must resign one; Sec 116A — appeal to the Supreme Court within 30 days of an HC election verdict.
- Sec 125 — promoting enmity in connection with elections; Sec 127A — printing of pamphlets; Sec 135B — paid holiday on polling day; Sec 135C — no liquor sale in the 48-hour period.

### Disqualification — constitutional vs statutory

- Constitutional (Arts 102/191): office of profit, unsound mind declared by court, undischarged insolvent, non-citizen, disqualified by law; plus the Tenth Schedule.
- President/Governor decide Art 102/191 questions on the ECI's binding opinion (Arts 103/192); Tenth Schedule cases go to the Speaker/Chairman.
- Office of profit: Parliament (Prevention of Disqualification) Act 1959 lists exempted posts; Jaya Bachchan (2006) — the test is whether the office is capable of yielding profit.
- A 2006 retrospective amendment exempting several posts (including NAC chair) was upheld (Consumer Education and Research Society, 2009).
- Delhi: the ECI recommended disqualifying 20 AAP MLAs over parliamentary secretary posts (2018); the Delhi High Court set it aside for lack of hearing.
- Sec 8(3) in action: Rahul Gandhi was disqualified after a two-year sentence (March 2023) and restored when the SC stayed the conviction (Aug 2023).

### Amendments to the RPA — timeline

- 1966 — election tribunals abolished; petitions go to High Courts.
- 1988–89 — EVMs (Sec 61A), booth capturing (Sec 58A, 135A), registration of parties (Sec 29A, 1989).
- 1996 — two-constituency limit (Sec 33(7)), higher deposits, more offences in Sec 8, countermanding only on death of a recognised party candidate.
- 2002–03 — Sec 33A affidavits after Union v ADR; 2003 — open ballot for Rajya Sabha, domicile condition removed, Secs 29B–29C on donations.
- 2010 — overseas electors (Sec 20A); 2013 — persons in custody stay on rolls (after a Patna High Court ruling), so undertrials can contest.
- 2017 — Finance Act changes for electoral bonds (exempting bond donations from Sec 29C reporting) — struck down with the scheme in Feb 2024.

### Punjab and the RPA in practice

- Punjab's CEO conducted the 2026 SIR under Sec 21(3) — the first intensive revision in about two decades; final roll 12 Oct 2026 (about 2.01 crore electors).
- Large numbers of service voters (serving soldiers) use postal ballots (ETPBS) or proxy voting (classified service voters).
- Overseas Punjabis can register under Sec 20A but must vote in person — a long-standing demand for proxy or e-postal voting for NRIs.
- By-elections under Sec 151A: Jalandhar West (July 2024) and Ludhiana West (June 2025).
- Seizures of drugs, liquor and cash during the Model Code period in Punjab link Sec 123 (bribery) and Sec 135C (liquor) to the state's drug problem.
- Undertrial MP: Amritpal Singh's 2024 win (while under NSA detention) illustrates the gap between voting and contesting rights under the RPA.

### Reform agenda and Mains framing

- Criminalisation: ADR found about 46% of 18th Lok Sabha MPs declared criminal cases; reform options — disqualification on framing of charges (Law Commission 244th), fast-track courts, lifetime ban (petition pending).
- Money: post-bonds funding via electoral trusts and direct donations; proposals for state funding (Indrajit Gupta), expenditure caps for parties, and audited accounts.
- Simultaneous elections: 129th Amendment Bill with JPC; would need RPA changes on schedules and term synchronisation.
- Migrants: Remote Voting Machine prototype (2023); postal options for 85+ and PwD voters are already in place at home.
- Technology: VVPAT counting debates; deepfakes and social-media rules under the MCC.
- Make the MCC statutory? ECI prefers flexibility; critics want enforceable penalties.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** Discuss the salient features of the Representation of the People Act, 1951. How far has it succeeded in ensuring free and fair elections? (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: RPA 1951 operationalises Art 324–329.
- Features: qualifications/disqualifications (Sec 8–11), party registration (29A), expenses (77), corrupt practices (123), election petitions, silence period (126).
- Successes: peaceful transfers of power, EVM/VVPAT, NOTA, disclosure affidavits.
- Gaps: criminalisation, expense limits flouted, weak party regulation (no de-registration power), slow petitions.
- Judicial role: Lily Thomas, ADR, PUCL, Public Interest Foundation.
- Reforms: Law Commission, statutory MCC, state funding.
- Conclusion: law sound, enforcement and political will key.

</details>

**2.** "Decriminalisation of politics requires more than disclosure." Examine in the context of provisions of the RPA and Supreme Court judgments. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: ADR data trend of MPs with criminal cases.
- Current law: Sec 8, Sec 33A affidavits, Lily Thomas.
- Judgments: Public Interest Foundation 2018, Rambabu Singh Thakur 2020.
- Limits: conviction-based disqualification, slow trials, 'winnability'.
- Proposals: disqualify at charge-framing for heinous offences with safeguards, special courts, ECI power to deregister.
- Conclusion: parties and voters must share responsibility.

</details>

**3.** Examine the provisions relating to voting rights of service voters and overseas electors. What reforms are needed? (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: Sec 20 RPA 1950; Sec 20A overseas electors.
- Service voters: postal ballot (ETPBS), proxy voting for classified service voters; 2021 gender-neutral change.
- Overseas electors: must be physically present; low turnout.
- Reforms: ETPBS for NRIs (proposed), remote EVMs for migrants, security of ballots.
- Conclusion: inclusion without compromising secrecy.

</details>
