## Short notes

- Planning Commission (1950, executive resolution) ran 12 Five-Year Plans; replaced by NITI Aayog on 1 Jan 2015 as a 'think tank' with cooperative federalism and no fund-allocation powers.
- Plan models: Harrod-Domar (1st Plan, agriculture), Mahalanobis (2nd Plan, heavy industry); 11th Plan theme 'faster and more inclusive growth'; 12th Plan (2012-17) 'faster, more inclusive and sustainable growth'.
- Growth = rise in real GDP/GDP per capita; development = growth plus improvement in health, education, freedoms (Sen's capability approach); HDI by UNDP since 1990.
- Resource mobilisation: tax revenue (direct, GST), non-tax (dividends, fees), disinvestment/asset monetisation, borrowings, external aid; India's tax-GDP ratio is low relative to OECD peers.
- GST (101st Amendment 2016, rolled out 1 July 2017) unified indirect taxes; GST Council (Art 279A) is a key forum of fiscal federalism.
- Employment-growth link: employment elasticity of growth has been low ('jobless growth' debate), with most workers in informal, low-productivity jobs.
- Inclusive growth: growth that broad-bases opportunities across regions, castes, gender; tools include financial inclusion (PMJDY 2014), DBT, social protection, MSME credit.
- Sustainable development: Brundtland Report 'Our Common Future' (1987) definition - meeting present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs.
- UN SDGs: 17 goals, 169 targets adopted 2015 for 2030; NITI Aayog publishes the SDG India Index to rank states.
- Constitutional budgeting: Art 112 (Annual Financial Statement, Union), Art 202 (States); Art 113-117 on demands, appropriation and finance bills; Art 266 Consolidated Fund, Art 267 Contingency Fund.
- Union Budget presented on 1 February since 2017; Railway Budget merged with General Budget in 2017; Plan/non-plan distinction removed from 2017-18.
- Deficits: Fiscal deficit = total expenditure minus (revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts); Primary deficit = FD minus interest; Revenue deficit = revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts.
- FRBM Act 2003 set fiscal rules; N.K. Singh FRBM Review Committee (2017) suggested general government debt of 60% of GDP (Centre 40%, States 20%) with fiscal deficit as the operational target.
- Budget reforms: outcome budget and gender budgeting (from 2005-06), zero-based budgeting, green budgeting, performance audit by CAG; capital expenditure push since 2020-21 for crowding-in private investment.
- Finance Commission (Art 280): 15th FC (N.K. Singh) kept states' share of divisible pool at 41%; 16th FC chaired by Arvind Panagariya covers 2026-31.
- Fiscal federalism issues: rising cesses and surcharges (outside divisible pool), state borrowing limits under Art 293, off-budget borrowings being counted against state limits.
- Challenges: revenue-heavy expenditure, interest burden, subsidies, freebies debate vs welfare, quality of expenditure, low capex by states.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab Budget 2026-27: outlay ₹2,60,437 crore, presented 8 March 2026 by FM Harpal Singh Cheema; fiscal deficit around 4.1% of GSDP.
- Punjab's debt-to-GSDP ratio is among the highest of major states (above 40% in recent years), making interest payments a large share of revenue receipts.
- Committed expenditure (salaries, pensions, interest) plus the power subsidy (free farm power since 1997; 300 free units for households since July 2022) leave little fiscal space for capex.
- Punjab Economic Survey 2025-26: real GSDP growth 6.1%, agriculture contracted 2.5% due to the 2025 floods; sector shares agriculture 23%, industry 29%, services 48%.
- Mukh Mantri Mawan Dhian Satikar Yojana (₹1,000/month to women 18+, ₹1,500 to SC women) is an example of the welfare-versus-fiscal-sustainability trade-off.

## How PPSC asks it

Questions link growth with jobs, inclusiveness and fiscal sustainability; examiners reward correct definitions of deficits, use of FRBM/Finance Commission references, and Punjab's fiscal stress with data and a balanced way forward.

## Deep dive

### Union Budget 2026-27 — architecture and numbers

- Presented on 1 February 2026 (a Sunday); framed around three 'Kartavyas' — accelerating growth, fulfilling aspirations, and 'Sabka Saath, Sabka Vikas'.
- Size: total expenditure ₹53.47 lakh crore (RE 2025-26: ₹49.65 lakh crore); capital expenditure ₹12.22 lakh crore; effective capex ₹17.15 lakh crore.
- Receipts: revenue receipts ₹35.33 lakh crore — net tax ₹28.67 lakh crore, non-tax ₹6.66 lakh crore (RBI and PSU dividends are the main items).
- Deficits: fiscal 4.3%, revenue 1.5%, effective revenue 0.3%, primary 0.7% of GDP.
- Interest payments ₹14.04 lakh crore ≈ 40% of revenue receipts — the binding constraint on welfare and capex.
- Financing the deficit: net market borrowing through dated securities about ₹11.7 lakh crore (gross ₹17.2 lakh crore), small savings securities ₹3.87 lakh crore, short-term T-bills ₹1.3 lakh crore.
- Transfers to states and UTs (devolution, grants, CSS releases) ₹25.44 lakh crore.
- Debt path: Central debt 56.1% of GDP (RE 2025-26) → 55.6% (BE 2026-27) → 50 ± 1% by 2030-31.
- Big-ticket announcements: seven high-speed rail 'growth connectors', Dankuni–Surat freight corridor, 20 new National Waterways, ₹20,000 crore for CCUS, ₹10,000 crore SME Growth Fund, Biopharma SHAKTI.

### Resource mobilisation — tax and non-tax reforms

- GST 2.0 (effective 22 September 2025): two main slabs 5% and 18%, 40% for demerit/luxury goods; compensation cess being wound up; individual life and health insurance exempted.
- New Income Tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 — simpler language, 'tax year' concept; no change in rates in Budget 2026-27.
- Budget 2026-27 tax measures: STT on futures raised to 0.05% and options to 0.15%; MAT cut to 14%; TCS on overseas tours and education/medical remittances cut to 2%; data-centre tax holiday till 2047 for foreign cloud service providers.
- Tax-to-GDP: Centre's gross tax about 11–12% of GDP; general government (Centre + states) about 18% — low for India's needs, high for its income level.
- Cesses and surcharges are outside the divisible pool, so their rising share reduces states' effective share below 41%.
- Non-tax: RBI surplus transfers (record levels since 2023-24) and PSU dividends; disinvestment/asset monetisation (₹80,000 crore 'other receipts' in 2026-27).
- National Monetisation Pipeline 2.0 (announced 2025) continues brownfield asset recycling.
- Borrowing quality: shift from short-term to long-dated securities; Indian bonds included in global indices (JP Morgan, 2024) widened the investor base.
- States' resource issues: GST compensation ended (June 2022), Article 293 borrowing limits, off-budget borrowings now counted.

### Fiscal federalism 2026–31 — Sixteenth Finance Commission

- Chair: Arvind Panagariya; report tabled with the Budget (February 2026); period 2026-27 to 2030-31.
- Vertical share unchanged at 41% of the divisible pool.
- Horizontal formula: income distance 42.5%, population (2011) 17.5%, demographic performance 10%, area 10%, forest 10%, contribution to GDP 10% (new); tax effort criterion dropped.
- Demographic performance now measured by population growth 1971–2011; GDP contribution uses the square root of GSDP to temper the advantage of big states.
- Grants about ₹9.47 lakh crore: local bodies ₹7.91 lakh crore, disaster management ₹1.56 lakh crore (Centre's share).
- Revenue deficit grants, sector-specific and state-specific grants discontinued — a loss for deficit states like Punjab.
- Fiscal rules: Centre to 3.5% FD by 2030-31; states capped at 3% of GSDP; off-budget borrowing to be ended; combined debt projected to fall from 77.3% to 73.1% of GDP.
- Punjab's share rises to 2.00% (15th FC 1.81%); Punjab's local bodies get basic grants of ₹6,789 crore (rural) and ₹6,267 crore (urban); disaster grant ₹2,477 crore.
- Debate: southern and better-off states argue 'contribution' is under-rewarded; poorer states want more weight to income distance.

### Budget process and public-finance tools (beyond the Articles)

- Stages: presentation → general discussion → departmental committee scrutiny → voting on demands (Art 113) → Appropriation Bill (Art 114) → Finance Bill (Money Bill, Art 110).
- Vote on Account (Art 116) keeps the government running before the full budget passes; 'guillotine' closes debate on remaining demands.
- Cut motions: policy cut (reduce to ₹1), economy cut (specific amount), token cut (₹100).
- FRBM documents with the Budget: Medium-Term Fiscal Policy cum Fiscal Policy Strategy Statement and the Macro-economic Framework Statement.
- Outcome Budget (from 2005-06) links outlays to outputs/outcomes; Gender Budget Statement (from 2005-06) shows allocations benefiting women.
- Zero-based budgeting reviews each scheme from scratch; accrual accounting and PFMS improve tracking.
- SNA-SPARSH (2023) releases CSS funds 'just in time' to cut idle balances in state treasuries.
- CAG audits and Public Accounts Committee review close the accountability loop.

### Growth, development and employment — data and debates

- New GDP series (base 2022-23) shows 7.2%, 7.1% and 7.7% growth for 2023-24 to 2025-26; Q1 2026-27 at 7.8%.
- Viksit Bharat @2047 needs about 8% sustained real growth — the Economic Survey 2025-26 stresses deregulation, state capacity and 'strategic resilience'.
- Jobs: PLFS 2025 unemployment 3.1% but youth unemployment 9.9% and urban youth 13.6%; female LFPR 40% (usual status).
- Employment elasticity: growth led by capital- and skill-intensive sectors creates fewer jobs per unit of output — the 'jobless growth' debate.
- Inclusive growth tools: DBT (savings estimated in lakh crores), JAM trinity, PM-VBRY employment incentives, VB-G RAM G rural work guarantee.
- Regional disparity: per capita income gap between richest and poorest states is several-fold — central to the 16th FC 'income distance' criterion.
- Development vs growth: HDI rank, learning outcomes and nutrition lag behind growth rates — Sen's capability argument.

### Sustainable development and green public finance

- Sovereign Green Bonds issued from January 2023 to fund eligible green projects.
- Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (2023) creates a domestic compliance carbon market.
- Budget 2026-27: ₹20,000 crore for carbon capture, utilisation and storage (CCUS) and Rare Earth Corridors in coastal states.
- Mission LiFE (2022) promotes sustainable lifestyles; green budgeting adopted by several states.
- Climate targets: net zero by 2070; 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030 (non-fossil crossed half of installed capacity in 2025).
- Trade-off for Punjab: free farm power subsidises groundwater depletion — a classic 'unsustainable subsidy' example.

### Punjab — budgeting and resource mobilisation (PRS, 2026-27)

- Total expenditure ₹2,60,437 crore includes ₹93,726 crore debt repayment (₹80,000 crore is short-term RBI Ways and Means Advances); net expenditure ₹1,66,711 crore.
- Borrowings ₹1,32,197 crore; fiscal deficit ₹39,971 crore (4.1% of GSDP); revenue deficit 2.2%.
- Spending priorities: education 12.9%, agriculture 9.2%, police 5.5%, health 4.7% of expenditure.
- Committed expenditure 72% of revenue receipts; power subsidy ₹15,550 crore; Mawan Dhian Satikar Yojana ₹9,300 crore.
- Own tax revenue up 15% over RE to ₹70,851 crore; SGST the biggest source.
- Loss of revenue deficit grants under the 16th FC makes own-revenue effort and subsidy rationalisation unavoidable.
- Options: targeted power subsidy (meter-based DBT), property tax reform in cities, asset monetisation, better GST compliance, curbing off-budget liabilities.

### Answer-writing angles PPSC rewards

- Use the latest budget numbers with year and source (Budget at a Glance, PRS).
- Contrast fiscal deficit vs debt anchors and explain why debt is now the anchor.
- For federalism questions, quote 16th FC weights and Punjab's 2.00% share.
- For growth vs development questions, pair GDP data with PLFS and NFHS indicators.
- Always add a Punjab paragraph (debt, power subsidy, committed spending) and a way forward.
- Avoid 'freebie' rhetoric; distinguish merit goods (health, education) from untargeted subsidies.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** 'India's growth has been robust but not sufficiently job-rich and inclusive.' Examine the statement and suggest measures to make growth more inclusive. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: define inclusive growth (11th Plan theme) and the jobless-growth concern.
- Evidence: low employment elasticity, high informality, large share of workforce still in agriculture, regional and gender gaps (low female LFPR).
- Achievements: financial inclusion (PMJDY), DBT, rural roads, social security for unorganised workers.
- Gaps: manufacturing share stagnant, skills mismatch, MSME credit constraints.
- Measures: labour-intensive manufacturing (textiles, food processing), MSME finance, skilling, urban infrastructure, care economy for women's work.
- Punjab angle: crop diversification and agro-processing to absorb rural youth.
- Conclusion: growth with jobs as the core of Viksit Bharat 2047.

</details>

**2.** Distinguish between fiscal deficit, revenue deficit and primary deficit. Why is the quality of fiscal deficit as important as its size? (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Define the three deficits with formula.
- Revenue deficit means borrowing for consumption; high RD is a red flag.
- Quality: FD financing capex creates assets and future growth; FD financing salaries/subsidies does not.
- Mention effective revenue deficit and FRBM targets.
- Example: Union capex push since 2020-21 vs states with high committed expenditure (Punjab).
- Conclusion: fiscal consolidation via better expenditure mix, not only cuts.

</details>

**3.** Sustainable development requires integrating environmental costs into growth planning. Discuss with reference to India's SDG commitments and suggest how a state like Punjab can align its development planning with sustainability. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: Brundtland definition; SDGs 2015, 17 goals.
- India: SDG India Index, LiFE mission, net-zero 2070 pledge, green budgeting.
- Tension: growth vs groundwater, air quality, soil health.
- Punjab: depletion of groundwater from paddy, stubble burning, flood vulnerability (2025 floods).
- Measures: crop diversification, solar pumps, paddy straw management, green budget tagging, district SDG dashboards.
- Conclusion: sustainability as precondition for long-term growth.

</details>
