## Short notes

- Police and public order are State subjects (Seventh Schedule, List II, entries 1 and 2); the Union assists through Central Armed Police Forces (CAPFs) and central agencies.
- CAPFs under MHA: BSF, CRPF, CISF, ITBP, SSB; Assam Rifles is administered by MHA but under Army operational control; NSG is a special counter-terror force.
- BSF (1965): guards borders with Pakistan and Bangladesh; also deployed for internal security duties.
- ITBP (1962): guards the India-China border (LAC); high-altitude specialisation.
- SSB (1963): border guarding force for Nepal (2001) and Bhutan (2004) borders.
- Assam Rifles (oldest paramilitary, 1835): counter-insurgency in the North-East and guarding the India-Myanmar border.
- CRPF: lead force for internal security, counter-insurgency and LWE operations; specialised units RAF (riot control, 1992) and CoBRA (jungle warfare, 2008).
- CISF (1969): security of public sector undertakings, airports, metro systems, sensitive installations; took over Parliament complex security in 2024.
- NSG (1984, NSG Act 1986): 'Black Cats' for counter-terror and anti-hijack operations; regional hubs created after 26/11.
- Armed forces under MoD: Army, Navy, Air Force; Chief of Defence Staff and Department of Military Affairs created in 2020; Indian Coast Guard (1977) for maritime security and coastal surveillance.
- Intelligence agencies: Intelligence Bureau (internal intelligence), R&AW (1968, external), NTRO (technical intelligence), Defence Intelligence Agency (2002), Multi-Agency Centre (MAC) and NATGRID for intelligence sharing.
- Investigation agencies: NIA (2008, after 26/11) for terror and specified offences; CBI; Narcotics Control Bureau (1986); ED; DRI.
- National security architecture: National Security Council, National Security Adviser, National Security Council Secretariat.
- Issues: overuse of CAPFs for internal duties, stress and suicides, career stagnation, inter-agency coordination gaps, police reforms pending.
- Prakash Singh v Union of India (2006): Supreme Court directives on police reforms (State Security Commission, fixed tenure of DGP, separation of investigation and law-and-order, Police Establishment Board, complaints authority).
- Committees: Kargil Review Committee (1999), Naresh Chandra Task Force (2012), Shekatkar Committee (2016) on defence reforms.

## Punjab angle

- BSF's Punjab Frontier guards the Indo-Pak border; BSF's area of jurisdiction in Punjab was extended to 50 km in 2021, a point of Centre-state debate.
- Punjab Police specialised units: Anti-Narcotics Task Force, Anti-Gangster Task Force (2022), State Special Operation Cell, Counter Intelligence wings.
- Indian Army's Western Command (headquartered at Chandimandir) and major formations are deployed along the Punjab border.
- Pathankot airbase attack (2016) highlighted coordination issues between NSG, Army, Air Force and Punjab Police.
- Punjab Home Guards and Civil Defence assist during floods and emergencies, as in the 2025 floods.

## Deep dive

### Central Armed Police Forces — origin, mandate, special units

- Assam Rifles (1835, as Cachar Levy): oldest paramilitary force; under MHA administratively but under Army operational control; guards the Myanmar border and does counter-insurgency in the North-East.
- CRPF (27 July 1939 as Crown Representative's Police; CRPF Act 1949): largest CAPF; internal security, LWE and J&K; Rapid Action Force (1992), CoBRA (2008), first Mahila battalion (1986).
- BSF (1 December 1965; first DG K.F. Rustamji): 'first line of defence' on the Pakistan and Bangladesh borders; has air, water and camel wings.
- ITBP (24 October 1962): high-altitude force on the China border; also first responder for Himalayan disasters.
- SSB (1963 as Special Service Bureau; renamed Sashastra Seema Bal in 2003): guards the Nepal (since 2001) and Bhutan (since 2004) borders.
- CISF (1969; Act of 1968): security of PSUs, airports (from 2000), metro systems, nuclear and space installations; Parliament complex since 2024; also offers consultancy to private industry.
- CAPFs together have roughly 10 lakh personnel; deployment for elections and internal security often pulls them away from border and training duties.

### Intelligence and investigation agencies

- Intelligence Bureau (origin 1887 as the Central Special Branch): domestic intelligence and counter-intelligence; under MHA.
- Research and Analysis Wing (21 September 1968, first chief R.N. Kao): external intelligence; under the Cabinet Secretariat.
- NTRO (2004): technical intelligence (satellite, signals); NCIIPC works under it. Defence Intelligence Agency (2002) coordinates military intelligence.
- Multi-Agency Centre (2001) in IB shares terror intelligence among central and state agencies; NATGRID links databases (banking, travel, telecom) for authorised agencies.
- NIA (NIA Act, December 2008, after 26/11): investigates scheduled offences — terrorism, and since 2019 human trafficking, arms, explosives and cyber terrorism — across states and abroad.
- CBI (1963; legal base: Delhi Special Police Establishment Act 1946): corruption, economic and special crimes; needs state consent (general or case-specific) to act in a state.
- Narcotics Control Bureau (1986) coordinates drug law enforcement; Enforcement Directorate (1956) handles PMLA and FEMA; DRI (1957) handles customs smuggling.
- Support bodies: Bureau of Police Research and Development (1970), National Crime Records Bureau (1986), I4C for cybercrime.

### Apex security architecture and armed forces

- Cabinet Committee on Security is the apex decision body; National Security Council (1998) with the National Security Adviser, NSC Secretariat, Strategic Policy Group and National Security Advisory Board.
- Chief of Defence Staff and Department of Military Affairs (1 January 2020) for jointness; theatre commands under discussion.
- New tri-service agencies (2019): Defence Cyber Agency, Defence Space Agency, Armed Forces Special Operations Division.
- Indian Coast Guard (1977; Coast Guard Act 1978) under MoD; lead agency for coastal security in territorial waters along with state marine police.
- Special Frontier Force (1962) operates under the Cabinet Secretariat for special missions.
- Agnipath scheme (2022): four-year recruitment of 'Agniveers' with 25% retained — affects recruitment from states like Punjab with strong military traditions.

### Other forces in the security grid

- Railway Protection Force (RPF Act 1957) under the Ministry of Railways; Government Railway Police are state police.
- National Disaster Response Force (2006, under the Disaster Management Act 2005) is drawn from CAPF battalions; states have SDRFs.
- Home Guards (from 1946; national scheme after 1962) and Civil Defence (Civil Defence Act 1968) are volunteer auxiliaries.
- India Reserve Battalions: centrally funded, state-run armed police units for internal security.
- State police special units: anti-terror squads, special task forces, cyber cells, marine police.
- Private security agencies are regulated under the PSARA Act 2005 and outnumber police personnel.

### Issues and reforms

- Prakash Singh v Union of India (2006): seven directives — State Security Commission, minimum two-year tenure for DGP and key officers, separate investigation and law-and-order wings, Police Establishment Board, Police Complaints Authority, National Security Commission for CAPF chiefs; compliance remains partial.
- Model Police Act 2006 (Soli Sorabjee Committee), Padmanabhaiah Committee (2000) and the 2nd ARC report on public order (2007) proposed similar reforms.
- CAPF concerns: long deployments, stress and suicides, slow promotions, limited leave; the Supreme Court (May 2025; review dismissed October 2025) asked the government to progressively cut IPS deputation to senior CAPF posts to improve cadre officers' careers.
- Modernisation: Assistance to States for Modernisation of Police, CCTNS (2009) and the Inter-operable Criminal Justice System link police, courts, prisons and forensics.
- New criminal laws (BNS, BNSS, BSA) in force from 1 July 2024 require forensic visits for serious crimes and time-bound investigation.
- Police–population ratio remains below the UN norm, with large vacancies in many states.

### Coordination lessons from incidents

- Kargil Review Committee (1999, K. Subrahmanyam) → Group of Ministers (2001) → MAC, DIA, border management reforms.
- 26/11 (2008) → NIA, NSG hubs, coastal security scheme, Coast Guard as coastal lead.
- Pathankot airbase attack (January 2016, Punjab) → questions on NSG–Army–Air Force–Punjab Police command and perimeter security.
- Naresh Chandra Task Force (2012) and Shekatkar Committee (2016) on defence reforms → CDS and jointness.
- Pahalgam (April 2025) and Operation Sindoor (May 2025) tested civil–military coordination and drone defence across Punjab and J&K.
- Lesson: clear lead agency, shared databases, joint training and standard operating procedures matter more than adding new agencies.

### Punjab's security forces map

- BSF Punjab Frontier (headquarters at Jalandhar) guards the 553 km border; its 50 km jurisdiction since 2021 overlaps with Punjab Police.
- Indian Army's Western Command (Chandimandir) and formations along Punjab; major IAF bases at Adampur, Halwara, Bathinda and Pathankot.
- Punjab Police units: State Special Operation Cell (counter-terror), Anti-Gangster Task Force (2022), Special Task Force on drugs (2017), replaced by the Anti-Narcotics Task Force (August 2024), Counter Intelligence wings, State Cyber Crime division.
- 'Baaz Akh' anti-drone systems (2025) give Punjab Police its own drone-interdiction layer behind the BSF.
- Coordination forums: joint BSF–Punjab Police operations, NCORD meetings on drugs, MAC subsidiary centre.

### Answer angles

- Group forces by function: border guarding, internal security, specialised (NSG, NDRF), intelligence, investigation.
- Give raising year and current mandate for each — examiners reward precision.
- Discuss issues (overuse of CAPFs, coordination, police reform) with judgments and committees.
- Use Pathankot 2016 and Punjab's drone problem as the state case.
- Conclude with reforms: police modernisation, intelligence fusion, clear SOPs, welfare of personnel.
- Trap: Assam Rifles is under MHA but operationally under the Army; the Coast Guard is under MoD, not MHA.
