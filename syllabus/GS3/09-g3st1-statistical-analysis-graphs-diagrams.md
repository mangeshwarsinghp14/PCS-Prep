## Short notes

- Arithmetic mean = sum of values / number of values; weighted mean = sum(w x X) / sum(w). Median = middle value of ordered data (average of two middle values if n is even). Mode = most frequent value.
- Percentage change = (New - Old) / Old x 100. Percentage points (pp) = simple difference between two percentages (e.g. 40% to 45% is +5 pp but +12.5%).
- Share (%) = Part / Total x 100. Ratio a:b reduced to lowest terms; per capita = Total / Population.
- CAGR = (End value / Start value)^(1/n) - 1, where n = number of years between the two values (not number of data points).
- Index number: Index = (Current value / Base value) x 100; Laspeyres uses base-year weights, Paasche uses current-year weights.
- Range = Max - Min; Standard deviation measures spread; Coefficient of Variation = SD / Mean x 100 (lower CV means more stable/consistent series).
- Correlation coefficient r lies between -1 and +1; correlation does not prove causation.
- Labour statistics: LFPR = Labour force / Population x 100; Unemployment rate = Unemployed / Labour force x 100; WPR = Employed / Population x 100.
- Pie chart: each 1% = 3.6 degrees; central angle = share x 3.6. Bar chart compares categories; stacked bar shows composition; line graph shows trends over time; histogram shows frequency distribution of continuous data; scatter shows relationship.
- Chart-reading method step 1: read title, units (crore, lakh, %, tonnes), base year, source and note whether values are nominal or real.
- Step 2: check axes - starting point (truncated axes exaggerate change), scale (linear vs log), and legend.
- Step 3: identify highest/lowest, trend direction, turning points, outliers and the sum/total check.
- Step 4: compute only what is asked - change, share, growth, ratio - showing working in one line each.
- Step 5: conclude in plain words: what changed, by how much, possible reason, and one caveat (base effect, small sample, nominal vs real).
- Common traps: confusing % with pp, ignoring base effect (high growth on low base), averaging percentages without weights, reading cumulative vs annual figures.
- Answer-writing tip: present derived data as a short text table, then 3-4 bullet conclusions and one policy implication.

## Deep dive

### Core calculations — worked on real Indian data

- Percentage points vs per cent: CPI inflation rose from 4.83% (August 2026) to 5.54% (September 2026) → +0.71 percentage points, but a relative rise of 0.71 ÷ 4.83 = 14.7%.
- Base effect: wheat MSP rose by ₹160 to ₹2,585 (RMS 2026-27) = 160 ÷ 2,425 = 6.6%; then by ₹25 to ₹2,610 (RMS 2027-28) = 25 ÷ 2,585 = 0.97%.
- CAGR = (End ÷ Start)^(1/n) − 1. Foodgrains: 251.6 MT (2015-16) → 357.7 MT (2024-25), n = 9 years → (357.7 ÷ 251.6)^(1/9) − 1 ≈ 4.0% a year.
- CAGR with fractional years: renewable capacity 76.38 GW (March 2014) → 253.96 GW (November 2025); ratio 3.325 over 11.67 years → about 10.9% a year.
- Share: Punjab wheat 17.99 MT of India's 117.9 MT (2024-25) = 15.3% — matches the Survey's 15.25% (rounding).
- Implicit deflator: nominal growth 8.9%, real growth 7.7% (2025-26) → (1.089 ÷ 1.077) − 1 ≈ 1.1% — explains why low inflation squeezed tax collections.
- Weighted average reasoning: male LFPR 79.1%, female 40.0%, overall 59.3% (PLFS 2025) → female share of the 15+ population w solves 79.1(1 − w) + 40.0w = 59.3 → w ≈ 0.51.
- Derived rate: unemployment rate = (LFPR − WPR) ÷ LFPR × 100 = (59.3 − 57.4) ÷ 59.3 ≈ 3.2% (reported 3.1% — difference due to rounding of inputs).
- Coverage ratio: services surplus US$ 213.9 bn ÷ merchandise deficit US$ 333.2 bn (2025-26) ≈ 64% of the goods gap covered.

### Pie charts, bar charts and composition — worked

- Pie angle = share (%) × 3.6°. Punjab committed expenditure 2026-27 = ₹90,335 crore: salaries ₹39,115 crore (43.3% → 155.9°), interest ₹28,755 crore (31.8% → 114.6°), pensions ₹22,465 crore (24.9% → 89.5°). Check: angles add to 360°.
- Shares of a pie cannot be compared across two pies of different totals without the totals — always note the base.
- Stacked bars show composition; clustered bars compare categories; line charts show trends; use a line only for ordered time data.
- Histogram (continuous classes, no gaps) is different from a bar chart (discrete categories, gaps).
- Dual-axis charts can mislead — check which series uses which axis before comparing slopes.
- Truncated y-axis exaggerates change; log scale makes equal percentage changes look equal.
- Per capita vs total: a state can rank high on total output but low per person (e.g. Uttar Pradesh in foodgrains vs per capita income).

### Index numbers — worked example and official indices

- Example: base year wheat ₹20 (10 units), milk ₹50 (4 units); current year wheat ₹24 (12 units), milk ₹55 (5 units).
- Laspeyres (base-quantity weights) = Σp1q0 ÷ Σp0q0 × 100 = (240 + 220) ÷ (200 + 200) × 100 = 115.0.
- Paasche (current-quantity weights) = Σp1q1 ÷ Σp0q1 × 100 = (288 + 275) ÷ (240 + 250) × 100 = 114.9.
- Fisher's ideal index = √(Laspeyres × Paasche) = √(115.0 × 114.9) ≈ 114.95; it satisfies the time-reversal and factor-reversal tests.
- Laspeyres tends to overstate inflation (ignores substitution); Paasche tends to understate it.
- CPI (new series, base 2024) uses a Laspeyres-type fixed basket from household consumption surveys; food now has a lower weight than in the 2012 series.
- GDP base moved to 2022-23 (released February 2026) with double deflation in manufacturing and agriculture.
- Rebasing changes levels and weights, so old and new series must be linked (back series) before comparing growth.

### Budget arithmetic — worked (Union Budget 2026-27, ₹ crore)

- Fiscal deficit = total expenditure − (revenue receipts + recovery of loans + other non-debt receipts) = 53,47,315 − (35,33,150 + 38,397 + 80,000) = 16,95,768.
- Primary deficit = fiscal deficit − interest = 16,95,768 − 14,03,972 = 2,91,796 (0.7% of GDP).
- Effective revenue deficit = revenue deficit − grants for creation of capital assets = 5,92,344 − 4,92,702 = 99,642 (0.3%).
- Interest as share of revenue receipts = 14,03,972 ÷ 35,33,150 = 39.7%.
- Implied GDP: 16,95,768 ÷ 0.043 ≈ ₹394 lakh crore; because 4.3% is rounded, the true base could lie roughly between ₹390 and ₹399 lakh crore — beware false precision.
- Effective capex = capex + grants for capital assets = 12,21,821 + 4,92,702 = 17,14,523.
- Always state units (crore vs lakh crore; ₹ vs US$) and whether figures are BE, RE or actuals.

### Central tendency and dispersion — worked on state unemployment data

- Data: CWS unemployment rate, April–June 2025, for 22 states/UTs (Economic Survey 2025-26 Table 8.12): lowest Gujarat 2.2%, highest Punjab 8.8%.
- Arranged in order, the 11th and 12th values are 5.7 and 5.8 → median = 5.75%.
- Simple mean of the 22 state rates = 5.67%; it differs from the all-India 5.4% because the all-India rate is weighted by each state's labour force.
- Mode: 6.5 and 6.9 each occur twice → the data are bimodal; mode is rarely useful for such data.
- Range = 8.8 − 2.2 = 6.6 percentage points; standard deviation and coefficient of variation (SD ÷ mean × 100) are better for comparing spread across years.
- Quartiles and percentiles locate a state's relative position (e.g. Punjab is in the top quartile).
- Never average percentages of unequal bases without weights.

### Data sources and release calendar (what to cite)

- GDP: quarterly estimates at the end of the second month after the quarter (e.g. Q1 on 31 August); annual Provisional Estimates at end-May/early June; Advance Estimates in January and February.
- CPI: monthly, around the 12th; WPI: around the 14th; IIP: monthly, near the end of the following month.
- PLFS: monthly bulletin (CWS) around the 15th, quarterly bulletins, and an annual report (calendar year from 2025).
- Budget data: Budget at a Glance, Receipt and Expenditure Budgets; RBI's State Finances study for states.
- Agriculture: Ministry of Agriculture advance estimates; Economic Survey Statistical Appendix tables.
- Social: NFHS (health), UDISE+ (schools), ASER (learning, NGO), Census 2011 (Census 2027 due).
- Punjab: Economic and Statistical Organisation (Statistical Abstract, Economic Survey of Punjab) and PRS state budget analysis.

### Interpreting and writing conclusions (method and traps)

- Read title, units, base year, source, period and whether values are nominal or real before any calculation.
- Identify the story: trend, turning points, outliers, gaps between groups (rural/urban, male/female, states).
- Compute only the 2–3 numbers the question needs; show each in one line.
- Conclude with cause (hedged), consequence and policy implication; add one caveat (sample change, rounding, base effect).
- Traps: % vs pp; correlation vs causation; cumulative vs annual; per capita vs total; comparing series across a methodological break (e.g. PLFS 2025 redesign).
- Simpson's paradox: aggregate trends can reverse within subgroups (e.g. overall rate falls while every state's rises because of changing weights).
- PPSC answer format: a short derived table, 3–4 interpretive bullets, one Punjab comparison, one recommendation.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** Worked DI 1 (hypothetical data). Wheat in State X. Table: Year | Area (lakh ha) | Production (lakh tonnes) -- 2020-21 | 35.0 | 175.0 ; 2021-22 | 35.0 | 150.5 ; 2022-23 | 34.5 | 165.6 ; 2023-24 | 35.0 | 182.0. (a) Find yield for each year. (b) % change in production from 2020-21 to 2021-22 and from 2022-23 to 2023-24. (c) Average production. (d) CAGR of production 2020-21 to 2023-24. (e) What does the data suggest?

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Yield = Production / Area: 2020-21 = 175.0/35.0 = 5.0 t/ha; 2021-22 = 150.5/35.0 = 4.3; 2022-23 = 165.6/34.5 = 4.8; 2023-24 = 182.0/35.0 = 5.2 t/ha.
- Change 2020-21 to 2021-22 = (150.5 - 175.0)/175.0 x 100 = -24.5/175 x 100 = -14.0%.
- Change 2022-23 to 2023-24 = (182.0 - 165.6)/165.6 x 100 = 16.4/165.6 x 100 = about +9.9%.
- Average production = (175.0 + 150.5 + 165.6 + 182.0)/4 = 673.1/4 = about 168.3 lakh tonnes.
- CAGR over n = 3 years = (182.0/175.0)^(1/3) - 1 = (1.04)^(1/3) - 1 = about 1.3% per year.
- Interpretation: area is almost constant, so production swings are driven by yield; the 2021-22 dip (yield 4.3) points to a weather shock such as terminal heat.
- Conclusion: long-term growth is slow (about 1.3% a year); policy should target heat-tolerant varieties and timely sowing rather than area expansion.

</details>

**2.** Worked DI 2 (hypothetical data). A state's revenue and capital spending in a year totals ₹2,00,000 crore and is shown as a pie chart with shares: Salaries 25%, Pensions 12%, Interest 15%, Power subsidy 10%, Capital expenditure 12%, Other revenue spending 26%. (a) Find committed expenditure (salaries + pensions + interest) in rupees. (b) Central angle of interest. (c) Ratio of capital expenditure to interest. (d) If next year total spending rises 10% and interest rises 20%, what is the new share of interest and the change in percentage points? (e) Interpret.

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Check total: 25 + 12 + 15 + 10 + 12 + 26 = 100%.
- Committed share = 25 + 12 + 15 = 52%; amount = 0.52 x 2,00,000 = ₹1,04,000 crore.
- Central angle of interest = 15 x 3.6 = 54 degrees.
- Capex : Interest = 12 : 15 = 4 : 5 (₹24,000 crore vs ₹30,000 crore).
- Next year: new interest = 30,000 x 1.2 = ₹36,000 crore; new total = 2,20,000; share = 36,000/2,20,000 x 100 = about 16.4%, a rise of about 1.4 percentage points (not 1.4%).
- Interpretation: more than half of spending is pre-committed and interest outgo exceeds capex, indicating fiscal rigidity and debt stress.
- Policy implication: contain debt growth, rationalise subsidies, protect capex to support growth.

</details>

**3.** Worked DI 3 (hypothetical data). State P labour data (in lakh persons). Table: Item | 2018 | 2024 -- Population aged 15+ | 200 | 220 ; Labour force | 105 | 128 ; Employed in Agriculture | 40 | 38 ; Employed in Industry | 25 | 30 ; Employed in Services | 35 | 52. (a) Compute LFPR and unemployment rate for both years. (b) Sectoral shares of employment and change in pp. (c) Growth of employment by sector. (d) Share of services in net job addition. (e) Interpret why unemployment rose even as employment grew.

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Total employed: 2018 = 40 + 25 + 35 = 100 lakh; 2024 = 38 + 30 + 52 = 120 lakh. Unemployed = labour force minus employed: 5 lakh (2018), 8 lakh (2024).
- LFPR: 2018 = 105/200 x 100 = 52.5%; 2024 = 128/220 x 100 = about 58.2%. Unemployment rate: 2018 = 5/105 x 100 = about 4.8%; 2024 = 8/128 x 100 = 6.25%.
- Shares 2018: agriculture 40%, industry 25%, services 35%. Shares 2024: agriculture 38/120 = about 31.7%, industry 30/120 = 25%, services 52/120 = about 43.3%. Change: agriculture -8.3 pp, industry 0 pp, services +8.3 pp.
- Growth 2018-24: agriculture (38-40)/40 = -5%; industry (30-25)/25 = +20%; services (52-35)/35 = about +48.6%; total (120-100)/100 = +20%.
- Net addition = 20 lakh jobs, of which services 17 lakh = 85%; agriculture shed 2 lakh, industry added 5 lakh.
- Interpretation: labour force grew 23 lakh (about 21.9%), faster than jobs (20 lakh, 20%), so unemployment rate rose despite job growth - rising participation (possibly women and youth) outpaced job creation.
- Conclusion: structural shift away from farming is under way but led by services; industry's share is stagnant, so labour-intensive manufacturing policy is needed.

</details>
