## Short notes

- Punjab economy pillars: high-productivity agriculture, MSME-based industry, and a growing services sector (Economic Survey 2025-26: agriculture 23%, industry 29%, services 48% of GSVA).
- Economic Survey 2025-26: real GSDP growth 6.1%; agriculture contracted 2.5% due to the 2025 floods; per capita income ₹2,30,523.
- Green Revolution (from mid-1960s) made Punjab India's granary; state led India in per capita income for long periods before slipping behind faster-growing states since the 1990s.
- Developmental planning: state plans prepared earlier with the Planning Commission; now through budget, sector policies, district plans and SDG localisation; Punjab State Planning Board advises.
- Planning concerns: over-dependence on wheat-paddy, groundwater depletion, stagnating farm incomes, fiscal stress, regional imbalance (Kandi area, border belt, south-west waterlogged belt).
- Industry profile: dominated by MSMEs; hosiery, bicycles and auto parts (Ludhiana), sports goods and hand tools (Jalandhar), steel rolling (Mandi Gobindgarh), machine tools/foundry (Batala), textiles (Amritsar), IT and electronics (Mohali).
- Constraints on industry: landlocked and far from ports, border-state risk perception, militancy-era disinvestment (1980s-early 1990s), incentives to neighbouring hill states, power and logistics costs.
- Industrial policy: Invest Punjab single-window, Punjab Right to Business Act 2020 (fast approvals for MSMEs), Industrial and Business Development Policy 2022.
- Infrastructure strengths: dense rural road network, near-universal village electrification, canal network, mandi network, two international airports.
- Infrastructure gaps: power sector finances strained by subsidy, urban infrastructure and waste management, cold chain, industrial parks, groundwater-dependent irrigation.
- Connectivity projects: Eastern DFC terminal near Ludhiana, Delhi-Amritsar-Katra Expressway, Amritsar-Kolkata Industrial Corridor concept.
- Fiscal position: high debt-GSDP ratio (above 40%), committed expenditure crowding out capex; Budget 2026-27 outlay ₹2,60,437 crore, fiscal deficit about 4.1% of GSDP.
- Human development: good on many indicators but challenged by drugs, emigration of youth, and declining sex ratio in earlier decades (improving since).
- Diversification strategy: horticulture, dairy, fisheries, food processing, agri-exports (basmati, kinnow), renewable energy (solar, biomass, compressed biogas from paddy straw).
- Services potential: education, health, IT-enabled services in the Mohali-Chandigarh region, tourism (Amritsar, Anandpur Sahib, heritage).
- Way forward: crop diversification with assured markets, power sector reform, fiscal consolidation, logistics hubs, skilling, NRI investment channels.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab has 23 districts (Malerkotla formed in 2021), 117 assembly seats, 13 Lok Sabha and 7 Rajya Sabha seats.
- 2025 floods hit agriculture output and rural infrastructure, showing climate vulnerability of the state's economy.
- Ludhiana is a major hub for bicycles, hosiery and auto components; Mandi Gobindgarh for re-rolling mills.
- Mukh Mantri Mawan Dhian Satikar Yojana and free household power illustrate welfare commitments shaping fiscal planning.
- Johl Committee recommendations on diversifying a part of paddy area remain a reference point for state planning.

## How PPSC asks it

PPSC asks directly about Punjab's development model, industrial stagnation, fiscal crisis and infrastructure; answers must use state data (Economic Survey 2025-26, Budget 2026-27), regions, clusters and a realistic reform roadmap.

## Deep dive

### Development planning in Punjab — timeline (year — event)

- 1947 — Partition: West Punjab's canal colonies and Lahore's industry go to Pakistan; East Punjab rebuilds with refugee resettlement and land consolidation (consolidation of holdings largely completed by the 1960s).
- 1950s — Bhakra-Nangal project; Bhakra dam completed 1963 (Nehru's 'temples of modern India'); Punjab Agricultural University, Ludhiana set up 1962.
- 1966 — Reorganisation of Punjab (1 November); Green Revolution begins with high-yielding wheat, assured irrigation, fertiliser and minimum support price with assured procurement.
- 1970s–80s — Punjab leads India in per capita income; Pong (Beas) dam (1974); first Johl Committee report on diversification (1986).
- 1980s–early 1990s — militancy hits investment, especially industry; capital and entrepreneurs move to neighbouring states.
- 1997 — free electricity for agriculture introduced; 2002 — second Johl Committee recommends moving about one million hectares out of the wheat–paddy rotation.
- 2003 — central tax incentives for Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand and J&K draw industry across Punjab's borders.
- 2009 — Punjab Preservation of Subsoil Water Act delays paddy transplanting to save groundwater.
- 2010 — Punjab State Electricity Board unbundled into PSPCL (generation/distribution) and PSTCL (transmission).
- 2015 onwards — planning shifts from Planning Commission plans to NITI Aayog, state budgets, sector policies and SDG targets; Punjab State Planning Board and District Planning Committees coordinate.
- 2022–26 — Industrial and Business Development Policy 2022; free 300 units/month for households (from July 2022); Goindwal Sahib 540 MW thermal plant bought by the state (January 2024, Rs 1,080 crore); 2025 floods; Budget 2026-27 with capital outlay of Rs 18,381 crore (up 76%).

### Fiscal profile that constrains planning (Budget 2026-27, PRS)

- GSDP 2026-27 projected at Rs 9,80,635 crore (current prices), 10% nominal growth; real growth 2025-26 estimated at 6.1% against India's 7.4%.
- Revenue deficit 2.2% and fiscal deficit 4.1% of GSDP (2026-27 BE); 2024-25 actual fiscal deficit was 4.7%, well above the 3.8% budgeted.
- Outstanding liabilities about 45.1% of GSDP at end 2026-27 — among the highest of major states; outstanding guarantees Rs 23,654 crore (2.7% of GSDP).
- Committed expenditure Rs 90,335 crore = 72% of revenue receipts (salaries 31%, pensions 18%, interest 23%); in 2024-25 it was 85%.
- Power subsidy Rs 15,550 crore (12.3% of revenue receipts): agriculture Rs 7,715 crore, households Rs 5,771 crore, industry Rs 2,064 crore; grew about 12% a year from 2018-19 to 2025-26.
- A study for the 16th Finance Commission found 75% of Punjab's highest-spending households receive free electricity — second highest among states studied (targeting problem).
- State PSUs: 49 SPSEs as of March 2023 (16 inactive); 18 loss-making, losses concentrated in PSPCL, the grains procurement corporation and Punjab Agro Foodgrains (CAG via PRS).
- 16th Finance Commission recommends a 3% of GSDP fiscal-deficit limit for states for 2026–31 (50-year interest-free capex loans excluded); NITI Fiscal Health Index 2025 ranked Punjab last of 18 major states.

### Industry — structure, clusters and policy

- Structure: dominated by MSMEs; few large units — e.g. the HPCL-Mittal Guru Gobind Singh refinery at Bathinda (commissioned 2012), fertiliser, auto-component, tractor and textile majors.
- Clusters: Ludhiana (hosiery, bicycles and parts, sewing machines, auto parts), Jalandhar (sports goods, hand tools, rubber goods, leather), Mandi Gobindgarh (steel re-rolling), Batala (castings, machine tools), Amritsar (textiles, food), Mohali (IT, electronics, pharma), Phagwara and Rajpura (food, engineering).
- Agro-processing: rice milling, dairy (Verka brand of Milkfed), sugar mills, cotton ginning, food parks — the natural partner of crop diversification.
- Constraints: landlocked and far from ports, border-state perception, higher land costs, power-cost cross-subsidy for industry, skill gaps, and investment diversion to hill states after 2003.
- Opportunities: food processing and agri-exports (basmati, kinnow, potato seed), compressed biogas and biomass power from paddy straw, textiles and apparel, electronics and IT around Mohali.
- Data discipline: quote Punjab's manufacturing share, growth and credit indicators from the RBI Handbook or Economic Survey with the year; avoid unsourced 'decline' claims.

### Infrastructure — power, water, transport, urban

- Hydel and storage: Bhakra (Sutlej, 1963) and Pong (Beas, 1974) under the Bhakra Beas Management Board; Ranjit Sagar (Thein) dam on the Ravi (2000); Shahpur Kandi barrage completed in 2024, ending Ravi water flowing unused to Pakistan.
- Thermal: state plants at Ropar (Guru Gobind Singh STP) and Lehra Mohabbat (Guru Hargobind TP); Bathinda's Guru Nanak Dev TP closed in 2018; private plants at Talwandi Sabo and Rajpura; Goindwal Sahib plant now state-owned.
- Canals: Bhakra Main Line, Sirhind Canal, Upper Bari Doab Canal, Rajasthan feeder; ageing channels and seepage reduce delivery to tail-end farms.
- Groundwater: most blocks over-exploited under Central Ground Water Board assessments; free power removes the price signal on pumping.
- Transport: dense rural road network; Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor begins near Ludhiana (Sahnewal); Delhi–Amritsar–Katra Expressway under Bharatmala; Integrated Check Post at Attari for trade with Pakistan and Afghanistan (trade largely suspended).
- Air: international airports at Amritsar and Mohali; domestic airports at Bathinda, Adampur (Jalandhar), Pathankot and Ludhiana.
- Urban: solid-waste and sewage treatment gaps (Buddha Nullah in Ludhiana is the best-known pollution case), smart city projects in Ludhiana, Jalandhar and Amritsar, urban flooding.
- Storage and markets: dense mandi network and FCI/state godowns for wheat and rice; need for cold chains and modern silos for fruit, vegetables and diversification.

### Agriculture–industry–services linkages (for 15-mark answers)

- 2025-26 sector shares (current prices): agriculture 23%, industry 29%, services 48%; agriculture output fell 2.5% after the 2025 floods.
- Per capita income Rs 2,30,523 (2025-26) — above India's Rs 2,19,575 but the gap has narrowed sharply since the 1990s.
- Wheat–paddy lock-in: assured MSP procurement for two crops, free power and canal water make them less risky than alternatives — diversification needs assured markets for maize, pulses, cotton and horticulture.
- Budget 2026-27 announces a JICA-backed programme to raise acreage under fruits and vegetables by 300% by 2035 — a test case for diversification policy.
- Rural non-farm jobs are thin, so youth migrate abroad; skilling and agro-processing jobs are the bridge.
- Services growth is concentrated in the Mohali–Chandigarh belt and Ludhiana; education, health, tourism (Amritsar's Golden Temple and Wagah, Anandpur Sahib) and logistics are scalable.

### Debates, data traps and PPSC angles

- Debate: free power to farms — equity and farm incomes vs groundwater, fiscal cost and poor targeting; options: direct benefit transfer, metering with credit, solar pumps, separating feeders.
- Debate: MSP-led procurement — stability for farmers vs crop monoculture; options: price-deficiency payments, procurement of alternative crops, crop insurance.
- Debate: industrial incentives — tax subsidies vs fixing power cost, land, logistics and skills.
- Trap: GSDP at current vs constant prices; growth rates in the Economic Survey are at constant (2011-12) prices unless stated.
- Trap: debt-to-GSDP (stock) vs fiscal deficit (annual flow); revenue deficit vs fiscal deficit.
- Trap: PSPCL (power generation and distribution) vs PSTCL (transmission) vs BBMB (central board for Bhakra–Beas assets shared by Punjab, Haryana, Rajasthan, Himachal and Chandigarh).
- Typical Mains questions: 'Examine the reasons for the slowdown of Punjab's industrial growth since the 1990s'; 'Discuss fiscal stress in Punjab and suggest reforms'; 'Assess infrastructure gaps that limit agricultural diversification'.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** Punjab, once the most prosperous state, has seen relative economic decline. Analyse the causes and suggest a development strategy for the state. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: Green Revolution success and later slowdown.
- Causes: agricultural plateau, wheat-paddy lock, slow industrialisation, militancy-era losses, fiscal stress, brain drain.
- Data: Economic Survey 2025-26 growth and sector shares; debt levels.
- Strategy: diversification, agro-processing, MSME upgrading, power reform, logistics, skilling, fiscal consolidation.
- Conclusion: from granary to diversified knowledge-agro economy.
