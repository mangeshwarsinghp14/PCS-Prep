## Short notes

- Cyber security protects confidentiality, integrity and availability (CIA triad) of information systems, networks and data.
- Threats: malware, ransomware (WannaCry 2017; AIIMS Delhi attack 2022), phishing, DDoS, advanced persistent threats, supply-chain attacks, zero-day exploits, data breaches, deepfake-based fraud.
- Critical information infrastructure (power grids, banking, telecom, transport) is a strategic target in hybrid warfare.
- Legal framework: IT Act 2000 (Section 43, 66 offences; 70 protected systems; 70A NCIIPC; 70B CERT-In), DPDP Act 2023, Telecommunications Act 2023.
- Institutions: CERT-In (national nodal agency for incident response), NCIIPC (under NTRO), I4C (2018, MHA), National Cyber Security Coordinator in the NSCS, Defence Cyber Agency.
- National Cyber Security Policy 2013; CERT-In 2022 directions require reporting of cyber incidents within six hours; National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal and helpline 1930 for financial fraud.
- Global: Budapest Convention (India not a party); UN Convention against Cybercrime adopted by the UN General Assembly in 2024.
- Gaps: shortage of skilled professionals, dependence on imported hardware, low awareness, fragmented agencies, weak state-level cyber policing.
- Money laundering: process of making illegal proceeds appear legitimate - placement, layering, integration.
- Channels: hawala, shell companies, trade-based laundering (over/under-invoicing), real estate, crypto assets, casinos and gaming, round-tripping through tax havens.
- Prevention of Money Laundering Act 2002 (in force 2005): Enforcement Directorate investigates and attaches property; FIU-IND (2004) receives suspicious transaction reports.
- Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v Union of India (2022): Supreme Court upheld key PMLA provisions including ED's powers of arrest and reverse burden of proof.
- Related laws: Fugitive Economic Offenders Act 2018, Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income and Assets) Act 2015, Benami Transactions Amendment 2016; virtual digital asset service providers brought under PMLA in 2023.
- FATF (1989) sets AML/CFT standards; India became a member in 2010; FATF's 2024 mutual evaluation placed India in 'regular follow-up', the best category.
- Way forward: beneficial ownership registry, data analytics across agencies, crypto regulation, faster trials, capacity building of state police, public awareness on digital fraud.

## Punjab angle

- Drug trade proceeds in Punjab are laundered through hawala, property and cash-intensive businesses; ED and state agencies have pursued narcotics-linked money laundering cases.
- Immigration and travel-agent frauds are common; the Punjab Prevention of Human Smuggling Act 2012 (renamed Punjab Travel Professionals (Regulation) Act) regulates agents.
- Punjab Police has a State Cyber Crime division and district cyber cells to handle rising online financial fraud.
- Remittance-heavy NRI districts face risks of illegal money transfer channels.

## Deep dive

### Cyber threat landscape (2024–26)

- 'Digital arrest' scams: fraudsters pose as police/CBI/ED officers on video calls and extort money — flagged nationally by the Prime Minister in 2024 and taken up by the Supreme Court.
- Investment and trading scams, fake loan apps, sextortion and job/task frauds run from scam compounds in South-East Asia using trafficked workers.
- Mule bank accounts and SIM cards rented from poor or young people move fraud proceeds — a link between cybercrime and money laundering.
- AI voice-cloning and deepfake videos used for impersonation and misinformation.
- Ransomware against hospitals, government departments and MSMEs (e.g. AIIMS Delhi, 2022); data leaks of large citizen databases.
- State-linked actors target critical information infrastructure; waves of DDoS and defacement attacks accompanied the May 2025 India–Pakistan conflict.
- Supply-chain risk: dependence on imported telecom gear, chips and software; 'trusted source' rules for telecom (2021) address part of it.

### Institutional architecture

- CERT-In (Section 70B, IT Act): national incident response; its April 2022 directions require reporting of incidents within 6 hours and logs to be kept for 180 days.
- NCIIPC (Section 70A, under NTRO): protects critical information infrastructure such as power, banking, telecom and transport.
- Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C, MHA): National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal, helpline 1930 and the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting and Management System for freezing stolen money fast.
- I4C tools: Samanvaya platform (inter-state linkages of cases), 'Pratibimb' mapping of crime hotspots, Suspect Registry (2024) shared with banks.
- RBI's MuleHunter.AI (2024) helps banks detect mule accounts; DoT's Sanchar Saathi and CEIR block fraudulent connections and stolen phones.
- National Cyber Security Coordinator in the National Security Council Secretariat; Defence Cyber Agency (2019) for the armed forces.
- Cyber Swachhta Kendra (botnet cleaning and malware analysis) and the Cyber Commandos programme for state police capacity.
- Gaps: shortage of trained investigators in state police, slow cross-border data requests, low conviction rates.

### Legal framework

- IT Act 2000: Section 43 (damage to systems, civil), 66 (computer offences), 66C (identity theft), 66D (cheating by personation), 66F (cyber terrorism, up to life imprisonment), 67 (obscene content).
- Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023: Section 111 makes organised crime — including organised cybercrime and economic offences — a specific offence.
- Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023 treats electronic records as primary evidence; BNSS allows e-FIRs and electronic summons.
- DPDP Act 2023: Rules notified in November 2025 with a 12–18 month phased roll-out; provisions establishing the Data Protection Board in force at once; penalties up to ₹250 crore per breach.
- Telecommunications Act 2023 (interception, SIM verification) and Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025 (ban on real-money games).
- National Cyber Security Policy 2013 remains the policy base; a new national cyber security strategy has long been under preparation.
- International: India is not a party to the Budapest Convention (2001); the UN adopted its first Convention against Cybercrime in December 2024.

### Money laundering — stages and modern channels

- Stages: placement (cash into the system), layering (complex transfers to hide origin), integration (funds return as 'clean' assets).
- Trade-based laundering: over- and under-invoicing of imports and exports, round-tripping through tax havens.
- Shell companies and nominee directors; benami property; cash-intensive businesses (hotels, real estate, jewellery).
- Hawala: informal value transfer without money movement — used by drug, terror and immigration-fraud networks.
- Crypto-assets and peer-to-peer exchanges; virtual digital asset service providers were brought under PMLA in March 2023.
- Online betting and gaming apps (e.g. the Mahadev app case, 2023) moved thousands of crores through mule accounts and hawala.
- Cyber-fraud proceeds are layered through thousands of mule accounts within minutes, then cashed out via crypto or hawala.

### Prevention framework — laws, agencies, judgments

- PMLA 2002 (in force 1 July 2005): reporting entities must do KYC, keep records and file reports with FIU-IND; ED investigates, attaches and confiscates proceeds of crime.
- FIU-IND (2004) receives Cash Transaction Reports (above ₹10 lakh), Suspicious Transaction Reports and cross-border wire reports.
- Amendments: 2019 (money laundering as a continuing, stand-alone offence), 2023 (VDA providers; chartered accountants, company secretaries and cost accountants when handling client money).
- Vijay Madanlal Choudhary (2022): Supreme Court upheld ED's arrest, search and attachment powers and the twin bail conditions; a review is pending.
- Allied laws: Fugitive Economic Offenders Act 2018; Black Money Act 2015; Benami Amendment Act 2016 (its retrospective criminal provisions were struck down in Ganpati Dealcom, 2022).
- Beneficial ownership: Companies (Significant Beneficial Owners) Rules 2018 require disclosure of real owners.
- FATF: India a member since 2010; the June 2024 mutual evaluation placed India in 'regular follow-up', the top outcome, while flagging slow prosecutions.
- Concerns: low conviction rate in PMLA cases, long pre-trial detention, and alleged selective use — courts increasingly stress speedy trial and bail rights.

### Punjab — cyber and money-laundering issues

- Drug-trade money is laundered through property, hawala and cash businesses; ED pursued PMLA cases in major Punjab drug rackets (e.g. the Jagdish Bhola case).
- Under 'Yudh Nashian Virudh', illegal properties of drug traffickers have been demolished or attached, alongside NDPS forfeiture.
- Immigration fraud: 'dunki' routes and fake travel agents; US deportation flights to Amritsar (2025) exposed agent networks and hawala payments.
- NRI-heavy Doaba districts face risks of illegal remittance channels and property frauds.
- Gangster extortion calls from abroad use VoIP and virtual numbers, merging cybercrime with organised crime.
