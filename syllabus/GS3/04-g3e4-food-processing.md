## Short notes

- Food processing converts raw farm produce into higher-value, longer-lasting products; levels: primary (cleaning, grading), secondary (milling, pulping), tertiary (ready-to-eat).
- Significance: reduces post-harvest losses, raises farm incomes, creates labour-intensive jobs (including for women), boosts exports, links agriculture to industry.
- ICAR-CIPHET study (2015) estimated post-harvest losses of major produce at about ₹92,651 crore a year; perishable fruits, vegetables and fish suffer most.
- Share of produce processed in India is low compared with developed economies, especially for fruits and vegetables.
- Ministry of Food Processing Industries set up in 1988; food processing counted as a sunrise sector.
- PM Kisan SAMPADA Yojana (2017) umbrella: mega food parks, integrated cold chain, agro-processing clusters, backward-forward linkages, food testing labs.
- PLI Scheme for Food Processing Industry (2021) supports ready-to-cook/eat foods, processed fruits/vegetables, marine products, mozzarella, millet-based products and branding abroad.
- PM Formalisation of Micro Food Processing Enterprises (PMFME, 2020) supports micro units using One District One Product (ODOP) approach.
- Operation Greens (2018) for tomato, onion, potato (TOP), later extended to more perishables to stabilise prices.
- FDI: 100% under automatic route in food processing; 100% under government route for trading (including e-commerce) of food products made in India.
- Location factors: raw-material oriented (perishable, weight-losing - sugar, fruit pulping, dairy) vs market oriented (bakery, beverages); also power, water, labour, cold chain.
- Upstream requirements: quality raw material, contract farming/FPOs, seeds suited to processing, aggregation centres, pack houses.
- Downstream requirements: packaging, branding, cold storage, reefer transport, retail and export marketing, testing labs and certification.
- Supply chain management: farm-gate aggregation, pre-cooling, grading, cold chain, warehousing, logistics, demand forecasting; aims to cut intermediaries and wastage.
- Regulation: FSSAI under Food Safety and Standards Act 2006; APEDA and MPEDA promote exports; GST rates and labelling norms matter.
- Challenges: fragmented holdings, seasonality, weak cold chain, high cost of credit for MSMEs, quality inconsistency, skill shortages, inverted duty issues.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab hosts mega food parks such as the International Mega Food Park at Fazilka and the Sukhjit Mega Food Park in Kapurthala district.
- Fruit and vegetable clusters: kinnow (Abohar-Fazilka), potato seed and processing (Doaba region), peas and tomato contract farming with processors.
- Punjab Agro Industries Corporation and PAGREXCO promote processing and export; Verka (Milkfed) is a leading dairy processor.
- Processing is central to diversification away from wheat-paddy and to absorbing rural youth in non-farm jobs.

## Deep dive

### Scale and significance — latest data (Economic Survey 2025-26)

- Food processing accounts for 12.91% of employment in India's organised manufacturing — one of the largest employers among factory industries.
- Agri-food exports (including processed food) were US$ 49.43 billion in 2024-25, about 11.2% of India's total exports.
- Share of processed food in agri-food exports rose from 14.9% (2017-18) to 20.4% (2024-25) — a sign of value addition.
- PMKSY: 1,185 projects completed by 30 November 2025 (cold chains, agro-processing clusters, food parks, linkages).
- PMFME: by 31 December 2025, about 1.73 lakh loans (₹14,190 crore) sanctioned to micro units and seed capital of ₹1,277 crore for 3.66 lakh women SHG members.
- Post-harvest losses remain large for fruits, vegetables, fish and livestock produce (earlier ICAR-CIPHET and later NABCONS studies put them at well over ₹1 lakh crore a year).
- Significance for GS III: links farm and factory, absorbs rural labour (especially women), stabilises farm prices and earns foreign exchange.

### Policy architecture

- Ministry of Food Processing Industries (1988) is the nodal ministry; food processing is a 'sunrise' and priority-lending sector.
- PM Kisan SAMPADA Yojana (2017) umbrella: Integrated Cold Chain and Value Addition, Agro-Processing Clusters, Backward and Forward Linkages, Food Safety and Quality Assurance infrastructure, Operation Greens; earlier Mega Food Parks.
- Operation Greens (2018) began with tomato, onion and potato (TOP) and was widened to other perishables to stabilise prices.
- PMFME (2020): credit-linked subsidy for micro units under the One District One Product approach, with special support for SHGs, FPOs and cooperatives.
- GST rationalisation (September 2025) moved many packaged foods (dairy products, namkeen, sauces) to the 5% slab, cutting costs for processors.
- World Food India (2017, 2023 onward annually) is the government's investor showcase; Budget 2025-26 announced a food technology institute in Bihar and a Makhana Board.

### Location of industry — factors and examples

- Raw-material-oriented (perishable or weight-losing inputs): sugar mills in cane belts, tea factories in estates, milk plants in dairy districts, mango pulp in Chittoor–Krishnagiri, kinnow in Abohar.
- Market-oriented (bulky or perishable outputs): bakeries, bottling plants, ice cream units near large cities.
- Port-oriented: marine processing and seafood export units in Kerala, Andhra Pradesh and Gujarat.
- Infrastructure-oriented: reliable power and water (for processing and cleaning), roads and reefer logistics.
- Cluster logic: shared cold storage, testing labs and effluent treatment in food parks lower unit costs.
- Policy pull: state incentives (capital subsidy, power tariff concessions, mandi-fee exemptions) influence location as much as raw materials.

### Upstream and downstream requirements; supply chain

- Upstream: processing-grade varieties (e.g. Kufri Chipsona potatoes for chips, high-pulp tomatoes), uniform quality, contract farming and FPO aggregation, pack houses and pre-cooling at farm gate.
- Midstream: grading, sorting, ripening chambers, controlled-atmosphere storage, food-grade water and power.
- Downstream: packaging technology, branding, reefer transport, modern retail and e-commerce, export certification.
- Quality and safety: FSSAI standards (Food Safety and Standards Act 2006), NABL-accredited labs, traceability systems for exports (APEDA's GrapeNet/HortiNet).
- Cold chain: National Centre for Cold-chain Development (2011) promotes integrated chains; gaps are mainly in pack houses and reefer vehicles, not in cold stores alone.
- Finance: priority-sector lending, Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (3% interest subvention) and NABARD's food processing fund.
- Supply-chain management goal: shorter, aggregated chains with fewer intermediaries and lower wastage.

### Challenges

- Fragmented small holdings make consistent bulk supply hard; seasonality leaves plants idle part of the year.
- Most units are micro and informal, with poor access to credit, technology and brands.
- Inverted duty structures and frequent changes in export bans on raw produce disrupt contracts.
- Weak cold-chain integration and high logistics costs raise prices of processed goods.
- Low domestic demand for processed foods outside cities; consumer preference for fresh produce.
- Regulatory burden: multiple licences, labelling rules and food-safety compliance for small units.
- Health concerns: high salt-sugar-fat processed foods; front-of-pack labelling debate.

### Punjab — opportunities and cases

- International Mega Food Park at Dabwala Kalan (Fazilka) and Sukhjit Mega Food Park (Kapurthala) provide shared processing infrastructure.
- Kinnow belt of Abohar–Fazilka, potato seed and processing in the Doaba region, and peas, tomato and chilli under contract farming are the main horticulture–processing links.
- PepsiCo's Punjab project (from 1989) pioneered contract farming of potatoes and tomatoes in the state.
- Industrial and Business Development Policy 2026 gives an extra 25% incentive to food processing and agro-waste processing, plus a ₹7.5 crore capital subsidy for paddy-straw boilers.
- A JICA-backed 10-year horticulture programme (Budget 2026-27) targets a 300% rise in fruit and vegetable area by 2035 — feedstock for processing.
- Processing is Punjab's best route to diversify from wheat–paddy and to create non-farm rural jobs.

### Answer angles

- Structure answers as: scope → significance → location factors → upstream/downstream needs → supply chain → challenges → schemes → Punjab example.
- Use Economic Survey 2025-26 numbers (12.91% of organised manufacturing employment, processed food 20.4% of agri-food exports).
- Link to farmer income (price stabilisation via Operation Greens) and women's employment (PMFME SHG support).
- Mention food safety and nutrition, not just growth.
- Close with cluster-based, FPO-led processing and cold-chain integration as the way forward.
- Common slip: treating cold storage capacity as the whole cold chain — the real gaps are pack houses, pre-cooling and reefer transport.

**2.** What factors determine the location of food processing industries? Illustrate with examples from India. (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Raw-material orientation: perishability, weight loss (sugar mills, dairies, fruit pulping near orchards).
- Market orientation: bakery, beverages, packaged foods near cities.
- Infrastructure: power, water, cold chain, ports for exports (marine products in coastal states).
- Policy: food parks, ODOP clusters, incentives.
- Conclusion: cluster approach optimises both.

**3.** Examine the potential of food processing for crop diversification in Punjab. What supply chain bottlenecks need to be addressed? (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: wheat-paddy lock-in and need for diversification.
- Potential: kinnow, potato, maize-based starch/ethanol, vegetables, dairy, basmati exports.
- Bottlenecks: weak cold chain, price volatility, landlocked logistics, limited assured buyers, high power costs.
- Measures: FPO-processor tie-ups, cold storage, mega food park utilisation, export facilitation, assured procurement for alternative crops.
- Conclusion: processing-led diversification for jobs and sustainability.

</details>
