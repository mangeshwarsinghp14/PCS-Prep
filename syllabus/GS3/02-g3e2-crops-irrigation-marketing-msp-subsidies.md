## Short notes

- Crop seasons: kharif (sown with monsoon, e.g. rice, cotton, maize), rabi (winter, e.g. wheat, mustard, gram), zaid (short summer crops, e.g. moong, vegetables).
- Cropping pattern = proportion of area under different crops at a time; shaped by agro-climate, irrigation, MSP/procurement, technology and market signals.
- Irrigation sources: canals, tanks, wells and tubewells; groundwater (tubewells) now irrigates the largest share of net irrigated area in India.
- Irrigation systems: flood/furrow, sprinkler, drip; micro-irrigation saves water and fertiliser. PMKSY (2015): 'Har Khet Ko Pani' and 'Per Drop More Crop'.
- Storage: Warehousing (Development and Regulation) Act 2007 created WDRA; negotiable warehouse receipts enable post-harvest credit; FCI and CWC handle central storage.
- Marketing: state APMC Acts regulate mandis; problems include few markets, commission agents, high fees, information gaps. e-NAM launched April 2016 to link mandis online.
- Model Agricultural Produce and Livestock Marketing Act 2017 and Model Contract Farming Act 2018 offered reform templates; the three central farm laws of 2020 were repealed in 2021 after farmer protests.
- E-technology for farmers: Kisan Call Centres, Soil Health Card (2015), mKisan, e-NAM, Kisan Suvidha, drones (Namo Drone Didi), Digital Agriculture Mission with AgriStack (2024).
- Direct subsidies: PM-KISAN (₹6,000/year income support since 2019); indirect subsidies: fertiliser (urea, Nutrient Based Subsidy since 2010), power, irrigation water, interest subvention on crop loans, crop insurance premium (PMFBY 2016).
- WTO Agreement on Agriculture: Amber box (trade-distorting, capped at de minimis 10% of production value for developing countries), Blue box, Green box (non-distorting); India secured a 'peace clause' on public stockholding (Bali 2013).
- MSP: recommended by CACP (set up 1965 as Agricultural Prices Commission) for 22 mandated crops plus FRP for sugarcane; since 2018-19 MSP fixed at least 1.5 times A2+FL cost.
- Swaminathan Commission (National Commission on Farmers, 2004-06) recommended MSP at C2+50%; demand for a legal MSP guarantee remains a farm-union issue.
- MSP effectiveness is uneven: procurement concentrated in rice and wheat and in a few states, distorting cropping patterns towards water-intensive cereals.
- Technology missions: Technology Mission on Oilseeds (1986), National Food Security Mission (2007), MIDH (horticulture), National Mission on Sustainable Agriculture, National Mission on Edible Oils - Oil Palm (2021).
- Animal rearing: India is the world's largest milk producer; Operation Flood (1970) built cooperative dairying; Rashtriya Gokul Mission (2014) for indigenous breeds; livestock gives steady income and insures against crop failure.
- Livestock challenges: low productivity per animal, fodder shortage, disease (FMD, brucellosis - National Animal Disease Control Programme 2019), stray cattle, weak cold chain for milk and meat.
- Way forward: crop diversification, FPOs (10,000 FPO scheme 2020), contract farming with safeguards, price deficiency payments, climate-resilient seeds, water pricing reform.

## Punjab angle

- Wheat-paddy rotation dominates Punjab; assured MSP procurement makes it a major contributor of rice and wheat to the central pool.
- Groundwater crisis: CGWB assessments class a large majority of Punjab's blocks as over-exploited; the Punjab Preservation of Subsoil Water Act 2009 bars early paddy transplanting.
- Johl Committee reports (1986, 2002) urged diversification away from paddy; the state now incentivises Direct Seeded Rice, maize, cotton and basmati.
- Punjab Mandi Board runs a dense network of regulated markets with arhtiyas (commission agents) central to procurement and credit.
- Paddy straw burning: Crop Residue Management machinery subsidies, in-situ/ex-situ use (biomass plants, CBG plants).
- Dairy via Milkfed (Verka) cooperatives; Punjab has among the highest per capita milk availability in India.

Expect questions on MSP reform, subsidies and WTO, agri-marketing reform, water-intensive cropping, and diversification; Punjab-specific data and schemes earn extra marks.

## Deep dive

### MSP architecture and latest prices

- Mandated crops: 22 (14 kharif, 6 rabi, 2 commercial — jute and copra); sugarcane gets a Fair and Remunerative Price under the Sugarcane (Control) Order, 1966.
- CACP cost concepts: A2 = paid-out costs; A2+FL = A2 plus imputed family labour; C2 = A2+FL plus imputed rent on owned land and interest on owned capital.
- Since 2018-19 MSP is fixed at least 1.5 times A2+FL; farm unions want 1.5 times C2 (Swaminathan formula) and a legal guarantee.
- Kharif 2026-27 (CCEA, 13 May 2026): common paddy ₹2,441 per quintal (+₹72), Grade A ₹2,461; cotton up ₹557; moong has the highest margin over cost (about 61%).
- Rabi marketing season 2026-27: wheat ₹2,585 (+₹160). RMS 2027-28 (approved end-September 2026): wheat ₹2,610 — a rise of only ₹25, the smallest in many years.
- RMS 2027-28 others: barley ₹2,286, gram ₹5,958, masur ₹7,390, rapeseed-mustard ₹6,613, safflower ₹7,215 (largest rise, ₹675) — tilt towards pulses and oilseeds.
- PM-AASHA (2018) umbrella: Price Support Scheme (physical procurement of pulses/oilseeds), Price Deficiency Payment (pay the gap to market price), Market Intervention Scheme for perishables.
- Shanta Kumar Committee (2015), using NSSO data, estimated only about 6% of farm households sold to procurement agencies at MSP — the reach problem.
- Effective MSP depends on procurement: strong for wheat and paddy in Punjab, Haryana, MP, Chhattisgarh, Telangana; weak for most other crops and states.

### Cropping pattern — production data (Economic Survey 2025-26 Statistical Appendix)

- 2024-25 (provisional): foodgrains 357.7 million tonnes — rice 150.2, wheat 117.9, pulses 25.7; oilseeds 43.0 million tonnes.
- Ten years earlier (2015-16) foodgrains were about 252 million tonnes — cereals drove most of the rise; pulses grew far less.
- Top wheat producers 2024-25: Uttar Pradesh (30.2%), Madhya Pradesh (20.8%), Punjab (15.3%, 18.0 million tonnes).
- Top rice producers 2024-25: Uttar Pradesh, Telangana, West Bengal — Punjab is not in the top three by production, though it is the biggest contributor to central procurement.
- Top foodgrain states: Uttar Pradesh (17.9%), Madhya Pradesh (13.0%), Punjab (9.2%, 32.8 million tonnes).
- Oilseeds: Rajasthan, MP and Maharashtra lead; India still imports over half of its edible oil needs.
- Pattern drivers: assured procurement and free power push cereals; price risk, low yields and weak markets hold back pulses, oilseeds and millets.

### Irrigation systems and water economics

- Groundwater (tubewells and wells) supplies the largest share of net irrigated area; canals are second; tanks have declined.
- CGWB classifies blocks by 'stage of extraction': safe (up to 70%), semi-critical (70–90%), critical (90–100%), over-exploited (above 100%).
- PMKSY (2015) components: Accelerated Irrigation Benefit Programme, Har Khet Ko Pani, watershed development, and Per Drop More Crop (micro-irrigation subsidy).
- Micro Irrigation Fund with NABARD (2018) helps states top up drip/sprinkler subsidies.
- Atal Bhujal Yojana (2019–20, with World Bank support) promotes community groundwater management in seven water-stressed states (Punjab is not among them).
- Free or flat-rate farm power makes the marginal cost of pumping near zero — the root of over-extraction in Punjab and Haryana.
- Punjab responses: Preservation of Subsoil Water Act 2009 (delayed paddy transplanting), Direct Seeded Rice incentive of ₹1,500 per acre, short-duration varieties such as PR 126, pilot 'Pani Bachao, Paise Kamao' metering.
- Budget 2026-27 allocates ₹6,587 crore to PMKSY.

### Storage, transport, marketing and e-technology

- Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (2020): ₹1 lakh crore of loans with 3% interest subvention for warehouses, cold stores and primary processing.
- Cooperative-sector grain storage plan (2023) aims to build godowns at PACS level — billed as the world's largest grain storage plan.
- WDRA electronic negotiable warehouse receipts allow pledge financing; Kisan Rail (2020) and Krishi UDAN 2.0 (2021) moved perishables.
- e-NAM (April 2016) links regulated mandis online; inter-mandi and inter-state trade remains small because of state fees and quality-assaying gaps.
- 10,000 FPO scheme (2020) target met by 2025; FPOs aggregate produce and bargain better.
- Kisan Credit Card limit under the interest subvention scheme raised to ₹5 lakh (Budget 2025-26); prompt payers get crop loans at an effective 4%.
- Digital Agriculture Mission (2024) with AgriStack; Budget 2026-27 adds Bharat-VISTAAR AI advisory.
- After the 2021 repeal of the three farm laws, reform has moved to states, FPOs and digital platforms rather than central legislation.

### Direct and indirect subsidies; WTO angle

- Budget 2026-27: urea subsidy ₹1.17 lakh crore, nutrient-based subsidy ₹54,000 crore, PM-KISAN ₹63,500 crore, PM-KUSUM ₹5,000 crore.
- Urea's fixed retail price versus decontrolled P and K prices (NBS since 2010) skews use towards nitrogen and harms soil.
- PM-PRANAM (2023) rewards states that cut chemical fertiliser use; nano urea and DAP promoted as alternatives.
- PMFBY farmer premium: 2% kharif, 1.5% rabi, 5% commercial/horticulture; states and Centre pay the rest.
- Indirect subsidies: power, canal water, credit (interest subvention), insurance; direct: PM-KISAN and state cash schemes.
- WTO: India's market price support for rice has exceeded the 10% de minimis limit; India invoked the Bali 'peace clause' (first in 2020) to protect public stockholding.
- PM-KISAN counts as a 'green box' (non-trade-distorting) measure; MSP procurement is 'amber box'.

### Technology missions and economics of animal rearing

- National Mission on Edible Oils – Oil Palm (2021) and – Oilseeds (2024); Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses (2025–31); National Mission on Natural Farming (2024).
- Earlier missions: Technology Mission on Oilseeds (1986, 'Yellow Revolution'), National Food Security Mission (2007), MIDH (horticulture), NMSA (sustainable agriculture).
- Livestock contributes roughly 30% of agricultural GVA and grows faster than crops — a hedge against monsoon risk.
- India produced about 239 million tonnes of milk in 2023-24 (per capita about 470 g/day) — the world's largest.
- Schemes: Rashtriya Gokul Mission (indigenous breeds, sex-sorted semen), National Livestock Mission, Livestock Health and Disease Control Programme, National Animal Disease Control Programme (FMD, brucellosis).
- Problems: low yield per animal, fodder deficit, lumpy skin disease (2022 outbreak), stray cattle, informal milk markets.
- Punjab has among the highest per capita milk availability (over 1 kg per person per day); Verka (Milkfed) runs the cooperative chain.
- Budget 2026-27: loan-linked capital subsidy to train 20,000 new veterinarians.

### Punjab — the MSP and procurement state

- Rabi 2026: Punjab procured 12.3 million tonnes of wheat against a 12.2 million tonne target; the Centre set a 180 lakh tonne paddy target for kharif 2026.
- The tiny ₹25 wheat MSP rise for RMS 2027-28 squeezes Punjab farmers whose costs (diesel, labour, inputs) rose faster.
- Arhtiyas (commission agents) remain central to mandi operations and farm credit; the Centre–state dispute over Rural Development Fee and market fee reimbursement affects rural road funds.
- Diversification tools: DSR, maize and cotton promotion, basmati for export, JICA-backed horticulture expansion (target +300% area by 2035).
- Stubble management: in-situ (Happy Seeder, super-seeder) and ex-situ (biomass power, compressed biogas plants); paddy straw boilers get capital subsidy under the 2026 industrial policy.
- 2025 floods cut farm output (agriculture −2.5% in 2025-26) and damaged land in Ravi–Beas–Sutlej plains.
- Johl Committees (1986, 2002) and later expert groups all recommended shifting about a million hectares from paddy.

### Answer angles

- MSP questions: explain cost concepts, reach, fiscal cost, WTO limits and the legal-guarantee debate — then propose price-deficiency payments and procurement for pulses/oilseeds.
- Irrigation questions: link free power, groundwater and cropping pattern; cite CGWB categories and PMKSY.
- Subsidy questions: contrast input subsidies (distorting) with income support (PM-KISAN).
- Animal husbandry: income stability, women's role, disease control and cold chain.
- Use Punjab data (procurement share, wheat 15% of national output) as the case study.
- Avoid claiming Punjab is the largest rice producer — it is the largest contributor to the central pool, not to production.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** The MSP regime has ensured food security but has also distorted cropping patterns, especially in Punjab. Critically examine and suggest reforms. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: MSP, CACP, 1.5x A2+FL formula.
- Positives: Green Revolution, food self-sufficiency, income stability, buffer stocks.
- Distortions: wheat-paddy bias, groundwater depletion, soil health decline, stubble burning, regional inequity in procurement.
- Fiscal and WTO dimensions: storage costs, amber box concerns.
- Reforms: MSP plus procurement for pulses/oilseeds/maize, price deficiency payments, per-acre diversification incentive, FPO-led marketing, crop-neutral support.
- Conclusion: shift from price support to income and sustainability support.

</details>

**2.** Discuss the role of e-technology in improving agricultural marketing and farm advisory in India. What are the bottlenecks? (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Tools: e-NAM, Soil Health Card, Kisan Call Centres, AgriStack, drones, weather apps.
- Benefits: price discovery, reduced intermediation, input efficiency, credit and insurance targeting.
- Bottlenecks: weak assaying and logistics, interstate trade barriers, digital literacy, land record gaps.
- Way forward: FPO integration, vernacular platforms, digitised land records.
- Conclusion: technology complements, not replaces, physical market infrastructure.

</details>

**3.** Animal husbandry can be a key pillar of farmers' income diversification. Examine its economics and the challenges faced by the sector, with reference to Punjab. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: India largest milk producer; livestock's growing share in agri GVA.
- Economics: regular cash flow, use of crop residues, employment for women, risk diversification.
- Challenges: low yields, feed/fodder costs, disease, marketing, heat stress, stray cattle.
- Schemes: Rashtriya Gokul Mission, NADCP, Animal Husbandry Infrastructure Development Fund, Kisan Credit Card for livestock.
- Punjab: Verka cooperative model, high milk availability, scope for fisheries in waterlogged south-west.
- Conclusion: integrated farming systems for resilient incomes.

</details>
