## Short notes

- PDS began as wartime rationing in the 1940s; expanded after the 1960s food crises with FCI (1965, Food Corporations Act 1964) and procurement at MSP.
- Objectives: price stability, access to staples for the poor, buffer against shortages, support to farmers via procurement.
- Revamped PDS (1992) targeted difficult areas; Targeted PDS (1997) introduced BPL/APL; Antyodaya Anna Yojana (2000) for the poorest households.
- National Food Security Act 2013 makes food a legal entitlement: covers up to 75% rural and 50% urban population; 5 kg grain per person per month for priority households, 35 kg per AAY household.
- NFSA also provides maternity benefit (at least ₹6,000), meals through ICDS and mid-day meal (now PM-POSHAN), and women aged 18+ as head of household for ration cards.
- Since 1 January 2023 NFSA grains are free (PMGKAY merged); free grains extended for five years from 1 January 2024.
- Functioning: Centre procures, stores, transports to state depots; states identify beneficiaries, issue ration cards, run Fair Price Shops (FPS).
- Decentralised Procurement (1997-98) lets states procure and distribute locally, cutting transport costs.
- Limitations: inclusion/exclusion errors, leakages and diversion, poor FPS viability, quality issues, high economic cost of FCI, narrow cereal basket that neglects nutrition.
- Shanta Kumar High Level Committee (2015) on FCI restructuring: flagged large leakages, suggested lower coverage, cash transfers and handing procurement to states with capacity.
- Reforms: end-to-end computerisation, Aadhaar seeding, ePoS devices at FPS, One Nation One Ration Card (portability; national rollout completed by 2022), cash DBT pilots in Chandigarh and Puducherry (2015).
- Rice fortification with iron, folic acid and B12 extended through PDS and welfare schemes to fight anaemia.
- Buffer stocks: held by FCI to meet NFSA needs, emergencies and price stabilisation; quarterly norms (highest on 1 July, about 41 million tonnes of rice and wheat).
- Open Market Sale Scheme (OMSS) offloads excess grain to cool prices; excess stocks raise carrying costs and storage losses.
- Food security (FAO, 1996): availability, access, utilisation, stability; India has moved from availability to nutrition security challenges (stunting, anaemia).
- Way forward: diversify basket (millets, pulses, edible oil), smart PDS with real-time monitoring, scientific storage (silos), targeting via dynamic databases, nutrition-sensitive agriculture.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab is a leading contributor of wheat and rice to the central pool; procurement agencies include PUNGRAIN, Markfed, Punsup and Punjab State Warehousing Corporation.
- Storage gaps: large stocks held under cover-and-plinth (CAP) storage; modern steel silos (e.g. Moga) improve storage quality.
- Punjab's Atta-Dal scheme (2007) was an early state-level food subsidy layered on PDS; beneficiaries now get free NFSA wheat.
- Centre-state friction over withheld Rural Development Fund and market fees on procurement affects mandi and rural road funding.
- Slow lifting of stocks and space shortage in Punjab delay paddy procurement and milling, hurting farmers and millers.

## Deep dive

### Evolution of PDS and food-security law (year — event)

- 1939–43 — Wartime rationing begins in Bombay; the Bengal Famine (1943) makes food distribution a state responsibility.
- 1964–65 — Food Corporations Act (1964); FCI starts on 14 January 1965 together with the Agricultural Prices Commission.
- 1992 — Revamped PDS for about 1,775 backward, hilly and tribal blocks.
- 1997 — Targeted PDS with BPL/APL prices; Decentralised Procurement (DCP) lets states procure and distribute themselves.
- 2000 — Antyodaya Anna Yojana for the poorest households (35 kg a month).
- 2001 onwards — PUCL v Union of India ('right to food' case): Supreme Court orders convert schemes like mid-day meals into entitlements.
- 2012–13 — Chhattisgarh Food Security Act (2012) precedes the National Food Security Act (September 2013).
- 2015 — Shanta Kumar High Level Committee report; cash-transfer pilots for food subsidy in Chandigarh and Puducherry.
- 2019–22 — One Nation One Ration Card pilot (2019), national roll-out completed by 2022; PMGKAY free grain during COVID (April 2020).
- 2024 — Supply of fortified rice through PDS and other schemes extended to December 2028.

### NFSA mechanics and numbers

- Coverage ceiling: about 81.35 crore people (75% rural, 50% urban of the Census 2011 population); state-wise ratios set from 2011-12 NSSO consumption data.
- Two categories: Antyodaya households (35 kg per household) and Priority Households (5 kg per person per month).
- Grain is now free (Central Issue Price zero since 2023); earlier prices were ₹3/kg rice, ₹2 wheat, ₹1 coarse grains.
- About 5 lakh Fair Price Shops form the last mile; nearly all now use ePoS devices with Aadhaar authentication.
- Grievance redress: District Grievance Redressal Officer and State Food Commission; food security allowance is payable if grain is not supplied.
- Budget 2026-27 allocates ₹2.27 lakh crore to PMGKAY — the largest single scheme in the Union Budget.
- Coverage is still tied to Census 2011 numbers, so population growth since then has not been reflected in entitlements.
- Per capita net availability of foodgrains rose to about 569 grams per day in 2022-23 (Economic Survey 2025-26 Statistical Appendix).

### Buffer stocks — norms and management

- Buffer norms (rice + wheat, including a strategic reserve of 3 MT wheat and 2 MT rice): 1 April 21.04 MT; 1 July 41.12 MT; 1 October 30.77 MT; 1 January 21.41 MT.
- 1 July has the highest norm because rabi wheat procurement ends in June.
- Purposes: meet NFSA and welfare-scheme needs, cushion production shocks, intervene in markets to stabilise prices.
- Open Market Sale Scheme (Domestic) offloads FCI wheat and rice to bulk buyers, flour mills and state governments to cool prices.
- Bharat Atta, Bharat Rice and Bharat Dal (2023) sold subsidised branded staples through NAFED, NCCF and Kendriya Bhandar.
- Price Stabilisation Fund (2014-15) maintains buffers of pulses and onions.
- Excess stocks raise carrying costs (storage, interest, losses); surplus rice has been offered for ethanol blending and exports.
- Storage modes: covered godowns, cover-and-plinth (CAP) open storage, and steel silos built under PPP (e.g. Moga, Punjab).

### Limitations — evidence to cite

- Shanta Kumar Committee (2015), using NSSO 2011-12 data, estimated leakage of about 46% in the PDS.
- Exclusion errors: biometric authentication failures and outdated beneficiary lists; inclusion errors where better-off households hold priority cards.
- Narrow basket: rice and wheat dominate; pulses, oils and millets are added only by some states (Tamil Nadu, Chhattisgarh).
- Nutrition outcomes lag: NFHS-5 shows 35.5% stunting among under-5 children and about 57% anaemia among women.
- Fiscal cost: FCI's economic cost per quintal is far above MSP because of procurement incidentals, transport, storage and interest.
- FCI's earlier off-budget borrowing from the National Small Savings Fund was ended from 2021-22 when the food subsidy was fully brought on budget.
- Distortion: open-ended procurement in a few states pushes water-intensive rice and wheat.

### Revamping PDS — reforms in place

- End-to-end computerisation of TPDS (digitised ration cards, online allocation, supply-chain tracking).
- One Nation One Ration Card: portability across states, vital for migrant workers; 'Mera Ration' app.
- Anna Chakra (2024): route optimisation of grain movement to cut logistics costs.
- Annapurti 'grain ATMs' (2024) for automated distribution; SMART-PDS for technology-based reform.
- Cash in lieu of grain (DBT) remains a pilot idea — supporters cite lower leakages, critics cite food-price risk and banking access.
- States show the way: Tamil Nadu (universal PDS), Chhattisgarh (community-run FPS), Odisha (millets in PDS).
- Way forward: update coverage with new data, add pulses/millets, scientific storage, decentralised procurement for coarse grains, grievance audits.

### Food security beyond PDS

- FAO's four pillars: availability, access, utilisation, stability.
- Trade tools: wheat export ban (May 2022) and curbs on non-basmati white rice exports (2023, eased in 2024) protected domestic stocks.
- Nutrition-sensitive schemes: PM POSHAN (₹12,750 crore in 2026-27), Saksham Anganwadi and POSHAN 2.0 (₹23,100 crore).
- Climate risk: El Niño years and heatwaves threaten wheat yields; September 2026 food inflation reached 7.74%.
- India became a net exporter of rice and a large wheat producer, so the challenge has shifted from availability to affordability of nutritious diets.
- WTO: public stockholding at administered prices is protected by the Bali (2013) peace clause until a permanent solution.

### Punjab — the granary's issues

- Punjab supplied over a third of the wheat procured nationally in rabi 2026 (12.3 MT of about 33 MT).
- State procurement agencies: PUNGRAIN, Markfed, Punsup and the Punjab State Warehousing Corporation, alongside FCI.
- Storage squeeze: slow movement of stored grain out of Punjab delays paddy milling and delivery of custom-milled rice, hurting millers and arhtiyas.
- Modern steel silos (such as the PPP silo complex at Moga, 2007) reduce losses compared with CAP storage.
- State welfare layer: Atta-Dal scheme (2007) was a forerunner; 'Meri Rasoi' quarterly ration kits get ₹900 crore in 2026-27.
- Centre–state issues: Rural Development Fee and market fee reimbursement on central procurement, which fund rural roads and mandis.
- Paradox: a food-surplus state that faces nutrition and drug-abuse challenges among its youth.

### Answer angles

- Explain PDS as a chain: procurement → storage → allocation → FPS → household, and identify leaks at each step.
- Use the buffer-norm table and the July peak to show understanding of stock management.
- Compare universal vs targeted PDS (Tamil Nadu vs NFSA) and grain vs cash transfers.
- Link food security to nutrition (NFHS-5) and to climate risk.
- Give Punjab's dual role: surplus producer and storage bottleneck.
- End with a reform package: data-driven targeting, diversified basket, storage modernisation, decentralised procurement.

**2.** What are buffer stocks? Discuss the problems of excessive buffer stocks in India and suggest a rational stock policy. (150 words)

**3.** 'Punjab feeds the nation, but the food procurement system is under stress in Punjab itself.' Discuss the issues in procurement, storage and movement of foodgrains in the state. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: Punjab's role in the central pool.
- Issues: storage shortages, slow lifting, milling bottlenecks, quality cuts, rural development fund dispute.
- Farmer impact: delayed payments, distress sales, lifting delays.
- Ecological cost of the procurement-led wheat-paddy cycle.
- Way forward: silo capacity, faster rail movement, diversified procurement, decentralised procurement in deficit states.
- Conclusion: rebalancing national procurement geography.

</details>
