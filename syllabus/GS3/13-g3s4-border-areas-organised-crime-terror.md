## Short notes

- India has a land border of about 15,100 km with seven countries and a coastline of about 7,500 km; Bangladesh is the longest land border.
- Border challenges: infiltration, cross-border terrorism, smuggling of drugs, arms and fake currency, illegal migration, cattle smuggling, and difficult terrain (riverine, desert, mountains, mangroves).
- Pakistan border: militancy, drones, narco-terror; China (LAC): undemarcated line, infrastructure race; Bangladesh: migration, smuggling; Nepal and Bhutan: open borders misused by criminals; Myanmar: insurgents, drugs.
- Kargil Review Committee (1999, K. Subrahmanyam) and the Group of Ministers (2001) led to 'one border, one force' and the Department of Border Management in MHA.
- Management tools: fencing and floodlighting, border outposts, Comprehensive Integrated Border Management System (CIBMS) with sensors and cameras (e.g. BOLD-QIT on riverine Bangladesh border).
- Development tools: Border Area Development Programme (from 1986-87), Vibrant Villages Programme (2023) for northern border villages and its second phase for other borders.
- Trade and movement: Integrated Check Posts managed by Land Ports Authority of India (2012); Attari ICP (2012) was India's main land trade point with Pakistan until it was closed after the April 2025 Pahalgam attack.
- Organised crime: continuing illegal enterprise for profit - drugs, arms, human trafficking, extortion, counterfeiting, contract killings.
- Crime-terror nexus: terror groups use criminal networks for logistics and funding; criminals gain protection and markets - e.g. D-company's role in the 1993 Mumbai blasts.
- Narco-terrorism: proximity to the Golden Crescent (Afghanistan-Iran-Pakistan) and Golden Triangle (Myanmar-Laos-Thailand) makes India a transit and consumer zone.
- Legal framework: NDPS Act 1985, UAPA 1967 (2019 amendment allows designating individuals as terrorists), NIA Act 2008 (2019 amendment added human trafficking, arms and cyber terror), state laws like MCOCA 1999.
- Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023 introduced specific offences of organised crime and terrorist act in the general criminal law.
- International: UN Convention against Transnational Organized Crime (Palermo, 2000), extradition treaties, Interpol notices.
- Way forward: technology-driven surveillance, anti-drone systems, intelligence fusion (MAC, NATGRID), financial tracking, coordination between border forces and state police, involving border communities.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab shares about 553 km of the international border with Pakistan, guarded by the BSF; it includes riverine stretches along the Ravi and Sutlej.
- Drones from Pakistan drop heroin and weapons; anti-drone systems and joint BSF-Punjab Police operations are key responses.
- Farmers cultivate land beyond the fence near the zero line through designated gates under BSF supervision, creating security and livelihood issues.
- Gangster networks with handlers abroad are linked to targeted killings and terror modules; Punjab created the Anti-Gangster Task Force (2022).
- Border districts (Amritsar, Tarn Taran, Gurdaspur, Pathankot, Ferozepur, Fazilka) face lower development, drug abuse and migration, needing border area development.

## Deep dive

### Border profile (MHA figures) and guarding forces

- Land border about 15,107 km: Bangladesh 4,096.7 km, China 3,488 km, Pakistan 3,323 km, Nepal 1,751 km, Myanmar 1,643 km, Bhutan 699 km, Afghanistan 106 km.
- Guarding: BSF — Pakistan and Bangladesh; ITBP — China; SSB — Nepal and Bhutan; Assam Rifles — Myanmar; the Army holds the LoC and parts of the LAC.
- Border states and UTs range from Punjab, Rajasthan and Gujarat in the west to West Bengal, Assam and all north-eastern states in the east, plus the UTs of J&K and Ladakh — border management is a shared Centre–state task.
- Coastline: traditionally 7,516.6 km; a fresh measurement for 2023-24 (National Hydrographic Office and Survey of India data, modern mapping) put it at about 11,098 km — guarded by the Navy, Coast Guard and state marine police.
- Punjab–Pakistan border: 553 km, guarded by BSF's Punjab Frontier; includes riverine stretches of the Ravi and Sutlej and fenced farmland beyond which farmers cultivate under BSF escort.
- Terrain types: riverine and char lands (Bangladesh, Punjab), desert (Rajasthan), high Himalaya (China), jungle-hill (Myanmar), creeks and mangroves (Gujarat, Sundarbans).

### Border-management architecture and schemes

- Group of Ministers report (2001) after Kargil: 'one border, one force'; Department of Border Management created in MHA (2004).
- Comprehensive Integrated Border Management System (CIBMS): sensors, radars, cameras and command centres; BOLD-QIT (2019) on the riverine Dhubri stretch of the Bangladesh border.
- Border Area Development Programme (from 1986-87) for basic infrastructure within 0–10 km of the border.
- Vibrant Villages Programme I (2023) for northern border villages; VVP-II (April 2025, about ₹6,839 crore) extends to villages on other land borders, including Punjab.
- Free Movement Regime with Myanmar ended (2024) and fencing of the whole Myanmar border approved.
- BSF's jurisdiction raised from 15 km to 50 km in Punjab, West Bengal and Assam (and cut from 80 km to 50 km in Gujarat) in October 2021.
- Immigration and Foreigners Act 2025 (in force September 2025) consolidated older laws on entry, stay and detection of foreigners.

### Border-wise challenges

- Pakistan: infiltration, cross-border tunnels (Jammu–Samba), drones carrying drugs and arms, sniping and ceasefire violations; four-day conflict in May 2025 (Operation Sindoor).
- China: undemarcated LAC, Galwan clash (June 2020), disengagement agreement on Depsang and Demchok (October 2024); infrastructure race (roads, tunnels, dual-use villages).
- Bangladesh: illegal migration, cattle and fake currency smuggling, unfenced riverine gaps; political change in Dhaka (August 2024) raised new concerns.
- Nepal: open border misused by criminals, terror suspects and human traffickers; Bhutan: low threat, cooperative.
- Myanmar: ethnic armed groups, refugee flows after the 2021 coup, methamphetamine and heroin from the Golden Triangle, link to Manipur's conflict.
- Maritime: drug consignments via the Gujarat coast (e.g. about 3,000 kg of heroin seized at Mundra port in 2021), 26/11-type sea-borne infiltration risk.

### Organised crime–terror nexus — concepts and cases

- Organised crime: continuing unlawful activity by a group for material benefit — drugs, arms, extortion, kidnapping, human and wildlife trafficking, cyber fraud.
- Forms of the nexus: coexistence (same territory), cooperation (buying services), convergence (one group doing both crime and terror).
- 1993 Bombay blasts: D-company smuggling networks delivered RDX for a terror attack — the classic Indian example.
- Narco-terror: heroin from the Golden Crescent (Afghanistan–Pakistan–Iran) funds terror groups; Punjab and J&K are transit and consumption zones.
- Fake Indian Currency Notes (FICN) printed abroad and pushed through Bangladesh and Nepal finance networks.
- Gangster–terror nexus: NIA's 2022 cases showed jailed and foreign-based gangsters supplying shooters, weapons and safe houses to Khalistani terror handlers.
- Arms: foreign-made pistols dropped by drones in Punjab; illegal arms factories in Bihar and MP supply gangs.
- Scam compounds in Myanmar, Laos and Cambodia use trafficked Indian youth for cyber fraud — crime, trafficking and laundering combined.

### Legal and institutional tools

- NDPS Act 1985 (property forfeiture under Chapter VA) and PIT-NDPS Act 1988 (preventive detention of traffickers).
- UAPA 1967: 2019 amendment allows designating individuals as terrorists; NIA Act amended 2019 to cover human trafficking, arms, explosives and cyber terrorism, and offences abroad.
- Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023: Section 111 (organised crime) and Section 113 (terrorist act) bring these into the general criminal law.
- State laws: MCOCA 1999 (Maharashtra), Gujarat GCTOC Act 2015, Karnataka KCOCA — stricter evidence and bail rules.
- Coordination: Multi-Agency Centre (intelligence sharing), NATGRID (database linking), NCORD (narco-coordination, 2016), MANAS helpline 1933 (2024) for drug reports.

### Punjab border — management and development

- BSF seized 272 drones, over 367 kg of heroin, over 10 kg of RDX and about 200 weapons on the Punjab border in 2025 (to late November); 3 Pakistani intruders were neutralised.
- State's 'Baaz Akh' anti-drone systems (2025) and joint BSF–Punjab Police operations form a two-layer defence.
- Kartarpur Sahib Corridor (opened November 2019) was suspended by India on 7 May 2025 and was still closed in September 2026 on security grounds — a people-to-people link affected by security.
- Border districts (Amritsar, Tarn Taran, Gurdaspur, Pathankot, Ferozepur, Fazilka) lag in industry and suffer drug abuse; the 2026 industrial policy gives border units an extra 25% incentive.
- Farmers with land beyond the fence face restricted hours and gates; the 2025 floods damaged fencing and posts along the Ravi and Sutlej.
- VVP-II and BADP fund roads, schools and health in border villages; community vigilance helps detect drone drops.
- Centre–state issues: BSF's 50 km jurisdiction, coordination with Punjab Police on narcotics cases, and control of the Attari route.
