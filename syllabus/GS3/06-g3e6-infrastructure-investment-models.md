## Short notes

- Infrastructure has strong backward and forward linkages; it lowers logistics costs, raises productivity and attracts investment.
- Energy: India is the world's third-largest electricity producer; coal still dominates generation, but non-fossil sources crossed half of installed capacity in 2025.
- Energy targets (COP26 Panchamrit): 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030, net zero by 2070; National Green Hydrogen Mission (2023) targets 5 MMT green hydrogen by 2030.
- Power sector issues: DISCOM losses (AT&C), cross-subsidies, delayed payments; reform schemes include UDAY (2015) and Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (2021); PM Surya Ghar (2024) for rooftop solar.
- Ports: Sagarmala (2015) for port-led development; Maritime India Vision 2030 and Amrit Kaal Maritime Vision 2047; major ports governed by the Major Port Authorities Act 2021.
- Roads: Bharatmala Pariyojana (2017) for economic corridors and expressways; PMGSY (2000) for rural connectivity; FASTag for electronic tolling.
- Railways: Dedicated Freight Corridors (Eastern: Ludhiana to Sonnagar; Western: Dadri to JNPT), Vande Bharat trains, Kavach anti-collision system, near-complete broad-gauge electrification.
- Airports: UDAN regional connectivity scheme (2016); number of operational airports roughly doubled since 2014; PPP in airport operation (Delhi, Mumbai, others).
- Integrated planning: PM Gati Shakti National Master Plan (2021) on a GIS platform; National Logistics Policy (2022) aims to cut logistics cost.
- Financing: National Infrastructure Pipeline (2019, about ₹111 lakh crore), National Monetisation Pipeline (2021, about ₹6 lakh crore), NaBFID (2021) as development finance institution.
- Investment models: EPC (government funds, contractor builds), BOT-Toll, BOT-Annuity, Hybrid Annuity Model (2016; government pays 40% during construction), TOT (toll-operate-transfer), DBFOT.
- PPP issues: aggressive bidding, land acquisition delays, renegotiation, stressed assets; Kelkar Committee (2015) recommended revisiting and revitalising PPPs.
- Viability Gap Funding (2006) supports socially needed but commercially unviable PPP projects; InvITs and REITs allow asset recycling.
- Asset monetisation: brownfield assets leased to private players to fund new projects without transferring ownership.
- Challenges: land acquisition, environmental clearances, cost and time overruns, contractual disputes, funding gaps for states.
- Way forward: robust dispute resolution, independent regulators, municipal bonds, green finance, climate-resilient design.

## Punjab angle

- Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor starts at Sahnewal (Ludhiana), aiding Punjab's industrial logistics.
- Delhi-Amritsar-Katra Expressway under Bharatmala will improve connectivity of Punjab with Delhi and J&K.
- Airports: Sri Guru Ram Dass Jee International Airport (Amritsar) and Shaheed Bhagat Singh International Airport (Mohali).

## Deep dive

### Infrastructure dashboard (Economic Survey 2025-26 and Budget 2026-27)

- Power: installed capacity 509.74 GW (November 2025), up 11.6% in a year; renewable capacity rose from 76.38 GW (March 2014) to 253.96 GW (November 2025).
- The Economic Survey 2025-26 reports the largest-ever annual addition of non-fossil capacity, led by solar (27.2 GW); India ranks third in total RE and solar capacity and fourth in wind.
- Railways: about 2,741 km (96.4%) of the 2,843 km Dedicated Freight Corridor network commissioned by October 2025 — Eastern DFC (1,337 km) complete; 1,404 km of the 1,506 km Western DFC done.
- Roads: high-speed corridors grew from 550 km (2014) to 5,364 km (December 2025); about 26,000 km targeted by 2032-33; NH construction target for 2025-26 was 10,000 km.
- Airports: operational airports rose from 74 (2014) to 164 (2025); UDAN has operationalised 657 routes linking 93 airports.
- Waterways: cargo on NW-1 (Ganga) up about 220% since 2014-15; Jalvahak scheme (2024) subsidises scheduled cargo services.
- Budget 2026-27: capex ₹12.22 lakh crore; seven high-speed rail 'growth connectors'; new Dankuni–Surat freight corridor; 20 new National Waterways; ₹20,000 crore for CCUS.
- Logistics: PM Gati Shakti (October 2021) GIS master plan and National Logistics Policy (September 2022) aim to cut logistics costs to global benchmarks.

### Investment models — how each works

- EPC: government pays the full cost; the contractor designs and builds; the state bears traffic and financing risk.
- BOT-Toll: the private concessionaire finances, builds and collects tolls for a concession period (traffic risk with the private party).
- BOT-Annuity: the concessionaire builds and the government pays fixed annuities (traffic risk with the government).
- Hybrid Annuity Model (2016): government pays 40% of project cost during construction, the remaining 60% is raised by the developer and repaid as annuities with interest; toll collection stays with NHAI.
- TOT (Toll-Operate-Transfer): operational highways leased for 15–30 years for an upfront payment — brownfield asset recycling.
- DBFOT/DBFOO: design-build-finance-operate-transfer/own-operate variants used in ports, airports and urban services.
- InvITs and REITs: trusts that hold income-generating assets and raise long-term money from investors; the first public InvIT of the Centre is planned for 2026.
- Viability Gap Funding (2006, revised later): up to a fixed share of capital cost for socially necessary but commercially weak PPPs.

### PPP and financing architecture

- PPP Appraisal Committee (PPPAC) appraises central PPP projects; a three-year PPP pipeline of 852 projects was prepared after the Budget 2025-26 announcement.
- Kelkar Committee (2015): rebalance risk, set up an independent regulator and a tribunal for disputes, avoid aggressive bidding.
- National Infrastructure Pipeline (2019, about ₹111 lakh crore) and National Monetisation Pipeline (2021); cumulative monetisation of ₹1.52 lakh crore through TOT and private InvITs.
- NaBFID (2021) is the development finance institution for long-tenor infrastructure loans; IIFCL refinances PPP debt.
- Municipal bonds: Budget 2026-27 offers a ₹100 crore incentive for cities issuing bonds above ₹1,000 crore.
- Problem areas: greenfield PPPs still depend on bank balance sheets; land acquisition and clearances are the main delay causes; state PPP cells lack capacity.
- Economic Survey 2025-26 urges moving from 'transaction-centric' to 'partnership-centric' PPPs with better risk–reward sharing.

### Energy sector issues

- Targets: 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030; net zero by 2070; non-fossil already over half of installed capacity (crossed in 2025).
- Coal still provides most electricity generation; storage (batteries, pumped hydro) and grid integration are the bottlenecks for renewables.
- DISCOM finances: AT&C losses and delayed subsidy payments by states; Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (2021) for smart meters and loss reduction.
- PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana (2024) for one crore rooftop solar homes; PM-KUSUM for solar pumps (₹5,000 crore in 2026-27).
- Green Hydrogen Mission (2023): 5 million tonnes by 2030.
- Nuclear: Budget 2025-26 set up a Nuclear Energy Mission for small modular reactors; Parliament passed the SHANTI Act (December 2025) to allow private participation in civil nuclear power.
- Transmission: Green Energy Corridors evacuate renewable power from Rajasthan, Gujarat and Ladakh.

### Transport sub-sectors — reforms

- Railways: Kavach anti-collision system, Vande Bharat trains, near-universal broad-gauge electrification, Amrit Bharat station scheme; freight share of rail still below the target of 45% by 2030.
- Roads: Bharatmala Pariyojana (2017) corridors, FASTag (mandatory from 2021), plans for satellite-based tolling.
- Civil aviation: greenfield airports policy (24 in-principle approvals; 13 operational including Navi Mumbai); airport PPPs (Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad and others).
- Urban transport: metro rail in over 20 cities; Kochi Water Metro (2023) shows low-cost water transit.
- Inland waterways: National Waterways Act 2016 declared 106 new NWs (111 total); Budget 2026-27 adds 20 more.

### Punjab infrastructure

- Eastern DFC begins near Ludhiana (Sahnewal) — the best logistics asset for Punjab's MSMEs and grain movement.
- Delhi–Amritsar–Katra Expressway (Bharatmala) and Amritsar–Jamnagar economic corridor improve access to Delhi and Gujarat ports.
- Power: PSPCL; state bought the Goindwal Sahib thermal plant (2024); captive Pachhwara coal mine in Jharkhand; power subsidy ₹15,550 crore in 2026-27.
- Hydro and water: Bhakra, Pong and Ranjit Sagar dams; Shahpurkandi dam completed in 2024, ending the flow of surplus Ravi water to Pakistan.
- Rural and urban: Punjab Mandi Board link roads, smart city projects in Amritsar, Jalandhar and Ludhiana; 2025 floods exposed weak embankments (dhussi bundhs).
- State PPP capacity and debt limit large greenfield projects — asset recycling and central schemes are the realistic routes.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** Compare the EPC, BOT and Hybrid Annuity models of infrastructure financing. Why did HAM become popular in highway development? (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Define each model with risk allocation.
- BOT problems: traffic risk, aggressive bids, stalled projects.
- HAM: 40% government support during construction, annuity payments, traffic risk with government.
- Benefits and issues of HAM (fiscal liability, private equity limits).
- Conclusion: mix models by project risk.

**2.** PPP projects in India have faced several problems. Discuss and suggest measures to revitalise PPPs in infrastructure. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: role of PPP in infrastructure gap.
- Problems: land acquisition, clearances, renegotiation, optimism bias, twin balance sheet problem.
- Kelkar Committee suggestions: balanced risk sharing, independent regulators, dispute resolution, avoid Swiss challenge.
- Recent steps: HAM, TOT, InvITs, NaBFID, Gati Shakti.
- Conclusion: trust-based contracts and institutional capacity.

</details>

**3.** Logistics and energy infrastructure are crucial for Punjab's industrial revival. Examine the infrastructure gaps in Punjab and suggest an investment strategy. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: landlocked, border state, distance from ports.
- Assets: Eastern DFC at Ludhiana, expressways, airports, power plants.
- Gaps: freight cost, power tariff and supply reliability, urban infrastructure, industrial parks.
- Strategy: multimodal logistics parks, dry ports, rooftop solar and storage, PPP for urban services, Gati Shakti use.
- Conclusion: infrastructure-led industrial corridor approach.

</details>
