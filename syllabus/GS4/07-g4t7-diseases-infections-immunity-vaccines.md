## Short notes

- Disease types: communicable (infectious) vs non-communicable (diabetes, cancer, heart disease); India faces a double burden with NCDs now causing the majority of deaths.
- Pathogens: bacteria (TB, typhoid, cholera), viruses (influenza, dengue, hepatitis, HIV, COVID-19), protozoa (malaria — Plasmodium), fungi, helminths (worms).
- Transmission routes: airborne/droplet (TB, influenza), water/food-borne (cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A and E), vector-borne (malaria and dengue by mosquitoes), sexual/blood-borne (HIV, hepatitis B and C), zoonotic (rabies, Nipah, avian flu).
- Common preventive measures: safe drinking water, sanitation and hand-washing, vector control (source reduction of stagnant water), nutrition, safe injection practices, vaccination.
- Outbreak response: surveillance (Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme, now on the Integrated Health Information Platform), rapid response teams, case isolation, contact tracing, quarantine, risk communication.
- Legal tools: Epidemic Diseases Act 1897 (amended 2020 to protect health workers), Disaster Management Act 2005 (used for COVID-19 from March 2020), state public health Acts.
- Immunity: innate (skin, phagocytes, fever) vs adaptive (B-cell antibodies, T-cells, memory); active (infection/vaccine) vs passive (maternal antibodies, antisera).
- Herd immunity: when a high share of the population is immune, transmission falls and protects the vulnerable.
- Vaccine types: live attenuated (BCG, OPV, MMR), inactivated (IPV), subunit/recombinant (hepatitis B), toxoid (tetanus), viral-vector and mRNA (COVID-19).
- Universal Immunisation Programme (1985); Mission Indradhanush (2014) to reach unvaccinated children; U-WIN digital registry for routine immunisation.
- Milestones: India declared polio-free (WHO, 2014) and eliminated maternal and neonatal tetanus (2015); goal of TB elimination ahead of SDG target (Pradhan Mantri TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan).
- Indigenous vaccines: Covaxin, CERVAVAC (HPV), rotavirus vaccine (Rotavac); India supplies a large share of world vaccines.
- Antimicrobial resistance (AMR): misuse of antibiotics in humans and livestock; National Action Plan on AMR (2017); One Health approach.
- Health system: Ayushman Bharat — Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (primary care) and PM-JAY insurance (2018); National Centre for Disease Control as nodal agency.
- Challenges: vaccine hesitancy and misinformation, urban slums and migrants missed, weak lab network in districts, out-of-pocket spending.
- Way forward: stronger primary care, district labs, One Health surveillance, community health workers (ASHAs), risk communication and climate-sensitive disease planning.

## Punjab angle

- Dengue outbreaks recur after the monsoon in Punjab's cities; Hepatitis C has a high burden — Punjab began free Hepatitis C treatment in 2016.
- Injecting drug use links the drug crisis to HIV and Hepatitis C spread; the state's 'Yudh Nashian Virudh' anti-drug campaign (2025) also has a health component.
- Aam Aadmi Clinics provide free consultation and tests at the community level.
- Floods (2023, 2025) raise risk of water-borne and vector-borne diseases — health camps, chlorination and fogging are part of flood response.

## Deep dive

### Recent outbreaks and preparedness — timeline (2018–2026)

- 2018 onward — Nipah outbreaks in Kerala (Kozhikode first), with recurrences in later years.
- 30 Jan 2020–5 May 2023 — COVID-19 was a WHO public health emergency of international concern; India gave over 220 crore vaccine doses through CoWIN.
- 2021 — mucormycosis surge; many states notified it as an epidemic disease.
- 2022 — lumpy skin disease devastated cattle in Punjab, Rajasthan and other states — a One Health warning.
- 2022–23 and 2024–25 — mpox declared a global health emergency twice (the second from August 2024 to September 2025).
- 2024 — Chandipura virus encephalitis among children in Gujarat (sandfly-borne).
- Jan 2025 — HMPV cases caused public alarm, though it is a known, usually mild respiratory virus.
- Jan–Feb 2025 — Guillain-Barré syndrome cluster in Pune linked to Campylobacter jejuni in contaminated water.
- May 2025 — WHO's World Health Assembly adopted the Pandemic Agreement (its pathogen access and benefit-sharing annex is still being negotiated); amended International Health Regulations with a new 'pandemic emergency' level entered into force in September 2025.

### Immunology — concepts examiners expect

- Innate immunity: skin, mucus, lysozyme, stomach acid, neutrophils, macrophages, natural killer cells, inflammation, fever, interferons and complement.
- Adaptive immunity: B cells make antibodies — IgG crosses the placenta, IgA is in breast milk and mucus, IgM appears first, IgE drives allergy; T cells — CD4 helpers (HIV's target) and CD8 killers; memory cells give lasting protection.
- Secondary (booster) responses are faster and stronger than primary ones — the logic behind booster doses.
- Tolerance and autoimmunity: regulatory T cells stop the immune system attacking the body (Nobel 2025); failures cause type 1 diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, lupus and multiple sclerosis.
- Hypersensitivity: allergies involve histamine (treated with antihistamines); anaphylaxis needs adrenaline. Immunodeficiency can be inborn (SCID) or acquired (HIV, chemotherapy, malnutrition).
- Monoclonal antibodies come from hybridomas (Köhler and Milstein, 1975); India's Rabishield (Serum Institute, 2016) was the world's first rabies monoclonal antibody.
- Herd-immunity threshold is roughly 1 − 1/R0; measles needs about 95% immunity.
- Immunotherapy: checkpoint inhibitors (Nobel 2018) and CAR-T cells harness immunity against cancer.

### Vaccine platforms and the Indian landscape

- Live attenuated (BCG, OPV, MMR, rotavirus), inactivated (IPV, Covaxin), recombinant subunit (hepatitis B, HPV), toxoid (tetanus), conjugate (Hib, PCV, typhoid conjugate), viral vector (Covishield), mRNA (Gennova's GEMCOVAC, India's first), DNA (ZyCoV-D, world's first for humans) and intranasal (iNCOVACC).
- Serum Institute of India is the world's largest vaccine maker by doses; India supplies a majority of global vaccine volumes to UN agencies.
- Vaccine Maitri (2021) sent Indian COVID-19 vaccines to about 100 countries.
- 2026 HPV drive: one dose of Gardasil-4 for 14-year-old girls; India's own CERVAVAC is also available.
- Pipeline: AdFalciVax (ICMR multi-stage falciparum malaria vaccine, licensed to five firms in 2025); DengiAll, a single-dose dengue vaccine by Panacea Biotec with ICMR, in phase-3 trials since August 2024; new TB vaccines in trials.
- Regulation: CDSCO/DCGI approves vaccines; the Central Drugs Laboratory, Kasauli, tests each batch; NTAGI advises on adding vaccines to the UIP; AEFI committees monitor safety.

### Antimicrobial resistance and One Health

- Bacterial AMR directly caused about 1.27 million deaths worldwide in 2019 and contributed to about 4.95 million; the UN General Assembly's 2024 political declaration targets a 10% cut in AMR deaths by 2030.
- Drivers in India: over-the-counter antibiotics, self-medication, hospital infections, antibiotics in poultry and fish farming, pharmaceutical effluents and poor sanitation.
- Responses: Schedule H1 (2014) prescription controls, the 'Red Line' campaign (2016), the National Action Plan on AMR (2017), ICMR's AMR surveillance network, and Kerala's state plan (2018).
- About 60% of known human infections and about 75% of emerging ones come from animals, so One Health links human, animal and environmental surveillance.
- India is building a National One Health Mission and a National Institute of One Health at Nagpur.
- WHO's AWaRe classification guides which antibiotics to use first and which to reserve.

### Outbreak response — the administrator's checklist

- Detect: IDSP on the Integrated Health Information Platform (syndromic, presumptive and lab-confirmed reporting), media scanning, virus research and diagnostic labs, and genomic surveillance.
- Coordinate: district health society and District Disaster Management Authority (Disaster Management Act 2005), rapid response teams, a control room with a single spokesperson.
- Legal tools: Epidemic Diseases Act 1897 (amended 2020 to punish violence against health workers with up to 5 years, or 7 years for grievous hurt) and state public health laws.
- Contain: isolation, contact tracing, quarantine, containment and buffer zones, ring vaccination, source reduction for mosquitoes (fogging only kills adults), water chlorination and food safety checks.
- Communicate: local-language risk messages, rapid rebuttal of rumours, involvement of religious and community leaders, ASHAs and schools.
- Care and continuity: surge beds, oxygen, triage and referral, PPE and biomedical waste rules (2016) — while keeping routine immunisation, maternal care and TB treatment running.
- Learn: after-action review; build integrated public-health labs and critical-care blocks in every district under PM-ABHIM.
- Equity: reach migrants, slum dwellers, the elderly and disabled; provide mental-health support.

### Punjab angle

- Dengue peaks in Punjab after the monsoon; the state's weekly 'Har Shukarvar Dengue te Vaar' drive targets breeding sites.
- Hepatitis C: Punjab's free treatment model (2016) predated the national programme; injecting drug use spreads hepatitis C and HIV, addressed through OOAT clinics (2017) and the 'Yudh Nashian Virudh' campaign (2025).
- Lumpy skin disease (2022) caused heavy cattle losses; GADVASU supported diagnosis and vaccination drives.
- Floods in 2023 and 2025 required health camps, ORS and zinc, chlorine tablets, and watch for leptospirosis and scrub typhus.
- PGIMER Chandigarh's virology laboratory served as a regional COVID-19 testing hub; government medical colleges at Patiala, Amritsar and Faridkot anchor state testing.
- Mukh Mantri Sehat Yojana (January 2026, Rs 10 lakh family cover) adds financial protection during epidemics.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** What lessons did the COVID-19 pandemic offer for managing future disease outbreaks in India? (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: pandemic as health and governance crisis.
- Lessons: surveillance and testing capacity, oxygen and ICU supply chains, migrant welfare, digital tools (CoWIN), legal clarity.
- Strengths: indigenous vaccines, ASHAs, mass vaccination.
- Gaps: misinformation, uneven district capacity, mental health, private-sector regulation.
- Way forward: Public health cadre, One Health surveillance, district labs, risk communication plans.
- Conclusion: preparedness is cheaper than response.

</details>

**2.** Explain the different types of vaccines with examples. Why does vaccine hesitancy persist and how can it be overcome? (200 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Types: live attenuated, inactivated, subunit, toxoid, vector, mRNA — examples.
- Hesitancy: misinformation, fear of side-effects, religious myths, distrust.
- Solutions: community leaders, ASHAs, transparent adverse-event reporting, local-language messaging.
- Mission Indradhanush, U-WIN tracking.
- Conclusion: trust as core vaccine infrastructure.

</details>

**3.** As a district officer, outline the preventive and control measures you would take during a dengue outbreak in a Punjab city. (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Surveillance: daily case mapping via IDSP/IHIP, hot-spot identification.
- Vector control: source reduction, larvicide, fogging, house-to-house checks with municipal staff.
- Health care: platelet and bed availability, fixed pricing for tests, referral protocol.
- IEC: dry day campaigns, school involvement.
- Coordination: municipal, health, education departments; daily review.
- Conclusion: community participation for sustained control.

</details>
