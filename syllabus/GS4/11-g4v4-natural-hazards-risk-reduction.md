## Short notes

- Floods: causes — heavy rainfall, cloudbursts, encroachment on floodplains, siltation, poor drainage, embankment breaches, dam releases; Rashtriya Barh Ayog (1980) estimated about 40 million ha flood-prone.
- Flood mitigation: structural (dams, reservoirs, embankments, drainage channels) and non-structural (floodplain zoning — CWC model bill, flood forecasting by CWC, early warning, insurance, flood-proof housing).
- Earthquakes: India's seismic zoning by BIS; building codes IS 1893 (seismic design), IS 4326 and IS 13920 (ductile detailing); retrofitting of schools and hospitals; lessons from Bhuj (2001).
- Tsunamis: 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami led to the Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre at INCOIS, Hyderabad (2007); coastal regulation, mangroves and shelter belts reduce impact.
- Landslides: GSI is nodal agency; National Landslide Susceptibility Mapping; causes — slope cutting, deforestation, heavy rain; recent events — Joshimath subsidence (2023), Wayanad (2024).
- Cyclones: IMD early warnings and the National Cyclone Risk Mitigation Project; Odisha's evacuation-based approach after 1999 sharply cut deaths in later cyclones (Phailin 2013, Fani 2019).
- Other hazards: heat waves (Ahmedabad Heat Action Plan 2013 as model), lightning, glacial lake outburst floods (South Lhonak, Sikkim, 2023), drought (Manual for Drought Management).
- Institutional set-up under DM Act 2005: NDMA (chaired by PM), National Executive Committee, SDMA (chaired by CM), DDMA (chaired by District Collector/Deputy Commissioner), NIDM, NDRF.
- DM (Amendment) Act 2025 strengthened NDMA/SDMA roles in planning, provided for Urban Disaster Management Authorities and national and state disaster databases.
- Financing (15th Finance Commission): National and State Disaster Risk Management Funds split 80% response (NDRF/SDRF) and 20% mitigation (NDMF/SDMF); response window split into response and relief, recovery and reconstruction, and preparedness and capacity building.
- Early warning: Common Alerting Protocol (SACHET) for SMS alerts, cell broadcast trials, IMD impact-based forecasts, CWC flood forecasts.
- Community-based DRR: Aapda Mitra volunteers, school safety programmes, mock drills, village disaster management plans, panchayat involvement.
- Risk reduction principles: risk-informed land use, resilient infrastructure (CDRI), insurance and risk transfer, ecosystem-based measures (wetlands, mangroves, forests).
- Recovery: 'build back better' — Post Disaster Needs Assessment, livelihood restoration, psychosocial care.
- Way forward: hazard and vulnerability mapping at village level, enforcement of building codes, floodplain zoning, climate adaptation, last-mile warning and trained local responders.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab's floods are driven by the Sutlej, Beas, Ravi and Ghaggar plus seasonal rivulets (choes) in the Kandi area; dhussi bundhs (earthen embankments) are the main defence.
- Illegal sand mining, encroachment on riverbeds and weak embankments worsened breaches in 2023 and 2025; dam release coordination with BBMB (Bhakra, Pong) is critical.
- The Ghaggar repeatedly floods Sangrur and Patiala districts at its narrow points; cross-border coordination with Haryana is needed.
- Deputy Commissioners chair DDMAs; SDMs lead sub-divisional flood control rooms, evacuation and relief camps.
- Pathankot, Gurdaspur, Hoshiarpur and Rupnagar face higher seismic risk (Zone IV); Kandi belt is prone to soil erosion and flash floods.

## Deep dive

### Floods — data and case studies

- Rashtriya Barh Ayog (1980) put flood-prone area at about 40 million ha; the Ganga and Brahmaputra basins (Bihar, Assam, UP, West Bengal) suffer most.
- Kosi, the 'Sorrow of Bihar', changed course after the 2008 Kusaha embankment breach; the Damodar was the 'Sorrow of Bengal' before DVC.
- Major floods: Mumbai (2005), Uttarakhand/Kedarnath (2013), Chennai (2015), Kerala (2018, nearly 500 deaths), Wayanad landslides (July 2024), Punjab (2023, 2025).
- Floodplain zoning: the CWC model bill (1975) has been enacted only by a few states (Manipur, Rajasthan, Uttarakhand), so encroachment continues.
- Flood Management and Border Areas Programme (2021–26) funds embankments, anti-erosion works and river management.
- GLOF risk: the October 2023 South Lhonak lake outburst in Sikkim destroyed the Teesta-III dam — a warning for Himalayan hydropower.
- Reservoir operation is a recurring controversy: Kerala 2018 and Punjab 2025 both raised questions about pre-monsoon storage levels and release timing.

### Earthquakes, tsunamis and landslides

- Major Indian earthquakes: Shillong (1897), Kangra (1905), Bihar–Nepal (1934), Assam–Tibet (1950, the largest recorded in India), Koyna (1967), Uttarkashi (1991), Latur (1993, a stable-shield quake), Chamoli (1999), Bhuj (26 January 2001), Kashmir (2005), Nepal–Gorkha (2015).
- Magnitude scales measure energy (Richter, moment magnitude); intensity scales (modified Mercalli) measure shaking and damage.
- Building safety: IS 1893:2016 (zoning and seismic design, restored after the 2025 revision was withdrawn), IS 13920 (ductile detailing), National Building Code 2016, retrofitting of schools and hospitals, techno-legal bye-laws.
- Uttarakhand's 'Bhudev' app (with IIT Roorkee) is India's first state earthquake early-warning system.
- Tsunami: the 26 December 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami (M 9.1) killed over 10,000 in India; INCOIS (Hyderabad) has run the Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre since 2007; Venkatraipur and Noliasahi (Odisha) were India's first UNESCO 'Tsunami Ready' communities (2020).
- Landslides: GSI is the nodal agency and runs the national susceptibility mapping and the Bhusanket portal; NRSC's Landslide Atlas (2023) ranked Rudraprayag and Tehri among the most exposed districts; NDMA's National Landslide Risk Management Strategy dates from 2019.
- Joshimath (2023) showed how unplanned construction and drainage on old landslide debris can trigger subsidence.

### Cyclones, heat waves, lightning and cold waves

- Odisha model: after the 1999 super cyclone (about 10,000 deaths) Odisha created India's first SDMA (OSDMA), built multipurpose shelters and community volunteers; mass evacuation kept deaths low in Phailin (2013) and Fani (2019).
- National Cyclone Risk Mitigation Project (World Bank-supported) funded shelters, evacuation roads and warning systems; mangroves and shelter belts reduce surge damage.
- Heat waves: the Ahmedabad Heat Action Plan (2013) is the template; NDMA heat guidelines (2016, revised 2019); heat is not in the centrally notified disaster list, so states notify it locally.
- Lightning killed over 1,310 people in India in 2025 (IMD) — the biggest single weather killer; the 'Damini' app (IITM Pune) warns of strikes.
- Cold waves and dense fog in north India cause road and rail accidents; Punjab's Sadak Suraksha Force (2024) patrols highways.
- IMD now issues impact-based forecasts; Mission Mausam (2024) adds radars, supercomputing and better models.

### Risk-reduction toolkit — technology, finance, community

- Early warning: IMD, CWC flood forecasts, INCOIS tsunami and ocean-state alerts, GSI landslide bulletins, and the Common Alerting Protocol platform SACHET that sends location-based SMS alerts nationwide.
- Community: Aapda Mitra volunteers (piloted 2016, scaled to 350 hazard-prone districts), school and hospital safety programmes, village disaster management plans and regular mock drills.
- Ecosystem-based DRR: mangroves (MISHTI), wetlands as sponges, floodplain restoration ('room for the river' approach from the Netherlands).
- Finance: 80:20 response–mitigation split under the 15th Finance Commission; insurance and catastrophe bonds for risk transfer.
- International: Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (2019, New Delhi) and India's 10-point agenda on DRR (2016); India sends NDRF teams abroad (Operation Dost in Türkiye, 2023).
- Recognition: Subhash Chandra Bose Aapda Prabandhan Puraskar is announced every year on 23 January.

### Punjab — hazard-wise action plan for an SDM

- Before the monsoon: desilt drains and choes, inspect and strengthen dhussi bundhs, map vulnerable villages, pre-position boats, fodder and medicines, and coordinate dam-release information with BBMB and the state irrigation department.
- During floods: run sub-divisional control rooms, evacuate early (with livestock), open relief camps with sanitation and health teams, and use SACHET alerts and gurdwara loudspeakers for warnings.
- 2025 benchmarks (early September): inflow into Ranjit Sagar about 1.7 lakh cusecs, about 2.9 lakh cusecs at Harike, Pong above its 1,390 ft danger mark, and standing crops on over 4 lakh acres submerged.
- After floods: special girdawari for crop loss (₹20,000 per acre in 2025), ₹4 lakh ex gratia for deaths, the 'Jisda Khet Usdi Ret' sand-removal policy, disease surveillance for dengue and diarrhoea, and repair of rural roads.
- Earthquake: retrofit schools and hospitals in Zone IV districts (Pathankot, Gurdaspur, Hoshiarpur, Rupnagar) and enforce building bye-laws.
- Industrial and fire risk: inspections of chemical units and sewer-gas risks (Giaspura 2023), fire-safety audits of factories and firecracker units (Batala 2019).
- Heat and fog: heat action plans with shifted working hours for labourers; fog-season traffic advisories.

### Way forward — key recommendations

- Make risk-informed land use binding: floodplain zoning, riverbed protection from sand mining and encroachment.
- Build hazard and vulnerability maps down to village level and feed them into the new state disaster databases.
- Enforce building codes with third-party certification; retrofit lifeline buildings first.
- Invest in last-mile warning (cell broadcast, sirens, community radio) and trained local responders.
- Integrate climate adaptation with DRR — heat, extreme rain and GLOF risks are rising.
- Use insurance and parametric products to protect farmers and the poor, and link reconstruction to 'build back better'.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** Recurring floods in Punjab expose gaps in both structural and non-structural measures. Suggest a comprehensive flood risk reduction strategy for the state. (250 words)

*Like a real PPSC question: [Mains 2026 · GS4 Q11](../../past-papers/mains_2026_GS4.md#q11)*

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: 2023 and 2025 floods; rivers and Ghaggar.
- Structural gaps: weak dhussi bundhs, choked drains, siltation.
- Non-structural gaps: floodplain encroachment, illegal mining, delayed warnings, dam-release coordination.
- Strategy: embankment audit and strengthening, desilting, floodplain zoning, real-time gauges, SACHET alerts, BBMB protocols.
- Community: village plans, Aapda Mitras, relief camp readiness.
- Finance: SDMF for mitigation, crop insurance.
- Conclusion: shift from relief to risk reduction.

**2.** Discuss the institutional and financial framework for disaster management in India. (200 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>
