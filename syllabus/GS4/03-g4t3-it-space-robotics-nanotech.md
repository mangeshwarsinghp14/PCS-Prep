## Short notes

- Artificial Intelligence: machine learning, deep learning and generative AI (large language models) now used in health diagnostics, agriculture advisories, policing, language translation (Bhashini).
- IndiaAI Mission (approved March 2024, about ₹10,372 crore): compute capacity (GPUs), datasets platform, indigenous foundation models, skilling and safe AI.
- AI risks: bias, deepfakes, opacity (black-box decisions), job displacement, misuse in fraud; need for explainability, human oversight and accountability.
- Telecom: 5G services launched October 2022; Bharat 6G Vision (2023); Telecommunications Act 2023 replaced the Indian Telegraph Act 1885.
- Semiconductors: India Semiconductor Mission (2021) supports fabs, ATMP/OSAT units (e.g. projects in Gujarat and Assam) and design-linked incentives; SCL Mohali is a government fab.
- Computing: National Supercomputing Mission (PARAM machines), cloud computing, edge computing, quantum computing under National Quantum Mission (2023).
- Space reforms: Indian Space Policy 2023; IN-SPACe (2020) authorises private players; NSIL as commercial arm; liberalised FDI in space (2024); private launches (Skyroot's Vikram-S, 2022).
- Recent space milestones: Chandrayaan-3 (2023), Aditya-L1 (2023), SpaDeX docking demonstration (January 2025), Shubhanshu Shukla's Axiom-4 mission to the ISS (2025), NISAR Earth-observation satellite with NASA (2025).
- Future plans: Gaganyaan human spaceflight, Bharatiya Antariksh Station targeted by 2035, Indian on the Moon by 2040.
- Space for governance: land records, crop estimation, disaster warnings, farm-fire monitoring, NavIC navigation, telemedicine via satellite.
- Robotics: industrial robots in auto and electronics manufacturing, surgical robots, robotic sewer cleaning (alternative to manual scavenging), drones for agriculture and delivery.
- Drones: Drone Rules 2021 liberalised use; PLI for drones; Namo Drone Didi gives drones to women SHGs for agricultural services.
- Nanotechnology: materials at 1–100 nanometre scale with new properties; uses in drug delivery, diagnostics, water filters, nano-fertilisers (IFFCO nano urea), coatings and batteries.
- Nano Mission (2007) of DST funds research; concerns include toxicity, environmental persistence and lack of specific regulation.
- Cyber and data governance: IT Act 2000, IT Rules 2021, DPDP Act 2023, CERT-In as nodal cyber agency.
- Way forward: compute and talent investment, ethical AI framework, chip ecosystem, regulatory sandboxes, public–private R&D, and skilling for an automating economy.

## Punjab angle

- Mohali hosts SCL (semiconductor fab) and INST (nanotechnology); Mohali–Chandigarh tri-city has an IT/ITES cluster.
- Satellite and drone surveillance support farm-fire monitoring and crop-loss assessment in Punjab.
- Drones for pesticide spraying are being promoted through PAU and Krishi Vigyan Kendras.
- AI and analytics can help Punjab Police in cyber-crime and drug-network tracking; border drones from Pakistan carrying drugs and arms show the security side of drone tech.

## Deep dive

### Space sector reforms and economy

- Indian Space Policy 2023 sets roles: ISRO focuses on R&D, NSIL (2019) is the commercial arm, IN-SPACe (2020) authorises and promotes private players, and the Department of Space sets policy.
- FDI rules (2024): up to 74% automatic route for satellite manufacturing and operations, 49% for launch vehicles, 100% for components.
- Private milestones: Skyroot's Vikram-S (2022, first private Indian rocket) and Agnikul's Agnibaan SOrTeD (2024), flown with a single-piece 3D-printed engine from a private launch pad at Sriharikota.
- India's space economy, about USD 8.4 billion in 2022, is targeted to reach USD 44 billion by 2033; a Rs 1,000 crore venture fund for space start-ups was approved in October 2024.
- Industry is taking over production: an HAL–L&T consortium builds PSLVs, and SSLV technology is being transferred to industry.
- New infrastructure: a third launch pad at Sriharikota (approved January 2025) for heavier rockets and a second spaceport at Kulasekarapattinam, Tamil Nadu, for small launchers.
- Two PSLV failures (May 2025 and January 2026) show the need for strong quality assurance as launch rates rise and commercial customers depend on reliability.
- Space for governance: NavIC, Bhuvan geoportal, NISAR radar data (frequent repeat imaging), EOS-05's geosynchronous imaging, disaster mapping and farm-fire monitoring.
- India signed the US-led Artemis Accords in June 2023; ISRO aims for 'debris-free space missions' by 2030 and tracks objects through its NETRA space situational awareness system.
- India still lacks a comprehensive national space activities law despite a 2017 draft — a regulatory gap for private players and liability.

### AI, computing and semiconductors — governance angles

- IndiaAI Mission (March 2024) pillars: subsidised GPU compute, IndiaAI Innovation Centre for indigenous foundation models, the AIKosh datasets platform, FutureSkills, start-up financing and safe and trusted AI.
- New Delhi Declaration (AI Impact Summit, February 2026): guided by 'Sarvajan Hitaya, Sarvajan Sukhaya', three sutras — People, Planet, Progress — and seven pillars including trustworthy and energy-efficient AI and democratised AI resources.
- Global context: Bletchley (2023), Seoul (2024) and Paris (February 2025, co-chaired by India) summits preceded New Delhi; the EU AI Act (in force 2024) regulates AI by risk tiers.
- ISM 1.0 (December 2021): Rs 76,000 crore framework with up to 50% fiscal support for fabs, display fabs, compound semiconductors, assembly/testing units and chip design.
- By December 2025, 10 projects worth Rs 1.60 lakh crore were approved in six states, including Tata–PSMC's Dholera fab (Rs 91,526 crore), Micron's Sanand assembly-test unit, Tata's Assam packaging unit and CDIL's Mohali expansion.
- ISM 2.0 (Budget 2026-27, Rs 1,000 crore for the year) targets equipment, materials and full-stack Indian chip IP; India's chip market is projected at USD 100–110 billion by 2030.
- National Supercomputing Mission (2015) built the PARAM series; National Quantum Mission (2023–31) set up thematic hubs for quantum computing, communication, sensing and materials.
- Concerns: GPU and chip import dependence, high water and power needs of fabs and data centres, talent shortage, and data-protection compliance for AI training.

### Robotics, drones and automation

- Robot types: industrial arms, collaborative robots (cobots), service robots, humanoids (ISRO's Vyommitra for Gaganyaan) and surgical robots — SSI Mantra is India's first indigenous surgical robot.
- Sanitation robots such as Bandicoot (Genrobotics, Kerala) clean manholes, supporting the Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers Act 2013 and the NAMASTE scheme (2023).
- Drone Rules 2021 created green, yellow and red airspace zones on the Digital Sky platform; a PLI scheme supports drone makers.
- Namo Drone Didi (2023) provides agricultural drones to women's self-help groups to offer spraying services.
- Other uses: SVAMITVA village property mapping, medical deliveries in hilly areas, infrastructure inspection, and border surveillance.
- Industry 4.0 combines IoT, AI, robotics and cloud; the SAMARTH Udyog Bharat 4.0 initiative builds demonstration centres for manufacturers.
- Concerns: job loss in routine manufacturing, accident liability, data security, and lethal autonomous weapons, which are debated at the UN Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons.

### Nanotechnology — science, uses, risks

- Nanoscale means 1–100 nanometres; quantum effects and a huge surface-to-volume ratio change colour, strength and reactivity (gold nanoparticles look red or purple).
- Origins: Richard Feynman's 1959 lecture 'There's Plenty of Room at the Bottom'; the term 'nanotechnology' by Norio Taniguchi (1974); scanning tunnelling and atomic force microscopes made atoms visible.
- Nano-forms: carbon nanotubes, graphene, quantum dots (Nobel Chemistry 2023; used in QLED screens), liposomes, and metal-organic frameworks (Nobel Chemistry 2025: Kitagawa, Robson, Yaghi) that can capture CO2 or pull water from desert air.
- Medicine: liposomal amphotericin B (kala-azar, mucormycosis), lipid nanoparticles that carry mRNA vaccines, targeted cancer drugs, nano-sensors for diagnosis.
- Agriculture: IFFCO launched Nano Urea (2021) and Nano DAP (2023); independent field trials, including some at PAU, have questioned claims that they can replace granular fertiliser.
- Environment and energy: nano-silver water filters, pollution sensors, better battery electrodes and solar cells.
- India's Nano Mission (DST, 2007) built centres such as INST Mohali; India ranks among the top countries in nanoscience publications.
- Risks: nanoparticles can be inhaled, cross biological barriers and persist in the environment; India issued guidelines for evaluating nanopharmaceuticals (2019) but lacks a dedicated nano-safety law.

### Digital governance — laws to cite

- DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025: consent managers, breach notification, verifiable parental consent for children's data, Data Protection Board, penalties up to Rs 250 crore.
- IT Act 2000 and IT Rules 2021 (intermediary due diligence; Grievance Appellate Committees since 2022); a Digital India Act to replace the IT Act has been under consultation.
- Telecommunications Act 2023; Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025.
- CERT-In 2022 directions: report cyber incidents within six hours and keep logs for 180 days.
- I4C and the 1930 helpline handle cyber fraud; NCIIPC protects critical information infrastructure.
- Balance: regulation must protect rights without chilling innovation — use 'risk-based, principle-led, light-touch but accountable' framing.

### Punjab angle

- SCL Mohali (set up in 1976 as Semiconductor Complex Ltd; badly set back by a 1989 fire) is India's government fab and will be modernised with Rs 4,500 crore while remaining government-owned.
- INST Mohali is a Nano Mission institute; IISER Mohali and IIT Ropar add physics, materials and robotics research.
- IIT Ropar's AWaDH hub develops sensors and IoT for agriculture and water management.
- Drones carrying narcotics and weapons from Pakistan make counter-drone technology a live security issue on the Punjab border.
- Satellite and drone surveillance assist Punjab in farm-fire tracking, crop-loss assessment after floods and land-record surveys.
- The Mohali–Chandigarh tricity IT cluster and Punjab's Industrial and Business Development Policy 2022 aim to attract electronics and IT investment.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** Discuss the opportunities and risks of artificial intelligence for governance in India. What regulatory approach would you recommend? (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: AI as general-purpose technology; IndiaAI Mission 2024.
- Opportunities: grievance analysis, crop and weather advisories, health screening, translation (Bhashini), fraud detection.
- Risks: algorithmic bias, privacy, deepfakes, opacity, accountability gaps, job loss.
- Existing frameworks: IT Act, DPDP Act 2023, CERT-In advisories.
- Recommended approach: risk-based, sector-specific rules, human-in-the-loop for decisions affecting rights, audits, sandboxes.
- Capacity: AI literacy for officials, data quality.
- Conclusion: pro-innovation but rights-protective regulation.

</details>

**2.** Explain how the opening of the space sector to private players has changed India's space ecosystem. (150 words)

*Like a real PPSC question: [Mains 2021 · GS4 Q1](../../past-papers/mains_2021_GS4.md#q1)*

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Reforms: IN-SPACe (2020), NSIL, Indian Space Policy 2023, FDI liberalisation 2024.
- Outcomes: private launch vehicles (Vikram-S), satellite start-ups, ground-station services.
- Benefits: ISRO focuses on R&D and deep-space missions; commercial market share; jobs.
- Concerns: need for a space activities law, liability, debris, security.
- Conclusion: public–private model to scale India's space economy.

</details>

**3.** What is nanotechnology? Discuss its applications in agriculture and health along with associated concerns. (200 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Define: engineering at 1–100 nm; changed physical/chemical properties.
- Agriculture: nano urea and nano DAP, nano-sensors for soil moisture, controlled-release agrochemicals.
- Health: targeted drug delivery, diagnostics, antimicrobial coatings.
- Concerns: toxicity, environmental accumulation, worker safety, regulatory gaps.
- India: Nano Mission 2007, INST Mohali.
- Conclusion: precautionary, evidence-based regulation alongside research support.

</details>
