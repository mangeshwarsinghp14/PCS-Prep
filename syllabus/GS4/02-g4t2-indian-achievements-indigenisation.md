## Short notes

- Early modern science: C.V. Raman (Raman effect, Nobel Physics 1930), S.N. Bose (Bose–Einstein statistics; 'boson' named after him), Meghnad Saha (ionisation equation), J.C. Bose (plant physiology, radio waves), Srinivasa Ramanujan (number theory).
- Nobel laureates of Indian origin in science: Har Gobind Khorana (Medicine 1968, genetic code), S. Chandrasekhar (Physics 1983, stellar evolution), Venkatraman Ramakrishnan (Chemistry 2009, ribosome).
- Institution builders: Homi J. Bhabha (atomic energy, three-stage nuclear programme), Vikram Sarabhai (space programme, ISRO founded 1969), Satish Dhawan, Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar (CSIR).
- A.P.J. Abdul Kalam: SLV-3 project director (Rohini satellite, 1980) and Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (Agni, Prithvi).
- Agriculture and food: M.S. Swaminathan (Green Revolution), Verghese Kurien (Operation Flood/White Revolution), Norman Borlaug's Mexican wheat adapted to Indian conditions.
- Space milestones: Chandrayaan-1 (2008, water signatures on Moon), Mars Orbiter Mission (2013, orbit 2014, first Asian nation at Mars), Chandrayaan-3 soft landing near lunar south pole (23 August 2023, now National Space Day), Aditya-L1 solar observatory (2023).
- Defence indigenisation: LCA Tejas, Arjun tank, Akash and BrahMos (Indo-Russian JV) missiles, INS Vikrant (first indigenous aircraft carrier, commissioned 2022), Positive Indigenisation Lists barring import of listed items.
- Policy tools for indigenisation: Make in India (2014), Atmanirbhar Bharat (2020), Production Linked Incentive schemes, Defence Acquisition Procedure 2020 with Buy (Indian-IDDM) priority, iDEX for defence start-ups.
- Strategic tech missions: India Semiconductor Mission (2021), National Quantum Mission (2023), National Supercomputing Mission (2015, PARAM series), NavIC regional navigation, IndiaAI Mission (2024).
- Health indigenisation: Covaxin (Bharat Biotech with ICMR-NIV), CERVAVAC indigenous HPV vaccine (Serum Institute), low-cost generics making India 'pharmacy of the world'.
- Research ecosystem: Anusandhan National Research Foundation Act 2023 to fund and coordinate research; gross R&D spending remains below 1% of GDP, far below US, China, Israel, South Korea.
- Private R&D share is low; India's patent filings and Global Innovation Index rank (39th in 2024) have improved but basic research funding and industry–academia links remain weak.
- Barriers to new technology: brain drain, procurement rules favouring lowest cost, weak venture capital for deep-tech, slow technology transfer from labs to market, import dependence for electronics and APIs.
- Developing new technology needs a full chain: basic research → applied R&D → prototyping → scale manufacturing → assured public procurement.
- Jugaad vs systemic innovation: frugal innovation (Mangalyaan's low cost) is a strength but must be backed by standards, quality and IP protection.
- Way forward: raise R&D to 2% of GDP over time, tax incentives for private R&D, mission-mode projects, attract diaspora scientists, protect and commercialise IP.

## Punjab angle

- Har Gobind Khorana was born in Raipur village, undivided Punjab (now in Pakistan); paleobotanist Birbal Sahni was also from undivided Punjab.
- Mohali is a science hub: IISER Mohali, Institute of Nano Science and Technology (INST), National Agri-Food Biotechnology Institute (NABI) and the Semi-Conductor Laboratory (SCL) of MeitY.
- PAU Ludhiana's wheat and rice varieties powered the Green Revolution; GADVASU Ludhiana leads veterinary research.
- Punjab's defence-heavy population and Army presence (border state) make defence indigenisation a relatable theme in answers.

Expect questions on why India lags in R&D despite achievements and how to achieve self-reliance; examiners reward specific missions, policy tools and a clear 'way forward' rather than a list of names.

## Deep dive

### Space achievements 2023–2026 (date — event)

- 23 Aug 2023 — Chandrayaan-3 soft-lands near the lunar south pole (India the fourth country to soft-land, first near the south pole).
- 2 Sept 2023 — Aditya-L1 launched; it entered its halo orbit around Sun–Earth L1 on 6 January 2024. 1 Jan 2024 — XPoSat, India's first X-ray polarimetry satellite.
- Sept 2024 — Cabinet approves Chandrayaan-4 (lunar sample return, Rs 2,104 crore), Venus Orbiter Mission (Rs 1,236 crore, launch planned March 2028), the first Bharatiya Antariksh Station module (BAS-1) by December 2028 within an expanded Gaganyaan programme (Rs 20,193 crore) and a Next Generation Launch Vehicle.
- Dec 2024–Jan 2025 — SpaDeX (PSLV-C60) makes India the fourth country to dock two satellites in orbit.
- 29 Jan 2025 — GSLV-F15/NVS-02 is ISRO's 100th launch from Sriharikota, though the navigation satellite could not reach its planned orbit.
- 18 May 2025 — PSLV-C61/EOS-09 lost after a third-stage pressure drop; 12 Jan 2026 — PSLV-C62/EOS-N1 also failed in the third stage — two rare setbacks for ISRO's workhorse rocket.
- 25 June–15 July 2025 — Group Captain Shubhanshu Shukla flies on Axiom-4, the first Indian on the International Space Station and the second Indian in space after Rakesh Sharma (1984).
- 30 July 2025 — GSLV-F16 launches NASA–ISRO NISAR, a dual-band radar imaging satellite.
- 24 Dec 2025 — LVM3-M6 places AST SpaceMobile's BlueBird Block-2, the heaviest payload launched by LVM3 from Indian soil, through NSIL's commercial service.
- Sept 2026 — GSLV-F17 places EOS-05, India's first imaging satellite for geosynchronous orbit.
- Late 2026 — the uncrewed Gaganyaan G1 flight carrying the humanoid Vyommitra is targeted (ISRO chief, October 2026); the first crewed flight is planned for 2027.

### Defence and strategic indigenisation

- Agni-5 with multiple independently targetable re-entry vehicles was tested under Mission Divyastra (11 March 2024).
- Operation Sindoor (May 2025) showcased indigenous systems such as the Akash surface-to-air missile, BrahMos and integrated air-defence networks.
- Platforms: INS Vikrant (2022), the nuclear submarine INS Arighaat (2024), Tejas Mk1A, the Prachand light combat helicopter and the ATAGS artillery gun.
- Defence production reached about Rs 1.5 lakh crore and exports about Rs 23,600 crore in 2024-25, both records.
- Policy tools: Positive Indigenisation Lists (import bans on listed items), Buy (Indian-IDDM) priority, iDEX for start-ups, DRDO's Technology Development Fund and defence corridors in Uttar Pradesh and Tamil Nadu.
- Mission Sudarshan Chakra (announced 15 August 2025) aims to build a nationwide air-defence shield by 2035.
- Gaps remain in jet engines, advanced semiconductors and some sensors — the main targets of future indigenisation.

### Health, biotech and technology firsts

- Vaccines and therapies: Covaxin; ZyCoV-D (2021, world's first DNA vaccine for humans); CERVAVAC HPV vaccine (2022); NexCAR19 CAR-T cell therapy (2023).
- AdFalciVax, an ICMR multi-stage malaria vaccine candidate against Plasmodium falciparum, was licensed to five Indian manufacturers in 2025.
- BIRSA-101 (November 2025): CSIR-IGIB's CRISPR-based (enFnCas9) gene therapy for sickle cell disease, transferred to Serum Institute for scale-up.
- Genome India released data on 10,000 genomes in January 2025; ICAR released the world's first genome-edited rice varieties in May 2025.
- Animal health: Biolumpivaxin, a lumpy-skin-disease vaccine by Biovet with ICAR, was approved in February 2025; GauSort indigenous sex-sorted semen technology claims up to 90% accuracy.
- Chips and computing: ISRO–SCL Vikram-3201 processor (2025), PARAM Rudra supercomputers (2024), QpiAI's 25-qubit 'Indus' quantum computer (2025).
- Infrastructure engineering: Kavach train protection, the Chenab rail bridge (world's highest railway arch bridge, opened June 2025), and indigenous 700 MW nuclear reactors.

### Indian scientists — extended table (name — contribution — hook)

- Prafulla Chandra Ray — founded Bengal Chemicals (1901); 'father of Indian chemistry'.
- Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar (born Bhera, Punjab, 1894) — first Director-General of CSIR; India's top young-scientist prize is named after him.
- Birbal Sahni (born Bhera, 1891) — palaeobotany; founded the institute in Lucknow that bears his name.
- Har Gobind Khorana (born Raipur, undivided Punjab, 1922) — cracked the genetic code (Nobel 1968) and made the first synthetic gene.
- Kalpana Chawla (born in Karnal, then in Punjab) — first India-born woman in space (1997); died in the Columbia disaster (2003).
- Narinder Singh Kapany (born Moga, 1926) — pioneer of fibre optics; Yash Pal (born Jhang, 1926) — cosmic-ray physicist and science communicator.
- Janaki Ammal (sugarcane hybrids, plant cytogenetics), Anna Mani (weather instruments), Kamala Sohonie (first Indian woman PhD in science), Asima Chatterjee (plant-based anti-epileptic and anti-malarial drugs).
- Gagandeep Kang — rotavirus vaccine research; first Indian woman elected Fellow of the Royal Society (2019). Tessy Thomas — 'missile woman', led Agni-IV.
- M.S. Swaminathan (Bharat Ratna 2024) and C.N.R. Rao (Bharat Ratna 2014); the Rashtriya Vigyan Puraskar (instituted 2023) gave its first Vigyan Ratna to biochemist G. Padmanabhan (2024).

### R&D ecosystem — data and reforms

- India's gross expenditure on R&D is only about 0.64% of GDP, with the private sector contributing roughly a third — far below leading innovation economies.
- Patent applications crossed 1,10,000 in 2024-25, with Indian residents filing over 61% — evidence of a rising domestic innovation base.
- Anusandhan National Research Foundation (Act 2023) is to channel about Rs 50,000 crore over five years, most of it from non-government sources.
- A Research, Development and Innovation scheme with a Rs 1 lakh crore corpus (approved July 2025) gives long-term low-interest finance for private R&D.
- One Nation One Subscription (approved November 2024) gives all public institutions access to major journals from 2025.
- Mission budgets: National Quantum Mission (about Rs 6,000 crore, 2023–31), IndiaAI (about Rs 10,372 crore), India Semiconductor Mission (Rs 76,000 crore incentive framework), National Green Hydrogen Mission, Nuclear Energy Mission (Rs 20,000 crore for small modular reactors).
- Persistent barriers: brain drain, lowest-cost procurement rules, weak industry–academia links, scarce deep-tech venture capital and slow lab-to-market transfer.

### Punjab angle

- Punjab-born scientists to cite: Khorana, Bhatnagar, Birbal Sahni, Kapany, Yash Pal and Kalpana Chawla.
- Mohali knowledge cluster: IISER Mohali (2007), Institute of Nano Science and Technology, BRIC-National Agri-Food and Biomanufacturing Institute (formed by merging NABI and CIAB, inaugurated October 2024 as India's first biomanufacturing institute), and the Semi-Conductor Laboratory.
- SCL Mohali will get a Rs 4,500 crore modernisation over three years (announced November 2025) to raise wafer output many times; CDIL Mohali's power-chip expansion was approved under ISM in August 2025.
- Chandigarh tricity adds PGIMER, CSIR-IMTECH and Punjab Engineering College; IIT Ropar hosts the AWaDH technology hub.
- PAU Ludhiana (1962) drove the Green Revolution; GADVASU Ludhiana (2005) leads veterinary science.
- Challenge: large-scale youth emigration on study visas drains talent that could feed Punjab's own research and start-up base.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** India's achievements in space are widely celebrated, yet its overall R&D ecosystem lags behind global leaders. Analyse the reasons and suggest measures. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: ISRO milestones — Chandrayaan-3, Mars Orbiter Mission, Aditya-L1.
- Why space succeeded: mission focus, stable leadership, frugal engineering, strategic priority.
- Why broader R&D lags: GERD below 1% of GDP, low private share, weak industry–academia link, brain drain.
- Systemic issues: procurement rules, slow lab-to-market transfer, limited deep-tech VC.
- Measures: ANRF, mission-mode programmes, R&D tax incentives, assured procurement, diaspora fellowships.
- Punjab angle: Mohali cluster (IISER, INST, NABI, SCL) as a regional innovation hub.
- Conclusion: replicate ISRO's mission culture across sectors.

</details>

**2.** What is meant by indigenisation of technology? Examine its significance for India's defence and strategic sectors. (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Define: replacing imported technology with domestically designed and manufactured equivalents.
- Significance: strategic autonomy, supply-chain security, foreign exchange savings, jobs.
- Examples: Tejas, INS Vikrant, Akash, Positive Indigenisation Lists, iDEX.
- Challenges: engine and semiconductor dependence, cost overruns, quality gaps.
- Way forward: DAP 2020 priorities, private sector and MSME participation, defence corridors.
- Conclusion: indigenisation as a long-term capability, not import substitution alone.

</details>

**3.** Discuss the contribution of any three Indian scientists to the development of science and technology in India and the lessons their careers offer for today's research policy. (200 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Choose three, e.g. Homi Bhabha, Vikram Sarabhai, M.S. Swaminathan.
- Bhabha: atomic energy and three-stage nuclear plan; institution building (TIFR, BARC).
- Sarabhai: space for development — communication, weather, education.
- Swaminathan: Green Revolution linking lab science to farm productivity (Punjab as key site).
- Lessons: autonomy for institutions, long-term funding, problem-driven research, nurturing talent.
- Conclusion: today's policies (ANRF, missions) should embed these lessons.

</details>
