## Short notes

- Frame: S&T shapes daily life through four channels — communication, health, energy/mobility and governance; answers should weigh benefits against new risks (privacy, fraud, divide, e-waste).
- Digital public infrastructure (DPI): Aadhaar (identity), UPI (payments, launched 2016 by NPCI), DigiLocker, eSign, Account Aggregator — the 'India Stack' model now promoted abroad.
- JAM trinity (Jan Dhan–Aadhaar–Mobile) enables Direct Benefit Transfer, cutting leakages and ghost beneficiaries in welfare schemes.
- Health tech: eSanjeevani teleconsultation, CoWIN (vaccination, later U-WIN for routine immunisation), Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission health IDs (ABHA), point-of-care diagnostics.
- Energy: LED bulbs under UJALA (2015) cut household bills; rooftop solar via PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana (2024) targets 1 crore households; EVs under FAME/PM E-DRIVE.
- Mobility and commerce: FASTag electronic tolling, ONDC (open e-commerce network), GPS/NavIC-based navigation, app-based mobility.
- Agriculture in daily life: weather and market advisories by SMS/app, Soil Health Cards, e-NAM online mandis, drones for spraying.
- Food and household science: pasteurisation, refrigeration/cold chain, fortified foods (iodised salt, fortified rice), water purifiers (RO/UV), pressure cookers and induction cooking.
- Governance: e-office, online land records (DILRMP), GeM procurement, CCTNS for police, online grievance portals (CPGRAMS) — transparency and speed.
- Negative effects: cyber fraud and digital arrest scams, deepfakes and misinformation, screen addiction, sedentary lifestyle, job displacement by automation.
- Privacy: K.S. Puttaswamy (2017) made privacy a fundamental right under Article 21; Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 sets consent-based data processing.
- E-waste: India is among the largest generators; E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022 use Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR).
- Digital divide: gaps by gender, rural–urban location, age and language; remedies include BharatNet, vernacular interfaces, Common Service Centres and digital literacy (PMGDISHA).
- Ethical lens: technology is value-neutral but its use is not — inclusion, consent, accountability and human oversight should guide adoption.
- Scientific temper is a Fundamental Duty under Article 51A(h); public awareness of science counters superstition and health misinformation.
- Way forward: cyber hygiene campaigns, grievance redress for digital failures (offline fallback), local-language content, safe AI rules, repair-and-recycle ecosystems.

## Punjab angle

- Sewa Kendras deliver hundreds of citizen services from single-window centres; doorstep delivery of services via helpline 1076 (launched December 2023).
- Aam Aadmi Clinics (from 15 August 2022) use digital records and tele-linked diagnostics for primary care.
- Punjab Remote Sensing Centre, Ludhiana uses satellite data to track farm fires; PAU Ludhiana runs farmer advisories and machinery such as the Happy Seeder.
- Pushpa Gujral Science City, Kapurthala promotes science awareness among students.
- Punjab is among states hit by cyber frauds and fake immigration/travel-agent scams — a ready example of tech misuse needing awareness and policing.

## How PPSC asks it

Questions ask how a technology changes daily life or governance and its downsides; reward a balanced answer with Indian examples (UPI, DBT, telemedicine), one law/judgment (Puttaswamy, DPDP Act) and a Punjab touch.

## Deep dive

### Latest data points for answers (with year)

- UPI handled a record 20.70 billion transactions worth Rs 27.28 lakh crore in October 2025 — the clearest proof of technology in daily life.
- India ranked 38th in WIPO's Global Innovation Index 2025 (81st in 2015, 48th in 2020) and first among lower-middle-income economies.
- 5G services began on 1 October 2022; BSNL's 4G built on an indigenous TCS–Tejas–C-DOT stack went live nationwide in September 2025.
- DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025, making the 2023 data protection law operational in phases over 12–18 months.
- India hosted the AI Impact Summit in New Delhi in February 2026; 88 countries and organisations adopted the New Delhi Declaration built on 'People, Planet, Progress'.
- Ten India Semiconductor Mission projects worth about Rs 1.60 lakh crore in six states had been approved by December 2025; Micron's Sanand plant began commercial output of memory modules in early 2026; ISM 2.0 was announced in Budget 2026-27.
- Non-fossil sources crossed half of India's installed power capacity in 2025, five years ahead of the 2030 pledge.
- Space in daily life: NavIC in phones, weather satellites for cyclone warnings, NISAR (launched 30 July 2025) for crops, floods and land subsidence, and EOS-05 (2026) for near-continuous imaging from geosynchronous orbit.

### How S&T has changed daily life — sector matrix

- Health: teleconsultation (eSanjeevani), AI-assisted X-ray reading for TB, molecular point-of-care tests (Truenat), digital health IDs (ABHA), U-WIN vaccination records, low-cost generics.
- Agriculture: Kisan drones, satellite-based crop and yield estimates for insurance, AgriStack farmer IDs under the Digital Agriculture Mission (2024), e-NAM mandis, SMS weather advisories.
- Finance: UPI, Aadhaar-enabled payments, Account Aggregators for consent-based credit, Direct Benefit Transfer that cut ghost beneficiaries.
- Education: DIKSHA, SWAYAM and PM eVIDYA; the COVID-19 school closures exposed the device and connectivity divide.
- Transport: FASTag, indigenous Vande Bharat trains, Kavach (indigenous train-collision avoidance), EVs, DigiYatra face-based airport entry.
- Household: LED lighting, LPG (Ujjwala, 2016), induction cooking, RO/UV purifiers, smart prepaid meters, rooftop solar under PM Surya Ghar.
- Governance: e-Office, GeM procurement, e-Courts, CCTNS for police, drone-based SVAMITVA property cards for villages.
- Disaster alerts: cell-broadcast emergency messages (C-DOT), the SACHET common alerting platform, Damini lightning alerts and IMD's impact-based forecasts.

### Science behind everyday products (concept → application)

- Microwave oven — dielectric heating of water; refrigerator — evaporation and vapour compression; refrigerants moved from CFCs to HFCs to low-warming gases under the Montreal Protocol and its Kigali Amendment (2016).
- Touchscreens use capacitance; GPS clocks must correct for relativity; QR codes (Japan, 1994) carry UPI payments.
- Pasteurisation, UHT milk, freeze-drying and food irradiation extend shelf life; fortified rice and salt fight hidden hunger.
- Biotechnology at home: enzymes in detergents, probiotics, insulin pens, pregnancy test kits that detect the hormone hCG with antibodies.
- Materials: lithium-ion batteries, LEDs, solar panels, non-stick PTFE pans, plastics and the shift to bioplastics.
- Vaccines and the cold chain, oral rehydration solution and water chlorination are among the cheapest life-saving technologies.

### Concerns and ethics — balanced answer kit

- Cyber fraud: 'digital arrest' calls, mule bank accounts, SIM-swap and fake investment apps; losses run into thousands of crores a year (I4C).
- Misinformation and deepfakes threaten elections and public order; platforms are held to due-diligence duties under the IT Rules 2021.
- Screen addiction: WHO recognised 'gaming disorder' in ICD-11 (2019); the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act 2025 banned real-money online games.
- Jobs and gig work: automation displaces routine tasks; the Code on Social Security 2020 recognises gig and platform workers; e-Shram registers informal workers.
- Privacy and surveillance: Puttaswamy (2017) tests of legality, necessity and proportionality apply to facial recognition and data collection; DigiYatra must stay voluntary.
- Environmental cost of digital life: data centres and AI training use large amounts of power and water.
- Ethical lens: technology should pass Gandhi's talisman — does it help the poorest? Scientific temper is a Fundamental Duty (Article 51A(h)).

### Punjab — applications to cite

- Satellite monitoring of farm fires by Punjab Remote Sensing Centre, Ludhiana: recorded fire events fell from about 71,000 in 2021 to about 11,000 in 2024, aiding enforcement and straw-management planning.
- Mukh Mantri Sehat Yojana (launched 22 January 2026) uses digital enrolment and cashless claims for Rs 10 lakh family cover; Aam Aadmi Clinics keep electronic records and e-prescriptions.
- Sewa Kendras and doorstep delivery of services via helpline 1076 (December 2023) show e-governance reaching citizens.
- Anti-drone systems and thermal cameras along the border counter drones carrying drugs and weapons from Pakistan.
- Pushpa Gujral Science City, Kapurthala (space and aviation gallery, digital planetarium, climate change theatre) spreads scientific temper.
- Fake immigration-agent and online job scams are a Punjab-specific misuse of technology needing awareness and policing.

### Answer skeleton and keywords

- Intro: define S&T's role with one statistic (UPI or DPI). Body: sector-wise benefits; risks; safeguards. Close with inclusive, ethical technology.
- Always add one law (DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025), one judgment (Puttaswamy 2017), one statistic and one Punjab example.
- Keywords: 'techade', leapfrogging, last-mile delivery, phygital, privacy by design, human in the loop, Antyodaya through technology.
- Way forward: digital literacy, grievance redress with offline fallback (e.g. when biometric authentication fails at ration shops), cyber hygiene, local-language interfaces (Bhashini), green IT.
- Avoid listing gadgets; examiners reward linking technology to equity, governance and citizen welfare.
- Conclude with a balanced line: technology is a tool whose value depends on institutions, ethics and access.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** Digital public infrastructure has transformed service delivery in India. Discuss its impact on the common citizen and the challenges it creates. (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: define DPI — open, interoperable digital rails (Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker).
- Impact: financial inclusion and small-ticket digital payments; DBT reducing leakages; paperless documents.
- Governance: speed, transparency, reduced discretion; Punjab Sewa Kendras and 1076 doorstep services.
- Challenges: cyber fraud, data privacy, exclusion due to authentication failure, digital divide for elderly and rural women.
- Safeguards: DPDP Act 2023, offline fallback, grievance redress, digital literacy.
- Conclusion: DPI must remain citizen-centric with trust as its foundation.

</details>

**2.** Technology is often said to be a double-edged sword. Illustrate with examples from health, communication and energy in everyday life. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: technology amplifies human intent; outcomes depend on governance.
- Health: telemedicine and vaccines vs misinformation, over-reliance on self-medication apps.
- Communication: smartphones and social media connect and inform vs deepfakes, addiction, fraud.
- Energy: LED/solar lowering bills vs e-waste and battery disposal issues.
- Cross-cutting: privacy (Puttaswamy), environmental footprint, jobs.
- Way forward: regulation, awareness, ethical design, scientific temper (Art 51A(h)).
- Conclusion: harness benefits while building institutions to manage risks.

</details>

**3.** As an SDM, how would you use technology to improve citizen service delivery in your sub-division while ensuring that no one is left behind? (200 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Map services and pain points; track pendency through dashboards.
- Online appointments, SMS updates, WhatsApp-based grievance tracking, e-office for files.
- Use Sewa Kendras and Common Service Centres as assisted digital access points.
- Inclusion: help desks for elderly/disabled, Punjabi-language interfaces, camps in villages.
- Data protection: access control, consent, staff training.
- Feedback loop: citizen ratings, monthly review, social audit.
- Conclusion: technology as enabler, empathy as the core.

</details>
