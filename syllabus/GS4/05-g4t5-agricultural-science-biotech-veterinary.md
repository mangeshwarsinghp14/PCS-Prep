## Short notes

- Green Revolution (mid-1960s): high-yielding wheat (Mexican dwarf varieties via Norman Borlaug) and rice (IR-8), with assured irrigation, fertilisers and MSP procurement; Punjab, Haryana and western UP led.
- Impact: food self-sufficiency and buffer stocks; but regional inequality, wheat–paddy monoculture, groundwater depletion, soil nutrient imbalance and pesticide overuse.
- Second-generation agri science: precision farming, micro-irrigation (PMKSY 'Per Drop More Crop'), Soil Health Cards (2015), laser land levelling, short-duration and climate-resilient varieties.
- Biofortification: ICAR and state universities have released zinc-, iron- and protein-rich varieties of wheat, rice, millets to tackle hidden hunger.
- Biotechnology in crops: Bt cotton (approved 2002) is India's only commercially cultivated GM crop; GEAC approved environmental release of GM mustard DMH-11 in 2022, but the matter remains before the Supreme Court (split verdict 2024).
- Genome editing: India exempted SDN-1 and SDN-2 genome-edited plants (no foreign DNA) from GM rules in 2022; ICAR released the world's first two genome-edited rice varieties, DRR Dhan 100 (Kamala) and Pusa DST Rice 1, in May 2025.
- Other biotech applications: tissue culture (banana, potato seed), marker-assisted breeding, biofertilisers and biopesticides, diagnostic kits for plant and animal disease.
- BioE3 Policy (2024) of the Department of Biotechnology promotes bio-manufacturing for economy, environment and employment.
- Agri-tech: drones for spraying, satellite crop-yield estimation, e-NAM, Digital Agriculture Mission (2024), AI-based pest alerts.
- Livestock: India is the world's largest milk producer; Rashtriya Gokul Mission (2014) for indigenous breeds; sex-sorted semen and IVF/embryo transfer to raise female calf births and productivity.
- Animal disease control: National Animal Disease Control Programme (2019) for foot-and-mouth disease and brucellosis vaccination; Lumpy Skin Disease outbreak (2022) led to the indigenous Lumpi-ProVacInd vaccine by ICAR institutes.
- Animal biotech milestones: cloned buffalo calves at ICAR-NDRI Karnal; Animal Husbandry Infrastructure Development Fund supports dairy and feed plants.
- One Health approach: links human, animal and environmental health — zoonoses (avian influenza, rabies), antimicrobial resistance from veterinary antibiotic misuse.
- Concerns: biosafety and gene flow from GM crops, farmer dependence on seed companies, IPR on seeds, residue limits for exports, cattle stray menace.
- Way forward: crop diversification, public-sector biotech with farmers' rights, strengthened extension (KVKs), veterinary infrastructure, evidence-based GM regulation.

## Punjab angle

- PAU Ludhiana's short-duration paddy PR-126 saves water and eases residue management; Punjab gives ₹1,500 per acre incentive for Direct Seeded Rice (DSR).
- Punjab Preservation of Subsoil Water Act 2009 fixes paddy transplanting dates to cut summer groundwater draft.
- Crop residue machinery (Happy Seeder, Super SMS, surface seeder) under central Crop Residue Management scheme; PAU-developed technology.
- GADVASU Ludhiana leads veterinary research; Lumpy Skin Disease (2022) caused heavy cattle losses in Punjab; dairy co-operative Verka (Milkfed) is a model for value addition.
- Punjab's Sahiwal cattle and Murrah buffalo germplasm are central to breed improvement.

## Deep dive

### Crop science and policy — latest

- Genome-edited rice (May 2025): DRR Dhan 100 'Kamala' (derived from Samba Mahsuri, higher yield and earlier maturity) and Pusa DST Rice 1 (tolerates saline and alkaline soils) — the first such rice varieties released anywhere.
- In 2022 India exempted SDN-1 and SDN-2 genome-edited plants (with no foreign DNA) from the strict GM rules of 1989.
- GM mustard DMH-11 (Delhi University, barnase–barstar hybrid system) was cleared for environmental release by GEAC in October 2022; on 23 July 2024 the Supreme Court gave a split verdict and asked the Centre to frame a national GM crop policy after consulting states, farmers and experts.
- Bt cotton remains the only commercial GM crop; pink bollworm has developed resistance and illegal herbicide-tolerant cotton seed circulates.
- In August 2024 the Prime Minister released 109 climate-resilient and biofortified varieties of 61 crops.
- National Mission on Natural Farming (November 2024, Rs 2,481 crore) aims to reach 1 crore farmers.
- PM Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana (approved 16 July 2025): Rs 24,000 crore a year for six years in 100 low-productivity districts, converging 36 schemes.
- Digital Agriculture Mission (September 2024) builds AgriStack — farmer IDs, a digital crop survey and a Krishi Decision Support System.
- Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses (from 2025-26) seeks self-sufficiency in pulses through seeds, procurement and area expansion.

### Agri-biotechnology beyond GM crops

- Biofertilisers (Rhizobium, Azotobacter, Azospirillum, phosphate-solubilising bacteria, mycorrhiza) and biopesticides (Bt sprays, Trichoderma, nuclear polyhedrosis virus) cut chemical use.
- IARI's Pusa decomposer, a fungal consortium sprayed on paddy stubble, speeds in-field decomposition as an alternative to burning.
- Tissue culture supplies disease-free banana, potato and sugarcane planting material under DBT's certification system; aeroponics produces seed-potato mini-tubers.
- Marker-assisted selection, speed breeding and doubled haploids shorten breeding cycles; biofortification adds zinc, iron or protein to staples.
- BioE3 Policy (August 2024) promotes bio-manufacturing: bio-based chemicals and enzymes, smart proteins, precision biotherapeutics, climate-resilient agriculture, carbon capture, marine and space biotech, with biofoundries and Bio-AI hubs.
- BRIC-National Agri-Food and Biomanufacturing Institute, Mohali (October 2024), merged NABI and CIAB; NABI earlier developed anthocyanin-rich coloured (black, purple, blue) wheat.
- Second-generation ethanol and compressed biogas from crop residues link biotechnology to clean energy and stubble management.

### Veterinary and animal science — latest

- 20th Livestock Census (2019): 535.78 million livestock including 302.79 million bovines; the 21st census was launched in October 2024.
- Milk output rose from 146.31 million tonnes (2014-15) to 239.30 million tonnes (2023-24), about 64% in ten years; India is the world's largest milk producer.
- Yield of indigenous and non-descript cattle rose from 927 to about 1,343 kg per animal per year between 2014-15 and 2024-25; buffalo yield from 1,880 to about 2,365 kg.
- National Animal Disease Control Programme (September 2019): twice-yearly FMD vaccination of all susceptible livestock and one-time brucellosis vaccination of 4–8-month female calves; over 147 crore FMD doses given by 2026.
- Results: FMD outbreaks fell from 132 (2019) to 40 (2025) and brucellosis outbreaks from 22 to 15; no FMD Asia-1 serotype outbreak for three years; NADCP is now part of the Livestock Health and Disease Control Programme (Rs 2,010 crore for 2026-27).
- Bharat Pashudhan portal has over 39 crore ear-tagged animals; over 4,000 mobile veterinary units provide doorstep treatment.
- Lumpy skin disease (a capripox virus spread by biting insects) killed large numbers of cattle in 2022; vaccines include the indigenous Lumpi-ProVacInd (ICAR, 2022) and Biolumpivaxin (Biovet, approved February 2025).
- Rashtriya Gokul Mission (2014) conserves indigenous breeds; indigenous GauSort sex-sorted semen (up to 90% accuracy) is produced at a Purnea, Bihar, facility inaugurated in September 2025; IVF and embryo transfer multiply elite animals.
- ICAR-NDRI Karnal produced India's first cloned Gir calf, 'Ganga', in 2023, building on earlier buffalo clones.
- One Health: zoonoses (bird flu, rabies), antibiotic residues and AMR from livestock; colistin was banned as a growth promoter in 2019.

### Impact assessment — Green Revolution legacy

- Gains: food self-sufficiency, buffer stocks behind the National Food Security Act 2013 and free grain under PMGKAY, rising rural incomes in irrigated states.
- Groundwater: Punjab extracts well over 150% of its annual recharge (CGWB assessments), the highest in India, with most blocks over-exploited.
- Soil: low organic carbon, zinc and other micronutrient deficiencies, and a skewed NPK ratio due to excess urea.
- Pesticides: residues, resistance in whitefly and pink bollworm, and health concerns in the cotton belt.
- Biodiversity: rice–wheat monoculture displaced pulses, millets, oilseeds and traditional varieties.
- Air quality: paddy-residue burning adds to the October–November smog over north India; the Commission for Air Quality Management (2021) coordinates action.
- M.S. Swaminathan's call for an 'evergreen revolution' — productivity without ecological harm — frames the way forward.

### Punjab — data and institutions

- PAU's short-duration PR-126 paddy saves water and leaves a longer window for straw management; zinc-biofortified PBW Zn1 wheat addresses hidden hunger.
- Pusa-44 (about 143 days, extra puddling) was banned for 2024; an April 2025 order also banned hybrid paddy, but the Punjab and Haryana High Court (18 August 2025) quashed the ban on notified hybrids while upholding it for non-notified seed.
- Punjab pays Rs 1,500 an acre for direct-seeded rice and fixes transplanting dates under the Punjab Preservation of Subsoil Water Act 2009.
- Satellite-recorded farm fires fell from about 71,000 (2021) to about 11,000 (2024), helped by subsidised straw-management machines and CBG demand such as Verbio's Lehragaga plant.
- GADVASU Ludhiana (2005) leads veterinary research and disease diagnosis; Punjab's Sahiwal cattle and Murrah buffalo are prized dairy breeds; Verka (Milkfed) is the state dairy co-operative.
- Mohali–Chandigarh biotech base: BRIC-NABI, CSIR-IMTECH (home of the national Microbial Type Culture Collection) and IISER Mohali.
- The 2025 floods hit crops and livestock hard — agriculture contracted about 2.5% in 2025-26 (Punjab Economic Survey) — raising risks of animal disease and fodder shortage.

### Way forward

- Diversify towards maize, cotton, pulses, oilseeds and horticulture with assured procurement or price-deficiency support.
- Frame the national GM crop policy the Supreme Court asked for; strengthen biosafety testing, labelling and post-release monitoring; treat genome editing on evidence.
- Keep strong public-sector breeding so farmers are not dependent on a few seed firms; protect farmers' rights under PPV&FR.
- Expand veterinary manpower, cold chains for vaccines and disease-free zones to open export markets.
- Use digital agriculture with farmer data protection and consent.
- Integrate One Health surveillance across animal husbandry, health and environment departments.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** The Green Revolution made Punjab India's granary but at an ecological cost. How can modern agricultural science help achieve a second, sustainable transformation? (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: Green Revolution gains and Punjab's role.
- Costs: groundwater over-exploitation, soil health decline, residue burning, pesticide load, health concerns.
- Science solutions: short-duration varieties (PR-126), DSR, micro-irrigation, biofortified and climate-resilient crops, residue management machines.
- Diversification: maize, pulses, horticulture, dairy with assured markets.
- Biotech and genome editing with biosafety safeguards.
- Institutions: PAU, KVKs, digital advisories, crop insurance.
- Conclusion: shift from yield-maximisation to resource-efficiency and income.

</details>

**2.** Examine the debate over genetically modified crops in India with reference to Bt cotton and GM mustard. (200 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Bt cotton (2002): yield and pesticide gains initially; later pink bollworm resistance.
- GM mustard DMH-11: GEAC approval 2022; litigation and split SC verdict 2024.
- Arguments for: productivity, edible oil import reduction, reduced pesticide.
- Arguments against: biosafety, gene flow, herbicide tolerance concerns, corporate control.
- Genome editing exemption (2022) as middle path.
- Conclusion: transparent, science-based, participatory regulation.

</details>

**3.** Discuss recent developments in veterinary and animal sciences in India and their relevance for doubling farmers' income. (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Livestock as risk buffer and income source.
- Developments: sex-sorted semen, IVF, Rashtriya Gokul Mission, NADCP vaccination, Lumpy Skin vaccine.
- Dairy co-operatives (Verka) for value addition.
- Challenges: fodder, disease surveillance, stray cattle, veterinary manpower.
- Conclusion: livestock as pillar of diversification in Punjab.

</details>
