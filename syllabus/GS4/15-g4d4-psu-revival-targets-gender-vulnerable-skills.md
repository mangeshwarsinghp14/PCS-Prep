## Short notes

- Decision framework for reviving a loss-making PSU: diagnose (financial ratios, debt, cash flow; operations — capacity use, manpower cost, technology; governance — interference, pricing freezes) → choose strategy → set milestones → monitor → decide on continuation, restructuring, JV or exit.
- Revival levers: financial restructuring tied to performance milestones, cost rationalisation, VRS with consultation, technology upgrade, asset monetisation of idle land, route/product rationalisation, professional board with independent directors, MoU-based performance targets with autonomy.
- Examples: BSNL revival packages (2019, 2022, 2023) including 4G spectrum; BSNL reported a quarterly net profit in 2024-25 for the first time in about 17 years; Air India strategic sale to the Tata group completed in 2022; New Public Sector Enterprise Policy (2021) distinguishes strategic and non-strategic sectors.
- Planning and target achievement: SMART targets, Results Framework, PERT/CPM and Gantt charts, key performance indicators, dashboards, weekly reviews, risk register, outcome budgeting (Union outcome budget since 2005-06).
- Target pitfalls: data fudging and 'target-chasing' (Goodhart's law), ignoring quality, unrealistic deadlines; use verified data, outcome indicators and incentives for honest reporting.
- Gender — constitutional base: Articles 14, 15(3), 39, 42, 51A(e), 243D and 243T (one-third reservation in PRIs and ULBs); Constitution (106th Amendment) Act 2023 (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam) for one-third reservation in Lok Sabha and Assemblies after delimitation.
- Gender laws: Dowry Prohibition Act 1961, PCPNDT Act 1994, Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005, POSH Act 2013 (Internal Committee where 10 or more employees; Local Committee constituted by the District Officer).
- Gender schemes: Beti Bachao Beti Padhao (2015), Mission Shakti (Sambal and Samarthya, including One Stop Centres and Women Helpline 181), PM Matru Vandana Yojana, DAY-NRLM self-help groups, gender budgeting (since 2005-06).
- Gender sensitisation: training of police, revenue and health staff; women help desks in police stations; gender audit of offices; safe public transport and lighting; engaging men and boys.
- Vulnerable sections — laws: SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989 (amended 2015 and 2018); Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016 (21 disabilities, 4% job reservation); Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2019; Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007 (Maintenance Tribunal presided over by an SDM-level officer).
- Child protection: Juvenile Justice Act 2015, POCSO Act 2012, Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act 1986 (amended 2016), Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act 1976 — SDM/DM vigilance committees.
- Education: Right to Education Act 2009; NEP 2020 (5+3+3+4 structure, foundational literacy, vocational exposure from middle school, target GER 50% in higher education by 2035); NIPUN Bharat (2021); PM POSHAN; Samagra Shiksha.
- Skills: Skill India Mission, PMKVY, National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme, ITIs upgrade, Recognition of Prior Learning; mismatch between training and local industry demand is the main weakness.
- Improving outcomes: learning-level assessment (not just enrolment), teacher training and accountability, school management committees, industry-linked courses, placement tracking, career counselling.
- Ethical lens: equity and social justice, empathy, inclusiveness, non-discrimination, and accountability for public money.

## Punjab angle

- State undertakings such as PRTC and Punbus face stress from ageing fleets and delayed reimbursement for free bus travel for women (since 2021); PSPCL bears a large power-subsidy bill after 300 free units per month for domestic users (from July 2022) and free farm power.
- PSPCL bought the private GVK Goindwal Sahib thermal plant in 2024 — an example of a state utility restructuring its supply base.
- Punjab has the highest Scheduled Caste share of any state (31.9%, Census 2011); sex ratio 895 and child sex ratio 846 (Census 2011) show the need for sustained gender action; women have 50% reservation in Punjab's PRIs and ULBs.
- Women-focused schemes: Mukh Mantri Mawan Dhian Satikar Yojana (₹1,000/month for women 18+, ₹1,500 for SC women; Budget 2026-27) and Ashirwad scheme (marriage assistance for SC/BC and poor families).
- Education: Schools of Eminence (from 2023) and entrepreneurship programme 'Business Blasters' in government schools; in the PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024 learning survey (results July 2025) Punjab ranked first in Classes 3 and 9 and was among the top states overall.
- Youth issues: drug abuse, unemployment and heavy outward migration (including via illegal agents) make skills and local job creation central to Punjab's development.

## How PPSC asks it

Cases test managerial skill (turnaround plan, targets) and sensitivity (gender, SC, elderly, disabled). Reward: phased, measurable plans; correct laws (POSH, PCPNDT, MWPSC, RPwD); stakeholder consultation; and avoidance of quick-fix or populist shortcuts.

## Deep dive

### Reviving a loss-making PSU — toolkit and India's framework

- Diagnosis: current ratio, debt-equity, interest coverage, cash burn, capacity utilisation, manpower cost as a share of revenue, receivables from government; SWOT and a product-portfolio check (BCG matrix) to decide what to keep, fix or exit.
- Turnaround sequence: stop the bleeding (cash control, stop loss-making lines) → stabilise (settle dues, restructure debt) → reposition (technology, new services) → grow; tie every bailout to milestones.
- Institutions: the MoU system (1986, from the Arjun Sengupta Committee) for autonomy-for-accountability contracts; Maharatna/Navratna/Miniratna status; Department of Public Enterprises; DIPAM (renamed in 2016) for disinvestment and asset monetisation.
- History: sick companies used to go to BIFR (Sick Industrial Companies Act 1985) — abolished in 2016 when the IBC came in; the Board for Reconstruction of Public Sector Enterprises (2004) advised on revival plans.
- New Public Sector Enterprise Policy (2021): a minimum presence in four strategic sectors and privatisation or closure elsewhere — so 'revive or exit' is now an explicit choice.
- Punjab state enterprises: PSPCL and PSTCL (unbundled from PSEB in 2010), PRTC and Punbus (transport), Markfed, Milkfed (Verka), Punsup, Punjab Agro. CAG's state audit reports regularly flag loss-making state PSUs and delayed accounts.
- Worked revival — PRTC: ring-fence and reimburse the free-travel-for-women subsidy (since April 2021) on time; add electric buses under PM e-Bus Sewa on gross-cost contracts; rationalise routes using e-ticket data; act against illegal private buses on profitable routes; redeploy rather than retrench staff; develop bus-stand land commercially through PPP; publish a monthly performance dashboard.

### Planning and target achievement — management tools for officers

- Goal-setting: SMART targets; OKRs (objectives and key results); Balanced Scorecard (Kaplan and Norton: financial, customer, internal process, learning and growth).
- Scheduling: PERT/CPM to find the critical path, Gantt charts, a RACI matrix (responsible, accountable, consulted, informed) to fix ownership.
- Continuous improvement: Deming's PDCA cycle (plan–do–check–act), Lean (remove waste in file movement), root-cause analysis (5 Whys, fishbone diagram).
- Government systems: the Results-Framework Document system (2009, Performance Monitoring and Evaluation System) was dropped in 2014; today it is PRAGATI, dashboards, and Aspirational Districts' delta ranking.
- Perils of targets: Goodhart's and Campbell's laws (a measure that becomes a target gets gamed). The Bilaspur sterilisation-camp deaths (Chhattisgarh, 2014) are a grim warning about target-driven health drives.
- Ethical planning: verify data with independent sample checks, reward honest reporting of shortfalls, weigh quality and equity indicators alongside numbers.

### Gender sensitisation and women's empowerment — deeper

- Frameworks: Naila Kabeer's resources–agency–achievements model; Caroline Moser's 'triple role' (productive, reproductive, community work); the shift from Women in Development to Gender and Development; gender budgeting (Part A: 100% women-specific schemes; Part B: 30–99% for women).
- Laws beyond the basics: Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act 2017 (26 weeks' leave; crèche where there are 50 or more employees); equal pay now under the Code on Wages 2019; BNS offences against women and children; zero FIR and e-FIR under BNSS S.173.
- Key judgments: Vishaka (1997) led to the POSH Act; Babita Puniya (2020) gave women officers permanent commission in the Army; Joseph Shine (2018) struck down the adultery offence.
- Labour force: national female labour force participation has risen in recent PLFS rounds, mostly through self-employment; northern states including Punjab remain below the national average. Use this for 'empowerment beyond welfare' arguments.
- Punjab good practice: Nawanshahr district's mid-2000s drive against sex-selective abortion (strict PCPNDT enforcement under its Deputy Commissioner) improved the child sex ratio and is a classic administrative case study; Saanjh Kendras (community policing) and women help desks; free bus travel for women since 2021; 50% reservation for women in PRIs and ULBs.
- Sensitisation methods: role-play training for police and revenue staff, gender audits of offices (toilets, crèche, transport), women nodal officers in every office, engaging men and boys through school programmes, campaigns against dowry and sex selection with gurdwara and community leaders.

### Empowerment of vulnerable sections — the SDM's own powers

- Senior citizens: under the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act 2007, the SDM usually presides over the Maintenance Tribunal (maintenance up to ₹10,000 a month; decide within 90 days; appeal to the District Magistrate). S. Vanitha (2020): the tribunal may order eviction in suitable cases, balanced against a woman's right of residence under the Domestic Violence Act.
- Bonded labour: the Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act 1976 gives the DM/SDM power to free bonded labourers and run vigilance committees at district and sub-division level; release certificates lead to rehabilitation under the central scheme (2016).
- Scheduled Castes in Punjab: the highest SC share of any state (31.9%, Census 2011), with the highest in Doaba (Shaheed Bhagat Singh Nagar about 42%). SCs own a very small share of farmland. One-third of panchayat (shamlat) land on lease is reserved for SCs — the 'Zameen Prapti Sangharsh Committee' struggles in Sangrur turned on enforcing this.
- Post-matric scholarship for SCs: Centre–state sharing became 60:40 from 2020-21 after years of arrears disputes, including in Punjab — a scheme-design lesson.
- Persons with disabilities: RPwD Act 2016 (21 disabilities, 4% reservation in jobs, 5% in higher education); UDID card; Rajive Raturi (2024) — the Supreme Court ordered mandatory, enforceable accessibility standards.
- Drug-affected youth: OOAT clinics (Outpatient Opioid Assisted Treatment, from 2017) and de-addiction centres; NDPS Act Section 64A protects users who volunteer for treatment — treat users as patients and target traffickers.
- Child labour and trafficking: Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act (amended 2016), PENCIL portal; transgender persons: Transgender Persons Act 2019 and Garima Greh shelters; NALSA (2014).

### Education and skill development

- NEP 2020: 5+3+3+4 school structure; foundational literacy and numeracy by Grade 3 (NIPUN Bharat, 2021); mother tongue or regional language as the medium 'wherever possible' up to at least Grade 5; target of 50% gross enrolment in higher education by 2035; public spending goal of 6% of GDP.
- Assessment: PARAKH as the national assessment body; Punjab topped Classes 3 and 9 in the PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024. Use this, but add that private-school competition and English-medium demand persist.
- Punjab initiatives: Schools of Eminence; 'Business Blasters' entrepreneurship for senior students; training of principals and teachers abroad (Singapore 2023, Finland 2024); Mission Samarth for foundational learning; Sikhiya Kranti 2.0 allocation of ₹3,500 crore in 2026-27 (PRS).
- Skilling: the Skill India Mission (PMKVY 4.0, ITI upgrades, National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme); PM Internship Scheme (2024); Punjab Skill Development Mission; district skill committees led by the DC.
- Problems: skills that do not match local jobs, low female enrolment in ITIs, certification without placement, and an 'IELTS mindset' that treats skilling as a route to emigrate. Remedies: cluster-linked courses (Ludhiana bicycles and hosiery, Jalandhar sports goods, Mohali IT), tracking where trainees end up, and an apprenticeship-first design.
- Ethics in education administration: honest exam conduct (Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act 2024), teacher attendance without humiliation, and inclusion of children with disabilities.

### Worked cases set in Punjab (approach in brief)

- Case 1 — Hoshiarpur: an elderly widow is pushed out of her house by her son's family while the son lives abroad. As presiding officer of the Maintenance Tribunal: interim maintenance order, police protection, restoration of possession where the law allows; involve the NRI Affairs wing; follow up with the social welfare department.
- Case 2 — tehsil office: a woman clerk complains of sexual harassment by a superior and the office has no Internal Committee. Form the IC at once (or refer to the district Local Committee), give interim relief (transfer of the accused, not of the complainant), keep it confidential, finish the inquiry within 90 days, and protect her from retaliation.
- Case 3 — Sangrur: dummy bidders take over the SC share of the panchayat land-lease auction. Re-auction openly with video recording and police presence; verify bidders' SC status; act under the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act against intimidation; publish the results.
- Case 4 — a skill centre shows 95% certification but almost no placements. Audit Aadhaar-based attendance; contact trainees by phone to check outcomes; recover funds and blacklist the centre if fraud is found; redesign courses with local industry associations.
- Case 5 — a target dilemma: the district is told to show 100% coverage under a scheme before a VIP review, but field data shows 70%. Report the true figure with a time-bound plan; do not manipulate data; explain the cost of false reporting to seniors — integrity and courage.
- Case 6 — Tarn Taran: three young men die of overdoses in one village in a month. Order an inquiry; strengthen the OOAT clinic and de-addiction ward; let police act on suppliers with community information (protect informers); treat users as patients (NDPS S.64A); counsel families; start sports, skills and job links for youth; review monthly with the panchayat and the gurdwara committee.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** You are appointed Managing Director of a state road transport undertaking headquartered in Patiala. It has accumulated heavy losses; a third of its buses are over-aged; salaries are paid late; private operators run on its most profitable routes, sometimes on expired permits; reimbursement from government for free travel of women is delayed; and the employees' union opposes any VRS or outsourcing. Prepare a two-year revival plan.

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Diagnosis: route-wise profitability, cost per km, fleet age, staff ratio, receivables from government.
- Revenue: rationalise routes, strict enforcement against illegal private operations with the transport department, e-ticketing to stop leakages, advertising and depot land monetisation.
- Government dues: secure time-bound reimbursement via a tripartite MoU; budget the social obligation transparently.
- Costs: preventive maintenance, fuel efficiency, phased fleet replacement (including e-buses under central schemes), digital depot management.
- People: consult the union, voluntary not forced VRS, retraining, performance incentives; pay salaries on time to rebuild morale.
- Governance: monthly KPIs dashboard, independent directors, public reporting.
- Conclusion: public service with financial discipline.

</details>

**2.** As SDM in Mansa district, you receive information that a mobile ultrasound van is conducting illegal sex determination in villages, and a local NGO points out that girls' enrolment in secondary school drops sharply in some villages. A woman employee in your office has also complained of harassment by a senior colleague. How will you address these issues of gender justice?

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- PCPNDT: inform District Appropriate Authority (Civil Surgeon), plan a decoy operation with NGO support, seal machines, prosecute; ensure informer protection.
- Awareness: BBBP activities, panchayat resolutions, celebrate girl births, ASHAs track pregnancies.
- Girls' education: identify dropout reasons (distance, safety, toilets), bicycles/transport, scholarships, women teachers, SMC meetings.
- POSH complaint: refer to Internal Committee immediately, protect complainant from retaliation, interim measures (transfer of the accused if needed), time-bound inquiry.
- Gender sensitisation training for office and field staff.
- Conclusion: zero tolerance plus long-term mindset change.

</details>

**3.** The Deputy Commissioner of Tarn Taran asks you to ensure that 3,000 young people from border villages are trained and at least 60% placed in jobs or self-employment within 18 months. Many youths want to go abroad, some are caught in drug use, and earlier training programmes had low placement. How will you plan and achieve this target?

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Baseline survey: youth aspirations, education levels, drug-dependence screening, local industry demand.
- Course mix: demand-driven trades (logistics, food processing, dairy, electrician, solar technician, healthcare), RPL for semi-skilled, English and soft skills; safe-migration counselling for those going abroad.
- Partnerships: ITIs, PMKVY centres, industry associations of Amritsar/Ludhiana, apprenticeships (NAPS), banks for MUDRA-linked self-employment.
- Drug recovery: link with de-addiction centres and counselling; involve families and gurdwaras.
- Targets: quarterly milestones, dashboard, third-party verification of placements, track retention after 6 months.
- Incentives: stipends, transport, women's participation, success stories.
- Conclusion: quality placements over headline numbers.

</details>
