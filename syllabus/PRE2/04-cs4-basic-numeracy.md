## Short notes

- Divisibility: 3 and 9 by digit sum; 4 by last two digits; 8 by last three; 11 by difference of alternate-digit sums being 0 or a multiple of 11.
- For two numbers: product = HCF x LCM. HCF of fractions = HCF of numerators / LCM of denominators; LCM of fractions = LCM of numerators / HCF of denominators.
- Number of factors: write N = p^a x q^b x r^c; factors = (a+1)(b+1)(c+1). Example 360 = 2^3 x 3^2 x 5 has 4 x 3 x 2 = 24 factors.
- Unit digit cycles: 2 (2,4,8,6), 3 (3,9,7,1), 7 (7,9,3,1), 8 (8,4,2,6) repeat every 4; 4 and 9 every 2; 0,1,5,6 never change. Use power mod 4 (remainder 0 means 4th term).
- Fraction-percent equivalents: 1/2 = 50%, 1/3 = 33.33%, 1/4 = 25%, 1/5 = 20%, 1/6 = 16.67%, 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/9 = 11.11%, 1/11 = 9.09%, 1/12 = 8.33%.
- Successive change of a% and b%: net = a + b + ab/100 (use minus signs for decreases). +20% then -20% = -4%.
- If price rises r%, consumption must fall r/(100 + r) x 100% to keep spending fixed; if price falls r%, consumption can rise r/(100 - r) x 100%.
- Profit % = profit/cost price x 100. Selling at x% profit: SP = CP x (100 + x)/100. Discount is always on marked price.
- Simple interest = PRT/100. Compound amount = P(1 + R/100)^T. For 2 years, CI - SI = P(R/100)^2.
- Ratio: if a:b = 2:3 and b:c = 4:5, make b common: a:b:c = 8:12:15.
- Average = sum/number. Adding a value equal to the average does not change it.
- Average speed for equal distances at x and y = 2xy/(x + y), not (x + y)/2.
- Relative speed: same direction subtract, opposite directions add. Convert km/h to m/s by multiplying by 5/18.
- Time and work: A alone in a days, B in b days; together = ab/(a + b) days. Use LCM of days as total work for quick sums.
- Mixtures (alligation): (cheaper : dearer) = (dearer price - mean price) : (mean price - cheaper price).
- Magnitude comparison: convert to a common base, power or decimal; for fractions cross-multiply (a/b > c/d if ad > bc).
- Square shortcuts: numbers ending in 5: n5^2 = n(n+1) followed by 25 (65^2 = 4225).

## How PPSC asks it

Numeracy is at Class 10 level but time-bound: percentages, ratios, averages, speed and number properties dominate. Memorise the shortcut formulas and fraction-percent table so most sums take under a minute.

## Deep dive

### What PPSC asks — pattern from 2021 and 2025

- Numeracy is the single largest block: about 30 questions in 2025 and about 19 in 2021 — mastering it alone can nearly clear the cut-off.
- 2025 themes: comparing decimals and powers, approximation and simplification ('13.08 x 24.98 + root 624'), ages, ratios and proportion, algebraic identities (a^3 - b^3), numbers added to make a proportion, reversed two-digit numbers, fractions and reciprocals, partnership with time, men-women work, mixtures, cylinder volume, arrangements of the letters of a word, digit-sum counting, speed and time.
- 2021 themes: time intervals, fractions of pizzas, percentage of employees, salary growth, pass marks, BMI formula, sets with 'at least', milk container fractions, area and cost of mowing, repeated replacement of alcohol, variation (coal consumption proportional to speed squared), work, Pythagoras (ladder), probability, LCM of alarms.
- Level: Class 8-10 mathematics, but with time pressure — the skill tested is choosing the fastest method.
- Many questions are 'word problems' set in everyday Punjab contexts — translate words into one equation quickly.
- Approximation questions reward rounding (24.98 to 25, root 624 to about 25) — exact calculation wastes time.

### Formula table — topics not in your short notes

- Ages: write present ages as x and y; 'n years ago' subtract n from both; 'after n years' add n to both; the age difference never changes.
- Partnership: profit is shared in the ratio of capital x time (capital-months); a sleeping partner's share is fixed by the same rule unless a salary is agreed.
- Work with groups: M1 x D1 x H1 / W1 = M2 x D2 x H2 / W2 (men, days, hours per day, work); convert men to women using the given equivalence first.
- Wages: divide wages in the ratio of work done, not time spent.
- Pipes and cisterns: filling pipe +1/a per hour, emptying pipe -1/b per hour; net rate = sum; time = 1/net rate.
- Boats and streams: downstream speed = boat + stream; upstream = boat - stream; boat = (down + up)/2; stream = (down - up)/2.
- Trains: time to cross a pole = train length / speed; to cross a platform or another train = sum of lengths / relative speed.
- Repeated replacement: after n operations of removing x litres from a vessel of V litres and refilling with water, pure liquid left = V x (1 - x/V)^n.
- Mensuration: cylinder volume pi r^2 h, curved surface 2 pi r h; cone volume (1/3) pi r^2 h; sphere volume (4/3) pi r^3, surface 4 pi r^2; 1 litre = 1,000 cubic cm; 1 cubic metre = 1,000 litres; 1 hectare = 10,000 sq m.
- Permutations: n distinct objects in n! ways; with repeats n!/(p! q! ...); circular arrangements (n-1)!; selections nCr = n!/(r!(n-r)!).
- Identities: (a+b)^2, (a-b)^2, a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b), a^3 - b^3 = (a-b)(a^2 + ab + b^2), a^3 + b^3 = (a+b)(a^2 - ab + b^2).
- Probability = favourable outcomes / total equally likely outcomes; P(not A) = 1 - P(A); for independent events multiply.
- Two-digit number with digits a and b: value 10a + b; reversing changes it by 9(a - b) — so any reversal difference is a multiple of 9.

### Speed techniques

- Approximate first: round each term to a convenient value, compute, then pick the closest option; check the unit digit if options are close.
- Use options: in equation-type questions, plug options into the condition (ages, numbers) — often faster than solving.
- Percentage as fractions: 12.5% = 1/8, 16.67% = 1/6, 37.5% = 3/8, 62.5% = 5/8, 87.5% = 7/8 — turns percentage chains into simple fractions.
- Ratio shortcut: if A:B = 2:3, A is 2/5 and B is 3/5 of the total; for three-part ratios make the common term equal first.
- LCM method for work and pipes: take total work as the LCM of the given times to avoid fractions.
- Digit sum (casting out nines) checks multiplication: the digit sum of the product equals the digit sum of the product of the factors' digit sums.
- Squares to remember: 11^2 to 30^2 and cubes to 15^3; root of a 4-digit perfect square from its unit digit and range.

### Time strategy for numeracy

- First pass: one-step items (percentage, ratio, simplification, unit digit, HCF-LCM) — under 1 minute each.
- Second pass: two-step word problems (ages, work, partnership, speed) — about 1.5 minutes each.
- Last pass: multi-step items (repeated replacement, permutations with conditions, digit counting) — only if time remains.
- Accuracy beats speed: since the cut-off is 40%, a careful 20 correct out of 25 attempted in numeracy is worth more than 30 rushed attempts.
- Keep a formula sheet of this section and revise it weekly; most errors come from forgetting a formula, not from arithmetic.
- Do the rough work in a neat column next to each question so a quick re-check is possible in the last 10 minutes.

### Common traps

- Partnership: sharing profit in the ratio of capital alone, ignoring time invested.
- Work: adding days instead of adding rates (work per day).
- Speed: averaging two speeds directly when distances are equal (use 2xy/(x+y)).
- Mensuration: mixing cm and m — convert everything to one unit before multiplying.
- Permutations: forgetting to divide by the factorial of repeated letters.
- Compound interest: assuming CI - SI = P(R/100)^2 also holds for three years — for three years it is P(R/100)^2 (3 + R/100).

## Practice MCQs (20)

**1.** The price of an item is first increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. The net change in price is:

(a) 4% decrease  
(b) No change  
(c) 4% increase  
(d) 2% decrease

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 4% decrease** — Net change = a + b + ab/100 = 20 + (-20) + (20 x -20)/100 = -4%. Check with 100: after +20% it is 120; 20% of 120 is 24, so it falls to 96, a 4% decrease. The fall is on a larger base, so the net is a loss.

</details>

**2.** The price of sugar rises by 25%. By what percentage must a family reduce consumption so that expenditure on sugar stays the same?

(a) 15%  
(b) 22.5%  
(c) 25%  
(d) 20%

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) 20%** — Required reduction = r/(100 + r) x 100 = 25/125 x 100 = 20%. Check: old price 100, quantity 1, spend 100. New price 125; to spend 100 the family can buy 100/125 = 0.8 units, a 20% cut.

</details>

**3.** What is the unit digit of 7^95?

(a) 3  
(b) 7  
(c) 9  
(d) 1

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 3** — Powers of 7 end in 7, 9, 3, 1 and repeat every 4. 95 divided by 4 leaves remainder 3, so the unit digit is the 3rd in the cycle: 3.

</details>

**4.** The HCF of two numbers is 12 and their LCM is 360. If one number is 72, what is the other?

(a) 30  
(b) 60  
(c) 90  
(d) 48

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 60** — Product of two numbers = HCF x LCM, so other = (12 x 360)/72 = 4320/72 = 60. Check: HCF(72, 60) = 12 and LCM(72, 60) = 360.

</details>

**5.** A car travels from Ludhiana to Jalandhar at 60 km/h and returns along the same road at 40 km/h. Its average speed for the whole journey is:

(a) 50 km/h  
(b) 52 km/h  
(c) 45 km/h  
(d) 48 km/h

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) 48 km/h** — For equal distances, average speed = 2xy/(x + y) = (2 x 60 x 40)/100 = 48 km/h. Check with 120 km each way: 2 h going + 3 h returning = 5 h for 240 km, so 48 km/h. The simple mean 50 is the trap.

</details>

**6.** A can finish a job in 12 days and B in 18 days. Working together, they will finish it in:

(a) 7.2 days  
(b) 15 days  
(c) 6 days  
(d) 7.5 days

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 7.2 days** — Take total work = LCM(12, 18) = 36 units. A does 3 units a day, B does 2; together 5 units a day. Time = 36/5 = 7.2 days. Formula check: ab/(a + b) = 216/30 = 7.2.

</details>

**7.** Ten years ago a father was four times as old as his son. Ten years from now he will be twice as old as his son. What is the father's present age?

(a) 45 years  
(b) 50 years  
(c) 60 years  
(d) 40 years

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 50 years** — Let the son be s and the father f today. Ten years ago: f - 10 = 4(s - 10), so f = 4s - 30. Ten years hence: f + 10 = 2(s + 10), so f = 2s + 10. Equate: 4s - 30 = 2s + 10, so s = 20 and f = 50. Check: ten years ago 40 and 10 (4 times); ten years later 60 and 30 (twice).

</details>

**8.** Gurpreet starts a business with Rs 60,000. After 3 months Navdeep joins with Rs 80,000. At the end of the year the profit is Rs 51,000. What is Navdeep's share?

(a) Rs 29,143  
(b) Rs 25,500  
(c) Rs 21,857  
(d) Rs 34,000

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Rs 25,500** — Profit is shared in the ratio of capital x time. Gurpreet: 60,000 x 12 = 7,20,000. Navdeep: 80,000 x 9 = 7,20,000. Ratio 1 : 1, so each gets 51,000 / 2 = Rs 25,500. Rs 29,143 is the trap from using capital alone (3 : 4).

</details>

**9.** 4 men or 6 women can finish a piece of work in 15 days. In how many days will 2 men and 6 women finish it?

(a) 10 days  
(b) 12 days  
(c) 7.5 days  
(d) 9 days

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 10 days** — 4 men = 6 women, so 1 man = 1.5 women. Then 2 men + 6 women = 3 + 6 = 9 women. Total work = 6 women x 15 days = 90 woman-days. Time for 9 women = 90 / 9 = 10 days.

</details>
