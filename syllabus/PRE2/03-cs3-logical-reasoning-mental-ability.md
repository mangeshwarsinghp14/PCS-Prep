## Short notes

- Syllogisms: draw Venn diagrams. 'All A are B' converts only to 'Some B are A'; 'Some A are B' converts to 'Some B are A'; 'No A is B' converts to 'No B is A'.
- Two particular statements ('Some...Some') give no definite conclusion; a conclusion must hold in EVERY possible diagram.
- Either-or case: if two conclusions are about the same pair, one is positive and one negative, and neither follows alone, answer 'either I or II follows'.
- Blood relations: draw a family tree with + for male, - for female, horizontal line for siblings, vertical for generations.
- Direction sense: plot on paper with North up. Right turn = clockwise 90 degrees, left turn = anticlockwise. Use Pythagoras for shortest distance.
- Clock angle between hands = |30H - 5.5M| degrees; if above 180, subtract from 360. Hands coincide 11 times in 12 hours (22 times a day).
- Calendar odd days: ordinary year = 1, leap year = 2. 100 years = 5, 200 = 3, 300 = 1, 400 = 0 odd days. Day codes: Sunday = 0 ... Saturday = 6.
- Leap year: divisible by 4; century years only if divisible by 400 (2000 leap, 1900 not). Check whether 29 February falls inside the period counted.
- Number series: check differences, second differences, squares/cubes (n^2, n^2+1, n(n+1)), alternate terms and multiplication patterns.
- Coding-decoding: compare letter positions (A=1...Z=26; reverse Z=1); look for +n shifts, reversal or opposite letter pairs (A-Z, B-Y: sum 27).
- Ranking: total persons = rank from top + rank from bottom - 1.
- Painted cube cut into n x n x n small cubes: 3 faces painted = 8; exactly 2 = 12(n-2); exactly 1 = 6(n-2)^2; none = (n-2)^3.
- Seating arrangement: fix the most definite clue first, draw all cases, and eliminate with negative clues ('X is not next to Y').
- Statement-assumption: an assumption is unstated and necessary for the statement to make sense; test by negating it.
- Course of action: accept only practical, legal and proportionate actions that directly address the problem.
- Analogy and odd-one-out: identify the relation type (part-whole, tool-worker, cause-effect, category) before checking options.
- Inequalities: combine A > B >= C to get A > C; a mixed chain like A > B < C gives no relation between A and C.

## How PPSC asks it

Reasoning questions are formula-driven and fast if the method is known: syllogism, direction, blood relation, clock, calendar, series and cube questions recur. Memorise the formulas above and practise drawing quick diagrams.

## Deep dive

### What PPSC asks — pattern from 2021 and 2025

- Reasoning is the second-largest block: about 23-24 questions in each of the 2021 and 2025 papers.
- Puzzles dominate: family puzzles with married couples and children, linear seating (facing north or south), circular arrangements (sweets around a table), scheduling (guest lectures on five days), teams with selection constraints, ranking of five students, factory-shift puzzles with 3 questions each.
- Logic: conditional statements ('If it's an apple, then it's a fruit'), 'Many A are B' style syllogisms, statements that logically follow, symbol-substitution equations.
- Number patterns: 2x2 boxes and 2x6 matrices with a missing number, digital-clock and calendar items (counting holidays when second and fourth Saturdays and Sundays are off).
- Coding: letter coding with insertions ('SOLID' written with extra letters) and word-number coding ('256 means red colour chalk').
- Visual: dice shown in several positions, street-map directions.
- Lesson: set puzzles (3 questions on one arrangement) are high-value — one correct grid gives three answers; practise drawing grids fast.

### Conditional logic and syllogism method sheet

- 'If P then Q' (P -> Q): valid inferences are 'P, so Q' (modus ponens) and 'not Q, so not P' (contrapositive). Invalid: 'Q, so P' (affirming the consequent) and 'not P, so not Q' (denying the antecedent).
- 'P only if Q' means P -> Q; 'P if Q' means Q -> P; 'P unless Q' means 'if not Q then P'; 'P if and only if Q' gives both directions.
- Necessary vs sufficient: in P -> Q, P is sufficient for Q and Q is necessary for P (being a fruit is necessary for being an apple).
- Syllogisms with 'some ... not': 'Some A are not B' cannot be converted; 'Some A are B' and 'No B is C' together give 'Some A are not C' (always), but nothing definite about 'Some C are A'.
- 'Many', 'most' and 'a few' are treated as 'some' unless the question defines them otherwise.
- Possibility questions: a conclusion is 'possible' if at least one valid diagram allows it; it 'follows' only if every valid diagram forces it.
- Complementary pair: if neither of two conclusions follows but together they cover all cases on the same subject-predicate (Some A are B / No A is B), answer 'either I or II'.

### Arrangement, dice and coding shortcuts

- Circular seating facing the centre: a person's right is the anticlockwise neighbour, left is the clockwise neighbour; facing outward, reverse it. Fix one person first to remove rotational duplicates.
- Linear seating: 'facing north' — right is east; 'facing south' — right is west; draw the row once and write the facing direction above it.
- Scheduling and grid puzzles: make a table (persons x attributes), fill definite clues, then use negative clues (not adjacent, not on Monday) to close cases; branch into at most two cases and test each.
- Dice: faces adjacent to a common face cannot be opposite to it; if two views share a face in the same position, rotate one view mentally to match the other; on a standard die opposite faces add to 7 (1-6, 2-5, 3-4) — use only if the question says 'standard die'.
- Letter positions: A=1 ... Z=26; reverse value = 27 minus position; EJOTY marks 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 — speeds up shifting.
- Word-number codes: compare two messages sharing one word to isolate its code ('256 = red colour chalk', '589 = green colour flower' share 'colour' and the digit 5).
- Ranking: total = rank from left + rank from right - 1; persons between two people = difference of their positions from the same end minus 1.
- Calendar holidays: count Sundays, then second and fourth Saturdays separately — months with five Saturdays still have only two 'second and fourth' holidays.

### Time strategy for reasoning

- Order of attempt: direct one-liners (coding, ranking, blood relation, direction, calendar) first, about 1 minute each; then set puzzles; leave dice and figure items for the end if they confuse you.
- For a 3-question set, invest up to 5-6 minutes on the grid — if the grid is not settling by then, guess and move on.
- Always re-read the question stem for 'NOT', 'cannot be determined', 'definitely true' and 'possibly true'.
- Target accuracy: 15 of about 23 reasoning questions plus language items already takes you most of the way to 32 correct.
- Practise with past PPSC papers in this app's exam mode; time each set separately.
- In the final month, do a 20-question reasoning drill in 25 minutes every other day and review every wrong grid — puzzles improve fastest through post-mortems.

### Common traps

- Confusing a statement with its converse — 'All officers are graduates' does not mean 'All graduates are officers'.
- Assuming a standard die when the question shows a non-standard numbering.
- Forgetting that 'immediate right' means adjacent, while 'to the right' may mean anywhere on that side.
- In calendar items, adding a leap day when 29 February lies outside the counted period.
- In coding, applying the shift to a reversed word when the rule reverses after shifting (order of operations matters).
- Treating 'cannot be determined' as a cop-out — in reasoning sets it is often the correct answer.

## Practice MCQs (20)

**1.** Statements: All officers are graduates. Some graduates are athletes. Conclusions: I. Some officers are athletes. II. Some athletes are graduates. Which conclusion(s) follow?

(a) Only II follows  
(b) Only I follows  
(c) Both I and II follow  
(d) Neither I nor II follows

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Only II follows** — II is the direct conversion of 'Some graduates are athletes', so it always holds. For I, draw the officers' circle inside graduates and the athletes' circle overlapping graduates but outside the officers' circle; this fits both statements yet no officer is an athlete. So I does not necessarily follow. Answer: only II.

</details>

**2.** What is the angle between the hour and minute hands of a clock at 3:40?

(a) 110 degrees  
(b) 130 degrees  
(c) 120 degrees  
(d) 140 degrees

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 130 degrees** — Angle = |30H - 5.5M| = |30 x 3 - 5.5 x 40| = |90 - 220| = 130 degrees. It is below 180, so it is the required angle. (Check: minute hand at 240 degrees, hour hand at 90 + 40 x 0.5 = 110 degrees; difference 130.)

</details>

**3.** Amrit walks 5 km north, turns right and walks 3 km, then turns right again and walks 9 km. How far and in which direction is he from his starting point?

(a) 5 km, South-East  
(b) 5 km, North-East  
(c) 7 km, South-East  
(d) 4 km, South

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 5 km, South-East** — Take start as (0,0). 5 km north: (0,5). Right turn from north faces east, 3 km: (3,5). Right turn from east faces south, 9 km: (3,-4). Distance = square root of (3^2 + 4^2) = 5 km. He is east (x positive) and south (y negative) of start: South-East.

</details>

**4.** Pointing to a photograph, Raman says, 'She is the daughter of the only son of my father's father.' How is the girl related to Raman?

(a) Sister  
(b) Cousin  
(c) Daughter  
(d) Niece

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Sister** — Raman's father's father is his paternal grandfather. The grandfather's only son must be Raman's father. The daughter of Raman's father is Raman's sister. A cousin would require the grandfather to have another child.

</details>

**5.** 15 August 2026 falls on a Saturday. On which day did 15 August 2024 fall?

(a) Saturday  
(b) Wednesday  
(c) Friday  
(d) Thursday

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Thursday** — From 15 Aug 2024 to 15 Aug 2025 there are 365 days (29 Feb 2024 is before August and 2025 is not leap): 1 odd day. From 15 Aug 2025 to 15 Aug 2026: 365 days, 1 odd day. Total 2 odd days. Going back 2 days from Saturday gives Thursday. Trap: counting 2024 as a leap year here gives Wednesday.

</details>

**6.** A cube of side 4 cm is painted on all faces and cut into 64 cubes of side 1 cm. How many small cubes have exactly two faces painted?

(a) 24  
(b) 32  
(c) 8  
(d) 36

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 24** — Here n = 4. Cubes with exactly two painted faces lie on the edges, excluding corners: 12 edges x (n - 2) = 12 x 2 = 24. The 8 corner cubes have three faces painted; 6(n-2)^2 = 24 have one face; (n-2)^3 = 8 have none. Check: 8 + 24 + 24 + 8 = 64.

</details>

**7.** Statement: 'If canal water reaches the tail end, the farmers will not pump groundwater.' Consider the following: 1. If the farmers pump groundwater, then the canal water did not reach the tail end. 2. If the canal water does not reach the tail end, then the farmers will pump groundwater. Which of the above follows logically from the statement?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 1 only** — Let P = 'canal water reaches the tail end' and Q = 'farmers do not pump'. The statement is P -> Q. Statement 1 says 'not Q -> not P' — the contrapositive, which is always valid. Statement 2 says 'not P -> not Q' — denying the antecedent, which is invalid (farmers might still avoid pumping, for example if it rains). So only 1 follows.

</details>

**8.** Six friends A, B, C, D, E and F sit around a circular table facing the centre. A sits opposite D. B is to the immediate right of A and C is to the immediate left of A. F is not adjacent to C. Who sits opposite B?

(a) D  
(b) C  
(c) E  
(d) F

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) E** — Number the seats 0-5 clockwise and put A at 0, so D (opposite) is at 3. Facing the centre, a person's right is the anticlockwise neighbour, so B is at 5 and C (left) is at 1. Seats 2 and 4 remain for E and F. C at 1 has neighbours 0 and 2, so F cannot be at 2: F is at 4 and E is at 2. Opposite B (seat 5) is seat 2, which is E. (Even if the right-left convention were reversed, the mirror arrangement still puts E opposite B.)

</details>

**9.** Two views of the same die are shown. View 1: top 1, front 2, right 3. View 2: top 1, front 3, right 5. Which number is opposite 2?

(a) 5  
(b) 4  
(c) 6  
(d) 1

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 5** — Both views have 1 on top, so view 2 is view 1 turned about the vertical axis. In view 1, 3 is on the right; in view 2 it has come to the front — a quarter-turn. In such a turn, the face that comes to the right is the face that was at the back in view 1. So 5 was at the back in view 1, i.e. opposite the front face 2. Answer: 5 (consistent with a standard die, where 2 + 5 = 7).

</details>

**10.** In a code language, each letter of a word is moved two places forward in the alphabet and the resulting word is then written in reverse. If PUNJAB is written as DCLPWR, how is MOGA written?

(a) OQIC  
(b) CQIO  
(c) CIQO  
(d) DJRP

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) CIQO** — Check the rule on PUNJAB: P+2 = R, U+2 = W, N+2 = P, J+2 = L, A+2 = C, B+2 = D gives RWPLCD; reversed it is DCLPWR — matches. Apply to MOGA: M+2 = O, O+2 = Q, G+2 = I, A+2 = C gives OQIC; reversed: CIQO. The option “OQIC” forgets to reverse; the option “CQIO” reverses incorrectly; the option “DJRP” uses +3 shifts.

</details>

**11.** Statements: Some officers are lawyers. No lawyer is a doctor. Consider the following conclusions:  
1. Some lawyers are not officers.  
2. Some doctors are officers.  
Which of the conclusions given above follow(s)?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Neither 1 nor 2** — Conclusion 1 does not follow: the statements allow every lawyer to be an officer, so 'some lawyers are not officers' is only possible, not certain. Conclusion 2 does not follow: the doctors' circle can lie wholly outside both the lawyers' and the officers' circles, so no doctor need be an officer. So neither conclusion follows.

</details>

**12.** If 'P # Q' means P is the father of Q, 'P @ Q' means P is the sister of Q, and 'P $ Q' means P is the wife of Q, then in the expression 'A $ B # C @ D', how is A related to D?

(a) Mother  
(b) Grandmother  
(c) Aunt  
(d) Sister

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Mother** — Decode left to right: A $ B — A is the wife of B. B # C — B is the father of C. C @ D — C is the sister of D, so C and D are siblings and B is the father of D as well. A, the wife of D's father, is D's mother. Answer: Mother.

</details>

**13.** If 26 January 2027 is a Tuesday, what day of the week will 15 August 2027 be?

(a) Sunday  
(b) Monday  
(c) Saturday  
(d) Friday

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) Sunday** — Count days from 26 Jan to 15 Aug 2027: rest of January 31 - 26 = 5, February 28 (2027 is not a leap year), March 31, April 30, May 31, June 30, July 31, August 15. Total = 5 + 28 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 15 = 201 days. 201 = 7 x 28 + 5, so 5 odd days. Tuesday + 5 = Sunday.

</details>

**14.** In a row of 40 candidates, Harpreet is 12th from the left end and Simran is 25th from the right end. How many candidates sit between them?

(a) 3  
(b) 4  
(c) 2  
(d) 5

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) 3** — Convert Simran's position to the left end: 40 - 25 + 1 = 16th from the left. Harpreet is 12th from the left, so the people between them are in positions 13, 14 and 15 — that is 16 - 12 - 1 = 3 candidates.

</details>

**15.** Statements: All ploughs are tools. No tool is a toy. Some toys are gifts.  
Consider the following conclusions:  
1. No plough is a toy.  
2. Some gifts are not tools.  
Which of the conclusions given above follow(s)?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Both 1 and 2** — Ploughs lie inside tools and tools share nothing with toys, so no plough can be a toy — the first conclusion follows. The toys that are gifts cannot be tools (no toy is a tool), so at least those gifts are not tools — the second also follows. Many pick '1 only' because 'gifts' and 'tools' are not directly linked. Answer: Both.

</details>

**16.** In a certain code, GARDEN is written as HZSWFM and FIELD is written as GRFOE. How will VILLAGE be written in that code?

(a) XRMOBTF  
(b) WRMOBUF  
(c) WRMOBTF  
(d) WQMOBTF

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) WRMOBTF** — Step 1: decode the rule from GARDEN → HZSWFM: letters in odd positions move one step forward (G→H, R→S, E→F); letters in even positions are replaced by their opposite letter, A↔Z, B↔Y, … (A→Z, D→W, N→M). FIELD → GRFOE confirms it. Step 2: VILLAGE: V→W, I→R (opposite), L→M, L→O (opposite), A→B, G→T (opposite), E→F = WRMOBTF. Answer: WRMOBTF.

</details>

**17.** A family has six members — A, B, C, D, E and F — with two married couples. D is the grandmother of A and the mother of B. C is the wife of B and the mother of F. F is the granddaughter of E. A is male.  
Consider the following statements:  
1. E is the maternal grandfather of F.  
2. A is the brother of F.  
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) 2 only** — B–C is one couple; since A and F are grandchildren, the other couple must be D and E, so E is D's husband and B's father. A and F are both children of B and C; A is male and F is female (granddaughter), so A is F's brother — the second statement is right. E is the father of B, F's father, so he is F's paternal, not maternal, grandfather — the first is wrong. Answer: 2 only.

</details>

**18.** Six officers K, L, M, N, O and P sit in a row facing north. M sits third from the left end. K sits at one of the ends. Exactly two officers sit between K and N. L sits immediately to the right of N. O does not sit next to M. Who sits at the right end?

(a) O  
(b) P  
(c) L  
(d) K

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) O** — Step 1: if K were at the right end (position 6), N would be at 3 — but M is at 3, so K is at position 1 and N at 4. Step 2: L is immediately right of N, so L is at 5. Positions 2 and 6 remain for O and P; O cannot sit next to M (position 3), so O is at 6 and P at 2. Order: K P M N L O. Answer: O.

</details>

**19.** At 7 a.m. Harjit stands so that his shadow falls exactly to his right. He walks 10 m straight ahead, turns left and walks 6 m, then turns left again and walks 18 m. How far is he now from his starting point, and in which direction?

(a) 14 m, north-east  
(b) 10 m, north-west  
(c) 10 m, south-east  
(d) 10 m, north-east

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) 10 m, north-east** — Step 1: at 7 a.m. the sun is in the east, so shadows fall to the west; a shadow on his right means he faces south. Step 2: 10 m south → (0, −10); left from south is east, 6 m → (6, −10); left from east is north, 18 m → (6, 8). Distance = √(6² + 8²) = 10 m, and he is east and north of the start. Answer: 10 m, north-east.

</details>

**20.** Find the next term in the series: B2, E5, J10, Q17, ?

(a) Z25  
(b) Z26  
(c) Y25  
(d) X26

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Z26** — Step 1: the numbers are the alphabet positions of the letters (B = 2, E = 5, J = 10, Q = 17). Step 2: they follow n² + 1 for n = 1, 2, 3, 4 (2, 5, 10, 17), so the next is 5² + 1 = 26, the letter Z. Answer: Z26.

</details>
