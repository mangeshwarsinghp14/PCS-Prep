## Short notes

- Read the questions first, then the passage: you then read with a purpose and mark only the lines that matter.
- Answer from the passage only. An option that is true in real life but not supported by the text is wrong.
- Main idea / best title: pick the option that covers the WHOLE passage, not one paragraph or one example.
- Inference = what must be true given the text. Assumption = what the author takes for granted without saying it. Conclusion = what follows from the stated facts.
- Eliminate extreme options using words like always, never, only, all, completely, solely; passages rarely support absolute claims.
- Tone questions: note adjectives and qualifiers (unfortunately, merely, remarkably) to tell critical, neutral, optimistic or sarcastic tone apart.
- Vocabulary in context: replace the word with each option and keep the one that keeps the sentence's meaning, not the dictionary's first meaning.
- Synonyms/antonyms: use roots and prefixes (bene- good, mal- bad, ante- before, circum- around, -cide killing, a-/in-/un- not) and sentence-test the word.
- Subject-verb agreement: 'Each/Every/Either/Neither + singular noun' takes a singular verb; with 'either...or / neither...nor' the verb agrees with the nearer subject.
- 'One of the + plural noun' takes a singular verb (One of the officers is absent); 'The number of' is singular, 'A number of' is plural.
- Common error areas: tense sequence, articles (a/an by sound: an hour, a university), prepositions (comply with, superior to, prefer A to B), comparatives (more better is wrong).
- Sentence arrangement (para-jumble): find the opening sentence (introduces the subject, no pronoun or connector), then link pairs using pronouns, 'therefore/however', and time order.
- Punjabi vocabulary: ਸਮਾਨਾਰਥਕ (synonyms) e.g. ਪਾਣੀ — ਜਲ, ਨੀਰ; ਸੂਰਜ — ਰਵੀ, ਭਾਨੂ; ਹਵਾ — ਪਵਨ; ਧਰਤੀ — ਭੂਮੀ, ਪ੍ਰਿਥਵੀ.
- Punjabi ਵਿਰੋਧੀ ਸ਼ਬਦ (antonyms) are often formed with prefixes ਅ-, ਨਿਰ-, ਬੇ-, ਨਾ-: ਆਦਰ — ਨਿਰਾਦਰ, ਗਿਆਨ — ਅਗਿਆਨ, ਇੱਜ਼ਤ — ਬੇਇੱਜ਼ਤੀ, ਆਸਤਕ — ਨਾਸਤਕ.
- Punjabi ਮੁਹਾਵਰੇ are tested for meaning: ਅੱਖਾਂ ਵਿੱਚ ਘੱਟਾ ਪਾਉਣਾ — ਧੋਖਾ ਦੇਣਾ; ਪਾਣੀ ਪਾਣੀ ਹੋਣਾ — ਬਹੁਤ ਸ਼ਰਮਿੰਦਾ ਹੋਣਾ; ਅੱਗ ਬਬੂਲਾ ਹੋਣਾ — ਬਹੁਤ ਗੁੱਸੇ ਹੋਣਾ.
- Time plan for CSAT: attempt comprehension early while fresh; budget about one minute per question and skip long passages if short ones remain.
- CSAT is qualifying (40% of 200 = 80 marks needed); accuracy matters more than attempting everything.

## How PPSC asks it

Expect short English passages with inference and main-idea questions, plus direct vocabulary and grammar items in both English and Punjabi. Memorise common Punjabi synonyms, antonyms and idioms, and practise elimination on passage questions.

## Deep dive

### What PPSC asks — pattern from the 2021 and 2025 Paper II

- Paper II: 80 questions x 2.5 marks = 200 marks in 2 hours; qualifying at 40% (80 marks = 32 correct answers); no negative marking, so every question must be attempted.
- 2025 paper: about 8 direct language items (English synonym 'preposterous', fill-in-the-blank, sentence improvement, choose the correct sentence; Punjabi ਬਹੁ-ਅਰਥਕ ਸ਼ਬਦ, ਅਖਾਣ, ਨਾਟਕੀ ਵਾਰਤਾਲਾਪ, ਸ਼ੁੱਧ ਸ਼ਬਦ-ਜੋੜ) plus roughly 10-11 questions on reading passages.
- 2021 paper: English correct-sentence and preposition-fill items, Punjabi ਮੁਹਾਵਰਾ and ਅਖਾਣ meanings, and several comprehension passages.
- Takeaway: language plus comprehension is about a quarter of the paper — the cheapest 20-25 marks towards the 80 needed.
- Punjabi items are short and fast for a native reader; English-medium aspirants should still revise Gurmukhi spelling rules and 100 common idioms and proverbs.
- Passages are usually social or administrative themes (governance, environment, education) of 200-400 words with 2-4 questions each.

### Comprehension method sheet — question types and attack

- Main idea / title: must cover the whole passage; reject options that describe only one paragraph or one example.
- Inference: something that must be true from the text without being stated; reject options needing outside knowledge.
- Assumption: an unstated premise the author's argument depends on — negate the option; if the argument collapses, it is the assumption.
- Author's purpose / tone: look at verbs (argue, warn, describe) and qualifiers (merely, unfortunately, remarkably) to tell critical, analytical, optimistic or ironic tones apart.
- Strengthen / weaken: new information that supports or attacks the link between evidence and conclusion, not the evidence itself.
- Application / analogy: choose the situation that follows the same logic as the passage.
- Reading routine: read the questions first, skim the first and last lines of each paragraph, then read actively, marking claims, contrasts (however, yet) and conclusions (therefore, hence).
- Elimination order: out of scope, then extreme words (all, never, only, always), then partially true options, then pick the best of the remaining two.

### English grammar rules that recur (beyond basic agreement)

- Hardly / Scarcely ... when; No sooner ... than — 'Hardly had he arrived when...', never 'hardly ... than'.
- Lest takes 'should' or the bare verb, never 'not': 'Work hard lest you should fail.'
- One of those who / which takes a plural verb ('one of those officers who are honest'); 'one of the officers is' remains singular.
- As well as, along with, together with, in addition to do not change the subject's number: 'The minister, along with his aides, was present.'
- Since + point of time, for + duration: 'since 2019', 'for five years'; both with the present perfect.
- Would rather + past form for present wishes: 'I would rather you left now.'
- Unless already means 'if not' — 'unless you do not work' is wrong.
- Pronoun case after prepositions: 'between you and me', not 'between you and I'.
- Prepositions often tested: abide by, accede to, adhere to, averse to, comply with, conducive to, deprived of, indifferent to, insist on, prefer to.
- Conditionals: if + present, will + verb; if + past, would + verb; if + past perfect, would have + past participle.

### Punjabi section method sheet (ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਭਾਸ਼ਾ)

- ਮੁਹਾਵਰਾ (idiom) is a phrase ending in a verb (-ਣਾ/-ਨਾ) whose meaning is figurative; ਅਖਾਣ (proverb) is a complete saying that expresses a general truth — exams ask which one fits a situation.
- High-frequency ਮੁਹਾਵਰੇ: ਨੱਕ ਵਿੱਚ ਦਮ ਕਰਨਾ (to harass), ਪੱਤਰਾ ਵਾਚਣਾ (to run away), ਘਿਓ ਦੇ ਦੀਵੇ ਬਾਲਣਾ (to celebrate), ਸਿਰ ਤੇ ਕੱਫ਼ਣ ਬੰਨ੍ਹਣਾ (to be ready to die), ਦੰਦ ਖੱਟੇ ਕਰਨਾ (to defeat badly), ਹੱਥ ਪੈਰ ਮਾਰਨਾ (to make every effort).
- High-frequency ਅਖਾਣ: ਨਾਲੇ ਚੋਰ ਨਾਲੇ ਚਤਰ (a wrongdoer who also acts clever and blames others), ਜਿਹੋ ਜਿਹਾ ਦੇਸ, ਉਹੋ ਜਿਹਾ ਭੇਸ (adapt to local ways), ਹੱਥ ਕੰਗਣ ਨੂੰ ਆਰਸੀ ਕੀ (the obvious needs no proof).
- ਬਹੁ-ਅਰਥਕ ਸ਼ਬਦ (one word, several meanings): ਕਲ (yesterday / tomorrow / peace of mind), ਹਾਰ (defeat / necklace), ਸੋਨਾ (gold / to sleep), ਫਲ (fruit / result).
- Nasal marks rule of thumb: ਟਿੱਪੀ goes with ਮੁਕਤਾ, ਸਿਹਾਰੀ, ਔਂਕੜ and ਦੁਲੈਂਕੜ (ਅੰਗ, ਸਿੰਘ, ਮੁੰਡਾ, ਮੂੰਹ); ਬਿੰਦੀ goes with ਕੰਨਾ, ਬਿਹਾਰੀ, ਲਾਂ, ਦੁਲਾਵਾਂ, ਹੋੜਾ, ਕਨੌੜਾ (ਮਾਂ, ਪੀਂਘ, ਗੇਂਦ, ਮੈਂ, ਸੌਂ) — with a few exceptions to memorise.
- ਅੱਧਕ (gemination mark) doubles the following consonant (ਪੱਕਾ, ਸੱਚ); spelling questions often hinge on a missing ਅੱਧਕ or a wrong ਲਗ.
- ਸ਼ੁੱਧ ਸ਼ਬਦ-ਜੋੜ practice list: ਅਧਿਆਪਕ, ਪ੍ਰਸ਼ਾਸਨ, ਵਿਦਿਆਰਥੀ, ਪ੍ਰੀਖਿਆ, ਜ਼ਿੰਮੇਵਾਰੀ, ਸਿੱਖਿਆ — check pairin (ਪੈਰ ਵਿੱਚ) letters and nukta (ਸ਼, ਜ਼, ਖ਼, ਗ਼, ਫ਼).
- ਨਾਟਕੀ ਵਾਰਤਾਲਾਪ items test whether a dialogue is natural, polite and in correct sequence — read it aloud mentally.
- Source: PSEB Punjabi grammar books (Classes 9-12) and Punjabi University's free learnpunjabi.org grammar and dictionary tools.

### Time strategy for clearing the 40% CSAT cut-off

- Target: 32 correct out of 80 — aim for 40-45 attempts with high accuracy plus intelligent guesses on the rest (no negative marking).
- Pass 1 (first 15 minutes): all direct language items (English and Punjabi) — about 30-40 seconds each; mark doubtful ones.
- Pass 2 (next 35 minutes): reading passages, starting with the shortest and most familiar topic; budget about 1 minute per question including reading time.
- Pass 3 (next 50 minutes): easy numeracy and data items (percentages, ratios, simple tables), then short reasoning (directions, blood relations, coding).
- Pass 4 (last 20 minutes): long puzzles and heavy calculations only if time remains; in the last 3 minutes fill every blank with the most plausible option.
- Practice rule: one full paper every week in the final year and one sectional drill twice a week earlier; log every error by type (vocabulary, inference, calculation slip).
- Never spend more than 3 minutes on any single question in the exam — the cut-off is low, so breadth beats depth.

### Common traps

- Choosing an option that is true in real life but not stated or implied in the passage.
- Mixing 'hardly...when' with 'no sooner...than'.
- Treating a ਮੁਹਾਵਰਾ as literal (for example, thinking ਦੰਦ ਖੱਟੇ ਕਰਨਾ is about sour food).
- Picking a synonym when the question asks for an antonym — circle the instruction word before answering.
- Over-reading tone: neutral, analytical passages are common; 'sarcastic' or 'hostile' is rarely right.
- Leaving questions blank in a paper without negative marking.

## Practice MCQs (20)

**1.** Passage: 'In many central blocks of the state, groundwater is drawn faster than it is recharged. Electricity for tubewells is free, so pumping carries no direct cost to the farmer, and paddy, a thirsty crop, dominates the kharif season.' Which assumption is most clearly implied by the author?

(a) Farmers are unaware that the water table is falling  
(b) Paddy cultivation should be banned in the state  
(c) Groundwater can never be recharged once depleted  
(d) The cost of power influences how much water farmers pump

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) The cost of power influences how much water farmers pump** — The author links free power to 'no direct cost' of pumping, which only matters if price affects behaviour, so the hidden assumption is that power cost shapes pumping. A ban is a recommendation the text never makes; 'never recharged' is extreme and contradicted by the word 'recharged'; farmer awareness is not discussed at all.

</details>

**2.** Choose the word closest in meaning to PRUDENT.

(a) Generous  
(b) Reckless  
(c) Arrogant  
(d) Judicious

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Judicious** — Prudent means acting with care and good judgement, especially about the future. Judicious (showing good judgement) matches. Reckless is its opposite; generous and arrogant describe unrelated qualities.

</details>

**3.** Identify the correction needed: 'Neither the Deputy Commissioner nor his assistants was present at the meeting.'

(a) Replace 'nor' with 'or'  
(b) Replace 'his' with 'their'  
(c) No correction is needed  
(d) Replace 'was' with 'were'

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Replace 'was' with 'were'** — With 'neither...nor' the verb agrees with the nearer subject. The nearer subject is 'his assistants' (plural), so the verb must be 'were'. 'Neither' correctly pairs with 'nor', and 'his' correctly refers to the Deputy Commissioner.

</details>

**4.** Arrange into a logical paragraph. P: As a result, the water table has fallen sharply in most blocks. Q: Since the 1970s, paddy cultivation has expanded rapidly in Punjab. R: Paddy needs far more water than the crops it replaced. S: Farmers met this extra demand largely by pumping groundwater.

(a) QSRP  
(b) RQSP  
(c) SQRP  
(d) QRSP

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) QRSP** — Q introduces the topic with a time marker and no pronoun, so it opens. R explains why paddy matters (more water). S says how that demand was met ('this extra demand' refers back to R). P begins 'As a result', so it is the consequence and comes last: Q-R-S-P.

</details>

**5.** ਸ਼ਬਦ 'ਆਦਰ' ਦਾ ਵਿਰੋਧੀ ਸ਼ਬਦ ਚੁਣੋ। (Choose the antonym of 'ਆਦਰ'.)

(a) ਸਤਿਕਾਰ  
(b) ਨਿਰਾਦਰ  
(c) ਮਾਣ  
(d) ਇੱਜ਼ਤ

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) ਨਿਰਾਦਰ** — ਆਦਰ means respect. Adding the prefix ਨਿਰ- (without) gives ਨਿਰਾਦਰ, disrespect, the antonym. ਸਤਿਕਾਰ, ਮਾਣ and ਇੱਜ਼ਤ all mean respect or honour, so they are synonyms, not antonyms.

</details>

**6.** ਮੁਹਾਵਰੇ 'ਅੱਖਾਂ ਵਿੱਚ ਘੱਟਾ ਪਾਉਣਾ' ਦਾ ਸਹੀ ਅਰਥ ਕੀ ਹੈ? (What does this idiom mean?)

(a) ਬਹੁਤ ਮਿਹਨਤ ਕਰਨਾ (to work very hard)  
(b) ਬਹੁਤ ਗੁੱਸੇ ਹੋਣਾ (to be very angry)  
(c) ਬਹੁਤ ਸ਼ਰਮਿੰਦਾ ਹੋਣਾ (to be deeply ashamed)  
(d) ਧੋਖਾ ਦੇਣਾ (to deceive)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) ਧੋਖਾ ਦੇਣਾ (to deceive)** — Literally 'to throw dust in the eyes', the idiom means to deceive someone so they cannot see the truth, exactly like the English 'throw dust in someone's eyes'. Very angry is ਅੱਗ ਬਬੂਲਾ ਹੋਣਾ and deeply ashamed is ਪਾਣੀ ਪਾਣੀ ਹੋਣਾ.

</details>

**7.** Passage: 'Wetlands such as Harike act as natural sponges. They store floodwater during the monsoon and release it slowly in the dry months. When such wetlands are drained for farming, the same rainfall produces higher flood peaks downstream and lower river flows in summer.' Which inference is best supported by the passage?

(a) Wetlands are useful only during the monsoon months  
(b) Harike is the largest wetland in the whole country  
(c) Farming should be banned near all rivers and lakes  
(d) Draining wetlands can worsen floods and water shortage

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Draining wetlands can worsen floods and water shortage** — Step 1: the passage gives two functions — storing monsoon water (which lowers flood peaks) and releasing it in dry months (which keeps summer flows up). Step 2: it says draining wetlands raises flood peaks and lowers summer flows, so removing them harms both. The option “Wetlands are useful only during the monsoo…” contradicts 'release slowly in the dry months'; the option “Farming should be banned near all rivers a…” is an extreme policy the author never proposes; the option “Harike is the largest wetland in the whole…” is outside information. Answer: “Draining wetlands can worsen floods and wa…”.

</details>

**8.** Passage: 'Many young people in rural Punjab now apply for study visas. Coaching centres for English tests have multiplied in small towns, and some families sell land to pay fees. Yet a growing number of students abroad report working long hours in low-paid jobs while studying.' Consider the following inferences:  
1. English-test coaching centres are the main reason why young people in rural Punjab want to migrate.  
2. All students who go abroad end up in low-paid work.  
Which of the inferences given above is/are valid?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) Neither 1 nor 2** — Inference 1 is not valid: the passage places coaching alongside the wish to migrate, which suggests demand for migration drives the coaching boom, not that coaching causes migration; the inference reverses cause and effect. Inference 2 is not valid: the passage says 'a growing number', not 'all', of students abroad work in low-paid jobs. So neither inference is valid.

</details>

**9.** Choose the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning to EPHEMERAL.

(a) Momentary  
(b) Fleeting  
(c) Enduring  
(d) Transient

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(c) Enduring** — Ephemeral means lasting a very short time (from Greek 'lasting a day'). Transient, fleeting and momentary all mean short-lived, so they are synonyms — the trap for anyone who misreads 'opposite'. Enduring means lasting a long time, the antonym.

</details>

**10.** Identify the correction needed: 'Hardly had the officer reached the village than the rain started.'

(a) Replace 'had' with 'has'  
(b) Replace 'than' with 'when'  
(c) Replace 'reached' with 'reach'  
(d) No correction is needed

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) Replace 'than' with 'when'** — The fixed pairs are 'hardly/scarcely... when' and 'no sooner... than'. The sentence uses 'hardly', so 'than' must become 'when': 'Hardly had the officer reached the village when the rain started.' The inverted past perfect 'had... reached' is correct after 'hardly', so “Replace 'had' with 'has'” and “Replace 'reached' with 'reach'” would introduce errors.

</details>

**11.** What does the idiom 'to bell the cat' mean?

(a) To take on a dangerous task for the group  
(b) To bribe a powerful official quietly  
(c) To keep a close watch on a rival's moves  
(d) To spoil a secret plan by talking early

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(a) To take on a dangerous task for the group** — In the fable, the mice agree that a bell on the cat would warn them, but no mouse dares to tie it. So 'to bell the cat' means to volunteer for a risky job that everyone agrees is needed. The other options describe surveillance, sabotage and bribery, which do not fit the story.

</details>

**12.** ਸ਼ਬਦ 'ਅਕਾਸ਼' ਦਾ ਸਮਾਨਾਰਥਕ ਸ਼ਬਦ ਚੁਣੋ। (Choose the synonym of 'ਅਕਾਸ਼'.)

(a) ਸਾਗਰ  
(b) ਪਾਤਾਲ  
(c) ਧਰਤੀ  
(d) ਅੰਬਰ

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(d) ਅੰਬਰ** — ਅਕਾਸ਼ means sky; its synonyms are ਅੰਬਰ, ਗਗਨ and ਅਸਮਾਨ. ਧਰਤੀ (earth) is its usual opposite pair, ਪਾਤਾਲ is the underworld, and ਸਾਗਰ is the sea — none means sky. Answer: ਅੰਬਰ.

</details>

**13.** ਮੁਹਾਵਰੇ 'ਦੰਦ ਖੱਟੇ ਕਰਨਾ' ਦਾ ਸਹੀ ਅਰਥ ਕੀ ਹੈ? (What does this idiom mean?)

(a) ਡਰ ਕੇ ਭੱਜ ਜਾਣਾ (to run away in fear)  
(b) ਬੁਰੀ ਤਰ੍ਹਾਂ ਹਰਾ ਦੇਣਾ (to defeat badly)  
(c) ਭੁੱਖੇ ਰਹਿਣਾ (to go hungry)  
(d) ਬਹੁਤ ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ ਹੋਣਾ (to be very happy)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b) ਬੁਰੀ ਤਰ੍ਹਾਂ ਹਰਾ ਦੇਣਾ (to defeat badly)** — Literally 'to make someone's teeth sour', the idiom means to give a crushing defeat — for example, soldiers who beat back an invader 'ਦੁਸ਼ਮਣ ਦੇ ਦੰਦ ਖੱਟੇ ਕਰ ਦਿੱਤੇ'. Running away in fear is expressed by ਪੱਤਰਾ ਵਾਚਣਾ, and the other two options have no link with the idiom.

</details>

**14.** Consider the following sentences:  
1. 'One of the applicants were not eligible for the post.'  
2. 'Each of the clerks have been transferred to the tehsil office.'  
Which of the sentences given above is/are grammatically correct?

(a) 1 only  
(b) 2 only  
(c) Both 1 and 2  
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

**(d) Neither 1 nor 2** — 'One of' and 'Each of' pick out a single person and take a singular verb. Sentence 1 should read 'One of the applicants was not eligible', and sentence 2 should read 'Each of the clerks has been transferred'. So neither sentence is correct.

**15.** Passage: 'Minimum support prices were introduced in the 1960s to persuade farmers to adopt new high-yielding seeds at a time of food shortage. The policy worked so well that the country now holds grain stocks far above its buffer norms. Yet the same assured price, available in practice for only two crops in a few states, now discourages the very diversification that soil and water conditions demand. A tool designed to overcome scarcity has become an obstacle in an age of surplus. Reform, therefore, need not mean abolishing support; it may mean redirecting it.' Which of the following best reflects the author's view?

(a) Price support should be redirected to suit present conditions, not abolished  
(b) Farmers should be stopped from growing wheat and paddy altogether  
(c) Price support failed to achieve the purpose for which it was introduced
