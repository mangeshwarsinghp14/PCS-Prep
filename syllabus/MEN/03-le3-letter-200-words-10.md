# Letter, 200 words (10)

## Short notes

- Formal letter layout (block format): sender's address, date, receiver's designation and address, subject line, salutation, body, complimentary close, signature and name.
- Salutation for officials: 'Sir/Madam'. Close: 'Yours faithfully'. To a known person by name: 'Dear Mr. Singh' and 'Yours sincerely'.
- Informal letters: address and date, 'Dear (name)', friendly body, close with 'Yours lovingly/affectionately/sincerely'.
- In exams, use 'Examination Hall' and 'A.B.C.' in place of your real name and address unless told otherwise; never reveal your identity.
- Subject line: one line stating the purpose, e.g. 'Subject: Request for repair of the village drinking-water supply'.
- Body paragraph 1: who you are and why you are writing. Paragraph 2: facts and details (dates, places, effects). Paragraph 3: the specific request or suggestion and a polite close.
- Types asked: application to an official (DC, SDM, Municipal Commissioner), letter to the editor, complaint, request, and personal letters.
- Letter to the editor: begin 'Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper, I wish to draw the attention of the authorities to...' and end with a request to publish.
- Tone: polite, direct and specific; avoid emotional or threatening language in complaints.
- Word limit 200: about 20 for headers, 160-170 for the body, rest for close; overshooting wastes time but no single word count is penalised exactly.
- Use dates in a clear format (3 November 2026) and give concrete facts so the receiver can act.
- Avoid outdated phrases like 'I beg to state'; prefer 'I wish to bring to your notice'.
- End with a clear action line: 'I request you to kindly take necessary action at the earliest.'
- Check: correct close (faithfully vs sincerely), subject line present, paragraphs separate, no spelling errors in designations.

## Punjab angle

- Common Punjab-themed topics: stubble smoke, drug abuse in a village, stray cattle on roads, poor water supply, dengue, flood damage claims (2025 floods).
- Officials to address: Deputy Commissioner (district), Sub Divisional Magistrate (sub-division), Commissioner of the Municipal Corporation, Senior Superintendent of Police.
- Mention Punjab-specific facts only if sure, such as the district name and the problem's effects, without invented statistics.

## How PPSC asks it

Marks are given for correct format, clarity of purpose, relevant details and polite, error-free language. Learn the block format thoroughly and practise one formal letter per week within 200 words.

## Deep dive

### Salutation–close pairs and format variants

- 'Sir' / 'Madam' / 'Sir or Madam' (to an office or unknown officer) → 'Yours faithfully'.
- 'Dear Mr Singh' / 'Dear Dr Kaur' (named person, formal) → 'Yours sincerely'.
- 'Dear Harpreet' (friend) → 'Yours affectionately' / 'With love' / 'Yours ever'; to elders → 'Your loving son/daughter'.
- Full-block format (everything aligned left, no indents) is the modern standard and safest in exams; semi-block (indented paragraphs) is also accepted.
- Subject line (formal letters) goes after the salutation in Indian convention or before it in the British convention — choose one and be consistent; most Indian answer keys place it before 'Sir'.
- Enclosures line ('Encl.: copy of FIR') and copy line ('Copy to: the SDM, Kharar') go below the signature.
- Date in full: 4 November 2026 — avoid 4/11/26, which is ambiguous between Indian and US styles.

### Officialese to avoid → plain modern English

- 'I beg to state that…' → 'I wish to bring to your notice that…'.
- 'Kindly do the needful' → state the exact action: 'Please get the hand-pump repaired this week.'
- 'Your good self' → 'you'; 'Respected Sir' → 'Sir'.
- 'Please revert back' → 'please reply'; 'discuss about' → 'discuss'.
- 'Thanking you in anticipation' → 'Thank you for your attention to this matter.'
- 'Prepone' (Indian English) is understood but better written as 'bring forward' in formal writing.
- One idea per paragraph, sentences under 20 words, active voice — the same rules the government's own plain-English guides use.

### Phrase bank by purpose

- Complaint opening: 'I am writing to draw your attention to the frequent power cuts in Ward No. 7, which have continued for two weeks.'
- Effect line: 'Students preparing for board examinations and patients on oxygen support are the worst affected.'
- Request line: 'I request you to direct the officials concerned to restore regular supply and announce scheduled cuts in advance.'
- Editor's letter opening: 'Through the columns of your newspaper, I wish to highlight…'; close: 'I hope this letter will prompt the authorities to act.'
- Job application: 'In response to your advertisement in The Tribune dated 2 November 2026, I wish to apply for the post of…'.
- Gratitude: 'I am grateful for the prompt action taken by your office on my earlier complaint.'
- Personal advice: 'I know the decision is difficult, but I would suggest…'; condolence: 'I was deeply saddened to learn of…'.
- Polite firmness: 'If the problem persists, I may be compelled to approach the higher authorities.'

### Model body — application to the Deputy Commissioner (about 160 words)

- Subject: Stray cattle causing accidents on the Kharar–Landran road.
- Para 1: 'I am a resident of Sector 115, Kharar, and wish to bring to your notice the danger posed by stray cattle on the Kharar–Landran road, especially after dark.'
- Para 2: 'In the last month alone, three two-wheeler accidents have occurred near the canal bridge; one young man suffered serious head injuries. Cattle sit in the middle of the road where there are no street lights.'
- Para 3: 'I request you to direct the Municipal Council to move the animals to the nearest gaushala, install reflective signs and repair the street lights on this stretch.'
- Close: 'Thank you for your attention to this matter. Yours faithfully, A.B.C., Examination Hall, 4 November 2026.'
- Why it scores: specific place, recent facts, clear harm, three concrete actions, polite tone, correct close.

### Other formats worth knowing

- Demi-official (D.O.) letter: officer-to-officer, personal tone ('Dear Shri Sharma'), close 'Yours sincerely' — useful if the paper asks for a letter as an official.
- Office memorandum / circular: no salutation or close; numbered paragraphs; issued 'by order' — know the idea, rarely asked at 10+2 level.
- Email: clear subject line, short paragraphs, signature block; no 'Dear Sir/Madam' if writing to a named person.
- Letter to the editor: 150–200 words, one issue, newspaper's name in the address, ends with your name and town.
- Job application with a separate CV: covering letter of 120–150 words plus a bio-data table — only if the question asks.
- Personal letters still need structure: greeting, news/reason, advice or response, warm close.

### Checklist and common errors

- Sender's address and date present (or 'Examination Hall' and date); receiver's designation and full office address.
- Subject line specific and under 12 words.
- Salutation and close match (Sir ↔ Yours faithfully).
- No real name or roll number; use A.B.C. or X.Y.Z.
- No apostrophe in 'Yours' ('Your's' is wrong); capital Y only for 'Yours'.
- Body in 3 paragraphs within about 150–170 words; no repetition of the subject line as the first sentence.
- Re-read for subject–verb agreement and articles — the most frequent slips in letters.

### Exact formal-letter layout (Examination Hall convention) and a 10-point checklist

- Sender block, top left: 'Examination Hall,' / 'Patiala.' (city only if the question names it) / '4 November 2026' — this replaces your real address; never write your name, roll number or village.
- Receiver block after one blank line: 'To' / 'The Deputy Commissioner,' / 'Patiala.' For an editor: 'The Editor,' / 'The Tribune,' / 'Chandigarh.' The 'To' line is the usual Indian school convention and is accepted; it may be omitted in full-block style.
- Subject line, then salutation: 'Subject: Request for repair of the Rajpura road damaged by floods' / 'Sir,' (or 'Madam,' / 'Sir or Madam,'). Indian answer keys normally place the subject before the salutation — keep one order throughout.
- Body in three paragraphs (about 150–160 words): purpose and context; facts and effects; specific request with a polite close ('I request you to … at the earliest. Thank you for your attention to this matter.'). Editor's letters open 'Through the columns of your newspaper, I wish to draw attention to …' and end with a request to publish.
- Close at the left: 'Yours faithfully,' / 'A.B.C.' Add 'Encl.: …' or 'Copy to: The Sub Divisional Magistrate, Rajpura' below the name only if needed.
- Check 1 — Letter type identified correctly: application to an official, letter to the editor, complaint, or informal letter; format follows the type.
- Check 2 — 'Examination Hall' and the date in full (4 November 2026); no personal identity anywhere.
- Check 3 — Receiver's designation and office place are correct and the officer can act on the issue (DC, SDM, BDPO, Municipal Commissioner, Executive Engineer, Editor).
- Check 4 — Subject line specific and under about 12 words, not a full sentence.
- Check 5 — Salutation and close match: Sir/Madam → Yours faithfully; Dear Mr Singh → Yours sincerely; no apostrophe in 'Yours'.
- Check 6 — Opening sentence states the purpose directly; no 'I beg to state'.
- Check 7 — At least two concrete facts (place, since when, who is affected) and two or three clear requests or suggestions.
- Check 8 — Polite, firm tone without threats or emotional exaggeration.
- Check 9 — Within about 200 words; paragraphs separated by a blank line; the subject line is not repeated as the first sentence.
- Check 10 — Final proof-read for subject–verb agreement, articles and spellings of designations (Commissioner, Magistrate, faithfully, sincerely, committee).

## Mains practice questions (7)

**1.** Write a letter to the editor of a newspaper about smoke from stubble burning in your area and suggest remedies (200 words).

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Header: Examination Hall, date; The Editor, (newspaper), city; Subject: Health hazard from stubble burning.
- Opening: 'Through the columns of your esteemed newspaper...' draw attention to smoke every October-November.
- Effects: breathing problems in children and elderly, road accidents due to low visibility, loss of soil nutrients.
- Causes briefly: short window before wheat sowing, cost of machinery.
- Suggestions: more custom hiring centres for residue machines, support for in-situ and ex-situ management, awareness through panchayats, monitoring of fire incidents.
- Close: request to publish; Yours faithfully, A.B.C.

</details>

**2.** Write an application to the Sub Divisional Magistrate requesting action against stray cattle on a highway near your village (200 words).

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Header: To, The Sub Divisional Magistrate, (sub-division); Subject: Request for action against stray cattle on the highway.
- Salutation: Sir/Madam.
- Facts: location, time of day, number of animals, recent accidents or near-misses.
- Impact: risk to commuters and school buses, crop damage.
- Request: coordinate with municipal/panchayat bodies and gaushalas to shift cattle, install warning signs, regular patrolling.
- Close: 'I request you to kindly take necessary action at the earliest.' Yours faithfully, A.B.C., resident of (village).

</details>

**3.** Write a letter to your younger brother advising him to stay away from drugs and focus on studies and sports (200 words).

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Informal format: address, date, 'Dear (name)'.
- Opening: warm enquiry and reason for writing (news about drug abuse among youth).
- Body: harms of drugs on health, studies, family and future; how peer pressure works and how to refuse.
- Positive alternatives: sports, gym, hobbies, good friends, talking to family when stressed.
- Encouragement and confidence in him; offer to help.
- Close: 'Yours lovingly, (name)'.

</details>

**4.** Write a letter to the editor of a newspaper about children working at roadside dhabas and repair shops in your town, and suggest steps to end child labour (about 200 words).

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Header: Examination Hall, (city), date; To The Editor, The Tribune, Chandigarh; Subject: Child labour at roadside dhabas and workshops.
- Opening: 'Through the columns of your newspaper, I wish to draw the attention of the authorities and the public to the children working at dhabas and repair shops along the highway in our town.'
- Facts: boys of about 10–14 serving tea, washing utensils and repairing tyres late into the night; most have left school; long hours, low pay, exposure to abuse and accidents.
- Law (state generally): employment of children is prohibited under the Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986 (as amended in 2016), and education up to 14 is a right under the RTE Act, 2009.
- Suggestions: joint inspections by the labour department and police; rescue and admission of children to nearby schools with mid-day meals; support to poor families through welfare schemes; fines on employers; helpline number displayed at dhabas; citizens refusing to patronise such places.
- Close: 'I hope this letter will prompt the authorities to act before more childhoods are lost.' Yours faithfully, A.B.C.
- Keep the tone concerned, not abusive; do not name a specific shop.

</details>

**5.** Write an application to the Sub Divisional Magistrate complaining about illegal sand mining in the river bed near your village and the danger it poses to the embankment (about 200 words).

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Examination Hall, date; To The Sub Divisional Magistrate, (sub-division); Subject: Illegal sand mining near village …… and threat to the river embankment.
- Para 1: 'I am a resident of village …… and wish to bring to your notice that sand is being dug illegally from the river bed close to the village every night.'
- Para 2 — facts and effects: tractors and machines working after dark; deep pits near the embankment; weakened bank raises flood risk during the monsoon; damaged village road; noise and danger to residents.
- Para 3 — requests: inspection by revenue, mining and drainage officials; seizure of machines and action under the law; night patrolling; repair of the weakened embankment before the monsoon.
- Polite firmness: 'If timely action is not taken, the village may face serious flood damage.' (no threats of protest).
- Close: Thank you for your attention to this matter. Yours faithfully, A.B.C.; Copy to: The Deputy Commissioner (optional).
- Do not invent names of offenders; give location, time and effects precisely.

</details>

**6.** Write a letter to the Commissioner of the Municipal Corporation about stagnant water and garbage in your ward, which have led to cases of dengue, and request urgent action (about 200 words).

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Examination Hall, date; To The Commissioner, Municipal Corporation, ……; Subject: Stagnant water, garbage and dengue cases in Ward No. ……
- Opening: 'I am writing to draw your attention to the unhygienic conditions in Ward No. ……, which have led to several cases of dengue in recent weeks.'
- Facts: blocked drains, water standing in vacant plots, garbage not lifted for days; mosquito breeding; families with fever cases; children and elderly most at risk.
- Requests: regular lifting of garbage; cleaning of drains; fogging and anti-larval spraying; awareness drive with the health department; penalties for dumping in vacant plots.
- Citizens' role (one line): residents are ready to cooperate in a ward cleanliness drive.
- Close: 'I request you to treat this as urgent.' Yours faithfully, A.B.C.
- Check: subject line specific; numbers of cases only if given in the question — otherwise write 'several'.

</details>

**7.** Write an application to the District Education Officer requesting the posting of teachers in your village government school, where several posts are lying vacant (about 200 words).

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Examination Hall, date; To The District Education Officer (Secondary/Elementary), ……; Subject: Request for filling vacant teaching posts in Government School, village ……
- Para 1: introduce yourself as a parent/member of the School Management Committee and state the problem.
- Para 2 — facts: posts of mathematics, science and English teachers vacant since the last session; one teacher handling several classes; syllabus not completed; students shifting to costly private schools or dropping out, especially girls.
- Para 3 — requests: immediate posting of regular teachers or temporary arrangement through deputation; filling posts before board examinations; regular inspection.
- Mention the right to education and the board-examination results only in general terms; avoid invented data.
- Close: 'We shall be grateful for your early action.' Yours faithfully, A.B.C.
- Common slip to avoid: 'Respected Sir' and 'Thanking you in anticipation' — use 'Sir' and 'Thank you for your attention to this matter'.

</details>
