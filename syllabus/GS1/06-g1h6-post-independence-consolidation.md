## Short notes

- Challenges in 1947: Partition and refugee rehabilitation, integration of princely states, Constitution-making, poverty and food shortage, linguistic demands, communal tensions, Kashmir war.
- Integration of princely states: Sardar Patel and V.P. Menon used Instruments of Accession (defence, external affairs, communications) and Standstill Agreements; later Privy Purses abolished (26th Amendment, 1971).
- Holdouts: Junagadh (plebiscite Feb 1948), Hyderabad (Operation Polo, Sept 1948), Jammu and Kashmir (Instrument of Accession, 26 Oct 1947).
- Colonial enclaves: French settlements transferred de facto in 1954 (de jure 1962); Goa, Daman and Diu liberated in Dec 1961; Sikkim became a state in 1975 (36th Amendment).
- Democratic consolidation: Constitution adopted 26 Nov 1949, in force 26 Jan 1950; Election Commission; first general elections 1951-52 with universal adult franchise.
- Linguistic question: Dhar Commission (1948) and JVP Committee (1949 - Nehru, Patel, Pattabhi Sitaramayya) advised caution; Potti Sriramulu's death after a fast (Dec 1952) led to Andhra State (1 Oct 1953).
- States Reorganisation Commission (1953; Fazl Ali, K.M. Panikkar, H.N. Kunzru) - report 1955; States Reorganisation Act 1956 created 14 states and 6 Union Territories, abolishing Part A/B/C/D categories.
- Later reorganisations: Gujarat and Maharashtra (1960), Nagaland (1963), Punjab-Haryana (1966), North-Eastern reorganisation (1971-72), Mizoram, Arunachal and Goa statehood (1987), Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand, Jharkhand (2000), Telangana (2014), J&K split into two UTs (2019), merger of Dadra-Nagar Haveli with Daman-Diu (2020).
- Language policy: Article 343 (Hindi in Devanagari as official language), Official Languages Act 1963, anti-Hindi agitation in Tamil Nadu (1965), three-language formula - compromise preserved unity.
- Economic consolidation: Planning Commission (March 1950), First Five-Year Plan (1951), Industrial Policy Resolution 1956 (Mahalanobis model), zamindari abolition, Bhoodan (Vinoba Bhave, 1951).
- Tribal and North-East integration: Fifth and Sixth Schedules, Nehru's 'Panchsheel for tribals' (Verrier Elwin), Naga and Mizo accords (Mizo Accord 1986).
- Institutions of unity: All-India Services, Finance Commission, Zonal Councils (SR Act 1956), Inter-State Council (Art 263; set up 1990 on Sarkaria's advice), single judiciary.
- Foreign policy consolidation: Non-alignment, Panchsheel (1954); wars of 1947-48, 1962, 1965, 1971 tested national cohesion.
- Grassroots democracy: Balwantrai Mehta Committee (1957) launched Panchayati Raj; constitutional status by 73rd and 74th Amendments (1992).
- Assessment: linguistic states, contrary to fears, deepened democracy and unity; but regional disparities, insurgencies and inter-state disputes (water, boundary) persist.

## Punjab angle

- Refugee rehabilitation in East Punjab: land allotment, new colonies and townships; capital shifted from Lahore, with Chandigarh planned by Le Corbusier as the new capital.
- PEPSU (Patiala and East Punjab States Union) formed in 1948 from princely states (Patiala, Nabha, Jind, Kapurthala, Faridkot, Malerkotla, etc.) and merged into Punjab in 1956.
- Punjabi Suba movement (Master Tara Singh, Sant Fateh Singh) led to the Punjab Reorganisation Act 1966: Punjab and Haryana created on 1 Nov 1966, hill areas to Himachal Pradesh, Chandigarh made a UT and joint capital.
- Unresolved issues: Chandigarh's status, Punjabi-speaking areas, river waters and the SYL canal; Anandpur Sahib Resolution (1973) and Rajiv-Longowal Accord (1985) remain reference points.

## How PPSC asks it

Typical questions: integration of princely states, linguistic reorganisation (did it strengthen unity?), and Punjab's reorganisation. Examiners reward use of commissions, dates and a balanced assessment linking to present federal issues.

## Deep dive

### Reorganisation timeline, 1947–2020 (date — event — legal basis)

- 5 July 1947 — States Department under Sardar Patel (secretary V.P. Menon); 15 August 1947 — nearly all states acceded on defence, external affairs and communications.
- 15 April 1948 — Himachal Pradesh formed from about 30 hill states as a Chief Commissioner's province; 15 July 1948 — PEPSU inaugurated (Rajpramukh: Maharaja Yadavindra Singh of Patiala).
- 1948–49 — unions of states: Saurashtra, Matsya, Vindhya Pradesh, Madhya Bharat, Travancore–Cochin; Rajasthan formed in stages (completed 1949); Manipur merger agreement (September 1949).
- 26 January 1950 — Constitution classifies states into Part A (former provinces), Part B (princely unions under Rajpramukhs), Part C (Chief Commissioner's states) and Part D (Andaman and Nicobar).
- 1 October 1953 — Andhra State (Andhra State Act, 1953) after Potti Sriramulu's death (15 December 1952); December 1953 — States Reorganisation Commission appointed.
- 30 September 1955 — SRC report; 1 November 1956 — States Reorganisation Act and Seventh Amendment: 14 states and 6 UTs; Part A–D classes abolished; five Zonal Councils created.
- 1 May 1960 — Bombay Reorganisation Act: Maharashtra and Gujarat; 1961 — Goa, Daman and Diu (Operation Vijay, December) and Dadra–Nagar Haveli (10th Amendment); 1962 — Puducherry de jure (14th Amendment).
- 1 December 1963 — Nagaland (13th Amendment, Article 371A); 1 November 1966 — Punjab, Haryana, transfer of hill areas to HP, Chandigarh UT.
- 25 January 1971 — Himachal Pradesh a full state; 21 January 1972 — Manipur, Tripura, Meghalaya (North-Eastern Areas Reorganisation Act 1971); 1975 — Sikkim (36th Amendment).
- 1987 — Mizoram and Arunachal Pradesh (February) and Goa (May) become states; 2000 — Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand, Jharkhand; 2 June 2014 — Telangana; 31 October 2019 — J&K and Ladakh UTs; 26 January 2020 — Dadra–Nagar Haveli and Daman–Diu merged.

### Integration of princely states — methods and special cases

- Scale: over 560 states covering roughly two-fifths of the subcontinent's area; paramountcy lapsed under the Indian Independence Act 1947.
- Two documents: Instrument of Accession (three subjects) and Standstill Agreement (continuing existing arrangements); later Merger Agreements and Covenants took full control.
- Three integration routes: merger into neighbouring provinces (Orissa and Chhattisgarh states, 1948), centrally administered units (HP, Kutch, Tripura, Manipur, Bhopal, Bilaspur), and unions of states under a Rajpramukh (PEPSU, Saurashtra, Rajasthan, Madhya Bharat, Travancore–Cochin).
- Privy purses guaranteed (Article 291); after the Supreme Court struck down the presidential derecognition (Madhav Rao Scindia case, 1970), the 26th Amendment (1971) abolished privy purses and princely privileges.
- Junagadh — Nawab acceded to Pakistan; blockade, Nawab fled, plebiscite (February 1948) — over 99% for India.
- Hyderabad — Standstill (November 1947), Razakars of Qasim Razvi, Telangana peasant struggle; 'Operation Polo' police action (13–18 September 1948).
- Jammu and Kashmir — tribal invasion, Instrument of Accession (26 October 1947), UN reference (January 1948), Article 370, Delhi Agreement (1952); Article 370 effectively abrogated on 5 August 2019.
- Lesson for answers: combination of persuasion, patriotism and pressure; Patel's 'iron hand in a velvet glove'; V.P. Menon's 'The Story of the Integration of the Indian States' (1956) is the primary account.

### Linguistic reorganisation — commissions and outcomes (body — year — view)

- Linguistic Provinces Commission (S.K. Dhar, 1948) — rejected language as the main basis; preferred administrative convenience and history.
- JVP Committee (Nehru, Patel, Pattabhi Sitaramayya; report April 1949) — postpone linguistic states, except possibly Andhra if Telugu-speakers agreed on Madras.
- Wanchoo Committee (1953) — examined financial and administrative issues of creating Andhra.
- SRC (Fazl Ali, K.M. Panikkar, H.N. Kunzru; 1953–55) — accepted language as an important but not sole basis; tests: unity and security, linguistic–cultural homogeneity, financial and administrative viability, welfare of people; rejected 'one language, one state'.
- SRC on Punjab: did not recommend a separate Punjabi-speaking state; recommended merging PEPSU with Punjab (done in 1956).
- Bombay as a bilingual state (1956) failed amid the Samyukta Maharashtra and Mahagujarat movements (police firing at Flora Fountain, 1956) — split in 1960.
- Language policy: Official Language Commission (B.G. Kher, 1955), Official Languages Act 1963 (amended 1967 — English continues indefinitely), anti-Hindi agitation in Madras (1965); Eighth Schedule grew from 14 languages to 22 (Sindhi 1967; Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali 1992; Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali 2003).
- Assessment: linguistic states strengthened democracy and regional identity without breaking unity (Nehru's fears proved largely unfounded), but left border disputes (Belgaum, Chandigarh) and river-water quarrels.

### Punjab 1947–1985 — consolidation and reorganisation in detail

- 1947 — East Punjab formed; temporary capital at Shimla; massive refugee resettlement — land allotted on 'standard acres', new townships (Rajpura, Faridabad, model towns), Rehabilitation Department.
- Chandigarh: planned first by Albert Mayer and Matthew Nowicki, then by Le Corbusier (from 1951) with Jeanneret, Fry and Drew; inaugurated as capital on 7 October 1953 by President Rajendra Prasad.
- PEPSU (1948–56): Patiala, Nabha, Jind, Kapurthala, Faridkot, Malerkotla, Kalsia and Nalagarh; PEPSU came under President's Rule in March 1953 (Punjab had seen India's first President's Rule in June 1951); merged with Punjab on 1 November 1956.
- Language compromises: Sachar Formula (1949) — Punjabi and Hindi zones for education; Regional Formula (1956) — regional committees of the legislature for Punjabi and Hindi regions.
- Punjabi Suba movement: Akali morchas of 1955 and 1960–61 (Master Tara Singh, Sant Fateh Singh's fast in December 1960); after the 1965 war, the Hukam Singh Parliamentary Committee (1965–66) supported a Punjabi-speaking state.
- Shah Commission (Justice J.C. Shah, report 31 May 1966) demarcated boundaries — its majority placed Kharar tehsil including Chandigarh in Haryana; the Centre instead made Chandigarh a Union Territory and joint capital.
- Punjab Reorganisation Act 1966 (Act 31; enacted 18 September 1966): Punjab and Haryana from 1 November 1966; Kangra, Kullu, Shimla, Lahaul–Spiti and parts of Hoshiarpur/Gurdaspur to Himachal; common High Court; Bhakra Management Board (later BBMB, 1976) under Sections 79–80.
- Unfinished issues: Darshan Singh Pheruman died fasting for Chandigarh (October 1969); 1970 Indira Gandhi award (Chandigarh to Punjab, Abohar–Fazilka to Haryana) never implemented; 1976 water notification and 1981 agreement on Ravi–Beas surplus; SYL canal foundation at Kapoori (April 1982).
- Anandpur Sahib Resolution (Akali Dal, October 1973) — greater state autonomy; Rajiv–Longowal (Punjab) Accord, 24 July 1985 — Chandigarh to Punjab (promised for 26 January 1986), tribunal on river waters (Eradi Tribunal, 1986), referral of the Anandpur resolution to the Sarkaria Commission.

### Economic and social consolidation (measure — year — significance)

- Planning Commission (15 March 1950); First Five-Year Plan (1951–56, agriculture); Second Plan (Mahalanobis model, heavy industry); Industrial Policy Resolution 1956 (mixed economy).
- Land reforms: zamindari abolition laws (UP 1951 and others); First Amendment (1951) added Articles 31A, 31B and the Ninth Schedule to protect them; tenancy reforms and ceilings (Punjab Security of Land Tenures Act 1953).
- Bhoodan (Vinoba Bhave, Pochampalli, 18 April 1951); Community Development Programme (2 October 1952); Balwant Rai Mehta report (1957) → Panchayati Raj at Nagaur (2 October 1959).
- Green Revolution from the mid-1960s — Punjab and Haryana with HYV wheat, Bhakra waters and PAU Ludhiana (1962) became India's granary; food security consolidated the nation after the 1960s shortages.
- Refugee and minority settlement: Ministry of Relief and Rehabilitation; Evacuee Property legislation (1950); Nehru–Liaquat Pact (8 April 1950) on minorities.
- Institutions of unity: Election Commission (Sukumar Sen ran the 1951–52 polls — about 173 million voters), All-India Services (Article 312), Finance Commission (1951), Zonal Councils (1956), Inter-State Council (1990).
- North-East: Naga insurgency (NNC under Phizo), Nagaland (1963), Shillong Accord (1975); Mizo uprising after the Mautam famine (1959) → Mizo Accord (30 June 1986); Assam Accord (15 August 1985); North Eastern Council (1971).

### Analytical debates and Mains frames

- Did linguistic states weaken unity? Evidence says they channelled identity into federal politics; secessionism arose where grievances were ignored (Naga, Mizo, Punjab in the 1980s) rather than where states were conceded.
- Federal design: 'holding together' federation (Alfred Stepan); Parliament's power under Article 3 to alter state boundaries without state consent — used flexibly to absorb demands.
- Historians: Granville Austin (Constitution as a 'social revolution'), Ramachandra Guha ('India After Gandhi' — 'an unnatural nation' that survived), Paul Brass on language and religion in Punjab politics.
- Punjab case study: language + religion overlap made the Punjabi Suba demand sensitive; reorganisation (1966) solved language but left Chandigarh, river waters and Punjabi-speaking villages as continuing disputes.
- Current relevance: demands for new states (Vidarbha, Gorkhaland, Bodoland, Harit Pradesh), calls for a second SRC, UT status changes (J&K 2019) — link history to present.
- Structure for a 250-word answer: context (1947 challenges) → integration → linguistic reorganisation → economic/institutional consolidation → Punjab example → assessment.

### PPSC-style asks and traps

- Andhra State (1953) was the first state formed on a linguistic basis; Andhra Pradesh (with Telangana areas) came in 1956.
- SRC chairman: Fazl Ali; members K.M. Panikkar and H.N. Kunzru — the 1948 Dhar Commission is a different body.
- Haryana and Punjab were created on 1 November 1966 — the same date as the 1956 reorganisation (1 November), a frequent confusion.
- India's first spell of President's Rule was in Punjab (June 1951); PEPSU followed in 1953.
- Chandigarh is a UT and the joint capital; it is not part of either state.
- The Punjab Reorganisation Act is Act 31 of 1966; the States Reorganisation Act is Act 37 of 1956.
- Privy purses were abolished by the 26th Amendment (1971), not the 24th or 42nd.

- Andhra and SRC 1955; SR Act 1956

- Arguments against: sons-of-soil politics, boundary and water disputes, linguistic minorities' grievances
