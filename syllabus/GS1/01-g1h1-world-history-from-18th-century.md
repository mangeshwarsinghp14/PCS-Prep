## Short notes

- Industrial Revolution began in Britain c. 1760s: coal and iron deposits, capital from trade and colonies, agricultural revolution (enclosures), colonial markets and a stable political order.
- Key inventions: Hargreaves' spinning jenny (1764), Watt's improved steam engine (1769), Cartwright's power loom (1785); railways from the 1820s-30s; 'second industrial revolution' (steel, electricity, chemicals) after c. 1870.
- Social effects of industrialisation: factory system, urban slums, child and women labour, rise of a working class and trade unions, Factory Acts, and later socialist critiques of capitalism.
- American Revolution (1775-83): 'no taxation without representation'; Declaration of Independence 4 July 1776; US Constitution 1787 gave the world written constitutionalism, federalism and separation of powers.
- French Revolution 1789: storming of the Bastille (14 July 1789), Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen; ideals of liberty, equality, fraternity drew on Rousseau, Voltaire, Montesquieu; Napoleon spread nationalism across Europe.
- 19th-century nationalism: unification of Italy (Mazzini, Garibaldi, Cavour; kingdom 1861, Rome 1870) and Germany (Bismarck's 'blood and iron', empire proclaimed 1871).
- New Imperialism: Berlin Conference 1884-85 set rules for the Scramble for Africa; Opium Wars forced China open; Japan modernised after the Meiji Restoration (1868) and itself became imperial.
- World War I (1914-18): long-term causes - militarism, alliance system (Triple Alliance vs Triple Entente), imperial rivalry, nationalism; trigger - assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand at Sarajevo (28 June 1914).
- WWI outcomes: Treaty of Versailles (1919) and harsh terms on Germany; League of Nations (1920); collapse of four empires - German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian, Ottoman.
- Russian Revolution 1917 (February and October/Bolshevik under Lenin); USSR formed 1922; Stalin's five-year plans and forced collectivisation - first experiment in a centrally planned communist state.
- Inter-war crisis: Great Depression after the Wall Street crash (1929); Roosevelt's New Deal; rise of Fascism in Italy (Mussolini, 1922) and Nazism in Germany (Hitler, 1933).
- World War II (1939-45): began with invasion of Poland (1 Sept 1939); Pearl Harbor (Dec 1941) brought the USA in; atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki (Aug 1945); UN came into existence on 24 Oct 1945.
- Redrawal of boundaries: post-WWI new states (Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia) and mandates in West Asia (Sykes-Picot 1916); post-WWII division of Germany and Korea, Partition of India 1947, Israel 1948; after 1991 the USSR broke into 15 states, Yugoslavia fragmented, Czechoslovakia split (1993), Germany reunified (1990).
- Decolonisation: Asian wave (India 1947, Indonesia 1949), African wave (Ghana 1957; 1960 'Year of Africa'); UNGA Resolution 1514 (1960) on granting independence; Bandung Conference 1955 and NAM (Belgrade 1961).
- Cold War: Truman Doctrine and Marshall Plan (1947), NATO (1949) vs Warsaw Pact (1955), Berlin Wall (1961-89), Cuban Missile Crisis (1962); ended with dissolution of the USSR (Dec 1991).
- Capitalism: private ownership, market prices, profit motive; Adam Smith's 'Wealth of Nations' (1776) and laissez-faire; after 1929 modified by Keynesian state intervention and the welfare state.
- Socialism: social ownership and equality - utopian (Robert Owen, Saint-Simon), Fabian/gradualist (influenced Nehru), democratic socialism; Communism: Marx and Engels' 'Communist Manifesto' (1848) - class struggle, historical materialism, dictatorship of the proletariat, classless stateless society; Marxism-Leninism, Maoism (China 1949).
- Effects on society: welfare states and mixed economies (India), totalitarian regimes and purges, human-rights and self-determination discourse, ideological inspiration to anti-colonial movements.

## Punjab angle

- Punjab supplied the largest share of Indian Army recruits in WWI; Sikh and Punjabi soldiers served in France (Ypres), Gallipoli and Mesopotamia.
- Ghadar Party (1913, San Francisco) tried to exploit WWI for an armed uprising in Punjab (Feb 1915); Komagata Maru episode (1914) exposed imperial racial exclusion.
- Russian Revolution influenced Punjab's left-revolutionary stream: Kirti Kisan Party, Naujawan Bharat Sabha and Bhagat Singh's HSRA ('Socialist' added 1928).
- Partition (1947) and the Radcliffe Line redrew Punjab - one of the largest forced migrations in history; Captain Mohan Singh raised the first INA (1942) in WWII.

## How PPSC asks it

Questions ask for causes-consequences analysis (industrialisation, world wars, decolonisation) or comparison of ideologies and their social impact. Examiners reward chronology with dates, cause-effect linkages, and a closing link to India or Punjab.

## Deep dive

### Master timeline, 1750–1991 (year — event)

- 1760s–1840s — First Industrial Revolution in Britain; 1776 — American Declaration of Independence; 1789 — French Revolution; 1799–1815 — Napoleonic era ends at Waterloo.
- 1815 — Congress of Vienna redraws Europe on 'legitimacy' and balance of power; 1830 and 1848 — waves of liberal–national revolutions.
- 1848 — Communist Manifesto; 1861 — Italian kingdom (Rome added 1870); 1861–65 — American Civil War; 1868 — Meiji Restoration; 1871 — German Empire proclaimed at Versailles.
- 1884–85 — Berlin Conference; by 1914 only Ethiopia (victory at Adwa, 1896) and Liberia remained independent in Africa.
- 1914–18 — First World War; 1917 — Russian Revolutions; 1919 — Paris peace treaties; 1920 — League of Nations; 1922 — USSR formed, Mussolini in power.
- 1929 — Wall Street crash and Great Depression; 1933 — Hitler Chancellor; 1936 — Keynes's 'General Theory'; 1939–45 — Second World War.
- 1944 — Bretton Woods (IMF, World Bank); 1945 — UN Charter in force (24 October); 1947 — Truman Doctrine and Marshall Plan; 1949 — NATO, People's Republic of China.
- 1955 — Bandung Conference; 1957 — Ghana independent; 1960 — 'Year of Africa' (17 new states) and UN Declaration on decolonisation (Resolution 1514); 1961 — NAM founded at Belgrade.
- 1962 — Cuban Missile Crisis; 1973 — oil shock; 1979 — Soviet entry into Afghanistan; 9 November 1989 — Berlin Wall falls; 3 October 1990 — German reunification; 26 December 1991 — USSR dissolved.

### Colonisation and decolonisation — phases and examples

- Mercantile colonialism (16th–18th c.): trading companies, plantations, slave trade (about 12 million Africans shipped across the Atlantic).
- Industrial/'New' imperialism (1870–1914): search for raw materials, markets and investment (J.A. Hobson 1902; Lenin's 'Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism' 1916); 'civilising mission' and Social Darwinism as justification.
- China: Opium Wars (1839–42, 1856–60), unequal treaties, spheres of influence, Open Door policy (1899), Boxer Rebellion (1900), 1911 Revolution.
- Asian decolonisation: Philippines (1946), India and Pakistan (1947), Burma and Ceylon (1948), Indonesia (1949, after war with the Dutch), Indochina after Dien Bien Phu (1954) and the Geneva Accords, Malaya (1957).
- African decolonisation: Ghana (1957, Nkrumah) first in sub-Saharan Africa; 1960 'Year of Africa'; Algeria (1962, after the 1954–62 war); Kenya (1963, after Mau Mau); Portuguese colonies (1974–75, after the Carnation Revolution); Zimbabwe (1980); Namibia (1990); end of apartheid (1994).
- Factors: weakening of Europe after WWII, nationalist movements, UN and superpower pressure, India's example (1947) and Bandung (1955) solidarity.
- Legacies: artificial borders (Africa's ruler-straight lines, Sykes–Picot in West Asia, Radcliffe Line), mono-crop economies, neo-colonialism (Nkrumah, 1965), ethnic conflicts.

### Redrawal of national boundaries (treaty/event — result)

- Vienna (1815) — restored monarchies, German Confederation; Italian and German unification (1861/1871) created two new great powers.
- Berlin Congress (1878) — independence of Serbia, Montenegro, Romania; Bulgaria autonomous — sowed Balkan conflicts (Balkan Wars 1912–13).
- After WWI: Versailles (Germany — Alsace-Lorraine to France, Polish corridor, colonies as mandates), St Germain (Austria), Trianon (Hungary), Neuilly (Bulgaria), Sèvres (1920) replaced by Lausanne (1923) for Turkey.
- New states 1918–20: Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, Finland, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania; Ottoman Arab lands became British and French mandates (Sykes–Picot 1916); Balfour Declaration (1917) on Palestine.
- After WWII: Yalta and Potsdam (1945) — Poland moved west (Oder–Neisse line), Germany divided (FRG and GDR, 1949), Korea split at the 38th parallel; partition of India (1947) and Palestine (1947–48).
- End of the Cold War: German reunification (1990); USSR dissolved into 15 republics (1991); Yugoslavia broke up (1991–92 onwards); Czechoslovakia split peacefully (1993).
- Use in answers: boundaries drawn by outside powers without regard to people (Africa, West Asia, Punjab–Bengal) left lasting disputes.

### Political philosophies — forms and effects on society

- Liberalism — Locke, Montesquieu, J.S. Mill ('On Liberty', 1859) — rights, rule of law, limited government; shaped constitutions and civil liberties.
- Capitalism — Adam Smith (1776) — private property, markets; forms: laissez-faire (19th c.), welfare/Keynesian capitalism (post-1945), neoliberalism (Hayek, Friedman; Thatcher and Reagan, 1980s; Washington Consensus).
- Socialism — utopian (Owen, Saint-Simon, Fourier), Fabian (1884; Webbs, Shaw — gradual reform), democratic socialism (British Labour's welfare state, NHS 1948; Scandinavian model).
- Communism — Marx and Engels (class struggle, historical materialism, dictatorship of the proletariat); Lenin (vanguard party, 1917); Stalin (five-year plans, collectivisation); Mao (peasant revolution, 1949; Great Leap Forward 1958; Cultural Revolution 1966).
- Fascism and Nazism — Mussolini's corporate state (1922), Hitler's racial nationalism (1933), cult of leader, suppression of dissent; led to WWII and the Holocaust (about 6 million Jews killed).
- Effects on society: welfare rights, universal suffrage, labour laws and public education on one side; totalitarian repression, famines (Ukraine 1932–33, China 1959–61) on the other.
- India's synthesis: Nehru's democratic socialism and mixed economy (Industrial Policy 1956), 'socialist' added to the Preamble (42nd Amendment, 1976), shift to liberalisation in 1991.

### World Wars — deeper notes (with India and Punjab)

- WWI course: Schlieffen Plan, trench stalemate (Marne 1914, Verdun and Somme 1916), Gallipoli (1915), US entry (April 1917, after unrestricted U-boat war and the Zimmermann telegram), Russia exits (Brest-Litovsk, March 1918), armistice 11 November 1918.
- Indian role in WWI: over a million Indians served overseas; about 74,000 died; Punjab supplied the largest share of recruits; Indian troops fought at Ypres, Neuve Chapelle, Gallipoli and in Mesopotamia (Kut, 1916).
- Inter-war: Wilson's Fourteen Points, League of Nations without the USA, reparations, hyperinflation in Germany (1923), Depression, appeasement and the Munich Agreement (1938).
- WWII course: Nazi–Soviet Pact (August 1939), fall of France (1940), Battle of Britain, Operation Barbarossa (June 1941), Pearl Harbor (7 December 1941), Stalingrad (1942–43), D-Day (6 June 1944), VE Day (8 May 1945), atomic bombs (6 and 9 August 1945), Japan surrenders (2 September 1945).
- Indian role in WWII: about 2.5 million volunteer army — largest in history; Kohima–Imphal (1944) turned the Burma campaign; INA fought on the Japanese side; Bengal famine (1943).
- Outcomes: UN system, Bretton Woods institutions, Nuremberg and Tokyo trials, Cold War bipolarity, accelerated decolonisation, the European Economic Community (Treaty of Rome, 1957).

### Cold War and after — key sequence

- 1946 — Churchill's 'Iron Curtain' speech; 1947 — Truman Doctrine (containment), Marshall Plan; 1948–49 — Berlin blockade and airlift.
- 1949 — NATO; 1955 — Warsaw Pact; 1950–53 — Korean War (India chaired the Neutral Nations Repatriation Commission).
- 1956 — Suez Crisis and Soviet intervention in Hungary; 1961 — Berlin Wall built; 1962 — Cuban Missile Crisis.
- 1964–75 — Vietnam War; 1968 — Prague Spring crushed; 1972 — Nixon in China, SALT I; 1975 — Helsinki Accords.
- 1979 — Soviet intervention in Afghanistan; 1985 — Gorbachev's glasnost and perestroika; 1989 — revolutions in Eastern Europe.
- 1991 — USSR dissolves; unipolar moment and globalisation; India's 1991 reforms followed the loss of its Soviet trade partner and a balance-of-payments crisis.
- India's position: non-alignment (Nehru, Nasser, Tito, Sukarno, Nkrumah), Panchsheel (1954), Indo-Soviet Treaty (1971).

### Answer-writing frames for GS I (PPSC Mains)

- For 'effects on society' questions, use a matrix: political, economic, social, cultural, global — each with one example and a date.
- For 'redrawal of boundaries', connect a treaty to a lasting conflict: Versailles → WWII; Sykes–Picot → West Asia; Radcliffe → Punjab and Kashmir.
- For decolonisation, compare two models: negotiated (India, Ghana) vs violent (Algeria, Kenya, Vietnam).
- For ideologies, define, show the forms, judge outcomes, and end with India's adaptation (mixed economy, welfare state, 1991).
- Punjab examples add value: WWI recruitment and the Ghadar link, Komagata Maru as imperial racism, partition migration (about 10 million people moved across the Punjab border), Soviet influence on Kirti Kisan and Naujawan Bharat Sabha.
- Quote thinkers in your own words with year (Marx 1848, Mill 1859, Keynes 1936, Fukuyama 'End of History' 1989/1992) — examiners reward precise references.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** 'The Industrial Revolution was as much a social revolution as a technological one.' Discuss with reference to its impact on European society and on colonies such as India. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: define the Industrial Revolution; Britain c. 1760s as origin and why (coal, capital, colonies, agricultural change)
- Technological dimension: textile machinery, steam power, railways, factory system
- Social dimension: urbanisation, working class, child labour, public health crisis, rise of trade unions and Factory Acts
- Ideological fallout: liberalism, socialism and Marxism as responses
- Colonial dimension: deindustrialisation of Indian handicrafts, India as raw-material supplier and market, drain of wealth
- Conclusion: technology triggered a reordering of class, family and global power

</details>

**2.** How did the two World Wars redraw national boundaries and accelerate the process of decolonisation? (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: wars as engines of territorial and political change
- WWI: collapse of four empires, new nation-states in Europe, mandates in West Asia, Wilson's self-determination
- WWII: division of Germany and Korea, weakening of Britain and France, UN Charter's self-determination principle
- Decolonisation waves: Asia (India 1947, Indonesia 1949), Africa (Ghana 1957, 1960), UNGA Res 1514
- Role of Cold War rivalry, nationalist movements, and NAM
- Legacies: arbitrary borders, partition violence, continuing disputes (Kashmir, Palestine, Korea)
- Conclusion: wars broke the imperial order; nationalism completed the work

</details>

**3.** Compare Capitalism, Socialism and Communism as political philosophies. Assess their effect on society and explain why India chose a mixed economy after 1947. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: three responses to the question of ownership and distribution after the Industrial Revolution
- Capitalism: private property, markets, efficiency and innovation; critique - inequality, cycles (1929)
- Socialism: social ownership, welfare, equality; variants - Fabian, democratic
- Communism: Marxist theory, Soviet and Chinese practice; gains in literacy and industry vs loss of liberty and collapse of USSR
- India's choice: Nehruvian mixed economy, Industrial Policy Resolution 1956, planning, DPSPs; 1991 shift towards markets
- Conclusion: pragmatic convergence - market economy with welfare state

</details>
