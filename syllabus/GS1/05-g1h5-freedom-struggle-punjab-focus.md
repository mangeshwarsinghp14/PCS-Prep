## Short notes

- Stages of the freedom struggle: Moderate (1885-1905), Extremist/Swadeshi (1905-1918), Gandhian mass movements (1919-1947), with a parallel revolutionary stream and constitutional developments.
- Moderates (Naoroji, Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta) used petitions and economic critique; Extremists (Lal-Bal-Pal - Lajpat Rai, Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal; Aurobindo) pushed swaraj, swadeshi and boycott.
- Punjab agrarian unrest 1907: protests against the Punjab Colonization of Land Bill and higher canal water rates ('Pagri Sambhal Jatta'); Lala Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh deported to Mandalay; the Bill was vetoed by Viceroy Minto.
- Ghadar Party (1913, San Francisco): Sohan Singh Bhakna (president), Lala Har Dayal; weekly 'Ghadar'; planned uprising of February 1915 failed; Lahore Conspiracy Case - Kartar Singh Sarabha hanged in 1915 aged about 19.
- Komagata Maru (1914): ship chartered by Gurdit Singh was denied entry at Vancouver; police firing at Budge Budge on return (Sept 1914).
- Jallianwala Bagh (13 April 1919): firing ordered by Brig.-Gen. Reginald Dyer on a peaceful Baisakhi gathering protesting the Rowlatt Act and arrests of Dr Satyapal and Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew; martial law in Punjab; Hunter Committee; Tagore renounced his knighthood.
- Udham Singh shot Michael O'Dwyer (Punjab Lt-Governor in 1919) in London in March 1940; executed July 1940.
- Akali movement (1920-25) ran parallel to Non-Cooperation; Babbar Akali (1921-24) was its militant offshoot in Doaba.
- Revolutionary stream in Punjab: Naujawan Bharat Sabha (1926, Lahore), Kirti Kisan Party (late 1920s), HSRA (1928, Delhi) under Chandrashekhar Azad and Bhagat Singh.
- Lala Lajpat Rai was injured in the lathi charge at Lahore (30 Oct 1928) against the Simon Commission and died in Nov 1928; Saunders was shot in Dec 1928 in retaliation.
- Bhagat Singh and B.K. Dutt threw bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly (8 April 1929) to protest the Public Safety and Trade Disputes Bills; Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru were hanged at Lahore on 23 March 1931.
- Lahore Congress (Dec 1929, president Jawaharlal Nehru) adopted Purna Swaraj; flag hoisted on the Ravi bank; 26 January 1930 celebrated as Independence Day.
- Unionist Party (Fazl-i-Husain, Chhotu Ram, Sikandar Hayat Khan) dominated Punjab under the 1935 Act; pro-peasant debt-relief and land laws of Chhotu Ram.
- INA: Captain Mohan Singh raised the first INA (1942); Red Fort trials (1945) of Shah Nawaz Khan, P.K. Sahgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon created nationwide upsurge.
- Contributions from other regions: Bengal (Swadeshi, Anushilan, Bose), Maharashtra (Tilak, Chapekars), Gujarat (Gandhi, Patel - Bardoli), South (V.O. Chidambaram Pillai, Subramania Bharati, Vaikom Satyagraha), North-East (Rani Gaidinliu), women (Sarojini Naidu, Aruna Asaf Ali, Usha Mehta).
- Women from Punjab: Bibi Gulab Kaur (Ghadar), Durgawati Devi ('Durga Bhabhi', HSRA), Rajkumari Amrit Kaur.
- Independence came with Partition: Punjab divided by the Radcliffe Line; millions displaced and lakhs killed in 1947.

## Punjab angle

- Memorials usable in answers: Jallianwala Bagh National Memorial (Amritsar), Hussainiwala National Martyrs Memorial (Ferozepur) for Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev; Khatkar Kalan (SBS Nagar), Bhagat Singh's ancestral village.
- Desh Bhagat Yadgar Hall, Jalandhar - Ghadar memorial; the annual Mela Gadri Babeyan Da keeps Ghadar memory alive.
- Sarabha village (Ludhiana) - Kartar Singh Sarabha; Sunam (Sangrur) - Udham Singh; Partition Museum, Amritsar (2017).
- Punjab's share among executed and transported revolutionaries (Ghadar, Babbar Akali, Kirti) was disproportionately large relative to population.
- 23 March (Shaheedi Diwas) is observed in Punjab; Amritsar was the venue of the 1919 Congress session after Jallianwala.

## How PPSC asks it

Expect questions on Punjab's specific role (Ghadar, Jallianwala, Akali, Bhagat Singh) and on the stages of the movement with regional contributions. Reward goes to precise events, ideology of revolutionaries and linkage of Punjab to the national mainstream.

## Deep dive

### Punjab timeline 1849–1947 (year — event)

- 1849 — Annexation; Board of Administration under Henry and John Lawrence disarms the Khalsa army.
- 1857 — Punjab largely held for the British; revolt of Ahmad Khan Kharal (Gogera); sepoys of the 26th Native Infantry killed at Ajnala and thrown into the 'Kalianwala khuh' (remains exhumed in 2014).
- 1872 — Kuka rising and Malerkotla executions; 1881 — The Tribune (Lahore); 1894 — Punjab National Bank, backed by Lajpat Rai and Dyal Singh Majithia.
- 1907 — Agitation against the Punjab Colonization Bill and higher water rates in the canal colonies; Lajpat Rai and Ajit Singh deported to Mandalay (May); Minto vetoes the Bill.
- 1913–15 — Ghadar Party; Komagata Maru (1914); failed Ghadar rising (Feb 1915); Singapore mutiny of the 5th Light Infantry (Feb 1915); Lahore Conspiracy Cases.
- 1919 — Rowlatt satyagraha, Jallianwala Bagh (13 Apr), martial law; Amritsar Congress session (Dec, president Motilal Nehru).
- 1920–25 — Akali (Gurdwara Reform) movement; 1921–24 — Babbar Akalis; 1923–25 — Jaito morcha.
- 1926 — Naujawan Bharat Sabha (Lahore) and the Kirti paper (Amritsar); 1928 — Kirti Kisan Party, HSRA, Simon Commission protest and Lajpat Rai's death.
- 1929 — Assembly bomb (8 Apr); Jatin Das dies after a 63-day hunger strike in Lahore jail (13 Sept); Lahore Congress adopts Purna Swaraj (Dec).
- 1931 — Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru hanged (23 Mar); 1937 — Unionist ministry under Sikandar Hayat Khan.
- 1940 — Lahore Resolution of the Muslim League (23 Mar, Minto Park); Udham Singh shoots O'Dwyer (13 Mar).
- 1946–47 — Unionist–Congress–Akali coalition under Khizr Hayat Tiwana; his resignation (2 Mar 1947); Rawalpindi riots (Mar 1947); Radcliffe Award published 17 Aug 1947.

### Personalities — name, role, why it matters

- Lala Lajpat Rai — Arya Samaj, PNB, Lakshmi Insurance, Servants of the People Society (1921), Young India (written in the USA); Simon Commission lathi charge, Lahore, 30 Oct 1928.
- Sardar Ajit Singh — Bharat Mata Society and the 1907 peasant agitation; long exile; returned in 1947 and died at Dalhousie on 15 Aug 1947.
- Lala Har Dayal, Sohan Singh Bhakna, Kartar Singh Sarabha — Ghadar ideologue, president and young organiser; Sarabha and six others hanged in Lahore (Nov 1915).
- Rash Behari Bose — coordinated the planned Ghadar rising of February 1915 from Lahore; betrayal by an informer (Kirpal Singh) wrecked it.
- Dr Satyapal and Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew — Rowlatt satyagraha leaders whose arrest (10 Apr 1919) led to the Amritsar disturbances.
- Baba Kharak Singh — first SGPC president, led the Keys morcha; Master Tara Singh — Akali leader across the 1930s–60s.
- Kishan Singh Gargaj — Babbar Akali leader, hanged 1926; the Babbars targeted informers (jholichuks) in Doaba.
- Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru, Bhagwati Charan Vohra, Durga Bhabhi — HSRA in Punjab; Bhagat Singh's essay 'Why I am an Atheist' (written in jail, 1930).
- Udham Singh (Sunam) — avenged Jallianwala in London (1940); hanged at Pentonville (31 July 1940); remains brought back to India in 1974.
- Seva Singh Thikriwala — Praja Mandal leader against princely rule; died in Patiala jail (1935).
- Sir Chhotu Ram — Unionist minister; peasant laws of 1934–39 (debt relief, mortgaged-land restitution, regulated markets).
- Mohan Singh, Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon, Shah Nawaz Khan — INA leaders from undivided Punjab; the Red Fort trials (1945) united Indian opinion.

### Jallianwala Bagh and martial law — the detailed account

- Rowlatt Act passed in March 1919; hartals on 30 Mar and 6 Apr; Ram Navami (9 Apr) saw Hindu–Muslim unity in Amritsar.
- 10 Apr: Satyapal and Kitchlew deported to Dharamsala; firing near the Hall Bridge; banks attacked and Europeans killed; Marcella Sherwood assaulted.
- 13 Apr (Baisakhi): Brig.-Gen. R.E.H. Dyer fired about 1,650 rounds for roughly ten minutes into a closed garden; the Hunter Committee accepted 379 dead, the Congress inquiry put the toll near 1,000.
- Martial law (from 15 Apr) in Lahore and Amritsar, extended to Gujranwala, Gujrat and Lyallpur; the 'crawling order' in Amritsar; public floggings; aerial bombing of Gujranwala (14 Apr).
- Hunter Committee (Disorders Inquiry Committee, Lord William Hunter) had three Indian members — Chimanlal Setalvad, Jagat Narayan, Sultan Ahmad Khan — who wrote a minority report.
- Congress inquiry: Gandhi, Motilal Nehru, C.R. Das, Abbas Tyabji and M.R. Jayakar; their report (1920) is a key primary source.
- Reactions: Tagore renounced his knighthood (letter of 30 May 1919); Sankaran Nair resigned from the Viceroy's Executive Council; Gandhi returned the Kaiser-i-Hind medal (1920); the House of Lords and a public fund backed Dyer.
- Memory: Jallianwala Bagh National Memorial Act 1951; memorial inaugurated in 1961 by President Rajendra Prasad (designer Benjamin Polk); the 2019 amendment removed the Congress president as a permanent trustee; renovated complex reopened in Aug 2021.

### Revolutionary and left organisations — ideology and methods

- Ghadar: secular, republican and anti-colonial; weekly 'Ghadar' from Yugantar Ashram, San Francisco (first issue 1 Nov 1913) in Urdu and Gurmukhi; mobilised returning emigrants and soldiers.
- Babbar Akalis (1921–24): broke with Akali non-violence; armed action in Doaba; their paper was 'Babbar Akali Doaba'; many hanged or transported.
- Kirti group: Santokh Singh and Bhag Singh Canadian started 'Kirti' (1926) — Marxist and Ghadar-inspired; Kirti Kisan Party (1928) organised peasants and workers.
- Naujawan Bharat Sabha (Lahore, March 1926): Bhagat Singh, Bhagwati Charan Vohra, Sukhdev; open youth front, secular and socialist, used lantern slide shows on martyrs.
- HSRA (Delhi, Sept 1928): 'Socialist' added to HRA; Saunders killing, Assembly bomb, Lahore Conspiracy Case; slogan Inquilab Zindabad popularised.
- Peasant struggles: 1907 'Pagri Sambhal Jatta'; Kirti Kisan agitations; Harsha Chhina morcha against canal rates (Majha, 1946); after 1947, the PEPSU muzara (tenant) movement led by Teja Singh Sutantar.
- Princely states: Punjab Riyasti Praja Mandal (1928) fought autocracy in Patiala, Nabha, Faridkot; the Jaito morcha (1923–25) began over the forced abdication of Maharaja Ripudaman Singh of Nabha.

### Constitutional politics and Partition of Punjab

- Unionist Party (1923): Fazl-i-Husain and Chhotu Ram's cross-communal rural coalition; won the 1937 elections; Sikandar Hayat Khan Premier (1937–42).
- Chhotu Ram's laws: Punjab Relief of Indebtedness Act (1934), Restitution of Mortgaged Lands Act (1938), Registration of Moneylenders Act (1938), Agricultural Produce Markets Act (1939).
- Quit India (1942) was weaker in Punjab because the Unionists backed the war effort; Akalis joined the war-time ministry through the Sikandar–Baldev Singh pact (1942).
- 1946 elections: Muslim League became the largest party; Khizr Hayat Tiwana formed a Unionist–Congress–Akali coalition; League civil disobedience forced his resignation (2 Mar 1947).
- Master Tara Singh's defiance at the Lahore Assembly (3 Mar 1947) and the Rawalpindi massacres (Mar 1947, including Thoha Khalsa) marked the slide into violence.
- Punjab Boundary Commission: chair Cyril Radcliffe; members Mehr Chand Mahajan, Teja Singh, Din Muhammad, Muhammad Munir; Award published 17 Aug 1947; three tehsils of Gurdaspur went to India.
- Aftermath: roughly ten million people uprooted across Punjab and estimates of deaths ranging from about 2 lakh to 20 lakh; Shimla served as interim capital; Chandigarh inaugurated on 7 Oct 1953; 14 August is observed as Partition Horrors Remembrance Day (from 2021).

### Answer-writing angles and traps

- Punjab's role across all three streams: constitutional (Unionists, Congress), mass (Akali, Rowlatt), revolutionary (Ghadar, Babbar, HSRA) — structure answers this way.
- Ghadar was the first organised secular-revolutionary movement with a mass emigrant base; link it to later HSRA ideology.
- Jallianwala made Gandhi turn from cooperation to non-cooperation — the key national consequence.
- Lahore features twice: Purna Swaraj (Dec 1929, Congress) and the Lahore Resolution (Mar 1940, Muslim League).
- Saunders (killed, 1928) ≠ Scott (intended target); O'Dwyer (shot, 1940) ≠ Dyer (died 1927).
- Komagata Maru survivors were fired upon at Budge Budge (Calcutta, 29 Sept 1914), not in Punjab.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** Trace the evolution of revolutionary nationalism in Punjab from the Ghadar movement to the HSRA. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: Punjab's soldier-peasant diaspora and agrarian grievances
- Ghadar: diaspora in North America, secular-egalitarian ideology, 1915 failure, repression
- Babbar Akalis: militant response in Doaba
- Kirti Kisan Party and Naujawan Bharat Sabha: Marxist influence, youth mobilisation
- HSRA: socialism, Assembly bomb, trial as propaganda, martyrdom of Bhagat Singh

**2.** 'Jallianwala Bagh was a turning point in India's freedom struggle.' Discuss. (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: Rowlatt Act, satyagraha, arrests of Kitchlew and Satyapal
- Massacre of 13 April 1919 and martial law
- Reactions: Tagore's renunciation, Hunter Committee, Congress inquiry
- Impact: end of faith in British justice, path to NCM, Khilafat alliance
- Long memory: Udham Singh, memorial
- Conclusion: converted loyalty to defiance

</details>

**3.** Identify the various stages of the Indian freedom struggle and highlight the contributions of different regions of the country, with special reference to Punjab. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: freedom struggle as a multi-stream, pan-Indian process
- Moderate phase: Maharashtra and Bengal leadership
- Extremist phase: Lal-Bal-Pal; Punjab's 1907 agitation
- Gandhian phase: Gujarat, Bihar, UP; Punjab's Akali movement and Jallianwala
- Revolutionary stream: Bengal, Punjab (Ghadar, HSRA), UP
- South and North-East contributions; INA and diaspora
- Conclusion: regional diversity strengthened national unity

</details>
