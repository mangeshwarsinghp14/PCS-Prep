# Indian culture — art, literature, architecture

> Official syllabus line: Salient aspects of art forms, literature and architecture from ancient to modern times.

## Short notes

- Harappan art: planned cities with grid streets and drains, Great Bath (Mohenjo-daro), dockyard at Lothal, bronze 'Dancing Girl' (lost-wax casting), steatite seals such as the 'Pashupati' seal.
- Mauryan art: polished Chunar sandstone Ashokan pillars (Sarnath Lion Capital is the national emblem), Barabar rock-cut caves for Ajivikas, early stupas such as Sanchi.
- Post-Mauryan sculpture schools: Gandhara (Greco-Roman influence, grey schist, wavy-haired Buddha), Mathura (indigenous, red sandstone, Buddhist, Jain and Brahmanical images), Amaravati (white marble, narrative reliefs).
- Gupta 'classical age': Dashavatara temple at Deogarh, Udayagiri caves (Varaha panel), Ajanta murals (largely Vakataka-Gupta period), Kalidasa's 'Abhijnanashakuntalam' and 'Meghaduta'.
- Temple styles: Nagara (north, curvilinear shikhara - Khajuraho, Konark), Dravida (vimana and gopuram - Brihadeeswara, Thanjavur, built by Rajaraja Chola I), Vesara (hybrid - Chalukya and Hoysala temples at Belur, Halebidu).
- Rock-cut marvels: Kailasa temple at Ellora (Rashtrakuta Krishna I, carved top-down from one rock), Elephanta caves, Pallava rathas and Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram.
- Indo-Islamic architecture: true arch and dome, lime mortar, calligraphy, arabesque and geometric ornament; Qutb Minar, Alai Darwaza, sloping Tughlaq walls, Gol Gumbaz (Bijapur).
- Mughal architecture: Humayun's Tomb (char-bagh garden tomb), Fatehpur Sikri with Buland Darwaza, Taj Mahal (white marble, pietra dura inlay), Red Fort.
- Painting traditions: Mughal miniatures (Hamzanama under Akbar; naturalism under Jahangir - Ustad Mansur), Rajasthani (Mewar, Kishangarh 'Bani Thani'), Pahari (Basohli, Guler, Kangra), Deccani.
- Folk and tribal art: Madhubani (Bihar), Warli (Maharashtra), Pattachitra (Odisha), Kalamkari (Andhra), Phad (Rajasthan), Gond (Madhya Pradesh) - many now GI-tagged.
- Performing arts: Sangeet Natak Akademi recognises 8 classical dances - Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Kuchipudi, Odissi, Manipuri, Mohiniyattam, Sattriya; Bharata's Natyashastra is the foundational text.
- Music: Hindustani (Amir Khusrau, Tansen, gharanas) and Carnatic (Purandara Dasa; trinity of Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, Syama Sastri).
- Literature: Vedic and epic Sanskrit corpus; Tamil Sangam texts (Tolkappiyam, Silappadikaram); Bhakti literature in regional languages (Tulsidas' Ramcharitmanas in Awadhi, Surdas, Mirabai); Persian works such as Abul Fazl's Ain-i-Akbari; Babur's memoir in Turki.
- Modern literature: Bankim Chandra's 'Anandamath' (Vande Mataram), Tagore's Nobel Prize (1913, Gitanjali), Premchand's realism, Subramania Bharati's nationalist poetry.
- Modern art: Raja Ravi Varma's oil paintings; Bengal School (Abanindranath Tagore's 'Bharat Mata'); Amrita Sher-Gil; Progressive Artists' Group (1947 - Souza, Husain, Raza).
- Colonial and modern architecture: Indo-Saracenic and Gothic revival buildings (Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus), Lutyens' New Delhi, Le Corbusier's Chandigarh Capitol Complex (part of a transnational UNESCO World Heritage listing, 2016).
- UNESCO World Heritage: India's list includes cultural, natural and mixed sites; Maratha Military Landscapes were inscribed in 2025.

## Punjab angle

- Ancient Punjab: Ropar (Rupnagar) is an excavated Harappan site; Sanghol (Fatehgarh Sahib) yielded a Kushan-era Buddhist stupa and Mathura-school sculptures.
- Guru Granth Sahib, compiled by Guru Arjan Dev (1604), unites bani of Sikh Gurus with Bhakti and Sufi saints (Kabir, Namdev, Ravidas, Sheikh Farid) - a landmark of composite literature in Gurmukhi script.
- Punjabi literature: Sufi poets Baba Farid, Shah Hussain, Bulleh Shah; Waris Shah's 'Heer' (18th c.); modern writers Bhai Vir Singh, Puran Singh, Amrita Pritam, Shiv Kumar Batalvi (youngest Sahitya Akademi awardee, 1967).
- Sikh architecture blends Mughal and Rajput elements: Harmandir Sahib (gilded under Maharaja Ranjit Singh), Qila Mubarak (Patiala), Gobindgarh Fort (Amritsar).
- Living traditions: Phulkari embroidery (GI-tagged), Bhangra and Giddha folk dances, Sikh court painting influenced by Pahari artists after Ranjit Singh's patronage.

## How PPSC asks it

PPSC asks analytical questions on synthesis (Indo-Islamic architecture, Bhakti-Sufi literature), comparisons (temple styles, sculpture schools) and Punjab's cultural heritage. Use named monuments, patrons and features; add a Punjab example.

## Deep dive

### Temple architecture — styles, sub-styles and dynasties

- Gupta beginnings (5th c.): flat-roofed shrines (Sanchi Temple 17, Tigawa) → early shikhara at Deogarh (Dashavatara) and brick Bhitargaon.
- Nagara sub-types: latina/rekha-prasada (single curved tower), phamsana (stepped pyramidal roof over mandapas), valabhi (barrel-vaulted, e.g., Teli ka Mandir, Gwalior).
- Odisha style: rekha deul (sanctum tower), pidha deul (jagamohana hall), khakhara deul (barrel roof) — Lingaraja (11th c.), Jagannath Puri (Anantavarman Chodaganga, 12th c.), Konark Sun Temple (Narasimhadeva I, 13th c., chariot with 24 wheels).
- Central India: Khajuraho group of the Chandelas (c. 950–1050; Kandariya Mahadeva, Lakshmana) — panchayatana plans, sculpted exteriors; UNESCO 1986.
- Western India (Solanki/Maru-Gurjara): Modhera Sun Temple (1026, Bhima I), Rani-ki-Vav stepwell at Patan (UNESCO 2014), Dilwara Jain temples (Vimala Shah 1031; Tejapala 1230).
- Dravida: Pallava rock-cut mandapas (Mahendravarman I), monolithic rathas (Narasimhavarman 'Mamalla'), structural Shore Temple and Kailasanatha, Kanchi (Rajasimha); Chola — Brihadeeswara, Thanjavur (Rajaraja I, 1010), Gangaikondacholapuram, Airavatesvara (Darasuram) — 'Great Living Chola Temples' (UNESCO).
- Later Dravida: towering gopurams of the Pandyas, Vijayanagara (Vitthala temple, stone chariot; Hampi, UNESCO 1986) and Nayakas (Madurai Meenakshi, thousand-pillared halls).
- Vesara/Deccan: Badami Chalukyas at Aihole ('cradle of temple architecture'; Ladh Khan, Durga temple), Badami caves, Pattadakal (Virupaksha, built by queen Lokamahadevi; UNESCO 1987); Rashtrakuta Kailasa at Ellora (Krishna I); Hoysala star-plan temples at Belur, Halebidu, Somanathapura (UNESCO 2023).

### Rock-cut, Indo-Islamic, colonial and modern architecture

- Ajanta: about 30 Buddhist caves in two phases (c. 2nd c. BCE and c. 5th c. CE under the Vakataka Harishena); Ellora: 34 caves — 1–12 Buddhist, 13–29 Hindu, 30–34 Jain; Elephanta: Maheshamurti (three-headed Shiva).
- Provincial Sultanate styles: Jaunpur (Sharqi, Atala mosque, massive propylons), Bengal (Adina mosque at Pandua; brick, curved 'do-chala' roofs), Gujarat (Ahmedabad Jama Masjid, Sidi Saiyyed jali), Malwa (Mandu — Jahaz Mahal, Hindola Mahal).
- Deccan: Gol Gumbaz (Muhammad Adil Shah, Bijapur; one of the largest domes, whispering gallery), Charminar (Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, 1591), Mahmud Gawan's madrasa at Bidar.
- Mughal features: char-bagh gardens, double domes, pietra dura, red sandstone to white marble shift (Akbar → Shah Jahan), pishtaq gateways, chhatris borrowed from Rajput styles.
- Sikh and Rajput palace styles combined Mughal arches with bangaldar roofs and frescoes — Harmandir Sahib's gilded upper storeys (Ranjit Singh's patronage), Qila Mubarak (Patiala), Gobindgarh Fort (Amritsar).
- Colonial: Indo-Gothic (Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus, F.W. Stevens, 1888; UNESCO 2004), Indo-Saracenic (Madras High Court, Mysore Palace, Victoria Memorial 1921), New Delhi of Lutyens and Baker (inaugurated 1931).
- Modern: Chandigarh by Le Corbusier with Pierre Jeanneret, Maxwell Fry and Jane Drew; the Capitol Complex is part of the UNESCO transnational 'Architectural Work of Le Corbusier' (2016); Nek Chand's Rock Garden; B.V. Doshi (Pritzker 2018).

### Painting traditions — from murals to modern art

- Murals: Ajanta (Padmapani Bodhisattva), Bagh (MP), Sittanavasal (Jain, Pandya era), Chola murals in Brihadeeswara, Lepakshi (Vijayanagara), Kerala temple murals (Mattancherry).
- Manuscripts: Pala palm-leaf Buddhist texts (Prajnaparamita), Western Indian/Jain Kalpasutra paintings (angular faces, protruding eye).
- Mughal: Humayun brought Mir Sayyid Ali and Abdus Samad; Hamzanama (about 1400 folios) under Akbar; Jahangir's naturalism — Ustad Mansur (birds, flowers), Abul Hasan, Bishandas; decline under Aurangzeb.
- Rajasthani: Mewar (Sahibdin), Bundi and Kota (hunting scenes), Kishangarh (Nihal Chand's 'Bani Thani', the 'Indian Mona Lisa'), Marwar, Bikaner.
- Pahari (Punjab Hills): Basohli (bold colours, beetle-wing insets; Rasamanjari), Guler (Pandit Seu and sons Manaku and Nainsukh), Kangra (lyrical; Gita Govinda, Raja Sansar Chand), Chamba, Mandi — Pahari is part of the cultural history of the old Punjab hill states.
- Company and folk: Patna and Murshidabad Company painting, Kalighat pats (Kolkata), Madhubani, Warli, Pattachitra, Kalamkari, Phad, Gond, Pithora, Tanjore (gold leaf).
- Modern: Raja Ravi Varma (oil, oleographs), Bengal School (Abanindranath Tagore's 'Bharat Mata' 1905, E.B. Havell, Nandalal Bose — Haripura posters and Constitution illustrations), Amrita Sher-Gil (1913–41), Progressive Artists' Group (1947: Souza, Raza, Husain, Ara, Bakre, Gade).
- Sikh/Punjab art: Lahore court painting under Ranjit Singh, Kapur Singh; naqqashi frescoes of Harmandir Sahib (Bhai Gian Singh Naqqash); Sobha Singh (Andretta) and the Punjab art of the 20th century.

### Literature — period-wise file (work — author — note)

- Sangam (c. 3rd c. BCE–3rd c. CE): Tolkappiyam (grammar), Ettuthogai and Pattuppattu anthologies; post-Sangam twin epics Silappadikaram (Ilango Adigal) and Manimekalai (Sattanar); Tirukkural (Tiruvalluvar).
- Classical Sanskrit: Bhasa (Svapnavasavadatta), Kalidasa, Bharavi (Kiratarjuniya), Magha (Shishupalavadha), Banabhatta (Harshacharita, Kadambari), Bhavabhuti (Uttararamacharita), Dandin (Dashakumaracharita), Kalhana (Rajatarangini, 1148 — first historical chronicle), Jayadeva (Gita Govinda), Somadeva (Kathasaritsagara).
- Regional classics: Telugu — Nannaya's Andhra Mahabharatam, Krishnadevaraya's Amuktamalyada and his Ashtadiggajas; Kannada — Pampa, Ponna, Ranna; Malayalam — Ezhuthachan; Bengali — Krittibas Ramayana, Chandidas; Assamese — Sankardeva; Marathi — Jnaneshwari.
- Persian and Urdu: Amir Khusrau; Abul Fazl; Faizi; Wali Dakhni (Urdu poetry in the Deccan), Mir Taqi Mir, Mirza Ghalib; Iqbal ('Saare Jahan Se Achha', 1904).
- Modern Indian: Bankim Chandra (Anandamath, 1882 — 'Vande Mataram'), Tagore (Nobel 1913 for Gitanjali), Premchand (Godan, 1936), Subramania Bharati, Sarojini Naidu; Jnanpith Award from 1965 (G. Sankara Kurup first).
- Punjabi tradition: Baba Farid's shlokas; Adi Granth (1604); Bhai Gurdas's Vaaran; Janamsakhis; Dasam Granth; qissas — Damodar (Heer, Akbar's time), Waris Shah (Heer, 1766), Peelu (Mirza–Sahiban), Hashim Shah (Sassi–Punnu), Qadir Yar (Puran Bhagat); Shah Muhammad's 'Jangnama' on the First Anglo-Sikh War.
- Modern Punjabi: Bhai Vir Singh (Sundari 1898, epic Rana Surat Singh; first Punjabi Sahitya Akademi award 1955), Puran Singh, Dhani Ram Chatrik, Nanak Singh, Amrita Pritam (Jnanpith 1981), Gurdial Singh (Jnanpith 1999), Shiv Kumar Batalvi (Sahitya Akademi 1967 for 'Loona', youngest recipient), Surjit Patar, Pash.

### Performing arts — classical, folk and Punjab

- Texts: Bharata's Natyashastra (rasa theory, 9 rasas with shanta added later), Matanga's Brihaddeshi (first defines raga), Sarangadeva's Sangita Ratnakara (13th c.), Man Singh Tomar's Mankutuhal (dhrupad, Gwalior).
- Classical dances recognised by Sangeet Natak Akademi (8): Bharatanatyam (Rukmini Devi Arundale, Kalakshetra 1936), Kathak (Lucknow, Jaipur, Banaras gharanas), Kathakali (Vallathol's Kerala Kalamandalam, 1930), Kuchipudi (Siddhendra Yogi), Odissi (from Mahari and Gotipua), Manipuri (Ras Lila), Mohiniyattam, Sattriya (added 2000); the Ministry of Culture also lists Chhau.
- Music: Hindustani forms dhrupad, khayal (Sadarang–Adarang), thumri, tappa (from Punjab camel-drivers' songs, refined by Shori Miyan); V.N. Bhatkhande's 10 thaats; Carnatic trinity (Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, Syama Sastri) and Purandara Dasa ('father of Carnatic music').
- Punjab music: Gurmat sangeet — the Guru Granth Sahib is arranged in 31 main ragas; Bhai Mardana's rabab; Patiala gharana (Ali Bakhsh and Fateh Ali; Bade Ghulam Ali Khan), Sham Chaurasi gharana (Hoshiarpur); Harballabh Sangeet Sammelan (Jalandhar, since 1875) — among India's oldest music festivals.
- Punjab folk dance and instruments: Bhangra, Giddha, Jhumar, Sammi, Luddi, Malwai Giddha; algoza, tumbi, chimta, dhad–sarangi (for Dhadi vaars); Haryana's Saang/Swang theatre.
- Theatre: Kutiyattam (Sanskrit, Kerala), Yakshagana, Jatra (Bengal), Tamasha (Maharashtra), Bhavai (Gujarat), Nautanki (UP); IPTA (1943) and modern theatre (Habib Tanvir, Balraj Sahni); Punjab's Gursharan Singh (people's theatre).
- UNESCO Intangible Heritage (India): 16 elements on the Representative List, including Kutiyattam, Vedic chanting, Ramlila (2008), Chhau (2010), the Thatheras of Jandiala Guru, Punjab (2014), Yoga (2016), Kumbh Mela (2017), Durga Puja (2021), Garba (2023) and Deepavali (2025).

### Punjab heritage file — monuments, crafts and institutions

- Monuments: Harmandir Sahib (foundation 1588–89, completed 1604; gold leaf under Ranjit Singh c. 1830), Akal Takht, Qila Mubarak (Bathinda; Razia's prison), Qila Mubarak and Sheesh Mahal (Patiala), Gobindgarh Fort (Amritsar), Jagatjit Palace (Kapurthala, French-style, 1908), Moorish Mosque (Kapurthala, modelled on Marrakesh's Koutoubia).
- Mughal-era Punjab: Nurmahal Sarai (Jalandhar), Aam Khas Bagh (Sirhind), Pinjore (Yadavindra) Gardens built by Fidai Khan (Haryana, Aurangzeb's reign), kos minars along the Grand Trunk Road.
- Archaeology: Ropar and Sanghol museums; Kushana sculptures of Sanghol in the Mathura style; Ropar's Harappan-to-medieval sequence.
- Crafts: Phulkari and Bagh embroidery (GI tag, 2011), Punjabi jutti, Thathera brass and copper work of Jandiala Guru (UNESCO 2014), durrie weaving, woodwork of Hoshiarpur (ivory/inlay tradition).
- Institutions: Punjabi University (Patiala, 1962; Punjabi language mandate), Guru Nanak Dev University (Amritsar, 1969), Punjab Digital Library (Chandigarh) for manuscripts; North Zone Cultural Centre (Patiala, 1985) under the Ministry of Culture.
- Fairs and festivals as living culture: Baisakhi (Khalsa 1699), Hola Mohalla (Anandpur Sahib, started by Guru Gobind Singh), Shaheedi Jor Mela (Fatehgarh Sahib), Kila Raipur rural games (Ludhiana).

### Answer-writing tips and common traps

- Questions usually ask 'salient features' or 'continuity and change' — organise by period, then by form (architecture, sculpture, painting, literature, performing arts).
- Always add one regional (Punjab) example: Sanghol sculpture, Pahari painting, Harmandir Sahib, Phulkari, Waris Shah, Patiala gharana.
- Gandhara = grey schist, Hellenistic; Mathura = red sandstone, indigenous; Amaravati = white marble, narrative — do not mix.
- Kailasa temple at Ellora is Rashtrakuta (Krishna I), not Chalukya; Kailasanatha at Kanchi is Pallava (Rajasimha).
- 'Bani Thani' is Kishangarh (Rajasthani), not Pahari; Basohli, Guler and Kangra are Pahari.
- Sattriya became the eighth SNA classical dance in 2000; Chhau is listed by the Ministry of Culture, not SNA's eight.
- Thatheras of Jandiala Guru (2014) is the only UNESCO intangible element specifically from Punjab — a likely PPSC question.

## Mains practice questions (3)

**1.** Distinguish between the Nagara, Dravida and Vesara styles of temple architecture with suitable examples. (150 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: Silpa texts classify temple styles by region and form
- Nagara: square plan, curvilinear shikhara, no boundary wall - Khajuraho, Konark, Odisha sub-style
- Dravida: pyramidal vimana, gopuram, enclosure walls, water tank - Brihadeeswara, Meenakshi
- Vesara: hybrid of the two, star-shaped plans - Chalukyan (Aihole/Pattadakal), Hoysala (Belur, Halebidu)
- Common elements: garbhagriha, mandapa, iconography
- Conclusion: regional diversity within a shared sacred architecture

</details>

**2.** Indo-Islamic architecture represents a synthesis of indigenous and Persian traditions. Elucidate. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: arrival of Turks and the Delhi Sultanate
- Imported elements: true arch, dome, minarets, lime mortar, calligraphy, arabesque, char-bagh
- Indigenous elements: Indian craftsmen, lotus, bell and kalash motifs, chhatris, brackets, local stone
- Evolution: Sultanate (Qutb Minar, Alai Darwaza), provincial styles (Bijapur, Gujarat, Bengal), Mughal climax (Humayun's Tomb, Fatehpur Sikri, Taj Mahal)
- Influence on later Sikh, Rajput and colonial Indo-Saracenic architecture (Harmandir Sahib example)
- Conclusion: architecture as evidence of cultural synthesis

</details>

**3.** Assess the contribution of Bhakti and Sufi traditions to the growth of vernacular literature in India, with special reference to Punjab. (250 words)

<details><summary>Answer outline</summary>

- Intro: medieval devotional movements moved religion from Sanskrit/Persian to people's languages
- Bhakti saints: Kabir, Tulsidas, Surdas, Mirabai, Namdev, Tukaram - regional languages flourish
- Sufi poets: Amir Khusrau, Baba Farid - earliest Punjabi poetry; Bulleh Shah, Shah Hussain, Waris Shah
- Guru Granth Sahib as a compendium of Bhakti and Sufi bani; standardisation of Gurmukhi
- Social message: equality, critique of ritual, communal harmony
- Conclusion: foundation of modern Indian literatures and composite culture

</details>
