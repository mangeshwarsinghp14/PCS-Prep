**Q1.** Standard of Dissent in Indian Electioneering: How Low is too Low? *(50 marks, 800 words)*

<details><summary>Model answer points</summary>

*Why it matters:* Election discourse essays recur near elections.

*Intro:* Open with an example of personal abuse dominating a campaign instead of issues.

*Points:*
- Dissent is the lifeblood of democracy; campaigns are where it is most visible.
- Decline: personal attacks, hate speech, communal appeals, fake news, deepfakes.
- Causes: polarisation, social media virality, competitive populism, weak enforcement of Model Code.
- Effects: voter cynicism, violence, erosion of trust in institutions.
- Legal limits: RPA Section 123 (corrupt practices), Section 125, ECI's Model Code; Abhiram Singh (2017) — no votes in the name of religion/caste.
- Remedies: ECI's stronger action, party codes, fact-checking, media ethics, voter education.
- Balance: robust criticism is healthy; abuse and incitement are not.

*Punjab:*
- Punjab campaigns also see personal attacks and online misinformation; civic groups promote issue-based debates.

*Conclusion:* Democracy needs sharp dissent with civility — the line is crossed when speech insults, divides or incites.

**Q2.** Mass Media versus Social Media: A Prognosis. *(50 marks, 800 words)*

<details><summary>Model answer points</summary>

*Why it matters:* Media and technology essays recur.

*Intro:* Contrast a newspaper's edited report with a viral WhatsApp forward.

*Points:*
- Mass media: gatekeeping, editorial accountability, but corporate and political pressure, TRP race.
- Social media: democratised voice, speed, citizen journalism, mobilisation.
- Problems of social media: misinformation, echo chambers, hate speech, algorithmic manipulation, privacy.
- Convergence: legacy media going digital; influencers as news sources.
- Regulation: IT Rules 2021, fact-check units, DPDP Act 2023; platform responsibility.
- Prognosis: hybrid ecosystem; credibility will be the scarcest resource; media literacy essential.

*Punjab:*
- Punjabi diaspora media and YouTube channels shape opinion in Punjab — both a strength and a misinformation risk.

*Conclusion:* The future belongs to whichever media earns trust; citizens must become discerning consumers.

**Q3.** Global Currency Wars and their implications for India. *(50 marks, 800 words)*

<details><summary>Model answer points</summary>

*Why it matters:* Specialised economic topic; rarely repeated in this form.

*Intro:* Define currency war: competitive devaluation to boost exports.

*Points:*
- History: 1930s beggar-thy-neighbour; 2010 'currency war' remark after quantitative easing.
- Tools: interest-rate cuts, QE, intervention, capital controls.
- US–China dispute over yuan valuation; dollar dominance and sanctions.
- Effects on India: capital flow volatility, rupee swings, imported inflation, export competitiveness.
- India's tools: RBI forex reserves, managed float, rupee trade settlement (2022), UPI links abroad.
- De-dollarisation debates, BRICS payment ideas.
- Way forward: strong fundamentals, diversified trade, credible monetary policy.

*Punjab:*


*Conclusion:* India gains most by stability and credibility rather than joining currency races.

**Q4.** Artificial Intelligence and Its Impact on our World. *(50 marks, 800 words)*

<details><summary>Model answer points</summary>

*Why it matters:* AI is the most current technology theme; very likely in future essays.

*Intro:* Open with everyday AI — voice assistants, chatbots, crop advisories.

*Points:*
- What AI is: machine learning, generative AI.
- Benefits: productivity, healthcare diagnosis, agriculture, education, governance (IndiaAI Mission, 2024).
- Risks: job displacement, bias, deepfakes, privacy, concentration of power, autonomous weapons.
- Ethics and regulation: EU AI Act (2024), global AI summits; India's light-touch, innovation-friendly approach.
- Inequality: digital divide between nations and within societies.
- Human role: reskilling, human-in-the-loop, values in design.
- Prognosis: AI as a tool for human flourishing if governed well.

*Punjab:*
- AI can help Punjab with pest prediction, water management and stubble monitoring; also risk of fake content in local politics.

*Conclusion:* AI will reshape the world; the task is to make it fair, safe and human-centred.

**Q5.** Implications of Rising Consumerism in the Indian Middle class. *(50 marks, 800 words)*

<details><summary>Model answer points</summary>

*Why it matters:* Society and economy essays recur.

*Intro:* Start with mall culture, EMIs and online sales.

*Points:*
- Drivers: liberalisation, rising incomes, credit, e-commerce, advertising, urbanisation.
- Positive: demand-driven growth, jobs, better quality of life.
- Negative: household debt, materialism, status anxiety, waste and environmental cost.
- Cultural shift from savings to spending; impact on family values.
- Inequality and exclusion of the poor from the consumption story.
- Way forward: mindful consumption (Mission LiFE), financial literacy, circular economy.

*Punjab:*
- Punjab's high consumption (luxury cars, lavish weddings) alongside rural debt shows the downside.

*Conclusion:* Consumerism can drive growth, but balance and sustainability must guide it.

**Q6.** Water Crisis and Riparian Laws. *(50 marks, 800 words)*

<details><summary>Model answer points</summary>

*Why it matters:* Water disputes and scarcity are central to Punjab (SYL, riparian rights, Indus Waters Treaty).

*Intro:* Open with Punjab's falling water table and the long-running SYL dispute.

*Points:*
- India's water stress: per-capita availability falling; groundwater over-extraction.
- Riparian principle: rights to water belong to those through whose land the river flows; vs equitable apportionment.
- Indian framework: Article 262, Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956; tribunals (Cauvery, Krishna, Ravi–Beas).
- Punjab's position: riparian state on Sutlej, Beas, Ravi; Punjab Termination of Agreements Act 2004 struck down (Presidential reference, 2016).
- International: Indus Waters Treaty 1960, placed in abeyance by India in April 2025; Helsinki rules, UN Watercourses Convention (1997).
- Solutions: demand management, crop diversification, water pricing, river basin organisations, data sharing, cooperative federalism.

*Punjab:*
- SYL canal, Ravi–Beas water sharing and BBMB disputes are core Punjab issues.

*Conclusion:* Law alone cannot solve water crises; equity, efficiency and cooperation must go together.
